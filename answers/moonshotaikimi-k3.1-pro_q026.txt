**All three—but the passage’s main emphasis is that speech reveals character.**

Jesus uses two complementary images:

- **“Abundance” suggests overflow.** What fills the heart eventually spills out through the mouth.
- **“Treasure” suggests a storehouse.** Speech draws on an accumulated inner supply—desires, convictions, loyalties, and habits—rather than arising from nothing at the moment of speaking. In biblical language, the “heart” is not merely emotion but the center of one’s inner life and moral orientation.

Accordingly, words function in several ways:

1. **As symptoms:** Like fruit indicating the condition of a tree (Matt. 12:33), speech indicates what is happening within. In context, the Pharisees’ claim that Jesus casts out demons by Beelzebul is not treated as a mere verbal mistake. Their interpretation of God’s work exposes a settled hostility and spiritual blindness.

2. **As morally accountable choices:** The good and evil persons actively “bring forth” what is in their treasuries. This does not mean every word is a completely free, isolated decision; character shapes and constrains speech. Yet Jesus immediately adds that people will give account for their words (12:36–37). Their origin in the heart therefore does not excuse them.

3. **As revelations:** Speech makes otherwise hidden allegiances visible. Especially decisive is how people speak about Jesus and the work of the Spirit: their response discloses what they ultimately value and refuse.

The passage should not, however, be turned into the claim that every isolated sentence is an infallible biopsy of the soul. People can deceive, flatter, speak carelessly, or utter something good from mixed motives. Jesus is addressing characteristic moral overflow—and here, a particularly grave and deliberate rejection—rather than offering a mechanical rule for interpreting every remark.

So the words are **chosen expressions with diagnostic force**: neither accidental noises nor morally neutral tools. They arise from an inner treasury, reveal something of that treasury, and leave the speaker responsible for what has been brought out.
