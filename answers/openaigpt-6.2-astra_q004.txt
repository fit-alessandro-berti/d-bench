Proverbs asks for wholehearted reliance on God, not the abandonment of judgment. The key word is *lean*: the warning is not against using your understanding, but against resting the whole weight of your confidence on it. Indeed, the same chapter calls the person who gains understanding happy (Proverbs 3:13).

Humble self-distrust says, “Even when my reasons seem sound, I may be mistaken, especially about a choice I want to make.” Anti-intellectualism says, “Reasons and evidence do not matter.” The difference shows up in how a person responds to correction: humility listens, while anti-intellectualism can dismiss a challenge by saying, “God is directing me.”

To acknowledge God “in all thy ways” is to bring the choice itself before him, not merely to ask him to bless a decision already made. That means thinking carefully, seeking counsel, and being willing to change course. The trust lies in following what is right even when you cannot foresee where the path will lead.
