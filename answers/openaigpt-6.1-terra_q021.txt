Proverbs links pride to collapse because arrogance does more than make a person morally culpable: it can distort how that person sees reality. The proud may exaggerate their own strength, dismiss warnings, and resist correction. Their confidence then makes them less able to recognize the danger that precedes a fall.

The contrast with “the lowly” adds a social dimension. To be “of an humble spirit with the lowly” is to value fellowship with people of modest status over the prestige and profit of joining the proud in dividing “the spoil.” Arrogance, by contrast, can make status and gain seem more important than justice or solidarity—and make the experiences of those beneath one’s notice seem irrelevant.

The proverb does not suggest that poverty automatically makes someone virtuous. Rather, it presents humility as both a moral and a perceptual strength: the humble are more open to limits, correction, and other people. Pride blinds partly because it persuades a person that such openness is unnecessary.
