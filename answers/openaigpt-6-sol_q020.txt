A **root** is hidden before its effects are visible. The image suggests that bitterness is not merely one angry act; it can be an inward, growing disposition that eventually produces words and actions. By the time it “springs up,” it may have taken hold for some time.

Its damage is also **delayed and spreading**. An unresolved grievance can turn into suspicion, harshness, or retaliation. When someone repeatedly voices that resentment, others may adopt it, take sides, or begin treating the same person with hostility. That is how one root can “trouble” a community and “defile many.”

The image likely echoes Deuteronomy 29:18, where a poisonous root threatens the covenant community. So Hebrews’ warning is broader than “don’t feel angry”: it calls believers to watch for a harmful inward turning—away from grace, peace, and holiness—before its fruit affects everyone.
