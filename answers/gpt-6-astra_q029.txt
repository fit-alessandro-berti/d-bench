The posture required is **moral seriousness without moral superiority**. Paul assumes that the fault should be addressed, but he also insists that the person correcting must stand beside the offender as a fellow vulnerable human being—not above them as though incapable of falling.

The three elements belong together:

- **Restoration gives correction its purpose.** “Restore” means putting something back into proper condition—the word can be used for mending or setting right. The aim is not to win, humiliate, or establish the corrector’s righteousness, but to help the person return to faithful living. The offender is someone to recover, not merely a fault to expose.

- **Meekness governs correction’s manner.** Gentleness is not indifference to wrongdoing. It is the refusal to make truth an instrument of contempt or domination. In the context of Galatians, gentleness is fruit of the Spirit (5:22–23). Thus “ye which are spiritual” is not permission for a superior class to judge others; their fitness to restore must show itself in how they treat the fallen.

- **Self-suspicion guards the corrector’s heart.** “Considering thyself” means remaining alert to one’s own susceptibility to temptation. It need not mean doubting every moral judgment or pretending not to recognize a real wrong. It means refusing the thought, “I am the sort of person who could never fall.” The corrector also needs grace and may be tempted even during correction—toward pride, harshness, or self-righteousness.

They are kept together because each protects the others. **Restoration without meekness can become coercion; meekness without restoration can become avoidance; correction without self-watchfulness can become self-exaltation.**

The next verse—“Bear ye one another’s burdens”—reinforces this posture. Correction is an act of shared responsibility, not distancing oneself from someone else’s disgrace.

In short: **“This is wrong, and I want to help you be restored; I speak gently because your dignity matters, and humbly because I too can fall.”**
