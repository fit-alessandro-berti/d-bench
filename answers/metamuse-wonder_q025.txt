It is the **craving to be seen**, not visibility itself.

The key phrase is *“to be seen of them”* — in Greek, *pros to theathēnai autois*, “in order to be gazed at by them.” The same root gives us “theatre.” Jesus is not condemning good deeds that happen to be public; He is condemning good deeds turned into performance.

That distinction is clear from Matthew 5:16:

> “Let your light so shine before men, that they may see your good works, and glorify your Father which is in heaven.”

In 5:16, people see the work and glorify **God**. In 6:1, people see the work and admire **the giver**. The visibility is the same; the intended audience and glory are different.

### Why is that morally dangerous?

**1. It changes the purpose of the act.**  
The needy person quietly becomes a prop in my story about myself. I am no longer simply asking, “What does love require?” but “How will this make me look?”

**2. It corrupts the deed from within.**  
The external act may still help someone — the money still feeds the poor — but the agent is being formed into someone who needs applause in order to do good.

**3. It makes goodness theatrical and competitive.**  
Once reputation is the reward, giving is shaped for optics: more visible causes, more dramatic generosity, more public displays than private faithfulness. Jesus’ next word for such people is “hypocrites” (6:2) — literally, stage actors.

**4. It exchanges one reward for another.**  
“Otherwise ye have no reward of your Father” does not mean God pettily refuses to pay. It means: *you already chose your payment.* If human praise was the thing you wanted, human praise is the thing you get. It is a real reward, but a small and fragile one.

So secrecy in Matthew 6 is a kind of spiritual training. Giving “in secret” (6:4) breaks the addiction to being noticed, and reorders the act toward love of neighbor and trust in God.

The danger, then, is **audience capture**: when the eyes of men become the reason for righteousness, goodness stops being worship or love and becomes self-display.
