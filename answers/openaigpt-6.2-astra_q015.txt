The image is sharp because the exaggeration makes the hypocrisy visible: a man who can spot a speck in his brother’s eye fails to notice a timber in his own. The contrast is not only between the sizes of their faults, but between the kinds of attention he gives them. He scrutinizes his brother and never examines himself. And because the obstruction is in his *eye*, it impairs the very faculty he needs to offer help. “Let me pull out the mote” sounds generous, but such delicate work requires clear sight.

The key words are **“first”** and **“then.”** Jesus does not say the mote is imaginary or that it must be left alone. He tells the would-be helper to remove his own beam—not merely admit it is there—so that he can “see clearly” to help his brother. The rebuke is aimed at correction that exempts the corrector from scrutiny, not at moral discernment itself.
