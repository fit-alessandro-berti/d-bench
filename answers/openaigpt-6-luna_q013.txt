The passage treats injustice not as a missing ingredient to be balanced by more sincere worship, but as a contradiction that makes the worship itself false. Israel performs covenant rituals while breaking the covenant in public life: elsewhere Amos condemns those who exploit the poor, manipulate the courts, and “trample” the needy (Amos 2:6–8; 5:10–12).

So the fraud is **using religious performance to claim God’s favor while resisting the justice God requires**. The feasts and offerings become a kind of cover—or attempted payment—rather than genuine allegiance. Their songs say, in effect, “We honor God,” while their treatment of the vulnerable says otherwise. That is why God does not merely ask them to add justice to their worship: he refuses the worship as a lie.

“Let justice roll down as waters” is therefore not a request for a better balance between ritual and ethics. It names what faithful life must look like: justice and righteousness flowing through the community, not confined to the sanctuary. The target is not worship itself, but worship severed from justice and used to disguise oppression.
