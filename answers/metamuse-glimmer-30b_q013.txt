Amos is not correcting a *quantity* of worship, he is exposing a *kind* of worship. The northern kingdom in the 8th century is prosperous, the cult is busy and correct on the surface — feast days are kept, burnt offerings, grain offerings and peace offerings of the best fat cattle are brought, the temple music is performed — and at the same time the social order is being systematically reversed.

Amos 5:11-13 has just said:

> You who trample the needy and bring the poor of the land to ruin... you build houses of hewn stone, but you shall not dwell in them; you plant pleasant vineyards, but you shall not drink their wine... for I know how many are your transgressions and how great are your sins, you oppressors of the righteous, takers of bribes, pushers aside of the needy in the gate.

The worship that follows in 5:21-24 is not a separate, neutral religious sphere that could be improved by a better balance with ethics. It is the cultic legitimation of that exploitation.

That is why the language is so absolute: *I hate, I despise, I will not smell, I will not accept, I will not regard, I will not hear*. The Hebrew is cultic language for abomination. God says the smell of the sacrifice is a stench, not a sweet savor, because the offering is bought with the wealth wrung out of the poor, and the music is sung with a conscience that has been quieted by the very ritual it ought to convict. To keep the feasts while crushing the afflicted is to use God as an alibi.

The fraud is spiritual and moral hypocrisy as self-deception and social deception:

* **Transactional piety.** The people act as if Yahweh can be managed, purchased and placated with the correct calendar and the correct animals, while the covenant stipulations about justice for the widow, orphan and stranger in Lev 19; Deut 24 are ignored. Worship becomes a way to buy divine favor and to clear a guilty conscience without changing conduct.
* **Ritual laundering.** The cult is used to make injustice appear holy. The temple becomes a moral cover. The feast days, which in the covenant were meant to rehearse liberation from Egypt and dependence on Yahweh, are now used to celebrate an economy of oppression. The worship is complicit in the violence it pretends to atone for.
* **Compartmentalization.** Sacred and secular are split. God is invited into the sanctuary but barred from the marketplace and the city gate. That is why Amos collapses them: “Let judgment run down as waters, and righteousness as a mighty stream.” Justice and righteousness are not an add-on to worship; in the prophetic theology they *are* worship. True cultic obedience is the outward expression of an inward alignment with the character of a God who hears the cry of the oppressed.

Hence Amos cannot ask for “a bit more justice alongside your worship.” If worship is offered in a context of injustice, it ceases to be worship and becomes blasphemy. The noise of the songs must be taken away because it is the sound of a people deceiving themselves that they can be religious and unjust at the same time.
