The text’s main logic is that **treasure trains or directs the heart**, not merely that it reveals it.

Jesus says, “For where your treasure is, there will your heart be also” (Matt 6:21). The order is crucial: **the heart follows the treasure**, not the other way around. The common reversal—“where your heart is, your treasure will be”—makes the heart the cause and treasure the effect. But Jesus says the opposite. He commands, “Lay up for yourselves treasures in heaven,” and then gives the reason: because your heart will go where your treasure is. So the point is formative: **what you store and invest in pulls your love and loyalty toward it.**

That does not mean there is no revealing aspect at all. A person’s treasure can indeed reveal the heart. But in this passage, Jesus’ emphasis is more on how treasure **shapes** the heart. He tells his hearers to redirect their treasure, not merely to examine it, precisely because the heart will follow.

How do possession, attention, and love become tied together?

- **Possession / treasure** is what you seek to secure, protect, and build your life upon. Earthly treasure is vulnerable—moth, rust, thieves—so it demands constant guarding.
- **Attention** follows possession. What you store up you also watch over, think about, worry about, and manage. Earthly treasure tends to absorb the mind with anxiety and maintenance. Heavenly treasure frees attention and directs it toward God and his kingdom.
- **Love / heart** follows attention. In biblical language, the “heart” is not just feelings; it is the center of will, loyalty, desire, and worship. Jesus later says, “You cannot serve God and money” (Matt 6:24). What you give your attention and resources to gradually becomes your master and your love.

So the chain in this passage runs like this:  
**What you possess or store becomes what you attend to, and what you attend to becomes what you love.**

Thus Jesus is not only saying, “Your treasure shows where your heart is.” He is saying, “Store your treasure in heaven, because your heart will follow it there.” Treasure, in this sense, is a discipline: what you invest in trains your heart toward God or away from him.
