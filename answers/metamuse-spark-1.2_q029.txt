In Galatians 6:1 correction is not framed as punishment, but as **surgery** - and Paul gives you the *goal*, the *method*, and the *guardrail* together because one without the others corrupts.

> "Brethren, if a man be overtaken in a fault [paraptoma], ye which are spiritual, restore [katartizete] such an one in the spirit of meekness [praotes]; considering [skopon] thyself, lest thou also be tempted."

### 1. The Posture Required: Spiritual Maturity, Not Moral Superiority

**"Ye which are spiritual"** does not mean an elite class. In context of Galatians 5:16,22-25, it means *those who are themselves walking by the Spirit* - bearing the fruit of the Spirit, living under grace not law. The qualification to correct is not sinlessness, but Spirit-dependence.

**"If a man be overtaken" [prolēmphthē]** - The image is not a man running into sin, but sin surprising and capturing a man. This is not the defiant, unrepentant person of 1 Corinthians 5, but a brother surprised, trapped, weighed down by a trespass. The posture required assumes solidarity, not superiority.

### 2. Why The Three Are Kept Together

Paul deliberately binds them as **Telos, Tone, and Check.**

#### a) RESTORATION [katartizo] - The Telos
This is the goal. *Katartizo* is a medical and artisan word: to set a broken bone, to mend a torn net, to equip and make complete again. As in Matthew 4:21 where fishermen were *mending* nets.

Correction is not to shame, cancel, win an argument, or make an example of someone. It is to *make whole* and bring them back to usefulness in the community.

> Without restoration, correction becomes retribution.

#### b) MEEKNESS [praotes] - The Tone
*Praotes* is not weakness or timidity. It is power under control - gentleness that comes from strength, like a gentle surgeon.

If the goal is restoration, the method cannot be harshness, harsh law, ridicule, or self-righteous anger. A broken bone cannot be set with a hammer. Meekness recognizes that the person is a brother, not an enemy.

> Without meekness, restoration becomes brutal. You may be theologically right and pastorally destructive.

This is critical in Galatians. The whole letter is against law-based, harsh, exacting religion. To restore legalistically would contradict the gospel of grace Paul just preached.

#### c) SELF-SUSPICION [skopein heauton] - The Guardrail
"Considering thyself, attentively looking at yourself, lest thou also be tempted."

This is the constant self-watch. `Skopeo` means to keep an eye on, to watch carefully.

Tempted by what? Two temptations:

1.  **The same sin** - "You are made of the same flesh, under the same pressures. You are not immune."
2.  **The temptation of pride** - the temptation to become the Pharisee in Luke 18:11 - "God, I thank thee that I am not as other men are." The most dangerous temptation for the corrector is self-righteousness, harsh judgment, and spiritual pride.

Self-suspicion kills pride. It reminds the restorer: *I am one beggar telling another beggar where to find bread. I stand only by grace.*

> Without self-suspicion, meekness becomes performance and restoration becomes pride. You start as a physician and end as a judge.

### In Summary:

Paul keeps them together because they correct each other:

* **Restoration without meekness** is cruelty in the name of truth.
* **Meekness without restoration** is sentimentality that leaves a brother broken.
* **Both without self-suspicion** is Pharisaism - it divides the church into the "spiritual" and the "failures."

The moral posture required is therefore **humble solidarity, not elevated judgment.**

The corrector does not stand *over* the fallen saying "How could you?" but *beside* him saying "This could be me - let me help carry you back," which is exactly what verse 2 goes on to say: "Bear ye one another's burdens, and so fulfil the law of Christ."
