## The Connection: Overflow and Treasure

Jesus uses two images. **"Abundance"** (*perisseuma*) means overflow, what a vessel can't contain. **"Treasure"** (*thēsauros*) means a storehouse deliberately accumulated over time. Together they suggest the heart is a reservoir filled gradually, and speech is what spills or is drawn out of it. The moral stockpile is hidden, but not permanently. Words are where it becomes visible.

The context matters. Jesus is answering Pharisees who have just attributed his exorcisms to Beelzebul (12:24). His point is that their slander is not a slip. It reveals what they have stored up. It also follows the tree-and-fruit image in v. 33: "make the tree good, or make the tree corrupt."

## Symptoms, Choices, or Revelations?

**Symptoms: yes.** Speech is downstream of something deeper. The "how can ye, being evil, speak good things?" question treats speech as a diagnostic sign. A person's words are evidence of their condition, not the condition itself.

**Revelations: yes, and this is probably the dominant emphasis.** The passage is about disclosure. What is hidden in the heart comes out. The verb "bringeth forth" (*ekballei*) is the same verb used for casting out demons in this chapter, which suggests something interior being brought into the open. Verses 36–37 reinforce this: people will give account for their words, because the words expose the person.

**Choices: yes, but indirectly.** The passage does not say each utterance is a fresh, neutral decision. It locates responsibility further back, in what has been accumulated. Yet several things keep it from being fatalistic:

- v. 33 commands, "*make* the tree good," which implies agency over the source.
- Treasure is *laid up*, so it is built by earlier choices, habits, and loves.
- v. 37 says "by thy words thou shalt be justified, and by thy words thou shalt be condemned," which holds people accountable for speech as their own.

So words are less like isolated choices and more like the cumulative expression of many prior ones.

## A Few Complications

1. **Hypocrisy.** People can say good things with evil hearts (Matt 15:8, "This people honoureth me with their lips, but their heart is far from me"). The passage seems to speak of what flows *abundantly* and habitually, not of any single carefully managed statement. Overflow, unlike performance, is hard to control over time.

2. **"Being evil."** Jesus does not say evil people can never say anything good. He says their good speech cannot be the reliable, characteristic product of their heart. The charge is about the source, not about every sentence.

3. **A feedback loop (an inference, not stated).** Elsewhere, Scripture treats speech as formative too (Prov 18:21; James 3). If words come from the heart but also reinforce it, the relationship may run both ways. The text emphasizes the first direction, but doesn't exclude the second.

## Summary

The passage treats words as **revelations of a hidden moral condition**, which makes them **symptoms**, and treats them as the responsible expression of a character that has been **built through choices**. The heart is the moral center, speech is its outflow, and Jesus' concern is that the Pharisees' problem is not their vocabulary but their source.
