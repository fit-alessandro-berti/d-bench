In Ezekiel 36:26–27, the difference is not merely *behavior* versus *motivation*, but **ontology**: external compliance is a set of actions performed by an unchanged self; a “new heart” is a new kind of self.

### 1. External compliance vs. a new heart
**External compliance** is what Israel had often practiced: ritually keeping statutes while the “heart” remained far from God (Isaiah 29:13). The Law was written on tablets of stone *outside* them; they could obey for reward, fear, or social pressure, but the obedience was brittle, self-interested, and ultimately “dead works.” It treats holiness as a behavior-modification problem.

**A new heart** is God’s promise to change the *source* of obedience. He says *“I* will give you… *I* will put my spirit *within* you… and cause you to walk” (v. 26–27). The initiative is entirely divine. The result is not just that Israel does God’s statutes, but that they *want* to, because God has replaced the controlling center of their will. This anticipates Jeremiah’s “new covenant” (Jeremiah 31:31–34), where God’s law is written *on* the heart, not just on stone tablets.

### 2. Why hardness is described as a material condition
The Hebrew imagery is stark: **stone (*even*) versus flesh (*basar*)**. A “stony heart” is not merely “stubborn”; it is **dead, inert, and impermeable**. Stone does not bleed, feel, or respond; it simply reflects back what is thrown at it. In biblical anthropology, the *lev* (heart) is the seat of intellect, will, and emotion—the “inner person.” When it becomes a stone, the creature is constitutionally unable to love God, repent, or generate genuine obedience.

That is why God does not say, “I will polish your stone heart” or “I will give you better instructions.” He says:
- **“I will take away”** (remove it),
- **“I will give”** (transplant a living organ).

It is surgical, not cosmetic. A heart of flesh is living tissue—soft, sensitive, capable of pain and affection. Only a heart that can *feel* can truly *obey* in love rather than coercion.

### 3. The mechanism: God’s own Spirit
The promise is completed not just by a new organ but by **indwelling presence**: “I will put my spirit within you” (v. 27). The “new spirit” is God’s own life-giving presence (the same Spirit later revealed in Ezekiel 37, the valley of dry bones). The result is that obedience becomes **fruit**, not **manufacture**: “cause you to walk in my statutes, and ye shall keep my judgments.”

### In short
- **Hardness is a material condition** because sin is not just a set of bad choices but a death-condition of the inner being; a dead heart cannot produce living obedience no matter how strict the external code.
- **A new heart** is a gift of God that changes what a person *is*, enabling what they *do*. External compliance asks, “Can I behave well enough?” The new covenant promises, “I will make you the kind of person who wants to live well.”

This is why Christian theology sees this passage as a foundation for regeneration: God does not improve the old self; He replaces it with a new creation, indwelt by His Spirit, so that holiness becomes natural rather than forced.
