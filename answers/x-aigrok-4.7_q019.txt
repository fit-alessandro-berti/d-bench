The contradiction is not merely bad manners. It is a split loyalty inside one person.

James has just said the tongue is a small member that steers the whole life, like a bit or a rudder, and that it is a fire no one can fully tame (3:3–8). Verses 9–10 name the clearest proof of that disorder: the same mouth blesses “God, even the Father,” and curses human beings “made after the similitude of God.” Blessing the Father and cursing his image cannot both be true reverence. To curse the likeness is to strike at the one whose likeness it is. A spring does not yield fresh water and salt water together (3:11–12); a heart that does is not merely inconsistent in speech but divided at the source.

Speech exposes that division so quickly because it is the least mediated act of the self. Deeds can be delayed, staged, or hidden. Words come out under almost no cover—in irritation, argument, worship, and ordinary talk—so they leak what the person actually honors before character has time to arrange a better face. James’s point is not that language is uniquely evil, but that it is the first and hardest place the untamed heart shows. The mouth that can praise God and curse his image in the same breath has not yet been brought under one Lord.
