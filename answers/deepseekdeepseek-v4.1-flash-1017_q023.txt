In Isaiah 55:7, “way” and “thoughts” are parallel Hebrew terms describing the whole person, not two unrelated things.

- **“Way”** (Hebrew *derekh*) means a person’s path, conduct, habits, and manner of life.
- **“Thoughts”** (Hebrew *machshavot*) means intentions, plans, schemes, and inner reasoning—the mind and imagination from which actions grow.

So repentance here is not merely cosmetic. The sinner must **forsake the path he walks** and **the inward plans and desires that lead him down that path**. It rules out two false repentances:

1. **Externalism**—changing behavior while keeping the same inward rebellion.
2. **Privatized spirituality**—feeling inwardly sorry while continuing in concrete sin.

**Outward conduct** matters because God calls for a real change of direction: the wicked forsakes “his way.” **Inward imagination** matters because actions do not come from nowhere; motives, fantasies, resentments, plans, and self-justifying narratives must also be surrendered to God. The mind is not neutral. Repentance means turning not only from wrong deeds but from the inner pattern that cultivates and excuses them. This does not mean every stray tempting thought is the same as a deed, but it does mean we are responsible for what we entertain, rehearse, and pursue inwardly.

Most importantly, the verse grounds mercy in **God’s character and promise**, not in the completeness of our repentance: “let him return unto the Lord, and he will have mercy upon him; and to our God, for he will abundantly pardon.” The call is to return, but the pardon is God’s abundant gift. Mercy is possible because God is more willing to forgive than we are to turn. Yet mercy is not cheap: it meets the whole person—conduct and thought—and calls for a real turning toward God.

So the verse implies: **repentance must be wholehearted rather than external only; inward life matters as much as outward behavior; and God’s mercy is wide enough to pardon both the wrong we have done and the wrong we have imagined and planned—when we return to Him.**
