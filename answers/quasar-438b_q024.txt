**External compliance** means your actions line up with the statutes from the outside: you obey, perform the judgments, “walk” in the required path. But you may do so reluctantly, strategically, under fear, or with inner resistance intact. The law remains outside you as a constraint.

Receiving a **“new heart”** is different: it is a change in the inner seat of willing, feeling, and judging. Ezekiel’s language says he will not merely make you follow his statutes; he will put a new principle inside you—“a new heart” and “a new spirit”—so that your own inward dispositions become the kind that can keep and enact his judgments. Compliance becomes secondary to conversion.

“Hardness” is treated almost materially because “the stony heart” is described as an object lodged “in your flesh,” something that must be taken out and replaced by “an heart of flesh.” Stone suggests rigidity, coldness, immobility, resistance to inward formation. Flesh suggests vitality, sensitivity, malleability, living receptivity. So the hard heart is not just a metaphor for bad behavior; it is pictured as a hardened internal organ that blocks transformation. It cannot be softened by mere commands, so it must be excised and replaced.

Thus the structure is:

- **External compliance**: you move according to the statutes.
- **New heart**: you become the kind of being for whom those statutes are inwardly held.
- **Hardness**: a recalcitrant core, figured as stone, that must be removed because it prevents the fleshly heart from forming.
