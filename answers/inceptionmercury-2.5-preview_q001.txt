

The passage portrays moral agency as a capacity for choice and self-mastery. Sin is depicted not merely as an internal flaw but as a lurking force, personified like a predator waiting at the threshold. It possesses a "desire" to dominate, yet the individual is granted authority to control it.

Responsibility rests on the person to regulate their emotional response and actively choose good conduct, thereby maintaining sovereignty over their actions rather than surrendering to unruly impulses. The text suggests that while temptation may approach like a beast, the human agent holds the power to rule over it.
