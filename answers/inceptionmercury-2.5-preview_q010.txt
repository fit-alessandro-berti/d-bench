

This text praises a power rooted in self-emptying, or kenosis. It is strength manifested through voluntary surrender and service rather than coercion. The authority here is not held tightly but given away.

Such humility paired with greatness feels unsettling because it subverts the logic of hierarchy. Open domination is predictable; we understand the mechanics of force and fear. But when supreme status chooses servitude, it creates a moral paradox. It disarms resistance by refusing to compete on usual terms. This dynamic forces a confrontation with our own ambition, acting as a silent mirror rather than a loud command. It demands transformation instead of mere compliance, which can be more invasive to the ego than any threat.
