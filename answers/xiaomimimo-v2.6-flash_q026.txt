# Speech as the Treasure-Vault's Outlet

## The "Stockpile" Metaphor at Work

The heart in this passage functions as a **treasury vault** (θησαυρός) — a deliberately organized storage space. This is not an image of the heart as a passive reservoir. A "treasure" has been *accumulated, sorted, and kept*. It implies:

- **Gradual accumulation**: One does not build a treasury in a moment. The stockpile was assembled through repeated choices, habits, and dispositions over time.
- **Hidden interiority**: Treasures are stored, not displayed. What is in the heart remains invisible to observers — until speech exposes it.
- **Overflow pressure**: The phrase "out of the *abundance* (περισσεία) of the heart" introduces the idea of **pressure toward expression**. When a treasury overflows, what is stored must come out. Speech is the spillover point.

So Jesus frames the moral interior as something like a treasury that *cannot remain sealed*. The mouth is the outlet pipe.

---

## Are Words Symptoms, Choices, or Revelations?

The passage supports **all three**, and the categories are not merely additive — they form a causal chain.

### 1. Words as **Revelations** (What is hidden becomes seen)

The primary rhetorical purpose of this passage is *exposure*. Jesus is addressing Pharisees who have just attributed his exorcisms to Beelzebub. Their evil words have revealed something their words were *designed* to conceal — their own corruption. The whole structure of the passage says:

> *"You cannot speak good things while being evil, because your words give away your stockpile."*

Words function as a **window into what would otherwise remain hidden**. The heart's treasure is invisible precisely because it is interior. Speech renders it visible.

### 2. Words as **Symptoms** (What is present in the interior manifests outwardly)

The medical metaphor is strong. A symptom is an outward sign of an underlying condition — it is *caused by* the interior state rather than independent of it. The passage insists on **correspondence**:

- Good treasure → good things brought forth
- Evil treasure → evil things brought forth

The mouth does not manufacture its own content *ex nihilo*. It **draws from a pre-existing store**. In this sense, speech is an involuntary **manifestation** — as a fever is a manifestation of infection. The Pharisees' evil speech is a *symptom* of what they have stored up.

### 3. Words as **Choices** (The act of bringing forth involves agency)

Crucially, Jesus does not describe speech as a mere *leak* or an *involuntary reflex*. He says:

> "A good man **bringeth forth** (ἐκβάλλει) good things; an evil man... **bringeth forth** evil things."

The active verb implies **intentionality**. The person *reaches into* the stockpile and *produces* its contents. The evil Pharisees did not accidentally say evil things — they chose to attribute Jesus' good works to a demon. Their words were:

- Drawn from an evil stockpile (symptom)
- Revealing of what was hidden (revelation)
- Actively produced by deliberate agency (choice)

---

## The Causal Chain

The three categories form a sequence rather than a mere list:

```
INTERIOR STATE (accumulated over time)
        ↓
    STOCKPILE / TREASURE
        ↓
   CHOICE to bring it forth
        ↓
   WORDS EXPRESSED
        ↓
   REVELATION of what was hidden
```

The heart's moral condition is the **root**. The stockpile is the **accumulated fruit** of that condition. The choice to speak is the **act of extraction**. The spoken word is the **evidence** — a revelation that functions as a symptom of the interior state.

---

## Why This Matters for the Pharisees

The rhetorical force is devastating precisely because Jesus turns their own speech against them. They believed their words could *redefine reality* (calling Jesus' good works "Beelzebub's works"). Jesus replies: **your words cannot redefine anything; they can only *disclose* what is already stored in you.** Speech is not a tool of self-creation but a mirror of self-possession. You do not become evil by saying evil words — you reveal that you already are, and that you have *stockpiled* that evil through a pattern of accumulated choices.

In short: **words are all three — symptom, choice, and revelation — because speech is the overflow point where an accumulated inner life becomes publicly observable.** The heart is the stockpile; the mouth is the forced outlet; the word is both the act of drawing from the vault and the evidence that exposes its contents.
