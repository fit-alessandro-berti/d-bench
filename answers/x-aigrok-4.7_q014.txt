The text says the heart follows the treasure, not merely that treasure reports the heart.

The command comes first: do not lay up treasure on earth; lay it up in heaven (vv. 19–20). The reason is given with “for” and a future: “where your treasure is, there will your heart be also.” In the Greek, that future (ἔσται) presents the heart’s location as what follows from where the treasure has been placed. Jesus is not only offering a diagnostic (“look at your stores and you will see your loves”). He is giving a motive for moving the stores: put the treasure in heaven, and the heart will be there. Correlation is real—treasure and heart end up together—but the rhetorical force is formative. Treasure trains, draws, and locates the heart. The revealing reading is a fair inference; it is not what the sentence is built to say.

Possession, attention, and love lock together in that “where… there.”

- **Possession.** “Treasure” here is what one stores up and counts as one’s wealth and security. Laying it up is an act of investment, not a mood. Earthly treasure is defined by what can ruin it—moth, rust, thieves—so holding it means holding something that keeps failing. Heavenly treasure is defined by what cannot.
- **Attention.** In this Gospel, the heart is the center of thought, desire, and loyalty, not a private feeling. Where the stores are, attention goes: you watch, worry, and orient yourself toward what you have laid up. The threats in v. 19 are not only warnings of loss; they are the kind of fragility that keeps an earth-stored heart busy with earth.
- **Love.** The verse does not use the word “love,” but the heart’s attachment is the point. What you treat as treasure claims affection and allegiance. The next lines make that explicit: the eye is single or bad according to this inner light, and no one can serve two masters, God and mammon (6:22–24). Love is not a third item beside possession and attention; it is the heart once it has settled where the treasure is.

So the three form one circuit. Storing something as treasure pulls attention after it; sustained attention deepens attachment; attachment confirms the storing. Jesus’ point is that you choose which circuit to enter. You do not get an earthly store and a heavenly heart. The heart will be where the treasure is.
