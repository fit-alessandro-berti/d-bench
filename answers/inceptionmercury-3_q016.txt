The elder brother’s deepest problem is **self-righteousness: pride in his own obedience that makes him unable to receive or rejoice in mercy.**

His anger, envy, and sense of wounded fairness are real, but they are symptoms. The root is that he sees his relationship with the father as a **transaction**: he has served, he has obeyed, and therefore he deserves to be rewarded. When the father shows free, unearned grace to the younger son, the elder brother finds it offensive because it threatens his whole system of earning acceptance.

The father’s reply exposes the true problem:

> “Son, thou art ever with me, and all that I have is thine.” (Luke 15:31)

The elder brother already has the father’s presence and all the father owns. But he does not experience himself as a son; he experiences himself as a servant. He cannot enjoy his own place in the father’s house, and he cannot celebrate the return of his brother, because his heart is fixed on merit, not grace.

So the deepest issue is **pride in obedience**: a self-justifying spirit that begrudges mercy to others and fails to understand that everything in the Father’s kingdom is already gift.
