Philippians is not praising power as the world normally defines it - power over, power to compel, power to be seen as great. It is praising the power of self-limitation.

Christ is "in the form of God" and "equal with God". That is a claim to absolute, uncreated greatness. And then Paul says He does not cling to it as a possession to be exploited. He "emptied himself" - *ekenōsen*, made himself of no reputation - and took the form of a slave, and the likeness of men, and went all the way to obedient death on a cross.

The power being praised is kenotic power: the power to retain identity and dignity while voluntarily renouncing status, rights and self-defence. It is power *for* rather than power *over*. The greatness is not cancelled by the humility; it is shown in the humility. The One who could not be robbed of equality chooses not to use it. The authority is proved precisely by the willingness to be powerless.

That is why a humble greatness is more unsettling than open domination. Open domination is legible. It plays by the rules we know: might makes right, and you can name the enemy, resist him, hate him, or flatter him.

A greatness that chooses humility breaks the rules. It is a power that cannot be bargained with because it refuses to demand. It cannot be discredited as weakness because everyone can see the greatness that is being restrained. It disarms you, because to fight it you would have to fight someone who will not fight back, and to dismiss it you would have to dismiss the cross as nothing.

It also implicates us. Domination lets the dominated keep a moral distance: *he is cruel, I am not*. Humility joined to greatness forces a question: if true greatness looks like this, what does my own desire for honor look like? It invites imitation rather than submission. The humble great one does not need to enforce obedience; he makes obedience desirable, and that is far harder to live with.

In Paul’s terms, that is the mind of Christ: power that is most itself when it serves, and most authoritative when it suffers.
