**1.  The setting of the verse**

Matthew 6:1 is spoken in the middle of the Sermon on the Mount, where Jesus is defining what true “righteousness” looks like **before God**.  He immediately follows the warning about almsgiving with the promise that those who give “in secret” will have their reward “in heaven” (v. 4).  The whole section (vv. 1‑8) is a call to **inner, sincere devotion** rather than outward performance.

**2.  Why public goodness can be morally dangerous**

| Danger | How it shows up in the text |
|--------|-----------------------------|
| **Hypocrisy** – the act becomes a façade for self‑glorification | “…to be seen of them” – the motive is human applause, not love of God or neighbor. |
| **Loss of authenticity** – the heart is not pure, so the deed has no spiritual fruit | When the goal is “to be seen,” the giver is more concerned with image than with the need. |
| **Spiritual self‑deception** – one thinks the reward is earned from men, not from the Father | The verse says “otherwise ye have no reward of your Father which is in heaven.” |
| **Reliance on earthly validation** – the believer’s sense of worth is tied to public praise, not on God’s unseen eye | The “Father which is in heaven” sees what is hidden (v. 4); the danger is that we try to buy that approval. |

In short, the danger is **not the act of giving itself**, but the **corruption of the motive** that underlies it.

**3.  Is the problem the visibility, or the craving to be seen?**

The verse points to the **craving to be seen** as the root problem.  Visibility is the *symptom*; the *disease* is the desire for human recognition.  

- **If the act were truly for God’s glory**, it would be performed in a way that honors Him, regardless of whether anyone else knows about it.  
- **If the act is done to be seen**, it becomes a performance—exactly the kind of “hypocrisy” Jesus denounces elsewhere (cf. Matt 23:25‑26).  

Thus, the verse condemns **public goodness when it is a means to an end** (human praise) rather than an end in itself (love of God and neighbor).  The “visibility” that Jesus warns against is only dangerous **when it is the goal** of the good work.

**4.  Biblical support for this reading**

1. **Matthew 6:4‑6** – “Pray in secret… the Father who sees in secret will reward you.”  The emphasis is on the *heart* of the practitioner, not the audience.  
2. **Luke 6:38** – “Give, and it will be given to you.”  No mention of making a show of it.  
3. **James 2:16** – “Faith without works is dead,” but James also cautions against boasting over works (James 2:4‑6).  The problem is the *motive*, not the deed.  
4. **The Parable of the Pharisee and the Tax‑Collector** (Luke 18:9‑14) – The Pharisee’s prayer is rejected because his motive was self‑exaltation, not humility.

**5.  Theological insight**

- **Reward belongs to God alone.**  The “reward” Jesus mentions is a *gift of grace* from the Father, not a transactional prize earned by public performance.  
- **True righteousness is secret before God.**  The early church fathers (e.g., Augustine) taught that almsgiving must be done “in the privacy of the heart” to avoid the sin of vanity.  
- **Love is the motive of the Kingdom.**  When we give, we imitate Christ’s self‑sacrificial love (Eph 4:28‑30); that love is invisible to the world but evident in the giver’s spirit.

**6.  Practical take‑aways**

| What to do | Why it matters |
|------------|----------------|
| **Give anonymously or in ways that hide the giver** (e.g., drop a donation in a collection box, send a gift without a name) | Removes the temptation to seek applause. |
| **Keep a private record of generosity** (a journal or prayer list) | Helps you see your own motives and grow in humility. |
| **Pray for a heart that loves the needy for their sake, not for your own fame** | Aligns your intention with the kingdom’s values. |
| **Remember that God sees the heart** (Matt 6:4) | Gives confidence that your work is already known to the Father, even if humans never notice. |

**7.  Bottom line**

The moral danger of public goodness in Matthew 6:1 lies in the **craving to be seen by people**—the desire to turn a righteous act into a platform for self‑glorification.  Visibility itself is not sinful; it becomes sinful when it is the *purpose* of the good work.  True righteousness, as Jesus teaches, is **secret before God**, performed out of love for Him and neighbor, and sustained by the assurance that the Father, who sees the hidden deed, will give the true reward.  Guard your motives, and let the “visible” part of your generosity be a by‑product of a heart that is already focused on the invisible, eternal prize.
