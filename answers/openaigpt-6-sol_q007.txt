Luke 6:37 does not say that right and wrong are indistinguishable. A few verses later, Jesus speaks of removing a speck from another person’s eye—*after* attending to the log in one’s own (Luke 6:41–42). The speck still matters, but so do humility and the way we approach the person.

**Discernment** asks, “What happened? Is it harmful? What would be truthful, protective, and restorative?” It can name wrongdoing, set boundaries, and seek accountability.

**The appetite to condemn** wants something more: to pronounce a final verdict on a person, take satisfaction in their disgrace, or treat oneself as above the same need for mercy. Jesus warns against that posture.

So “forgive” need not mean pretending no harm was done or removing every consequence. It means refusing to let a real moral judgment become a desire to reduce someone to their worst act.
