The posture required is restorative humility, not superior judgment. Those who are “spiritual” are told to mend the one overtaken in a fault, and to do it in meekness while watching themselves.

Those three belong together because each corrects the others. Restoration names the aim: the person is to be set right and brought back, not exposed, crushed, or written off. Meekness names the manner: strength held gently, without harshness or self-display. Self-suspicion names the guard: the corrector is not above the same temptation, so pride would turn the act into a fall of its own. Without restoration, meekness can become mere softness; without meekness, restoration becomes punishment; without self-suspicion, both become an occasion for the corrector’s pride. The verse keeps correction inside shared frailty and the Spirit’s gentleness, so that helping another does not become a way of standing over them.
