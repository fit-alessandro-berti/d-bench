## The contradiction: one mouth, two opposite verdicts

James is not merely noting that people are inconsistent. He is pointing at a single organ performing two mutually cancelling acts: *"therewith"* bless we God — *"therewith"* curse we men. The same tongue that ascribes worth to God withdraws worth from a creature. Blessing and cursing are both acts of valuation; they say *this one matters* and *this one is contemptible*. When both come out of the same mouth, the mouth is confessing two different worlds.

The theological sting lies in the clause James deliberately adds: men *"which are made after the similitude of God."* He does not say men who are righteous, or kind, or useful to us. He uses a perfect participle — those who *have been made* and *stand* in God's likeness. The dignity is not a reward for behavior, so it cannot be forfeited by behavior. It is a fact about their origin. Which means the curse cannot be aimed at a man without striking through him at his Maker. In the ancient world, defacing the emperor's statue was not vandalism but treason: the image carries the honor of the original. To bless God while defacing his likeness is to praise the Original and spit on the portrait — and the praise is thereby emptied. The worship is not merely inconsistent with the contempt; it is falsified by it.

So the contradiction is not "a good man who sometimes slips." It is a divided heart (*dipsychos*, James 1:8, 4:8) that has managed to keep its compartments sealed in public and leaks at the seam. What James exposes is a lie inside the blessing itself.

## Why the tongue gives the heart away so fast

**Because speech is overflow, not manufacture.** "Out of the abundance of the heart the mouth speaketh" (Matt. 12:34). Words are the surplus of the interior spilling over the rim. We tend to think of speech as something we construct; Scripture treats it as something that escapes. That is why it is evidentiary.

**Because it is nearly instantaneous.** A deed requires opportunity, means, nerve, and the reckoning of consequences — a dozen filters between desire and act. A word requires only breath. The interval between impulse and utterance is so short that the will has no time to dress the heart for company. Sins of action can be postponed indefinitely and thus concealed indefinitely; the tongue runs ahead of the censor.

**Because it is incessant.** A man can hold a posture for an hour; he cannot supervise ten thousand words a day. The mask survives in ceremony and collapses in traffic, in the household, in the unguarded sentence about someone who is not present. Volume defeats performance.

**Because it is disproportionate.** James's own images — bit, rudder, spark — are all small things that govern or consume the whole. The tongue is where the entire self is visible at leverage: a small motion revealing which way the horse is actually going, a small heat revealing what the whole forest is made of.

## Nature's rebuke

Then James reaches for a spring, a fig tree, a vine, salt water. His point is that unreasoning creation is *unified in its output* because it acts out of what it is. A fig tree does not occasionally produce olives. Sweet water does not intermittently run bitter. Nature is honest. The divided tongue is therefore not natural but monstrous — a moral disorder that puts a man below the consistency of a shrub.

Notice what this rules out as a remedy. If the stream is bitter, the problem is not the mouth but the spring. You cannot fix output by technique while the source stays corrupt. This is why James ends not with etiquette but with *"these things ought not so to be"* — and then, in the following paragraphs, with the wisdom from above that is pure and peaceable, and with the call to *purify your hearts, ye double minded* (4:8). The cure for a divided mouth is an undivided heart.

Two questions the passage quietly puts to any reader: Whom is it currently safe for me to despise? And does the mouth that blesses on the Lord's day recognize itself in the mouth that speaks on Tuesday? If the two are strangers, the passage says the second one is telling the truth about the first.
