External compliance is doing what a rule requires while the person’s inner loyalties and desires remain unchanged. It may be temporary, reluctant, or motivated by pressure. In Ezekiel’s promise, obedience comes from a changed inward life: God gives a “new heart” and puts his Spirit within the people, so that they are moved to walk in his ways. The passage does not set inward renewal against obedience; it presents renewal as the source of faithful obedience.

“Heart” in biblical language means more than emotion: it is the center of thought, desire, choice, and allegiance. The “stony” heart pictures a person or people unresponsive to God—resistant, as hard stone does not yield—while a “heart of flesh” suggests a living, responsive heart. The material image makes the depth of the problem vivid: the need is not merely for better instructions or stricter outward behavior, but for a transformation of the very disposition from which behavior flows.

So the metaphor describes a spiritual condition, not a literal material change. And the promise emphasizes that this renewal is God’s work: he does not merely demand a different life; he gives the Spirit who enables it.
