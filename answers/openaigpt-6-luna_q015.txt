The image is sharp because the proportions are absurd: someone with a beam lodged in his own eye is fussing over a speck in another person’s. The eye is delicate, so the would-be helper’s condition makes his offer both ridiculous and dangerous. He cannot see clearly enough to help—and yet he acts as if he can.

The passage condemns **hypocritical judgment**, not moral correction itself. The speck may really be there; Jesus does not say to leave it alone. Instead, he gives correction an order: first face and address your own fault, so that you can see clearly; **then** help your brother. Self-examination replaces superiority, and correction becomes an act of clear-sighted care rather than condemnation.
