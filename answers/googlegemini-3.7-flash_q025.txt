Based on the text and its broader biblical context, the moral danger lies **not in visibility itself, but entirely in the *craving* to be seen**—the underlying motive of seeking human praise rather than honoring God.

Here is a breakdown of why this distinction matters and what makes this craving morally perilous:

---

### 1. The Distinction: Visibility vs. Intent
The verse does not simply say, *"Do not do good works where people might see you."* Instead, it specifies: *“to be seen of them.”* 

* **The Greek Nuance:** The phrase "to be seen" comes from the Greek verb *theaomai* (the root of the English word *theater*). It implies performing intentionally for an audience or staging an act for spectacle.
* **The Contrast with Matthew 5:16:** Just one chapter earlier in the Sermon on the Mount, Jesus says: *“Let your light so shine before men, that they may see your good works, and glorify your Father which is in heaven.”* 

When Matthew 5:16 and Matthew 6:1 are read together, the difference becomes clear:
* **Good visibility (5:16):** People see the work, and the result is that **God** is praised.
* **Corrupt visibility (6:1):** The work is staged so that the **doer** is praised.

Visibility is incidental; the craving for an audience is intentional.

---

### 2. What Makes This Craving Morally Dangerous?

#### A. It Turns Others into Props
When giving to the needy (*alms*) is done to win public admiration, the suffering of the poor is reduced to a stage prop for the giver’s ego. The moral danger is that love for neighbor is replaced by self-aggrandizement.

#### B. It Replaces God with the Crowd
The craving for human approval fundamentally shifts the spiritual focus:
* The audience shifts from an unseen God to a visible crowd.
* Public opinion becomes the ultimate moral arbiter.
* Righteousness ceases to be an act of devotion and becomes a marketing strategy.

#### C. It Results in a Shallow, Forfeited Reward
Jesus warns that those who perform for an audience *“have no reward of your Father.”* In the verses immediately following (Matt 6:2), he adds, *“They have their reward.”* 
The moral tragedy is a bad trade: exchanging the eternal, spiritual favor of God for the fleeting, superficial applause of mortals.

---

### Summary
Public goodness becomes spiritually toxic when **performance replaces genuine love.** Visibility is merely a circumstance, but the *desire* for visibility corrupts the virtue of the act, turning charity into vanity and worship into theater.
