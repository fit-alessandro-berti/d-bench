The verse treats love for one’s “brother” as a test because **love for God should become visible in how one treats people**. A person can claim devotion to an unseen God without that claim being directly observable. But the people around us present concrete opportunities to show—or withhold—care.

In 1 John, “brother” particularly refers to a fellow believer. The letter describes love not merely as an emotion or a profession, but as action: helping someone in need and loving “in deed and in truth” (1 John 3:17–18). It also connects love of God with God’s command to love one another (4:21).

The self-deception, then, is imagining that religious devotion can coexist with contempt or indifference toward others: *I love God, even if I refuse to love the people God calls me to love.* The verse challenges that separation. It does not demand flawless feelings; it warns against calling a love for God genuine while persistently denying love in practice.
