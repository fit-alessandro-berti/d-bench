Jesus is calling people to deny the **self that insists on being its own master**—the self that would protect its status, safety, or control even at the cost of following him. In context, Peter has just resisted Jesus’ prediction that he will suffer and be killed. “Take up his cross” therefore means accepting that following Jesus may involve real shame, loss, or even death, not merely putting up with everyday annoyances.

That can sound **liberating** because it loosens the grip of fear and the exhausting need to preserve oneself at all costs. Jesus’ paradox is that a life given “for my sake and the gospel’s” is not finally lost.

It can sound **offensive** because it challenges our claim to direct our own lives—and because the language of self-denial has sometimes been used to pressure people to endure abuse or surrender their dignity. The passage does not authorize someone else to impose a cross on them. Jesus calls people to follow *him*, not to treat their personhood as worthless.
