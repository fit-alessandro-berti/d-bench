James 3:9–10 exposes a **practical contradiction between worship and ethics**: the same mouth praises God while dehumanizing and condemning persons made in God’s image.

The inner disorder is this:

- If human beings are “made after the similitude of God,” then they carry God’s likeness and dignity.
- To bless God sincerely is to honor Him as Creator and Father.
- But to curse people—especially in the sense of contemptuous rejection, malicious wish, or dehumanizing condemnation—is to wound what belongs to God’s image.
- Thus blessing God while cursing His image-bearers is not just inconsistent behavior; it is **an affront to God Himself**.

In other words, James is saying: *You cannot truly honor the Artist while despising His work.*

Speech reveals moral disorder so quickly because words are the most immediate outward expression of the inner life. The tongue is fast, public, and difficult to tame; it often slips out before the will can restrain it. James has already said the tongue is like a small rudder steering a great ship and like a fire that can destroy a whole forest. In biblical terms, “out of the abundance of the heart the mouth speaks” (Matthew 12:34). So speech exposes what we really value, fear, resent, pride in, and dehumanize.

A person can pretend to be devout in private worship, but the moment the tongue is stretched—especially in irritation, rivalry, gossip, anger, or spiritual pride—the divided heart is revealed. That is why James concludes: **“Out of the same mouth proceedeth blessing and cursing. My brethren, these things ought not so to be.”** It is morally and theologically incoherent.
