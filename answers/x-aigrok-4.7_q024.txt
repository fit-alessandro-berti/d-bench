The difference is where obedience comes from. External compliance is behavior produced by command, pressure, habit, or fear while the person remains unchanged. A “new heart” is God replacing the inner source of will and desire so that walking in his statutes becomes the person’s own bent, not a performance laid over a resistant core.

In Ezekiel 36 the promise is not “try harder” or “keep the statutes more carefully.” It is “I will give… I will put… I will take away… I will cause.” The statutes stay the same; the subject who meets them does not. Israel already had the law. What they lacked was a heart that could receive it. External compliance can look like keeping judgments. It cannot make a person love them, or keep them when no one is watching, or stop returning to the idols the old heart still wants. The new heart is the condition in which the Spirit indwells and *causes* obedience—not by overriding the person, but by remaking what the person wants.

Hardness is described as a material condition because, in the prophet’s picture, the problem is not a missing technique. Stone does not soften by being instructed. It does not become responsive flesh by being carved with better rules. A stony heart is dead to address: it can be struck, threatened, or decorated with religious practice and still not yield. Flesh, in this contrast, is living and permeable—able to feel, turn, and be moved. So the remedy is replacement, not refinement. God does not sand the stone; he takes it out and gives a heart of flesh, then puts his Spirit within so the new heart actually walks.

That is why the image is surgical rather than educational. Commands can expose a heart of stone. Only an act of God can exchange it.
