**The passage rejects the worship because it is empty, hypocritical, and offensive to God—not a matter of “more justice plus the same rituals,” but a qualitative fraud that makes the ceremonies themselves an abomination.**

Amos (mid-8th century BCE, northern kingdom of Israel) speaks into a time of relative prosperity, elaborate cultic life (Bethel, Gilgal, etc.), and simultaneous systemic injustice: oppression of the poor, corrupt courts, luxury built on exploitation (see the surrounding oracles in Amos 2–6, 8). The people treat festivals, burnt offerings, grain offerings, peace offerings, and liturgical music as sufficient to maintain covenant standing. God, through the prophet, declares the opposite: He hates and despises the feasts, will not accept the sacrifices, and will not listen to the songs. The demand is not “do both better”; it is “let justice roll down like waters, and righteousness like an ever-flowing stream.” Ritual without that is worse than useless—it is a stench.

This is the same prophetic logic found in Isaiah 1:11–17, Jeremiah 7, Hosea 6:6, Micah 6:6–8, and later in Jesus’ citations of “I desire mercy, not sacrifice.” The issue is not quantity or balance of religious activity versus ethics. It is that the worship has become a substitute for, and a cover for, the very things God requires: right relationship expressed in justice toward the vulnerable and integrity before Him. Performing the liturgy while the community practices (or condones) oppression is treated as an attempt to manipulate or deceive God—as if the smell of fat or the sound of viols could cancel the reality of injustice. That is the fraud: treating covenant signs as magic or as a transaction that leaves the heart and social order untouched. It is self-deception dressed as piety, and it is why the rejection is total rather than a call for incremental improvement.

The text therefore functions as a diagnostic: authentic worship cannot be separated from the life it is supposed to form. When the two diverge sharply, the ceremonies themselves become the problem.
