Repentance in Isaiah 55:7 is deliberately two-sided because sin is two-sided:

> *"Let the wicked forsake his **way**, and the unrighteous man his **thoughts**..."*

The Hebrew makes the distinction clear:

* **"Way" - *derek*:** Your path, journey, outward conduct, habit of life. The visible course of actions, choices, and practices.
* **"Thoughts" - *machashaboth*:** Your plans, reasonings, imaginations, inner schemings. Not just fleeting thoughts, but the entire mindset, worldview, and inner logic that *justifies* and *plots* the way.

God pairs them because He is describing complete repentance. Parallelism in Hebrew poetry is not repetition - it is completion.

### 1. Why Both Are Required

**a) The Way without the Thoughts is only reformation, not repentance.**
A man can change his outward behavior through willpower, fear, shame, or social pressure while keeping the same inner desires and rationalizations. That is behavior management. Jesus called it whitewashed tombs - clean outside, unchanged inside. If the imagination is not forsaken, the old way will eventually return.

> *For as he thinketh in his heart, so is he. Proverbs 23:7*
> *For out of the heart proceed evil thoughts... Matthew 15:19*

**b) The Thoughts without the Way is only sentiment, not repentance.**
A man can feel sorry, admit his thinking is wrong, and even desire to be different, yet continue walking in the same path. That is conviction without conversion.

True biblical repentance - *shuv* in Hebrew, to turn back/return, and *metanoia* in Greek, a change of mind - requires both. The inner imagination produces the outer way, and the outer way reinforces the inner imagination. God calls for the root *and* the fruit to be forsaken.

The text even distinguishes the persons:

* *The wicked [rasha] - his way* - The man guilty of outward rebellion, lawless deeds.
* *The unrighteous man [ish aven] - his thoughts* - The man of iniquity, vanity, whose inner moral reasoning is perverse.

Together they cover all of us. No one can say "my life is mostly good, it's just my thoughts" or "my thoughts are good, it's just my actions."

To **forsake - *ya'azov*** - means not to hide, excuse, manage, or reduce, but to *abandon, leave behind entirely*, as one would abandon a road that leads to destruction.

### 2. What This Implies

**About Outward Conduct:** God cares about how you live. Faith is not abstract. Your path matters, your habits matter, your associations and doings matter. Repentance must become visible.

**About Inward Imagination:** God does not only judge action, He judges the imagination that breeds action. You cannot return to the Lord while holding onto the thought-system that made sin seem reasonable, desirable, or necessary. This is why the very next verses say:

> *For my thoughts are not your thoughts, neither are your ways my ways, saith the Lord. Isaiah 55:8-9*

Our thoughts must be forsaken *in order to receive* His thoughts. Repentance is an exchange of minds, not just morals. It is Romans 12:2 - *be transformed by the renewing of your mind.*

### 3. The Possibility of Mercy

> *"...and let him return unto the Lord, and he will have mercy upon him; and to our God, for he will abundantly pardon."*

This is the gospel logic of the passage:

**Mercy is conditional, but not scarce.** The condition is *return* - a complete forsaking and turning. God does not pardon the way while we cling to the thoughts, nor the thoughts while we cling to the way. He pardons the *person who returns*.

**Mercy is complete because repentance is complete.** If God only forgave actions but left the imagination corrupted, we would remain unclean. If He only forgave thoughts but not deeds, we would remain guilty. 

The two promises correspond to the two forsakings:

* *He will have mercy [yerachem]* - a compassionate, tender, motherly mercy for the helplessness of our condition.
* *He will abundantly pardon [yarbeh lisloach]* - literally "he will multiply to pardon." Where sin abounded in way and thought, pardon super-abounds. No measure to His forgiveness.

And note verse 6 that frames it all:

> *Seek ye the Lord **while he may be found**, call ye upon him **while he is near**.*

There is a nearness of God now in invitation that will not always be opportunity. Mercy is abundant today, but it requires an immediate, whole-person response.

**In sum:** God demands we forsake both our outward walk and our inward world because sin has corrupted both. But for that very reason, His mercy is more glorious - He does not merely modify your conduct or improve your thinking; when you return, He pardons and cleanses the whole man, abundantly.
