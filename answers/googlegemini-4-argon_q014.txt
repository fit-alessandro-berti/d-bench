In the original context and grammar of Matthew 6:19–21, the text primarily argues that **treasure trains (or directs) the heart**, though in doing so, it ultimately **reveals** it as well. 

Because we live in a modern culture that views feelings as coming before actions, people often misquote verse 21 backward: *"Where your heart is, there your treasure will be."* If Jesus had said that, it would mean treasure merely *reveals* the heart (i.e., your bank statement is a symptom of what you already love). 

Instead, Jesus reverses the order: **"Where your treasure is, there *will* your heart be also."** The heart follows the treasure. Here is how the text explains the training of the heart, and how possession, attention, and love become bound together.

### 1. How Treasure Trains the Heart
In biblical anthropology, the "heart" (*kardia*) is not just the seat of romantic or emotional feeling; it is the control center of the human being—encompassing the mind, the will, the desires, and the emotions. 

By using the future tense ("there *will* your heart be"), Jesus is describing a psychological and spiritual law: **investment creates attachment.** Your heart is not a static thing; it is dynamic and follows your investments. If you want to change what you care about (your heart), you must change where you put your resources (your treasure). Therefore, the physical act of "laying up" treasure is a formative practice that *trains* the heart to love whatever it is investing in.

*(Note: Once the heart is trained, the treasure then **reveals** the heart as a byproduct. You can look at your treasure to diagnose where your heart has migrated, because the two refuse to live in different places.)*

### 2. The Tie Between Possession, Attention, and Love
In these three verses, Jesus exposes the invisible tether that connects what we own (possession), what we think about (attention), and what we worship (love). 

**First, Possession demands Attention.**
Jesus contrasts earthly treasures with heavenly ones by introducing three threats: **moths, rust, and thieves.** 
*   "Moths" represent the decay of luxury (in the ancient world, fine garments were a primary form of wealth). 
*   "Rust" (literally *brosis*, meaning "eating" or "consumption") represents the decay of grain and food stores by vermin, worm, or rot.
*   "Thieves" (literally "mud-diggers" who dug through the dried-mud walls of ancient houses) represent the violent loss of gold and silver.

Because earthly possessions are inherently vulnerable, **to possess them is to be possessed by the anxiety of keeping them.** If your treasure is on earth, your *attention* is held hostage by maintenance, security, and the fear of loss. You must constantly watch for the moth, the worm, and the thief. 

**Second, Attention cultivates Love.**
What captures your attention eventually captures your affection. The Greek verb Jesus uses for "lay up" is *thesauellō*, and the noun for "treasure" is *thesauros* (the root of our word *thesaurus*, a storehouse). Literally, verse 19 reads: *"Do not treasure up for yourselves treasures..."*

"Treasuring" is an active verb. When you work for something, store it, count it, polish it, and protect it from thieves, your mind is consumed by it. The sheer gravity of your daily attention pulls your inner self—your "heart"—toward that object. You begin to love the thing you are working so hard to protect.

**Third, Love determines Destiny.**
Because possession demands attention, and attention cultivates love, your *heart* eventually merges with your *possessions*. This is why Jesus gives this warning not primarily as an ethical guilt-trip, but as a pragmatic rescue mission. 

If your treasure is bound to the earth, and your heart is bound to your treasure, then when the moth, the rust, and the thief inevitably destroy your treasure, **your heart will be destroyed with it.** 

### Summary
Matthew 6:19–21 teaches that we do not always act our way into a feeling, we often **invest our way into a feeling.** 

Possession is never neutral. To *possess* something is to give it your *attention*, and to give it your attention is to train your *heart* to love it. By commanding his listeners to move their treasure to heaven (through generosity, justice, and Kingdom-minded living), Jesus is showing them how to liberate their attention from anxiety, and how to train their hearts to love what is eternal and indestructible.
