**Short answer:**  
Matthew 12:34‑35 (and the nearby “viper” parables) teaches that what we say comes **from** the hidden moral stockpiles inside our hearts.  In other words, words are **symptoms** of the heart’s inner goods or evils, yet they also **reveal** those hidden stocks.  The passage does not treat speech as a purely voluntary choice; it shows that the heart’s nature tends to drive the mouth, although a person can try to steer the flow.  So the best description is that words are **all three**—they are symptoms of the heart, they can be chosen, and they also serve as revelations of what lies beneath.

---

### 1.  What the text actually says  

> “O generation of vipers, how can ye, being evil, speak good things? for out of the abundance of the heart the mouth speaketh.  
> A good man out of the good treasure of the heart bringeth forth good things: and an evil man out of the evil treasure bringeth forth evil things.”

*Key phrases*  

| Phrase | Meaning |
|--------|---------|
| **“out of the abundance of the heart”** | The heart holds a large, hidden reserve of either good or evil. |
| **“the mouth speaketh”** | The tongue/mouth releases whatever is stored there. |
| **“good treasure … bringeth forth good things”** / **“evil treasure … bringeth forth evil things”** | The heart’s contents determine the quality of speech. |

---

### 2.  Connecting speech to “hidden moral stockpiles”

- **Hidden stockpiles** = the inner reservoirs of desire, sin, virtue, or grace that reside in the heart but may stay dormant until they are expressed.  
- **“Abundance of the heart”** = a surplus of these hidden goods.  
- **“Mouth speaketh”** = the moment those goods overflow into words.  

Thus, **speech is the outward expression of what is already inside**.  When the heart is full of good, the mouth naturally produces good words; when it is full of evil, the mouth produces evil words.  The connection is causal and automatic: the heart’s nature *drives* the tongue.

---

### 3.  Does this make words **symptoms, choices, or revelations?**

| Dimension | How the passage treats it |
|-----------|---------------------------|
| **Symptoms** | ✔️ The text equates speech with the heart’s output.  People often notice their words before they recognize the inner corruption (e.g., a deceitful person still speaking kindly). |
| **Choices** | ➡️ The passage stresses the *cause* (the heart) rather than a deliberate decision.  It warns that trying to speak well while the heart is evil is futile (“how can ye…?”).  Human effort to choose otherwise is acknowledged only in the sense that we *can* try, but success depends on the heart’s inclination. |
| **Revelations** | ✔️ By uttering words we force the world to confront the hidden moral reality inside us.  The act of speaking makes the secret stockpile public, turning private sin or grace into testimony. |

**Therefore:**  
- **Primary role:** *Symptom* – words flow from the hidden goods in the heart.  
- **Secondary role:** *Revelation* – speaking exposes those hidden goods.  
- **Tertiary nuance:** *Choice* – we may attempt to direct the flow, but the text suggests the heart’s nature dominates unless we consciously override it.

Most theologians and commentators read the whole picture as **words being both symptoms and revelations of the heart’s hidden stockpiles**, with a thin thread of human agency woven in.

---

### 4.  Practical take‑away

1. **Listen to your own motives.**  Notice whether your speech aligns with the “treasure” you feel inside.  
2. **Be aware of the gap.**  Even a good person can unintentionally speak poorly if the heart’s hidden goods surface unexpectedly.  
3. **Use speech as a tool for exposure.**  When you speak, you turn private virtues or vices into shared witness, allowing the hidden stockpile to be seen and corrected.  
4. **Recognize the struggle.**  Choosing to speak rightly while an evil heart lingers is hard; the passage warns against naïve confidence in one’s ability to control words alone.

---

**Bottom line:** Matthew 12:34‑35 paints speech as the inevitable outflow of the heart’s hidden moral reserves.  It is **not** merely a symptom (though it certainly is), nor is it simply a free choice; it is also a **revelation** of what lies beneath.  All three elements intertwine, but the central message is that our words are the visible sign of the invisible stockpile inside us.
