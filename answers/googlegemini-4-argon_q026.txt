In Matthew 12:34–35, Jesus establishes an unbreakable link between a person’s internal moral state and their external verbal expression. By using the metaphors of "abundance" and "treasure," the passage frames speech not merely as a series of isolated communication acts, but as the inevitable outflow of what a person has cultivated and stored within themselves. 

Here is an analysis of how the passage connects speech to these "hidden moral stockpiles," followed by an examination of whether words function as symptoms, choices, or revelations.

---

### 1. The Connection: Speech and the "Moral Stockpile"

The passage uses two key concepts to connect the heart (the seat of moral will, thought, and emotion in Hebrew thought) with the mouth:

*   **The Principle of Overflow ("Abundance"):** The Greek word translated as "abundance" is *perisseuma*, which refers to that which is left over, a surplus, or an overflow. The imagery is hydraulic: the heart is a reservoir, and when it is filled to capacity with a certain moral quality, that quality spills over the brim and exits through the mouth. Speech is the pressure-relief valve of the soul.
*   **The Principle of Inventory ("Treasure"):** In verse 35, the Greek word for "treasure" is *thēsauros* (the root of the English word *thesaurus*), meaning a storehouse, vault, or treasury. A treasury does not generate its own wealth; it holds what has been deliberately deposited into it over time. You cannot withdraw gold from a vault where you have only deposited lead. Similarly, the Pharisees ("generation of vipers") cannot produce genuinely good speech because they have only stockpiled malice, pride, and hypocrisy in their internal vaults.

---

### 2. Are Words Symptoms, Choices, or Revelations?

The theological and psychological depth of this passage suggests that words are **all three simultaneously**. Each category highlights a different stage of how the heart’s "treasure" reaches the listener's ear.

#### Words as Symptoms (Involuntary Diagnosis)
**Textual basis:** *"How can ye, being evil, speak good things? For out of the abundance of the heart the mouth speaketh."*

In this sense, words are **symptoms** of an underlying spiritual condition. Just as a physician looks at a patient's tongue or listens to their cough to diagnose an internal disease, Jesus listens to the Pharisees' speech (who had just accused him of casting out demons by the power of Satan) to diagnose their evil hearts. 
*   The rhetorical question *"How can ye...?"* implies a moral impossibility. A diseased heart cannot naturally produce healthy speech, at least not consistently. Sooner or later, the "abundance" will force its way out—often in unguarded moments—serving as an involuntary symptom of what rules the inner person.

#### Words as Choices (Deliberate Moral Agency)
**Textual basis:** *"A good man out of the good treasure... bringeth forth good things..."*

While verse 34 sounds deterministic (an overflow), verse 35 introduces the agency of a steward. The phrase "bringeth forth" (Greek: *ekballei*, literally to cast out, send forth, or draw out) depicts a person actively going into their storehouse, selecting an item, and presenting it to the world. 
*   Words are **choices** in two ways. First, a person *chooses* over a lifetime what to deposit into their heart (what they meditate on, value, and harbor). Second, in the moment of speaking, they *choose* to draw from that supply. 
*   Furthermore, in the very next verses (Matthew 12:36–37), Jesus states that humans will give an account on the day of judgment for "every idle word," and by their words they will be justified or condemned. Moral accountability requires moral agency; therefore, speech remains a deliberate choice.

#### Words as Revelations (Making the Invisible Visible)
**Textual basis:** The entire metaphor of bringing hidden "treasure" into the open.

A treasure chest or vault is, by definition, hidden from public view. No one knows what is inside another person's heart until the vault is opened. Speech is the mechanism of **revelation**.
*   In the broader context of Matthew 12, Jesus uses the parallel metaphor of a tree and its fruit (v. 33). You may not be able to identify a tree by its bark or roots, but when the fruit appears, the nature of the tree is revealed. 
*   Words take the invisible, abstract nature of a person’s character and make it audible and concrete. When a person speaks, they are unveiling their inner sanctuary to the world.

### Summary
In Matthew 12:34–35, Jesus teaches that speech is the **revelation** of a hidden internal inventory, the **symptom** of an overflowing spiritual condition, and the result of a **choice**—both the long-term choice of what one deposits into the heart, and the immediate choice of what one draws out of it.
