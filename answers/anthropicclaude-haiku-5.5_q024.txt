## External compliance vs. a new heart

**External compliance** means doing what is required while the inner orientation stays the same. Behavior is shaped by fear of punishment, hope of reward, social pressure, or habit, and the person would act differently if the pressure were removed. Israel in Ezekiel's time illustrates this. They kept outward forms while their hearts "went after their idols" (Ezek. 33:31; cf. 14:3), and the prophet exposes the gap between their words and their desires (33:31–32).

**A new heart** changes the source of obedience. The will itself is reoriented, so that God's ways become what the person wants. The text says God will "cause you to walk in my statutes" and that "ye shall keep my judgments, and do them." The verbs of obedience are real, but the verse makes God the agent: he puts his Spirit within and produces the obedience. The aim is not a better performance of the law but a person whose desires align with it. Jeremiah 31:33 expresses the same idea as the law being written on the heart.

So the difference lies less in the outward act than in its origin. Compliance can be extracted by pressure; a new heart produces obedience that flows from delight and loyalty. The passage also makes clear that this change is God's gift, not a product of Israel's self-improvement. Ezekiel stresses that the restoration happens "not for your sakes" (36:22, 32).

## Why hardness is described as a material condition

In Hebrew thought, *lev* (heart) is not primarily the seat of emotion. It is the center of thought, decision, will, and moral identity. When the heart is "stony," the problem is not a lack of effort or information but a deep unresponsiveness at the level of who a person is.

The contrast between stone and flesh works on several levels:

- **Stone is unresponsive.** It cannot feel, respond to touch, or be moved by a word. Flesh is sensitive and alive. A stony heart hears God's commands and does not register them as living demands.
- **Stone cannot fix itself.** You cannot will a rock to become soft. Exhortations to "try harder" cannot dissolve it. Hence the remedy is replacement, not repair or polishing. The verb "I will take away" and "I will give" signals a new creation rather than a moral tune-up.
- **Stone is an idolatrous image.** Idols were carved from stone and wood, and Psalm 115:8 says that those who make idols become like them. A heart of stone is one shaped by what it worships, which is why the imagery connects to the book's repeated charges of idolatry.
- **Stone is not foreign to the person.** The stony heart is "out of your flesh," meaning it has become part of their own nature. The hardness is not an external obstacle but a condition they have absorbed, which is why only God can remove it.

The material language therefore expresses a diagnosis and a hope. The diagnosis: sin has hardened the very substance of the person, so that rules and pressure cannot reach the core. The hope: God will replace the heart itself, making a responsive, living center capable of the obedience the law always demanded.
