In this text Jesus does not present “treasure” as a mere thermometer that reads the temperature of your heart; he treats it more like a magnet. The grammar and the surrounding context suggest that treasure **trains and directs** the heart, even though it also inevitably **shows** what the heart is bent on. Where you store up treasure is where your heart is headed.

Several clues point in this direction. First, the command is not merely diagnostic but active: *“Do not lay up… but lay up…”* Treasuring is something you practice, not just feel. Second, the conclusion uses the future—“there your heart **will be**” (ἐκεῖ ἡ καρδία σου ἔσται). Jesus is describing movement and destination, not merely current location. Your heart follows your treasure. It can still reveal what you already love, because no one stores up what they do not value; but the emphasis falls on the heart being shaped, claimed, and drawn by the practice of investing oneself.

To see how possession, attention, and love interlock, it helps to keep the Sermon’s flow in view. The verse about treasure is immediately unpacked by the saying about the eye (6:22–23) and then by the saying about masters (6:24). These are not random illustrations; they are two ways of saying the same thing about “heart.”

**Possession** in this verse is not mere “stuff”; it is whatever you store as security. The moth, rust, and thieves are not just examples of decay but of failed trust. What you hoard is what you count on for the future, and that counting-on forms you. **Attention** follows, because the “eye” is the body’s lamp. What you look at fills you. If your treasure is on earth, your gaze stays on earth, anxious about moths, thieves, loss, and enough; if your treasure is in heaven, your gaze is pulled upward into God’s safe-keeping. **Love** then appears in v. 24 as the deepest layer. Jesus says you cannot serve two masters; you will hate one and love the other. To treasure something is to serve a master, and to serve a master is to love him. That is why the heart cannot remain neutral.

So the triad is locked together like this: **What you possess (your security), you attend to; what you attend to, you love; what you love possesses you.** Jesus is diagnosing and prescribing at once. Your earthly hoarding already reveals a divided heart, and it is precisely that practice that—if continued—trains the heart to trust in what perishes. If instead you act into the economy of heaven—the kingdom trust of 6:25–34—your heart is pulled toward the only Master whose treasure cannot rot or be stolen.
