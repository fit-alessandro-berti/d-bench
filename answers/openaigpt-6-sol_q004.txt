The proverb calls for **wholehearted reliance on God**, not for abandoning thought. “Lean not unto thine own understanding” means not treating your judgment as self-sufficient or final—especially when it conflicts with God’s wisdom. “In all thy ways acknowledge him” makes that trust practical: bring your choices, motives, and plans under his direction.

**Humble self-distrust** says, “My understanding is limited and can be distorted; I need God’s wisdom.” **Anti-intellectualism** says, “Thinking carefully is unnecessary or suspect.” The proverb teaches the first, not the second. Careful reasoning, learning, and seeking counsel can themselves be ways of acknowledging God—so long as they serve trust rather than replace it.
