The passage rejects worship so sharply because the worshippers are treating ritual as proof of loyalty to God while their public life contradicts what God requires. In the surrounding chapter, Amos accuses them of corrupting justice, taking bribes, and oppressing the poor (Amos 5:10–12). Their feasts and songs are not simply one good thing that needs to be “balanced” with another. In that setting, they help sustain the claim that all is well.

The fraud is **using devotion to God to conceal disobedience to God**—as though offerings could secure divine approval while injustice continues. That is why God calls the songs “noise” and refuses the sacrifices. Amos is not saying that worship is inherently worthless; he is saying it cannot stand in for righteousness. Justice must run like a stream: not appear briefly on ceremonial occasions, but flow through the community’s ordinary dealings.
