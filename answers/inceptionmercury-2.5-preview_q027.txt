

The Pharisee is condemned not for false doctrine, but for **pride and self-righteousness**, specifically manifested through comparison and contempt. The parable opens by noting it targets those who "trusted in themselves that they were righteous, and despised others." His prayer catalogs his religious practices as evidence of superiority rather than gratitude to God. By framing virtue as a way to distance himself from others, he seeks validation through human merit instead of divine grace.

The tax collector’s posture is treated as spiritually truer because it embodies **humility and dependence on mercy**. Standing afar off and refusing to lift his eyes signals a recognition of unworthiness. His prayer asks for nothing but compassion ("God be merciful to me a sinner"), acknowledging that justification must come from God rather than self-generated merit. Jesus contrasts these postures to show that those who humble themselves are exalted, while those who exalt themselves are humbled.
