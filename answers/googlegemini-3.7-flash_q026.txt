In Matthew 12:34–35, Jesus establishes an organic and causal relationship between interior character and exterior speech. By using the metaphors of an **“abundance” (overflow)** and a **“treasure” (a stockpile or storehouse)**, the passage explains that speech is never generated in a vacuum; it is the direct product of what has been accumulated within the soul.

The passage ultimately indicates that words are **all three: symptoms, revelations, and choices**, functioning together within a unified view of human moral agency.

---

### 1. How Speech Connects to the "Moral Stockpile"

The Greek word used for "treasure" is *thesauros* (the root of *thesaurus*), referring to a deposit, treasury, or storage vault. Jesus depicts the human heart not as a neutral organ, but as a container that is continuously filled with either good or evil over time. 

* **The Principle of Inevitable Overflow:** The phrase *"out of the abundance [perisseuma – fullness, surplus] of the heart the mouth speaketh"* implies a hydraulic principle. What fills the reservoir inevitably spills over through the valve of the tongue. 
* **The Impossibility of Moral Disconnect:** Jesus’ rhetorical question (*“how can ye, being evil, speak good things?”*) rejects the idea that words can be genuinely divorced from character. Speech is the currency drawn from the heart’s reserve; one cannot withdraw gold from a vault filled only with poison.

---

### 2. Are Words Symptoms, Choices, or Revelations?

The passage operates on multiple levels, demonstrating that words function simultaneously as all three:

#### A. Words as **Symptoms** (The Diagnostic Level)
Words act as clinical indicators of an underlying spiritual condition. 
* Just as a physical symptom (like a fever) indicates an internal infection, corrupt speech indicates a corrupt heart. 
* When Jesus calls the Pharisees a *"generation of vipers,"* he points to their venomous speech as a natural symptom of their venomous nature. The mouth simply manifests the pathology of the heart.

#### B. Words as **Revelations** (The Epistemological Level)
Words unveil what is otherwise invisible. 
* The "treasure" of the heart is hidden within the interior life of a person, inaccessible to outside observers. Speech is the mechanism by which this hidden stockpile is brought to light (*“bringeth forth”*).
* In the immediate context, the religious leaders were outwardly pious, but their words—accusing Jesus of working by the power of Beelzebub (Matt. 12:24)—stripped away their disguise and exposed the evil stored in their hearts.

#### C. Words as **Choices** (The Moral/Volitional Level)
While the passage highlights the inevitability of the overflow, it does not absolve the speaker of moral responsibility.
* The accumulation of the "treasure" is the result of continuous moral choices—what a person loves, meditates on, harbors, and desires. 
* Furthermore, the act of "bringing forth" (*ekballei*—to cast out or produce) reflects agency. (This is reinforced just two verses later in Matthew 12:36–37, where Jesus warns that people will *"give account"* for every idle word and will be *"justified or condemned"* by them).

---

### Conclusion

Matthew 12:34–35 integrates these three concepts:

1. **Words are Choices:** We choose what we deposit into the storehouse of our hearts and how we direct our speech.
2. **Words are Symptoms:** Over time, these choices form a dominant moral state that naturally erupts through our language.
3. **Words are Revelations:** When that speech occurs, it acts as an unvarnished window into the true moral reality of the speaker.
