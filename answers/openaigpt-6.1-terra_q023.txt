Repentance involves both **“way” and “thoughts”** because it is a turning of the whole person toward God.

- **“Way”** points to one’s outward course of life: actions, habits, and dealings with others. Repentance is more than regret; it involves leaving harmful practices behind.
- **“Thoughts”** points to the inner life: intentions, plans, desires, and ways of imagining what is good or possible. The call is not to police every passing thought, but to abandon the inward purposes that sustain wrongdoing.

The passage also makes mercy central. The wicked and unrighteous are invited to return to God, who “will abundantly pardon.” Repentance does not earn that mercy; it is the turning toward the God who promises it. “Seek … while he may be found” gives the invitation urgency, while the promise of abundant pardon gives it hope.
