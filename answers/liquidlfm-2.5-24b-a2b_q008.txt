**1. Why the “visible neighbour” becomes the litmus‑test for love of the invisible God**

| Biblical background | Theological logic |
|---------------------|-------------------|
| **John 4:20** is spoken by Jesus while ministering to a Samaritan woman – a stranger from a despised ethnic group. He is making the point that true worship must be “spirit and truth” (v. 23‑24). | God is *invisible* (cf. 1 John 4:8 – “God is love”). Because He cannot be seen, the only reliable evidence that a person really knows Him is how they treat the people around them. |
| **Leviticus 19:18 & Deut 6:5** command “love your neighbour as yourself.” The neighbour is the concrete, everyday person who shares our space, culture, and needs. | The neighbour is a *visible* manifestation of God’s love. All creation bears God’s image (Genesis 1:27); loving a fellow‑human is therefore a tangible way of loving the Creator. |
| **Jesus’ own words** (Matt 22:37‑40) call loving God and neighbour together the “greatest commandment.” | If a person can claim to love the unseen God yet despise the seen neighbour, there is a logical contradiction: the love they profess for God is either insincere or incomplete. The neighbour’s presence forces the issue – love cannot be a private, abstract sentiment when there is a real person right in front of you. |

In short, the verse treats love of the visible neighbour as a **test** because:

1. **It provides a verifiable, external criterion.** Love for an unseen deity is invisible; love shown toward a real person is observable.  
2. **It reflects the image of God.** The neighbour is a piece of God’s creation; loving him/her is a way of honoring the One who made us.  
3. **It exposes the inconsistency of “private” faith.** If one truly trusts God, that trust must spill over into concrete, relational love.  

---

**2. What kind of self‑deception does this expose?**

The passage is aimed at a **hypocritical self‑deception** that Jesus repeatedly condemns elsewhere in the Gospels (e.g., Matt 23:27‑28, “Woe to you, scribes and Pharisees, hypocrites! For you devise ways of entering the kingdom of heaven, but you do not practice its deeds”). The specific forms of self‑deception are:

| Form of self‑deception | How it shows up in the text |
|------------------------|-----------------------------|
| **Intellectual‑only faith** – believing that doctrinal assent to “I love God” is sufficient for a genuine relationship with Him. | The speaker says, “If a man say, I love God…” – the claim is verbal, not demonstrated. |
| **Moral compartmentalisation** – separating “spiritual” love (for God) from “social” love (for the neighbour). | He can profess love for the invisible God while harbouring hatred toward his brother (the neighbour). |
| **Self‑righteousness / spiritual pride** – thinking that one’s inner devotion exempts them from the command to love others. | The phrase “he is a liar” indicates that the liar has fooled himself into thinking his love for God is pure, while actually being hostile to the visible world. |
| **Cognitive dissonance** – holding two contradictory beliefs at once: “I love God” and “I hate my brother.” | The verse points out the impossibility of both being true simultaneously; the contradiction reveals the lie. |
| **Rationalisation of neglect** – convincing oneself that love for the neighbour is a lower, less important duty than love for God. | By equating love of the unseen with love of the seen, the deceived person downplays the practical outworking of faith. |

In essence, the self‑deception is **the illusion that one can love God in the heart while ignoring the concrete demands of His command to love one’s fellow humans**. The verse strips away that illusion by showing that love for the neighbour is the *visible proof* of love for the invisible God. When that proof is missing, the claim of divine love collapses into hypocrisy.

---

### Bottom line

- **Test of authenticity:** Because God is unseen, the only way to demonstrate genuine love for Him is through actions that reveal love for the people He created—our neighbours.  
- **Self‑deception exposed:** The verse confronts the hypocrisy of those who claim love for the invisible God while despising the visible neighbour, exposing a blend of intellectualism, moral compartmentalisation, and self‑righteous pride that masks a lack of true love in practice.  

True worship, therefore, is not a private mental exercise; it is lived out in the concrete, relational love that mirrors God’s love toward us. The neighbour becomes the mirror in which we see whether our love for God is real or merely a self‑serving story we tell ourselves.
