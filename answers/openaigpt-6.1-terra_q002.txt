The psalmist asks for a clean heart because forgiveness alone, understood as release from guilt or punishment, would not address what led to the sin. He wants not only a cleared record but a changed life.

Wanting **escape from guilt** can mean wanting relief from shame, fear, or consequences. Those desires are understandable, and pardon matters: earlier in the psalm, the speaker asks God to blot out his transgressions. But a person can want the pain of guilt to stop without yet wanting to give up what caused the harm.

Wanting **inner change** goes further. A “clean heart” is a desire for renewed motives, affections, and integrity. The prayer acknowledges that the problem is not merely an outward act but something within that needs cleansing and reorientation. “Create” also suggests that the psalmist sees this renewal as God’s work, not simply a matter of trying harder.

The rest of the passage shows what he hopes that change will make possible: continued life in God’s presence, restored joy, and a spirit sustained in faithfulness. The aim is not merely to feel innocent again, but to live differently in relationship with God.

So the prayer does not set pardon and transformation against each other. It asks for both: **forgiveness for what has been done, and renewal of the person who did it.**
