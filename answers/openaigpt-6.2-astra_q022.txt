To ask for defense is to ask God to judge a wrong done *to* me. To ask to be searched is to allow that there may also be a wrong *in* me. The psalmist has just spoken fiercely against those he sees as God’s enemies. Rather than take his own indignation as proof that he is right, he places his own heart under the same scrutiny.

There is trust in that request. The psalm begins, “O LORD, thou hast searched me, and known me.” By the end, “Search me” is not a plea for God to discover something new; it is the psalmist’s willingness to be shown what God already sees. The honesty modeled here is neither self-defense nor self-condemnation. It is the humility to admit that even sincere convictions can conceal motives one does not recognize.

The final words give that honesty its purpose: “lead me.” He wants more than a verdict on who he is; he wants help changing direction where he needs to.
