It says **both**, but the wording especially stresses that treasure can **train the heart**. Jesus does not only say, “Your treasure shows what you already love.” He says, “Where your treasure is, there your heart will be also”: what you invest in draws your attention and affection toward it.

The connection works like this:

- **Possession:** What we store up or invest in becomes something we have a stake in.
- **Attention:** We tend to watch over, think about, and worry about what we value.
- **Love:** Repeated attention and care deepen attachment. What we treat as precious can increasingly claim our desire and loyalty.

So treasure is both a **sign** of the heart’s priorities and a **means of shaping** them. Our existing loves influence what we pursue, but our pursuits can also cultivate new loves. In the wider passage, Jesus contrasts earthly security with the enduring things of God’s kingdom; he is warning not merely that wealth can reveal misplaced devotion, but that storing up wealth can pull the heart toward it.
