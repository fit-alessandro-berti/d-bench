This passage does **not** say treasure “reveals” the heart in the sense of merely exposing what is already there; it says treasure **determines or draws** the heart: “where your treasure is, **there will** your heart be also” (emphasis added). The tense is forward-looking and consequential, not diagnostic. So the text is closer to “treasure **locates and forms** the heart” than to “treasure merely reveals” it.

But it would be wrong to make it say “treasure **trains** the heart” as a simple moralistic mechanism either. The verb is “be” — the heart’s place follows the treasure’s place. The logic is more metaphysical and relational than behavioral training: the heart is constituted around what one ultimately values and rests upon. Treasure is not just a reward that later shapes the heart; it is the dispositive center around which the heart’s affection, ambition, and security settle.

The three terms you name fit together this way:

1. **Possession** — “Lay up… for yourselves” is active, deliberate, and directional. It concerns where one stores one’s life, one’s trust, one’s plans. Earthly treasure is real but mutable: moth and rust, thieves. Heavenly treasure is incorruptible and secure. The text is not anti-things but anti-ultimate-investment in things.

2. **Attention** — Where one stores is where one looks. “Lay up” implies habitual looking, planning, weighing, and anxiety. Attention is not a separate discipline added to possession; it is the interior practice of possession. What you keep returning to, you come to see as finally real.

3. **Love** — The text does not use the word “love,” but it presupposes it in the older biblical sense of *treasure* as that which one cherishes, trusts, and serves. The heart is the organ of affection and allegiance, not merely of feeling. “There will your heart be” means: your affection will take root where you have placed your ultimate investment. You do not merely notice your loves; you become them, or rather your loves are settled where your treasure is.

So the sequence is: **possession locates → attention habituates → love entwines.** The heart is not a neutral screen on which treasures are displayed; it is a feeding and forming center. The passage’s contrast is between two rival centers of treasure and therefore two rival homes for the heart: earthly security, which decays and can be stolen, and heavenly treasure, which does not. The invitation is not to inspect the heart and then pretend it is innocent; it is to reorder treasure so that the heart has a true and lasting place to rest. In short: treasure does not just reveal the heart; it **captures, locates, and forms** it.
