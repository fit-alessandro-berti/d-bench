This verse is best read not as a choice between “treasure reveals the heart” or “treasure trains the heart,” but as a statement of both. Jesus says:

> “For where your treasure is, there will your heart be also.”

The heart is tied to the treasure—so closely that the treasure is both a mirror and a magnet for the heart.

## Treasure reveals the heart

Jesus’ words are diagnostic. If you want to know what a person truly loves, values, and desires, look at what they *treasure*—what they save, collect, protect, pursue, or sacrifice for. A person’s treasure is not only money; it can be reputation, comfort, security, relationships, leisure, power, or any number of things. But whatever it is, it exposes where the heart actually lives.

This is why Jesus says, “Lay not up for yourselves treasures upon earth” and then immediately says, “For where your treasure is, there will your heart be also.” The “for” shows that the second statement explains the first. Earthly treasures reveal an earthly heart. Heavenly treasures reveal a heavenly heart. Your real priorities are not hidden in your words; they are written in your daily investments.

## Treasure trains the heart

But the verse is also formative. The heart follows the treasure, not just in the sense of “being exposed” but in the sense of *being drawn and shaped*. As you intentionally store up treasure in heaven—through generosity, obedience, prayer, fasting, serving the poor, and seeking God’s kingdom—your heart is reoriented toward heaven. Jesus commands “lay up for yourselves treasures in heaven” because He knows that where you put your treasure, your affections will follow.

This is not a cold or mechanical formula. It is a spiritual reality: the heart is formed by what it loves, and love is directed by what we value and give ourselves to. So if you want your heart to be in heaven, begin storing treasure there. If you want to love God more, invest your life in the things of God.

## How possession, attention, and love become tied

- **Possession**: “Treasure” is something you possess and protect. To treasure something is to regard it as valuable, to keep it close, to make it part of your identity. What you possess says what you have chosen.
- **Attention**: What you treasure gets your attention. You think about it, plan for it, worry about it, and enjoy it. The heart follows the mind’s attention. If your treasure is an earthly bank account, your mental life gravitates there. If your treasure is the kingdom of God, your thoughts dwell there.
- **Love**: In the Bible, the heart is the center of the will, the emotions, and the deepest loyalties. Love is the heart’s attachment to what it treasures. So possession draws attention, attention deepens love, and love drives more pursuit of that treasure. These three weave together into a single allegiance.

That is why Jesus later says, “You cannot serve God and money” (Matthew 6:24). You cannot have two ultimate treasures, because your heart cannot live in two places. Your treasure—your actual, practical, daily investment—claims your attention and captures your love.

So the answer is: *both*. The text says that your treasure reveals your heart, and it also says that your heart is drawn to your treasure. Jesus invites us to examine what we treasure and to deliberately place our treasures in heaven—so that our hearts may be there also.
