The contradiction James exposes is a **divided heart expressed through one mouth**.

With the same tongue a person says, “God is Father”— blesses Him, praises Him, invokes His name—and then curses, slanders, mocks, or despises other human beings who are “made after the similitude of God.” The inconsistency is not merely that religious people sometimes say bad things; it is deeper:

- They **worship God** with their words.
- They **dishonor God’s image** in others with those same words.

If God is truly acknowledged as Father, then His image-bearers should not be treated with contempt. To bless God and curse His creatures is practical hypocrisy: the lips confess reverence, but the heart reveals pride, hatred, or disregard.

Speech reveals moral disorder so quickly because **words are the overflow of the heart**. In moments of anger, gossip, mockery, or self-justification, people often speak before they can religiously edit themselves. What comes out shows what is really governing them: love or resentment, humility or superiority, faith or flesh.

That is why James treats the tongue as so serious. It is small, but it reveals and releases enormous power—blessing or cursing, life or death, peace or destruction. The mouth is like a window into the inner person. A person may appear devout in outward religious acts, but their speech quickly shows whether their heart is actually ordered toward God and love of neighbor.

So the passage is saying: **you cannot rightly bless the Father while cursing those who bear His likeness.** Such speech exposes a split within the soul.
