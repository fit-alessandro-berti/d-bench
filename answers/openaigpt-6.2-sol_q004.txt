Proverbs 3:5–6 asks for **wholehearted reliance on God, not the suspension of judgment**. The key word is *lean*: the verse does not say, “Do not use your understanding,” but “Do not make it what bears your full weight.”

**Humble self-distrust** means recognizing that even careful judgment can be limited or bent by fear, pride, or desire. It says, “I have reasons for thinking this, but I may be missing something, and I’m willing to be corrected.” **Anti-intellectualism**, by contrast, treats careful thought, evidence, or questions as threats to faith. That reading clashes with Proverbs itself, which urges readers to seek understanding (2:2–6) and listen to counsel (12:15).

To “acknowledge him” in all your ways, then, is to take God into account as you deliberate, not merely to ask him to bless a decision you have already made. The trust is not that God will explain every turn in advance, but that you can follow his wisdom without having to see the whole road.
