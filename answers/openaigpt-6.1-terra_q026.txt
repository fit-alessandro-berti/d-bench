The passage uses **“treasure”** as an image for what a person stores within: desires, beliefs, attitudes, and moral habits. As a tree produces fruit according to its kind (Matthew 12:33), so the heart supplies what the mouth brings forth.

Words are therefore **all three**:

- **Symptoms:** They can indicate the condition of the heart beneath them.
- **Revelations:** They make inward attitudes audible to others.
- **Choices:** Speech is something people are responsible for, as the following verses emphasize (12:36–37).

Jesus is addressing Pharisees who have accused him of acting by demonic power. Their words reveal a hostile disposition toward what he is doing. The point is not that every isolated remark infallibly exposes a person’s entire character, or that outwardly good speech cannot be faked. Rather, speech—especially as a pattern—both expresses and enacts what is stored in the heart.
