James 3:9–10 exposes the contradiction of a person who claims to honor God while using the same God-given faculty of speech to degrade human beings, who bear God’s image. The mouth that blesses “God, even the Father” also curses people “made after the similitude of God.” James says this should not be so because it divides worship from love, reverence from contempt, and devotion from cruelty.

The inner contradiction is essentially this: one cannot truly honor the Creator while despising His image-bearers. To bless God is to acknowledge His goodness, majesty, and authority; to curse those made in His likeness is to insult the very dignity God has placed upon them. The same tongue cannot consistently flow with praise toward God and poison toward man.

Speech reveals moral disorder quickly because words are among the most immediate expressions of the heart. James has already described the tongue as small but powerful, like a bit in a horse’s mouth or a spark that sets a forest on fire. Speech is often spontaneous, unguarded, and revealing. It discloses what a person truly loves, fears, resents, or values before they have time to manage appearances.

Jesus taught the same principle: “Out of the abundance of the heart the mouth speaks” (Matthew 12:34). A person may control outward behavior for a time, but speech—especially under pressure, offense, or excitement—quickly betrays the inner condition. Thus, when blessing and cursing proceed from the same mouth, it shows not merely inconsistency in words, but disorder in the soul.
