Amos rejects the worship so sharply because the problem is not too much worship and too little justice. It is worship being offered as though it could make injustice beside the point.

Earlier in the chapter, Amos accuses the people of trampling the poor, taking bribes, and denying the needy justice at the city gate (Amos 5:11–12). Yet they still bring offerings and sing as if their relationship with God were sound. The fraud is the claim of devotion to God while refusing what God requires in their dealings with their neighbors. That fraud need not be consciously cynical: people can be sincere in their ceremonies and still deceive themselves about what those ceremonies mean.

That is why God refuses the whole performance—the assemblies, sacrifices, and songs. “Let judgment run down as waters” is not a call to add a charitable act to the religious calendar. “Judgment” here means justice: the fair treatment that people were being denied. The image of a mighty stream calls for justice to flow steadily through public life. Worship cannot substitute for it.
