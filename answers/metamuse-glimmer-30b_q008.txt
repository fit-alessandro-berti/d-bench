In John love is not a sentiment, it is obedience. 

In 1 Jn 3:11-24; 4:7-21 love is the commandment *from the beginning* that Jesus gave, "love one another as I have loved you". God is love, 4:8,16, and to know God is to abide in that love. That is why the test of the children of God is not correct doctrine about the invisible God but visible obedience to the commandment to love.

The brother is seen, God is not seen. The logic is not that love for the neighbour causes love for God, but that love for God is necessarily evidenced in love for the neighbour, because the neighbour is the visible image of the invisible God.

* If you love the source you must love what is from the source. The brother is created in the image of God and is the member of God's family. To hate him is to hate the image-bearer of God and therefore to hate God in him.
* Love is practical and testable. You can never prove to another, and you cannot finally prove even to yourself, that you love an invisible Being. You can prove it by how you treat a visible person. The unseen love is authenticated in the seen love. John puts it as a reductio: He who does not love the brother whom he has seen, how can he love God whom he has not seen?
* In John's incarnational theology God became visible. God was seen in Jesus who "loved us and gave himself for us". The church is the continuing visibility of that love. To claim love for the invisible Father while refusing love for the visible members of His Son is a contradiction.

That is the self-deception exposed: the divorce of vertical piety from horizontal ethics. It is the lie of a purely spiritual, interior religion in which love of God is reduced to an idea, a feeling, a profession of words, while the clear command of God is disobeyed.

He is a liar, *pseudes estin*, not simply a false speaker but a person who lives in falsehood. 1 Jn 2:4 "He who says, 'I know him,' and does not keep his commandments, is a liar, and the truth is not in him." It is the self-deception of thinking you can be in the light with God while walking in darkness toward your brother, 2:9-11. It is spiritual pride that lets a man say "I love God" about an abstraction he never sees, while his heart is cold, indifferent or hostile toward the concrete neighbour God has put before him.

In short, John is exposing the hypocrisy of loving the concept of God and hating God’s children, the claim to have intimacy with the unseen while refusing fellowship with the seen. That is not love for God at all, it is self-deception about one’s own state before God.
