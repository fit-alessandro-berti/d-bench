

The metaphor of a root emphasizes origin and potential over immediate visibility. Unlike a singular act, a root implies a living, growing source that exists beneath the surface. This suggests several key dynamics:

1.  **Hidden Development:** Just as roots grow unseen underground, resentment often takes hold silently. It begins as an internal attitude rather than an outward action, allowing it to strengthen without immediate correction.
2.  **Delayed Consequences:** A root must mature before it produces fruit or structural damage. Similarly, the harm of resentment may not be immediate but accumulates over time, eventually disrupting peace and stability.
3.  **Communal Contamination:** Roots spread and intertwine. The text warns that this growth troubles the individual and defiles many. This implies that unchecked personal bitterness can poison the wider community, affecting relationships and shared moral health.

In essence, the imagery warns against ignoring internal attitudes, as they possess the power to grow, destabilize, and spread beyond the initial source.
