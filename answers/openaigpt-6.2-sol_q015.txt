The image is sharp because it is ridiculous: someone with a beam sticking out of his own eye offers to perform delicate work on someone else’s. The contrast between the speck and the beam exposes a double standard: he notices the smallest fault in his brother while overlooking a glaring fault in himself. And the beam isn’t just a bigger fault—it’s what keeps him from seeing clearly enough to help.

The key words are **“first”** and **“then.”** Jesus doesn’t say the speck is imaginary or that it should stay where it is. He says to remove the beam *first*; *then* you can see clearly to help your brother. The passage calls for honest self-correction, not moral silence. Its aim is to turn correction from an occasion for superiority into an act of care.
