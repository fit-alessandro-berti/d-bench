Bitterness is called a **root** because the danger is not just a single angry act. An act is visible; a root can grow unseen. A wrong may be real, and the hurt it causes is not itself bitterness. The warning is about what can take hold beneath the surface and begin to shape how a person sees and treats others.

The phrase **“springing up”** points to delayed damage. By the time bitterness shows itself in a cutting remark, a refusal to seek peace, or a desire to see someone fail, it may already be well established. “Looking diligently” means paying attention before those signs appear, rather than dealing only with the damage once it is visible.

And **“many be defiled”** makes this a communal warning. One person’s resentment can become a group’s suspicion: a grievance is retold, people are pressed to take sides, and others begin to carry a hostility that was not originally theirs. The image echoes Deuteronomy 29:18, where a poisonous root represents a hidden turning away from God within the community. So Hebrews is concerned with more than a private feeling: what takes root in one person can undermine the peace and faithfulness of many.
