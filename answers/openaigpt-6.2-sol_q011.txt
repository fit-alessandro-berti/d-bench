Isaiah moves from “cease to do evil” to “learn to do well” because stopping harm is necessary, but it is not the whole of repentance. A person can refrain from exploiting the vulnerable and still do nothing when others exploit them.

The word “learn” suggests that goodness takes practice. The commands that follow show what that practice looks like: seek justice, help the oppressed, defend the fatherless, and plead for the widow. These are not simply ways to keep one’s own hands clean; they require noticing who is being wronged and acting on their behalf.

So goodness includes restraint, but it does not end there. In Isaiah’s vision, love takes the form of justice: not only refusing to do wrong, but learning to stand up for those who are wronged.
