Light is hated here not because it is harmful, but because it makes concealment impossible. A person may want to keep both an evil deed and a good opinion of themselves; light threatens that arrangement. To be seen clearly can mean facing guilt, losing control of one’s reputation, and feeling the shame of being known.

That helps explain the passage’s contrast. Those who “doeth evil” avoid the light *lest their deeds should be reproved*—exposed for what they are. The person who “doeth truth” comes to it. That does not necessarily mean they have nothing to confess. It means they are willing for reality to be known, including their need for God. Their deeds being “wrought in God” leaves little room for boasting.

John’s image also distinguishes exposure from humiliation. Light reveals; it need not delight in shaming. In this Gospel, the light comes into the world through Christ, who offers life. The tragedy is that shame can make the very place where one might be healed feel like the place one must avoid.
