The verse treats love of the visible neighbor as the test of love for the invisible God because, in 1 John, love for God is not merely an inward feeling or a religious claim; it is a living reality that must take visible, concrete form.

John’s logic is: if love does not work where it can be seen — in actual relationships with actual people — then the claim that it exists toward God, whom one has not seen, is empty. The unseen is verified in the seen.

In context, John has just said:

- “God is love” — 1 John 4:8, 16
- “We love because he first loved us” — 4:19
- “If we love one another, God abides in us” — 4:12
- “Whoever loves God must also love his brother” — 4:21

So love of God and love of the brother are not two separate virtues. The one is the root; the other is the fruit. If God’s love truly abides in a person, it will show itself in love toward those made in God’s image, especially toward fellow believers.

The verse exposes a specific kind of self-deception: the belief that one can love God while harboring hatred, contempt, or indifference toward other people. That is easy to do because God is invisible. One can say, “I love God,” and keep that claim in the realm of ideas, emotions, doctrine, or private piety. But the brother is visible. He has needs, weaknesses, sins, irritating habits, and claims on our patience and sacrifice. Love for him is testable.

So John is unmasking a “spiritual” love that costs nothing — a love of God in the abstract that does notTranslate into mercy, forgiveness, patience, and practical care. The person may be sincere in saying the words, but John calls that self-deception a lie because the claim is contradicted by the person’s actual life.

This fits the repeated pattern in 1 John:

- “If we say we have fellowship with him while we walk in darkness, we lie” — 1:6
- “If anyone says, ‘I know him,’ but does not keep his commandments, he is a liar” — 2:4
- “If anyone says, ‘I love God,’ and hates his brother, he is a liar” — 4:20

In each case, the problem is not merely imperfect obedience but a profession of relationship with God that is contradicted by conduct.

The verse does not mean that love of neighbor replaces love of God, or that neighbor-love earns God’s love. Rather, it means that genuine love of God must become visible in neighbor-love. If it does not, what someone calls “love of God” is likely an abstract, self-comforting illusion rather than the love that comes from God.
