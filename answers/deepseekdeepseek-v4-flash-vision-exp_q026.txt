The passage invites exactly that “all three” answer, but with an important biblical nuance: speech is not just an involuntary symptom or a bare choice; it is the overflow of the heart’s stored-up moral condition.

### 1. The heart is a hidden moral stockpile

In Scripture, the “heart” is not just feelings. It is the inner center of thinking, willing, and loving—the core of who you are. Jesus calls the heart a “treasure” that can be good or evil.

> “A good man out of the good treasure of the heart bringeth forth good things: and an evil man out of the evil treasure bringeth forth evil things.”

The word “treasure” suggests a hidden storehouse. What you put in—truth or lies, love or resentment, purity or corruption—accumulates there. It is “hidden” until it comes out. But it is not neutral. The heart is always storing something. And eventually, what is stored will show.

### 2. Words are symptoms

Jesus says:

> “Out of the abundance of the heart the mouth speaketh.”

The idea is like a container that overflows. When the heart is full, the mouth pours out what is in it. Words are therefore a diagnostic sign—like a fever reveals an infection. If a man speaks evil, the evil was already there. The words do not create the condition; they expose it.

So yes, words are symptoms in that sense: they point to the hidden condition of the heart. A good tree bears good fruit; a bad tree bears bad fruit. Fruit doesn’t lie about the tree.

### 3. Words are choices

But this does not make speech innocent or automatic. Jesus is not saying, “It’s okay—you’re just overflowing.” He is confronting them:

> “O generation of vipers, how can ye, being evil, speak good things?”

This is a moral challenge. They are responsible for their words. In Greek, the phrase “bringeth forth” means the man actively draws out and presents what is in the treasury. The good man *does* bring forth good things; the evil man *does* bring forth evil things. Words are not accidental emissions. They are choices made by the will, though the will is shaped by the heart.

Jesus makes this even clearer in the next verses:

> “Every idle word that men shall speak, they shall give account thereof in the day of judgment. For by thy words thou shalt be justified, and by thy words thou shalt be condemned.”  
> — Matthew 12:36–37

Words are accounted for. They are acts for which human beings are morally responsible.

### 4. Words are revelations

Finally, words reveal the hidden self. The word “treasure” implies concealment. The heart is a hidden vault. But when a person speaks, the vault is unlocked. Speech brings the hidden moral stockpile into the open.

This is not “revelation” in the sense of supernatural prophecy. It is “revelation” in the sense of exposure: words reveal who you truly are. You may hide a heart of pride behind polite words for a time, but eventually the overflow has a way of giving you away. Jesus even says “out of the abundance,” meaning the heart is *full*, and the mouth speaks what is too full to stay hidden.

### So: all three

Words are **symptoms** because they point to an inner condition.  
Words are **choices** because a responsible person speaks from the heart and is accountable for it.  
Words are **revelations** because they make hidden moral treasure visible.

The passage teaches that these three are not competing categories. They are unified: the heart is the hidden treasury, the will brings forth from it, and speech is the visible evidence. If you want different speech, you need a different heart. That is why Jesus’ call is not “fix your mouth” but “make the tree good.”
