The verse does both, and the grammar and context show how. Read as a bare proverb, "where your treasure is, there your heart will be" describes a correspondence. But Matthew doesn't leave it bare. It sits inside a set of imperatives ("Lay not up... but lay up"), and the saying is introduced with "for" (*gar*), so it functions as the reason for the command. Jesus is telling his hearers what to do and why it matters. That pushes the saying toward formation: where you deposit your life determines where your heart ends up.

**Reveal and form are two sides of one loop**

- *Revelation:* Treasure is defined by what you hold dear. Whatever you invest your time, money, and anxiety in is already what your heart prizes. The treasure exposes the heart.
- *Formation:* Attention and investment are not neutral. What you guard, return to, and worry about reshapes your affections over time. The treasure pulls the heart toward it.

Neither reading alone is complete. "Treasure reveals heart" leaves the command pointless, since you can't choose a diagnosis. "Treasure trains heart" leaves unexplained why the heart must already be somewhere for the treasure to be called "yours." Matthew holds both: the heart shows up in what you treasure, and what you treasure in turn moves the heart.

**How possession, attention, and love connect**

1. *Possession* is the concrete object: the storehouse, the account, the thing you own and must protect. The imagery of moth, rust, and thieves is about things that can be lost, which means they demand watchfulness.
2. *Attention* is what possession demands. You watch what you own, count it, fear losing it. Verses 22–23 make this explicit with the eye: "If thine eye be single, thy whole body shall be full of light." The eye directs the body, and attention determines the whole orientation of a person.
3. *Love* (the "heart," *kardia*, in Hebrew thought the center of will and desire, not just feeling) is what attention accumulates into. What you keep looking at, you come to want. Verse 24 names the stakes: "Ye cannot serve God and mammon." Service is love expressed as loyalty.

So the chain runs possession → attention → love → further possession. Love chooses the treasure, the treasure captures attention, attention deepens love. This is why Jesus can make a claim that sounds like a psychological observation and a moral command at once.

**Why this matters for the reading**

The passage suggests that the heart is not a fixed inner essence that we merely inspect. It is shaped through practices of holding and guarding. That is why the remedy Jesus offers is relocation of treasure, not first a purification of feelings. Change where you invest, and over time the heart follows. The verse is diagnostic in that it tells you where your heart is by where your treasure is, and it is formative in that moving the treasure is how the heart gets moved.
