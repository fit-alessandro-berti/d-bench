In the surrounding passage, John says that God loved us first, so those who love God are commanded to love one another (1 John 4:19–21). Love of a visible brother or sister is therefore the concrete expression of love for the God we cannot see. The neighbor is not a substitute for God; rather, our treatment of people exposes whether our professed devotion to God is real.

The verse challenges a convenient kind of spirituality: it is easy to claim love for an unseen God in words, while avoiding the demands of love in an actual relationship. John calls that claim a lie because it contradicts God’s command and God’s own love. In 1 John, love is practical—not merely a feeling or profession, but care shown in action (3:17–18).

“Hateth” need not mean only intense hostility; it can include settled rejection or indifference. John is describing a contradiction in a person’s stance, not saying that every struggle, conflict, or failure to love proves someone’s faith is false. The test is whether a professed love for God is taking shape in real concern for other people.
