James 3:9–10 exposes the contradiction of a divided heart: the same person uses his mouth to bless God, yet uses that same mouth to curse human beings who bear God’s image. It is spiritually incoherent to honor the Creator while dishonoring those made in His likeness. To curse a person is, in effect, to insult the One whose image that person carries. It is like praising an artist while defacing his portrait.

The deeper contradiction is this: worship without love is inconsistent. If God is honored with our lips, then those made in His image should be treated with reverence, not contempt. A tongue that blesses God and curses people reveals a heart that is not fully under God’s rule.

Speech reveals moral disorder quickly because words are the overflow of the heart. They expose what is truly ruling us—pride, bitterness, envy, anger, or self-righteousness. Speech is immediate, spontaneous, and often unfiltered, so before we can disguise our inner state, the tongue betrays it. James’s point is that the tongue is small, but it discloses the condition of the whole person. If we cannot govern our words, we reveal that our hearts are still disordered. “These things ought not so to be” because God’s people are called to integrity: the same mouth that blesses God should bless those who bear His image.
