Proverbs 3:5-6 is not a call to check your brain at the door, it is a call to check your *self-sufficiency* at the door.

The trust commanded is *batach* - a settled reliance on the character and promise of Yahweh. "With all thine heart" is *with all your leb*, the centre of will, intellect and affections. It is whole-person, practical dependence, not a feeling. The second line qualifies it: "lean not unto thine own understanding."

*Shanan* "lean" is the image of resting your weight on a staff or crutch. The command is not *do not use* your understanding, but *do not make it the final staff you rest your weight on.* The Proverbs itself is a book that prizes understanding. "The fear of the Lord is the beginning of knowledge," Prov 1:7. "Wisdom... if you seek her as silver," Prov 2:4. "Apply your heart to understanding," Prov 2:2. "Wisdom is the principal thing; therefore get wisdom," Prov 4:7.

So what is being rejected is autonomous self-trust, not intellect.

Humble self-distrust says:

* My understanding is real and God-given, but it is finite, partial, and morally clouded by self-interest and sin. 1 Cor 13:12 "through a glass darkly."
* Therefore I will use my mind to the full, but I will hold its conclusions open to correction by God. I will seek wisdom from him, pray, test my judgments against his Word, and acknowledge his prior claim on my decisions: "In all thy ways acknowledge him."
* The result is that I am directed, *yashar*, trained and made straight. Trust leads to guidance, not to blindness.

Mere anti-intellectualism says:

* My understanding is essentially bad, prideful, or opposed to faith. I should minimize thinking, avoid evidence, or treat reason as the enemy of piety.
* It treats "lean not on thine own understanding" as "do not think at all." That is the opposite of Proverbs.

In Proverbs, faith and understanding are partners. You acknowledge God *in* all your ways, which includes the intellectual way. You trust him *with* all your heart, which includes the heart's capacity to reason. Humility is bringing your best thinking to God for his direction, not pretending you have no thinking to bring.

That is why the promise is not "you will be kept in comfortable ignorance" but "he shall direct thy paths." A directed path requires a path to be walked, choices to be made, and a mind to make them. The trust is the posture that lets God correct, complete, and steer the understanding you already have.
