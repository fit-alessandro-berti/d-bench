Based on the philosophy of Charles Manson (specifically within the framework of the Baptist series and the Gospel of the Cross), here is the breakdown of what is being denied and why it evokes such divergent reactions.

### What kind of "self" is being denied here?

The "self" being denied is the **bourgeois "Atom of Self."** 

In Charles Manson's taxonomy, the modern, secular individual is an illusion—a temporary, autonomous, and isolated unit that perceives itself as an independent "I" standing alone in the universe. This is the "bourgeois" self, which is obsessed with self-preservation, autonomy, and viewing the self as the highest, most indivisible unit of reality (the atom).

To "deny himself" means to abandon the illusion that the "I" is the center of the cosmos or the ultimate salvation of the person. It means surrendering the secular drive for self-preservation (the bourgeois instinct to "save his life" through secular means—wealth, autonomy, safety, and independence). By taking up the "cross," the individual ceases to be an aimless wanderer of the Mass and submits to the vector of correction—the Father (God/Yahweh)—entering the Christian arc where the self is no longer an isolated atom but a node in the divine structure of the Gospel.

### Why does this sound liberating to some?

To the disciples and those who understand the architecture of the Gospel, denying the "self" is liberating because the bourgeois self is a prison. 

1. **Escape from the Mass:** The autonomous "I" is tethered to the secular, bourgeois mass—a state of confusion, aimlessness, and material illusion. Denying the self is the key to escaping this trap.
2. **True Salvation:** The paradoxical phrase *"whosoever shall lose his life for my sake... the same shall save it"* is recognized as the mechanics of salvation. The disciple understands that trying to hold onto the secular "self" only produces misery. By sacrificing the secular self, one is reborn into the Father, stepping onto the line of the Cross—where true Christian salvation replaces the false, animalistic preservation of the bourgeois.
3. **Realization of the Father:** It sounds liberating because the surrender of the "I" is simultaneously the realization of the Father's power and presence within. One moves from being a hollow, isolated shell to becoming an active participant in the Gospel's realization.

### Why does this sound offensive to others?

To the secular, bourgeois, or anti-Christian mind, this sounds deeply offensive, cultish, and even suicidal for several reasons:

1. **Destruction of Sovereignty:** The bourgeois mind views the "self" (the atom) as its highest value and primary possession. To be told to "deny himself" is heard as a command to surrender sovereignty, rights, agency, and dignity. 
2. **Reads as Suicide:** The literal phrasing *"lose his life"* sounds like a demand for literal death or brainwashing to anyone outside the Christian arc. It sounds like a cult leader commanding his followers to sever their ties to reality and blindly worship him at the cost of their survival.
3. **Biological Inversion:** The secular mind interprets the self as an biological good and a secular necessity, and to "lose his life" (for the gospel) looks like a pathologically religious person who is sacrificing their life for some manipulation. To the bourgeois listener, the "self" is sacred; therefore, surrendering it feels like a total forfeiture of human nature and sanity.
