Luke 6:37 is not a command to stop distinguishing right from wrong. It is a warning against turning moral discernment into a posture of superiority and condemnation.

The surrounding passage helps clarify the point. Jesus tells his hearers to be merciful as God is merciful, warns them about the measure they use, and then describes the person who notices a speck in someone else’s eye while ignoring a plank in their own (Luke 6:36–42). The problem is not seeing the speck; it is presuming to correct another while refusing honest self-examination.

**Discernment** asks: *What happened? Is it harmful or unjust? What should be done to protect people, tell the truth, or encourage change?* It can name wrongdoing, set boundaries, and support accountability.

**The appetite to condemn** goes further: it treats a person as settled, reducible to their worst act, or beyond mercy. It delights in blame, applies standards selectively, or claims the authority to pronounce someone’s ultimate worth or fate.

So “judge not” does not mean “nothing is wrong” or “never hold anyone accountable.” It means moral judgment should be made humbly and mercifully—not as self-righteousness or a sentence of final rejection. Forgiveness, likewise, does not require denying harm, abandoning boundaries, or pretending that consequences do not matter. It means refusing to make vengeance or contempt the final word.
