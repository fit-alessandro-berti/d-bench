The passage praises **power expressed through self-giving rather than domination**. Christ’s greatness is not displayed by insisting on status or shielding himself from suffering, but by taking “the form of a servant” and becoming obedient even to death on a cross. His humility is presented not as a lack of greatness, but as the way that greatness is revealed.

Humility joined to greatness can feel unsettling because it disrupts our usual expectations of power. Open domination is recognizable: it demands submission and gives us an obvious force to resist. But someone with great power who willingly serves cannot be understood so easily in those terms. Such humility can expose our own attachment to status and control—and invite a response not of fear, but of gratitude, trust, or imitation.
