

The passage calls for a comprehensive turn, addressing both external behavior and internal disposition. Forsaking one’s "way" refers to abandoning sinful actions and lifestyle choices. Similarly, leaving behind "thoughts" targets the plans, intentions, and mental patterns that drive those actions.

This dual requirement implies that genuine repentance is not merely surface-level correction. Outward conduct must change, but so must the inward imagination that fuels it. Sin often originates in the mind before manifesting in deeds; therefore, true restoration requires aligning both heart and hand.

The promise of mercy is tied to this wholehearted return. Because the turn is total, the resulting pardon is described as abundant. It suggests that divine compassion is fully available to those who seek it completely, transforming both what they do and what they envision.
