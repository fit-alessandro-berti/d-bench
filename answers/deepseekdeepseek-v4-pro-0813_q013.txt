The sharpness is deliberate. Amos is not saying Israel’s worship is merely “out of balance” with its ethics. He is saying that, under the conditions of their injustice, their worship has become a lie—worse, an insult to God. God “hates” and “despises” their feasts because the people are using them as a religious mask for oppression.

The immediate context is crucial. In Amos 5:10–12 the prophet condemns those who:

- trample the poor,
- extract grain from them,
- take bribes,
- push aside the needy at the city gate,
- afflict the righteous.

Then, in Amos 5:21–24, he says that these same people are bringing offerings, keeping feast days, singing hymns, and playing instruments. The moral and spiritual fraud is this: they are performing covenant worship while violating the covenant in their social and economic life. Their liturgy gives them a sense of being right with God, but their hands are full of injustice.

That is why God does not ask for “better balance.” If God merely asked them to add a little more justice alongside their sacrifices, that would imply their worship was basically acceptable. But Amos says the worship itself is rejected. God does not want their sacrifices or their songs while they are oppressing the poor. The only thing God will accept is a transformed common life:

> “Let judgment run down as waters, and righteousness as a mighty stream.”

So the fraud exposed is not simply “empty ritual.” It is worship used as a cover for sin. The people treat sacrifice and song as a transaction that makes them acceptable to God, while their daily lives deny God’s character. In prophetic thought, love of God and love of neighbor cannot be separated. Worship without justice is not worship; it is hypocrisy.
