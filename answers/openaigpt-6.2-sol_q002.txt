The psalmist asks for a clean heart because pardon answers for what he has done, while a clean heart changes what he loves, wills, and chooses. He wants forgiveness, but he does not want to be forgiven and remain the same.

Wanting escape from guilt can mean wanting the shame, fear, or consequences to end without facing what made the wrong seem worth doing. Wanting inner change means asking a harder question: *Even if no one found out and I never had to pay for it, would I still want to turn away from it?* The difference is not how intensely one feels remorse, but what one wants remorse to lead to.

“Create in me” makes this more than a promise to try harder. The psalmist asks God to do in him what he cannot accomplish by resolve alone. And “restore unto me the joy of thy salvation” shows that repentance is not a wish to feel guilty forever. He wants the joy of being near God again—and a willing spirit to sustain a different way of living.
