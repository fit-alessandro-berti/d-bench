

The passage rests on the premise that concrete relationships validate abstract claims. Since the divine is unseen, true devotion must be observable through how one treats visible people. If affection cannot be shown to a tangible neighbor, the profession of love for an invisible deity remains unverified.

This exposes a deception where inner feelings are mistaken for actual virtue. It is easier to claim attachment to a distant ideal than to endure the challenges of loving a flawed peer. Such individuals may believe they are spiritually sound while neglecting the practical demands of faith. The text warns that without ethical action toward others, religious sentiment becomes merely an illusion.
