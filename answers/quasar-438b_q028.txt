It says, in effect: “You may condemn her, but only if you yourself have never sinned.” Since no human is sinless, the condition is almost impossible to meet. The sentence appears to license judgment morally while making that license practically unusable.

So it does several things at once:

1. **It forbids unqualified condemnation.**  
   It does not say, “Do not judge.” It says, “Only the perfectly pure may judge.” That makes moral criticism hostage to the speaker’s own purity.

2. **It exposes hypocrisy.**  
   The person who throws the stone is claiming the right to judge, but the line reminds us that that person too has sin. It makes the thrower’s act confess something about himself.

3. **It interrupts mob-righteousness.**  
   A crowd can feel safely righteous only if its members can pretend their sins are irrelevant. The sentence inoculates them: no one is morally spotless. Moral outrage becomes self-indicting.

4. **It shifts guilt by association.**  
   If anyone throws the stone, he convicts himself in front of the others. The woman’s guilt rebounds back onto the thrower.

So it does all the things you name, but something deeper too.

It forbids **unearned judgment**: not all judgment, only judgment from a false position of purity.

It exposes **hypocrisy**: the one who casts the stone declares his own sinfulness by casting it.

It interrupts **mob-righteousness**: a crowd can stay righteous only so long as no one asks whether any of them is pure. The line makes that question unavoidable.

The deeper destabilization is that it converts **accusation into self-accusation**. The woman’s guilt no longer stays with her; it returns upon the man who strikes her. The stone becomes a mirror. And because everyone is sinful, the sentence leaves the community in an impossible posture: morally everyone may be condemnable, but morally no one is authorized to condemn. That is why it is not merely wise counsel; it is a rhetorical trap that reveals that punishment sooner or later turns back upon the system of punishers.
