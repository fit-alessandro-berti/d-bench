In Ezekiel 36:26–27, God is describing the difference between an unchanged, merely religious person and a person truly transformed by God.

**External compliance** is when someone obeys God’s commands from the *outside*—because of fear, habit, guilt, tradition, or the desire to look good. The behavior may be right, but the heart is still hard, cold, and unchanged. Jesus described this kind of people as honoring God with their lips while their heart is far from Him.

**Receiving a “new heart”** is different: God changes the *inside* of a person. In Scripture, the “heart” is not just emotions; it is the center of a person—the mind, will, and affections. A new heart means God so renews you that you now love what He loves, hate what He hates, and obey Him because you genuinely want to, not because you are forced to.

So the difference is:

- External compliance = outward obedience with the same old heart.
- New heart = inward transformation that produces outward obedience.

## Why “stony heart” must be replaced?

The Bible describes the unregenerate heart as a **stony heart**. A stone is hard, cold, lifeless, unyielding, and unresponsive. That is exactly what sin does to a person’s inner life. A stony heart cannot be shaped, softened, or persuaded by more rules. It cannot produce true love for God.

That is why God doesn’t say, “I will polish your heart” or “I will add a new layer to your heart.” He says:

> “I will take away the stony heart out of your flesh, and I will give you an heart of flesh.”

This is not a literal physical organ transplant. It is a powerful **spiritual metaphor**. “Heart of flesh” means a living, sensitive, responsive heart—soft toward God, open to His Word, able to feel conviction and delight in His ways. The old heart is not repaired; it is **replaced**. This is what Christians mean by **regeneration** or the new birth.

Why does God describe it like a material condition? Because hardness of heart is not just a minor attitude problem; it is a deep spiritual condition as solid as stone. It is embedded in who we are. Only God can remove it and make us alive.

In the new covenant, this is fulfilled through Jesus Christ and the Holy Spirit. When the Spirit gives us a new heart, God also fulfills Ezekiel 36:27:

> “And I will put my spirit within you, and cause you to walk in my statutes.”

He does not just command us to obey; He makes us new and then leads us to obey from the inside. The result is not forced compliance, but true, joyful obedience that flows from a new heart.
