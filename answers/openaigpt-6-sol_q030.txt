Paul names so many things love *does not* do because pride, envy, irritability, and suspicion can masquerade as strength. They promise to protect the self or secure its importance, but they make other people into rivals, threats, or debts to be collected.

The strength pictured here is **self-command in service of another’s good**. Love can bear injury without rushing to retaliate, rejoice in another’s gifts without feeling diminished, and remain patient when patience costs something. Yet it is not blind or passive: it “rejoiceth in the truth,” not in wrongdoing. “Believeth all things” expresses a generous disposition, not an obligation to ignore harm.

Paul’s portrait makes love strong precisely because it need not win every contest of ego. It can endure, hope, and act kindly without surrendering its commitment to truth.
