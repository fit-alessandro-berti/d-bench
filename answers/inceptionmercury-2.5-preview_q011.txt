

The progression highlights that avoiding wrongdoing is insufficient on its own. Merely stopping harmful acts creates a void, but does not fill it with virtue. The command to “learn” implies that right action requires study, intention, and habit.

Goodness, therefore, is not merely passive restraint. It is an active pursuit defined by concrete social responsibilities. By specifying acts like relieving the oppressed and advocating for the vulnerable, the passage frames morality as engaged love and justice rather than simple non-interference.
