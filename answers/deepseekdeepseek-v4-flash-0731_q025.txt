The danger is **not visibility itself**, but **the craving to be seen**—the heart’s desire for human applause rather than God’s approval.

Jesus says:

> “Take heed that ye do not your alms before men, **to be seen of them**…”

The Greek phrase is *pros to theathēnai autois* — “with the aim/purpose of being seen by them.” The sin is not that others happen to see your good deed; it is that you perform the deed *in order to* be seen. That turns goodness into a performance for an audience.

Notice that Jesus does **not** say all public goodness is wrong. Earlier in Matthew 5:16 He says:

> “Let your light so shine before men, that they may see your good works, and glorify your Father which is in heaven.”

Here, the same visibility is good—because the goal is that God gets glory, not that I get praise. So the moral difference is **motive**: Is the good deed pointed toward God’s glory and neighbor’s good, or toward my own reputation?

The danger of public goodness is that it feeds the **craving to be seen**. That craving is idolatry: it substitutes human approval for the reward of the Father. Jesus says such people “have their reward” already—the applause they wanted is all they get.

So the problem isn’t that people see you. The problem is that you want them to see you—and shape your “goodness” around that desire. The solution is not to hide all good works, but to purify the heart, practice secret obedience, and seek the Father who “sees in secret.”
