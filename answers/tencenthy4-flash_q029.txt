The correction required in Galatians 6:1 is **restorative, gentle, and self-watchful**—the posture of a fellow sinner helping to mend another, not of a superior judging an inferior.

Paul says, “ye which are spiritual, restore such an one.” The “spiritual” person is not the one who is most severe or most confident in his own purity, but the one governed by the Spirit, whose fruit includes meekness, patience, and self-control (Gal. 5:22–23).

The three elements belong together:

1. **Restoration is the goal**  
   “Restore” means to mend, set right, bring back into place. The fault is real and must not be ignored, but the aim is not punishment, exposure, embarrassment, or victory over the offender. The aim is the person’s recovery.

2. **Meekness is the manner**  
   Correction can be true and still be cruel. Harshness often hardens the offender, drives them into hiding, and turns a fall into shame or resentment. Meekness does not weaken the truth; it makes the truth bearable. It corrects without trampling.

3. **Self-suspicion is the guard of the heart**  
   “Considering thyself, lest thou also be tempted” means the corrector must remember: “I am not beyond this. I too am vulnerable.” This prevents correction from becoming self-righteousness, superiority, or moral display. The danger is not only that I might commit the same outward sin, but that I might fall through pride, harshness, or spiritual vanity while correcting someone else.

So Paul keeps these together because each protects the others:

- **Restoration without meekness** can become coercive or humiliating.  
- **Meekness without restoration** can become sentimental permissiveness.  
- **Correction without self-suspicion** becomes hypocrisy.  
- **Self-suspicion without restoration** becomes avoidance of duty.

The moral posture is therefore: **truthful accountability exercised in humility, with the aim of healing rather than condemnation.** The offender is treated as a brother or sister to be regained; the corrector is reminded that he also stands in need of mercy.
