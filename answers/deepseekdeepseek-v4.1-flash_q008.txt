1 John 4:20 treats love for the visible brother as a test of love for the invisible God because love for God is not a mere inward feeling or verbal claim. It must become concrete. God is unseen, so a person can easily say, “I love God,” without anyone being able to verify it directly. But the brother is seen. God places the visible brother/neighbor in front of us as the practical sphere where love for God must show itself.

In 1 John, love is Christ-shaped and active, not sentimental. God’s love became visible when he sent his Son (4:9–10), and our love becomes visible when we lay down our lives for one another (3:16–18). The command is inseparable: “Whoever loves God must also love his brother” (4:21). So if someone hates the brother he can see, his claim to love the God he cannot see is contradicted. The logic is not that the neighbor is God, but that God has bound love for himself to love for the neighbor—especially the brother in Christ.

The self-deception being exposed is the illusion that one can love God while hating people. It is:

- **Verbal piety without ethical love** — saying “I love God” while living in hatred, contempt, unforgiveness, or neglect.
- **A private, invisible spirituality detached from visible relationships** — as if devotion to God can be real while love for others is optional.
- **Using God’s invisibility as a cover** — since God cannot be seen, one can claim to love him; but the visible brother exposes whether that claim is true.
- **False assurance** — thinking that hating a brother is compatible with loving God.
- **A loveless orthodoxy or emotional mysticism** — right words or religious feelings without concrete love.

John calls such a person a liar. The point is not that love for neighbor earns love for God, but that love for God is authenticated by love for the brother. The unseen God is loved truly when his visible command is obeyed in the visible neighbor.
