The passage suggests **all three**—but not as separate, competing categories. Jesus joins them together.

The “heart” in biblical language is not just feelings; it is the hidden center of thought, will, and moral character. The “treasure” (Greek *thēsauros*) is what a person has stored up there: loves, desires, convictions, habits, intentions. Speech is the way that hidden stockpile becomes public.

### 1. Words are symptoms

> “Out of the abundance of the heart the mouth speaketh.”

“Abundance” means fullness, overflow. What fills the heart spills out through the mouth. The surrounding context says a tree is known by its fruit—so words are fruit that reveal the condition of the tree. The Pharisees’ hostile accusation against Jesus was not a random slip; it was an outflow of a corrupt heart.

### 2. Words are choices

> “A good man out of the good treasure of the heart bringeth forth good things: and an evil man out of the evil treasure bringeth forth evil things.”

The verb “bringeth forth” is active. A person is not a passive pipe through which inner feelings leak. The person *speaks*, *decides*, and *produces*. The evil man does not get a free pass because he is evil; rather, his evil speech is his own moral act. His words are not just symptoms; they are deeds he is responsible for.

### 3. Words are revelations

Jesus continues:

> “Every idle word that men shall speak, they shall give account of in the day of judgment. For by thy words thou shalt be justified, and by thy words thou shalt be condemned.”

Words expose what was hidden. They reveal the heart’s treasure to other people—and they serve as evidence before God. Speech is the visible disclosure of the invisible self.

So the answer is: **all three**. Words are **symptoms** because they reveal the heart’s moral state, **choices** because the person actively brings them forth, and **revelations** because they uncover what was stored secretly inside. The mouth is the outlet; the heart is the storehouse; the words are the overflow—and the overflow proves what was really in the treasury.
