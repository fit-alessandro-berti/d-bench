Strictly speaking, the text says that treasure **reveals** the heart—“where your treasure is, there will your heart be also” functions as a diagnostic indicator. If you want to know where someone’s heart truly lies, look where they place their treasure. 

However, the verse only works as a revelatory sign because of an underlying psychology in which treasure **trains** the heart. Augustine’s famous line rests on a theory of attachment: love is the residue of sustained attention, and attention is the energy expended securing possession. So the verse reveals the heart by pointing to the treasure that has trained it.

Here is how possession, attention, and love become tied together:

**1. Possession demands attention.**  
To “lay up” a treasure is to secure it against threats. Augustine explicitly lists the enemies of earthly treasure: *moth, rust, thieves*. Possession is not a static legal state but a continuous labor. Whatever you own, you must attend to—guarding it, repairing it, watching it. Earthly treasure is efficient at anchoring attention to earthly things.

**2. Attention trains love.**  
Attention is the medium through which affection grows. When we repeatedly concentrate our energy on an object or place, love crystallizes around it. We think love antecedently selects the treasures we pursue, but Augustine suggests the causal flow also runs the other way: the act of securing a treasure trains affection upon it. To guard the treasure is to love the treasure.

**3. Possession therefore dictates love.**  
If possession requires attention, and attention trains love, then the site of possession determines the object of love. Earthly treasures demand earthly attention; earthly attention trains earthly love. Hence “For where your treasure is, there will your heart be also.” 

This chaining is why Augustine’s recommendation is not merely prudential but spiritual. Earthly treasures corrupt not just because they rust, but because they fix our heart to the wrong station. By contrast, treasures laid up in heaven are safe from moth and thief; they release attention from worldly decay and train love toward what does not corrupt. Relocating treasure thereby relocates the heart.
