The passage’s power lies in its **absurdist hyperbole** and its **reversal of priorities**. Jesus takes a common proverbial saying (likely already circulating in Jewish wisdom traditions) and inflates it into a grotesque visual impossibility: a person with a construction timber (*dokos*) lodged in their eye offering to extract a speck of sawdust (*karphos*) from someone else’s. The image is sharp because it is physically ridiculous—you cannot see with a beam in your eye, let alone perform delicate surgery on another. This absurdity exposes the logic of self-deception: the critic is literally blind to their own catastrophic flaw while fixated on a trivial imperfection in others.

**How it criticizes hypocrisy without banning correction:**

The passage does not forbid moral correction; it **resequences** it. The Greek structure is crucial:

1. **"First cast out the beam... and then (*tote*) shalt thou see clearly"** – The word *prōton* (“first”) establishes a prerequisite, not a prohibition. The command to remove the brother’s mote remains in force; it is simply contingent on prior self-examination.

2. **The problem is not the act of correction but the posture of the corrector.** The hypocrite (*hypokritēs*, originally an actor wearing a mask) is condemned not for wanting to help, but for performing righteousness while being unrighteous— for presuming to judge while refusing to be judged. The issue is **credibility and clarity**: one cannot “see clearly” (*diablepō*) to extract a mote while blinded by a beam.

3. **It assumes both parties have real faults.** Jesus does not deny that the brother has a mote; He insists that the corrector’s beam is a more urgent, more disqualifying problem. This prevents the passage from becoming an excuse for moral passivity. Instead, it demands **integrity**—practice what you preach before you preach it.

In short, the image is sharp because it makes hypocrisy look as foolish as it is. And it preserves the possibility of brotherly correction by making it dependent on humility: you may remove the mote, but only after you have stopped being blinded by your own beam.
