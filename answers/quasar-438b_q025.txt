The danger is not visibility itself, but **vanity-motivated visibility**: doing good deeds *in order to be seen*.

“Do not your alms before men” does not mean charity becomes bad merely because others notice it. It means: do not make charity into a public display, as if the point were to appear benevolent, noble, or praiseworthy before others. If the reward sought is recognition—being “seen of them”—then the action’s moral worth is corrupted.

So:

- **Visibility itself?** No. An act may be visible without being morally compromised.
- **Craving to be seen?** Yes. That is the vice: generosity as spectacle, charity as self-glorification.

The verse’s force is: if you seek praise from men, you forfeit the deeper “reward” that belongs to genuine charity. Goodness becomes morally dangerous when it is instrumentalized for reputation.
