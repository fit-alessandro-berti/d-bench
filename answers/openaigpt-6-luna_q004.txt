The trust Proverbs 3:5–6 asks for is **whole-person reliance on God**—not merely agreeing that God exists, but letting confidence in God shape how one chooses and acts. The image of “leaning” suggests treating something as the weight-bearing support for your decisions. The warning is against making your own understanding that support as though it were complete or infallible.

That is **humble self-distrust**, not contempt for thought. It means recognizing that our judgment is limited and can be distorted by fear, desire, pride, or incomplete knowledge. It does **not** mean refusing to reason, learn, seek evidence, or listen to wise counsel. Proverbs itself praises wisdom and understanding. Rather, we use those gifts without treating them as ultimate authorities, remaining willing to have our conclusions corrected by God.

So a person might gather facts, consult knowledgeable people, think carefully, pray, and then act—while acknowledging that their view may be partial and staying open to correction. “Acknowledge him” means recognizing God in every area of life, not just religious decisions.

Nor is the promise that every choice will feel obvious or that life will be easy. It is a call to orient one’s path toward God and trust his guidance, rather than to demand that one’s own understanding be sufficient.
