Based on the text of Matthew 6:19–21, the passage primarily points to **treasure revealing the heart**, though it also implies a formative or training dimension through that revelation. Let’s break this down:

### 1. The text explicitly says: treasure reveals the heart
The final sentence is the clearest statement: *“For where your treasure is, there will your heart be also.”* This is a principle of inner orientation: what we value, pursue, and prioritize inevitably reveals what we truly love. The “heart” here is not merely an emotion but the center of will, desire, and allegiance. So the text teaches that our spiritual center is mapped onto where we invest our lives. If treasure is earthly, the heart is oriented toward earthly things; if treasure is heavenly, the heart is oriented toward heaven.

### 2. The text implies, but does not explicitly command: treasure trains the heart
The verses preceding the conclusion show why this matters: earthly treasures decay, get destroyed, and are vulnerable to theft, while heavenly treasures are secure and eternal. This contrast isn’t just an observation; it’s a call to reorientation. By repeatedly laying up treasures in heaven, a person gradually cultivates a heart that desires what is lasting, trustworthy, and aligned with God. In that sense, part of the teaching is hortatory: we are invited to choose actions (investing in God’s kingdom, loving others, seeking righteousness) that shape our desires. But the text’s defining logic is *reveal*, not merely *train*. It doesn’t say, “Accumulate heavenly treasure and your heart will be shaped”; it says, “Wherever your treasure is, your heart will be there.” The implication is that we learn who we are by what we consistently pursue.

### 3. How possession, attention, and love become tied together
The connection between these three elements is woven through the concept of **allocation of value**:
- **Possession** is not just having things; it is where we choose to anchor our security, identity, and hope. The text redirects us from possessions that are fragile and temporary to those that are permanent.
- **Attention** follows possession naturally. Wherever we place our resources, our minds, time, and loving care tend to follow. We cannot truly hold two incompatible treasures without one eventually crowding out the other.
- **Love** is the deepest layer. The passage suggests that love isn’t just a feeling we feel; it is a direction of faithful commitment. What we possess, we invest our energy in; what we invest our energy in, we attend to; and what we attend to and purpose to protect becomes what we love. Thus, love is revealed through the pattern of our possessions.

In short, the text teaches that **we become what we treasure**. Our hearts are not neutral; they are drawn toward what we pursue, guard, and commit ourselves to. So loving God is not only an inward state but a practical orientation: where we invest, what we protect, and what we believe is worth our deepest loyalty will shape and reveal who we are. Jesus’ invitation is to align our possessions, attention, and loves with the eternal, so that our hearts are firmly anchored in God rather than in what decays.
