The key difference is **one is outward behavior; the other is inward transformation**.

**External compliance** means doing what God commands—or what religion requires—because you feel you must, not because you want to. It can be motivated by fear, habit, social pressure, or even a desire to look righteous. But the heart itself remains unchanged: cold toward God, resistant to His love, and still enslaved to self-centeredness. This is what “stony heart” means—hard, unresponsive, unable to truly love or obey God from the heart.

**Receiving a “new heart”** means God changes the very core of your being: your desires, loves, and motivations. It is not merely deciding to try harder; it is a spiritual resurrection. A person with a new heart doesn’t just perform God’s commands—he or she now wants to walk in them, because God has planted His Spirit within.

That’s why the question says, “I will put my spirit within you, and **cause you to walk** in my statutes.” The new heart and the indwelling Spirit make obedience natural and willing, not forced.

**Why is hardness described as a material condition?**

Because hardness is not just a bad habit or a moral lapse—it is a condition of fallen human nature. The phrase “stony heart out of your flesh” pictures sin as something built into us: a heart of stone is lifeless, calloused, cold, and unyielding. No amount of external instruction, punishment, or discipline can turn a stone heart into a living one. It must be **removed and replaced**.

So God says, “I will take away the stony heart … and I will give you a heart of flesh.” Heart of flesh is not “soft” in the sense of weak; it is alive, sensitive, responsive to God, able to feel His love and bear the imprint of His will. This is the new birth—the only thing that can change us from the inside out.

In short:

- **External compliance = doing right without a renewed heart.**
- **New heart = being made right, so that doing right flows from who you are in Christ.**

The promise in Ezekiel is fulfilled in the New Covenant through Jesus:  
> “I will give you a new heart and put a new spirit in you… I will put my Spirit in you” (Ezek. 36:26–27)  
This is the work of God, not human effort—a spiritual heart transplant that only He can perform.
