Matthew 6:21 is a proverb, and like all proverbs it is both descriptive and prescriptive. It tells you how the heart works and then tells you to work the heart.

The Greek is not "where your treasure is, there your heart *is*". It is hou gar estin ho thesaurus humon, ekei estai kai he kardia humon, "For where your treasure is, there *will be* your heart also." Future indicative. The location of the treasure and the location of the heart are co-located, and the treasure is given the logical priority.

That is why Jesus can give the command in v.19-20: *thesauresate* "lay up treasure for yourselves". Lay up treasure on earth... But lay up treasure in heaven. The imperative is not "look at your treasure and see what your heart is", it is "move your treasure and you will move your heart". The heart is the centre of the will, desire and intention in Hebrew thought, *leb*. It is not a feeling only; it is where a person is at home, where he rests his security.

Jesus is using the ordinary psychology of possession. In biblical anthropology possession, attention and love are not three separate things, they are three moments of one attachment.

* Possession is the outward act of laying up, investing, storing. It is where you put time, money, energy, reputation. Earthly treasure is moth-eatable, rustable and stealable, heavenly treasure is not.
* Attention is the inward gaze that follows possession. What you own you think about, you plan for, you protect, you talk about. You cannot possess something and not attend to it.
* Love is the settled attachment, the weight that holds the heart. Augustine says love is the weight that pulls the heart where it is. *Ubi thesaurus ibi cor* is the statement that love follows the weight.

So the treasure reveals the heart because the heart is already showing itself in what you have chosen to store up. But it also trains the heart because the heart is formed by the habit of storing. You do not first get the heart right and then put the treasure where it belongs. You put the treasure where you want the heart to go, and the heart goes with it.

That is the point of the whole antithesis to mammon in Matthew 6:24. You cannot serve God and mammon. The service is not abstract. Service is laying up, spending, attending, trusting. Where you lay up your security is where you will learn to love, and where you learn to love is where you will lay up.

Jesus is therefore telling the disciples to do the very thing that will make their hearts heavenly: intentionally invest in the imperishable kingdom, in mercy, prayer, forgiveness, the poor, the Word, so that attention will be drawn there and love will be trained there. The heart does not float free of the hand.
