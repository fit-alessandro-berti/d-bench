This passage operates on two levels simultaneously, and answering your question requires distinguishing between what the text **explicitly states** and what it **implicitly teaches** about spiritual formation.

### 1. Does it say treasure *reveals* the heart, or *trains* it?
**Primarily it says treasure reveals the heart, but the command itself implies it trains the heart.**

- **Revelation is explicit.** The conjunction `γὰρ` (*gar*, “for”) in verse 21 introduces an explanatory clause: your ultimate concern is disclosed by what you actually invest in. In biblical anthropology, the “heart” (`καρδία`) is not merely an emotional center but the core of human motivation, allegiance, and identity. Jesus is giving a diagnostic principle: track your long-term investments (time, resources, ambition, anxiety), and you will see where your loyalty truly rests. Earthly treasures inevitably breed fear, insecurity, and self-reliance; heavenly treasures cultivate peace, generosity, and devotion to God. The treasure doesn’t just symbolize the heart’s location; it makes that location visible.

- **Training is implicit in the imperative.** “Lay up” (`θησαυρίζετε`) is a present active imperative: a continuous, deliberate action. Ancient Mediterranean culture understood accumulation as formative. When you repeatedly prioritize certain ends, your desires adapt to match them. Spiritual traditions across ages call this *habitus*: repeated action shapes inclination. So while the text’s explicit claim is correlational/revelatory (“where X is, Y will be”), the pastoral intent is formative. You don’t simply discover your heart’s location; you participate in moving it by what you choose to store up.

In short: **Treasure diagnoses the heart’s current allegiance, but sustained investment reforms it.** Jesus couples warning with invitation: stop hoarding what decays, start investing in what endures, and your affections will follow.

---

### 2. How do possession, attention, and love become tied together?
These three concepts form a psychological-spiritual feedback loop that Matthew 6:19–21 quietly maps out:

| Concept | Role in the Passage | Connection to Heart/Treasure |
|--------|---------------------|------------------------------|
| **Possession** | “Lay up treasure” | Not passive ownership, but active stewardship. What you accumulate signals what you consider eternally valuable. Earthly riches demand defensive guarding; heavenly riches multiply when given away. |
| **Attention** | Implied by the verbs “break through,” “steal,” “corrupt,” and the contrast between earth/heaven | Where you put your treasure dictates where your mental and emotional energy flows. Earthly wealth breeds vigilance against loss; heavenly wealth draws focus toward God’s kingdom and neighbor’s need. Attention is the bridge between resources and affection. |
| **Love** | “There will your heart be” | In Scripture, love (`ἀγάπη`/desire/affection) is the gravity that orbits around what we value most. We love what we consistently attend to, and we attend to what we possess/invest in. Treasures function as silent worshipped objects: they train us to love what lasts or what fades. |

**How they interlock:**
1. **Possession → Attention:** What you hold onto consumes your cognitive and emotional bandwidth. Thieves, moths, and rust force the earthly investor into perpetual anxiety; eternal treasures release that tension and redirect focus upward and outward.
2. **Attention → Love:** Sustained focus cultivates affection. Augustine would later phrase this as *ordo amoris* (the order of love): we come to love what we regularly contemplate and prioritize.
3. **Love → Possession:** Your cultivated desires then drive future choices, creating a self-reinforcing cycle. This is why Jesus adds in verse 24: “No one can serve two masters… You cannot serve God and wealth.” Two sets of possessions demand two different attentions, which produce two incompatible loves, leaving no room for unified allegiance.

Verse 21 is the hinge: **“Where your treasure is, there your heart will be also.”** 
- *Treasure* = concentrated possession (what you’ve invested in long-term)
- *Heart* = integrated attention and love (where your loyalty, fear, hope, and devotion reside)

---

### Pastoral & Theological Synthesis
Jesus is not making a simplistic moral rule about wealth, but revealing a law of spiritual gravity: **your affections are engineered by your investments.** The passage functions both as a mirror (showing you where your heart already is) and as a chisel (shaping where it should go). 

If you treat this purely diagnostically, you risk quietism: “My treasure just shows where my heart is, nothing more.” But if you treat it purely practically, you risk legalism: “I must stuff up heaven’s bank account.” The biblical balance is covenantal: treasure reveals because it trains; we are called to align our possessions with God’s kingdom, trusting that faithful stewardship will gradually reorient our attention, purify our loves, and locate our hearts where they belong.
