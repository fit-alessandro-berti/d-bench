Amos rejects the worship so sharply because the problem is not a simple imbalance—too much liturgy, too little ethics. The problem is covenantal contradiction. The people are not merely neglecting justice while sincerely worshiping God; they are using worship to cover, legitimize, and perpetuate injustice. Their feasts, offerings, and songs have become a religious façade. In the prophetic view, worship severed from justice is not partial obedience; it is false worship, an insult to God. So God speaks with visceral rejection: “I hate, I despise… I will not smell… I will not hear.”

The fraud being exposed is something like this:

- **Transactional religion:** sacrifices and songs are treated as a way to buy God’s favor, as if ritual could compensate for oppression.
- **Covenant hypocrisy:** the people claim to be God’s own while trampling the poor, taking bribes, and perverting justice in the gate (Amos 5:11–12).
- **Liturgical cover-up:** noisy piety drowns out the cries of the oppressed and gives public respectability to greed.
- **Idolatrous self-deception:** they worship a domesticated god who blesses their status quo, not Yahweh who demands justice.

That is why the passage does not say, “Balance your worship with a little more justice.” It says, “Let justice roll down like waters, and righteousness like a mighty stream.” Justice and righteousness are not optional additives to worship; they are covenant loyalty embodied in social life. Worship without them is not acceptable worship—it is fraud. God is not asking for better balance; God is rejecting the whole pretense.
