In Matthew 12 this is not a proverb about manners, it's an indictment. And Jesus uses two different images of the interior to make the same point:

> **v.34b:** *ek tou perisseumatos tes kardias to stoma lalei* — out of the **overflow** of the heart the mouth speaks.
> **v.35:** *ek tou agathou thesaurou... ekballei* — a good person out of his good **treasury/storehouse** brings forth good things.

Luke has the parallel in Luke 6:45 without the confrontation. Matthew keeps the confrontation, which matters for the meaning.

### 1. The context: why "vipers"?

In Matthew 12:22-32, Jesus has healed a demon-oppressed man, and the Pharisees say he does it by Beelzebul, the prince of demons. Jesus answers: a kingdom divided against itself cannot stand; your accusation condemns you.

Then 12:33-37:

33 Make the tree good and its fruit good...
34 O brood of vipers [ *gennemata echidnon* ], how can you, being evil, speak good things?
36-37 Every careless [*argon*, idle, unproductive] word men shall speak, they shall give account in the day of judgment. By your words you will be justified and by your words you will be condemned.

So "heart" here is not modern sentimentalism. In Hebrew *lev* / Greek *kardia* it's the integrating center — mind, will, affections, loyalties together. And "good" speech doesn't mean polite grammar. It means speech that aligns with God's reality and bears good fruit — truthful, life-giving, God-honoring.

His question in 34a is rhetorical: An evil orientation cannot sustainably produce truly good speech, any more than a diseased tree can keep producing good fruit. You might fake it briefly, but pressure will tell.

Two metaphors for the hidden:

**1. Abundance / Overflow (*perisseuma*)** — The heart as a spring or vessel filled past the brim. Speech is what spills over when bumped. This stresses inevitability and spontaneity.

**2. Treasure / Treasury (*thesauros*)** — The heart as a vault, chest, storehouse where you accumulate over time. You deposit what you love, rehearse, justify, cherish, resent. Speech is what you then spend. This stresses history and accumulation.

That's why translators speak of "stockpiles." Jesus is saying we are all curators of an inner collection, and our talk is the currency.

### 2. Symptom, Choice, or Revelation? All three, in sequence.

The passage deliberately holds them together:

**1. As symptom — diagnostic:**
"Out of the abundance..." Your words are not random. Like a cough reveals lungs, patterns of cynicism, contempt, flattery, lying, blessing, encouragement reveal the system's condition. Proverbs 4:23 behind this: "Keep your heart with all vigilance, for from it flow the springs of life."

In this sense you don't *decide* to reveal yourself — you leak.

Modern psychology would agree in its own idiom: unguarded speech, especially under stress, anger, or anonymity, predicts values better than prepared statements. Jesus would say that's because the reservoir has to go somewhere.

**2. As choice — culpable action:**
v.35 switches to an active verb: *ekballei* — he *brings forth, casts out, sends out* (it's the same verb Matthew uses for exorcisms). The image shifts from passive overflow to deliberate withdrawal from storage.

You chose what to store, and you choose to bring it out. An "evil man" is not a victim of a bad tongue; he is a person who has cultivated an evil treasury and now spends from it willingly.

That's why vv.36-37 can talk about judgment for "every careless word." If speech were *only* symptom, you couldn't be blamed for it. Matthew insists: what overflows was also once deposited choice by choice.

**3. As revelation — apocalyptic disclosure:**
In Matthew, revelation always has two horizons: now and on the Last Day.

Now: Your words reveal *to others* who you really are. The Pharisees' accusation "by Beelzebul" isn't a debating point; it's a revelation in public of a heart so committed to its position that it calls good evil.

Later: Your words will be *exhibited as evidence*. The courtroom language in 36-37 is intentional. God doesn't need words to know the heart; but in judgment our words justify themselves — they will be read back as the transcript of the heart's true allegiance.

So: **symptom** describes the *mechanism* (overflow), **choice** describes the *responsibility* (treasuring and bringing forth), **revelation** describes the *function* (showing forth to neighbor and to Judge).

### 3. The implication Matthew intends

You can't fix speech at the level of speech. Behavior modification — just trying to be more positive or polite — is like catching overflow in a pan without turning off the tap.

Because the treasury is built over time, transformation, in Matthew's theology, has to happen at the level of the treasure itself. What you behold, love, forgive, resent, meditate on — that is what you store. Then speech changes because stock has changed.

And conversely, you can't claim "I didn't mean what I said, it's just words." Jesus is saying there are no such things as "just words." They are always telling the truth about the treasure-house, even when you're trying to lie with them.
