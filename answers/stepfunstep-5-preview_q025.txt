The danger is **not visibility itself**, but **doing good in order to be seen**.

Jesus says: “Take heed that ye do not your alms **before men, to be seen of them**.” The key phrase is *“to be seen of them”* — a purpose, not merely a circumstance. The moral problem is not that others notice the good act; it is that the actor wants to be noticed as the center of the goodness.

There is a crucial difference between:

- doing good **where others may see**, and  
- doing good **so that others will praise you**.

In the second case, public goodness becomes moral theater. The act may look generous or righteous, but its inner aim is self-display. It seeks human approval instead of God’s approval. That is why Jesus says such a person “has no reward” from the Father: they already got what they wanted — being admired by men.

This also fits the surrounding context: Jesus condemns the hypocrites who pray, fast, and give alms publicly so people will applaud them. Visibility becomes dangerous because it tempts us to perform virtue rather than embody love.

The verse is not commanding secret holiness as another legalism. Just a few chapters earlier Jesus says, “Let your light shine before men, that they may see your good works and glorify your Father.” Public goodness is fine when the glory goes to God and the benefit goes to the neighbor. It becomes morally dangerous when the glory is consumed by self.

So the craving to be seen is the problem; visibility is only the occasion where that craving gets exposed.
