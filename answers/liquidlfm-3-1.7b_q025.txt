**Short answer:**  
The “danger” isn’t the mere fact that you make your alms public; it’s the **human craving to be noticed and praised**. When you give before anyone else knows, you invite the eyes of others to see your generosity *before* you’ve revealed it to God. Those eyes can turn your kindness into a tool—either to lure you into further giving, to bribe you, or to claim credit for your work. The real moral hazard is therefore the **desire for recognition**, not the visibility itself.

---

## 1. What the verse actually warns against  

> **Matthew 6:1** – “Take heed that ye do not your alms before men, to be seen of them: otherwise ye have no reward of your Father which is in heaven.”

| Phrase | Meaning |
|--------|---------|
| **“your alms”** | Money or goods given as charity. |
| **“before men”** | Give it to ordinary people *without telling them* that you’re doing so. |
| **“to be seen of them”** | Let the recipients notice you handing over the items. |
| **“otherwise ye have no reward … in heaven”** | If you keep it secret, God will still bless you; if you make it public, He may withhold His favor. |

The warning is directed at **giving generously in front of the crowd**—the very moment when others can observe your act and possibly exploit it.

---

## 2. Why public giving is risky  

### A. Potential for exploitation
- **Bribery / manipulation**: Someone who sees you hand out food or clothing might think you’re offering a “gift” to win their favor, or they might use your resources to entrap you.
- **Social pressure**: Others may encourage you to give more, turning your modest act into a performance.
- **Loss of trust**: By revealing your charity, you signal that you rely on others’ goodwill, undermining the humility God expects.

### B. Pride and vanity
- Giving before you know you’re giving can create a sense of **self‑promotion** (“Look how much I care!”). This mirrors the temptation to boast, which the Gospel repeatedly condemns (e.g., James 2:17–18).

### C. God’s discernment
- Scripture teaches that God judges our hearts, not our hands. If you give **secretly**, He can see whether your motive is genuine love or a desire for praise. If you give **publicly**, He may not be able to distinguish the two, and the reward can be withheld.

---

## 3. Visibility versus the craving to be seen

| Aspect | Role in the danger |
|--------|---------------------|
| **Visibility itself** | Necessary for the threat to exist. Once the act is observed, others can react. But on its own, a single glance doesn’t guarantee harm. |
| **Craving to be seen / to be recognized** | The *root* cause. People give alms publicly partly because they want to **show off**, earn God’s favor, or fit in socially. This internal drive pushes them to act before they’ve told God. |

**Conclusion:** The problem is **not merely the act of making something public**; it is the **internal impulse to be noticed and celebrated**. Even if you intended to give quietly, the urge to “let them know” can lead you to give before they do—so the craving fuels the exposure. Therefore, the moral danger arises from the **combination of the desire for acknowledgment and the subsequent public disclosure**.

---

## 4. How to avoid the trap  

1. **Give privately.** Hand out the alms after you’ve spoken with the recipient, or give them in a setting where they’ll know you’re sharing freely.  
2. **Keep the purpose hidden from them.** You can still give generously, but you don’t announce it or let them see the transaction.  
3. **Align your motive with God’s heart.** Ask yourself: *Am I doing this out of love for the poor, or out of a wish to be admired?* Choose the former.  
4. **Trust God’s timing.** When you give secretly, He can judge your intent and grant the heavenly reward.

---

### Bottom line
Public charity isn’t inherently evil; it becomes morally hazardous **when it springs from a human longing for visibility and praise**. The verse cautions us to guard against that longing by giving **after the fact**, so that only God sees our true character and our reward follows accordingly.
