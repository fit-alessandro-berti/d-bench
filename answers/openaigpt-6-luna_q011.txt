The movement is from **turning away** to **turning toward**. “Cease to do evil” names the necessary break with wrongdoing; “learn to do well” shows that repentance cannot end with stopping. A life must be redirected.

The word **“learn”** suggests that doing good is practiced and formed over time—not merely a feeling or a passive refusal to harm. Isaiah makes that practice concrete: pursue justice, help the oppressed, and defend those without power, especially the orphan and widow. In the chapter’s context, this is a rebuke to people who maintain religious observances while tolerating injustice.

So goodness here is more than restraint. Restraint matters, but it is incomplete unless it becomes active, sustained love expressed as justice for one’s neighbors. The paired commands describe a change of life: put injustice away, and learn to do what restores and protects others.
