The saying is causal and directional, not merely diagnostic. Jesus does not write “where your heart is, there your treasure will be.” He writes the reverse: the location of the treasure determines the location of the heart (“there will your heart be also”). The imperative that precedes the saying therefore functions as a training regimen: by deliberately placing resources, time, and energy in one realm rather than the other, a person relocates the heart itself.

Possession, attention, and love are thereby fused. What one stores up must be watched (earthly goods invite moth, rust, and thieves; heavenly goods do not). The act of watching becomes habitual attention; habitual attention becomes attachment. The heart simply follows the investment. The three realities are not sequential add-ons but a single movement: treasure is the concrete form that love takes, and attention is the medium through which that love is trained.
