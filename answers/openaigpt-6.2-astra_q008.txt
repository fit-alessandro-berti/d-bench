John is not saying that loving a neighbor proves someone loves God. He is saying that **hatred of a brother or sister contradicts a claim to love God**. The reason lies in the surrounding passage: “We love him, because he first loved us” (4:19). If a person’s love for God is a response to being loved by him, it cannot be indifferent to God’s command to love the people before them (4:21).

The contrast between *seen* and *unseen* gives the test its force. A person can speak sincerely about devotion to the unseen God while avoiding the cost of loving someone they can see. Earlier, John gives a concrete example: someone sees a brother in need, has the means to help, and closes his heart (3:17). The visible need gives love something to do; the response reveals what a profession of love might conceal.

The self-deception, then, is not simply feeling anger or finding someone difficult to love. It is **mistaking the intensity of one’s religious feelings for the reality of love**, as though cherished hatred had no bearing on one’s relationship with God. John refuses that separation.
