“Way” names the course a person takes; “thoughts” names the plans and purposes by which a person chooses that course. Isaiah sets them in parallel because repentance is a turning of the whole person. Changing outward conduct while still cherishing the old purpose leaves that purpose untouched; feeling remorse while continuing harm leaves the old way intact.

Inward imagination matters because it is where we can rehearse a wrong, justify it, or begin to envision a different course. The call is not to prevent every unwanted thought from arising, but to relinquish the designs we have made our own.

The decisive word is **“return.”** Isaiah does not say to perfect oneself and *then* approach God. He calls people to turn from the wrong and toward the Lord, who “will have mercy.” That mercy neither excuses harmful conduct nor stops at what others can see: even the purposes known only to the person and to God are within the reach of abundant pardon.
