The text most directly says that **treasure and heart belong together**: “Where your treasure is, there will your heart be also.” It does not explicitly explain whether treasure *reveals* the heart or *trains* it. But both ideas fit, with an important distinction.

- **Treasure reveals the heart.** What we accumulate, protect, and treat as indispensable can disclose what we value and trust.
- **Treasure can also train the heart.** Jesus commands his hearers to lay up treasure in heaven, then says their hearts will be there too. The implication is that where they deliberately invest themselves matters for where their affections go. The verse suggests that direction, though it does not spell out a psychological mechanism.

Possession, attention, and love become tied together because what we regard as treasure tends to claim our care. We spend time acquiring it, protecting it, and thinking about it; in turn, that attention can deepen our attachment. The movement can run both ways: **we invest in what we love, and we may grow to love what we invest in.**

The contrast is not simply between having material things and having none. It is between storing up what is vulnerable and temporary and investing in what has enduring value before God. In the surrounding chapter, Jesus connects that heavenly orientation with practices such as generosity and with seeking God’s kingdom (Matthew 6:1–4, 33).
