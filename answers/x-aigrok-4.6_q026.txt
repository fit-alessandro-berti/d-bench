The passage treats the heart as a storehouse whose contents (good or evil “treasure”) determine what the mouth can produce. Speech is therefore the overflow of whatever has already been accumulated inside; it cannot be better than its source. That makes words:

- **revelations** of the hidden stockpile (they make the unseen moral inventory public);
- **symptoms** of the heart’s condition (just as fruit diagnoses the tree);
- **choices** in the limited sense that a person “bringeth forth” from what is already there—yet the quality of the choice is constrained by the store itself (“how can ye, being evil, speak good things?”).

Thus the text presents words as all three at once: an act of will that simultaneously discloses and diagnoses the inner moral treasury.
