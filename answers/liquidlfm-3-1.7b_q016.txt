**Answer: Wounded fairness**

In Luke 15:28‑32 the elder brother first **angers** himself and refuses to enter his father’s house, but the real “deepest” trouble he feels is that **his honest, year‑long obedience has not been met with the promised reward**.  

He tells his father:

> “These many years do I serve thee, neither transgressed I at any time thy commandment… yet thou never gavest me a kid, that I might make merry with my friends.”

This shows he **knows he has obeyed** (no commandments broken) yet **still receives nothing**—no child, no gift, no chance to enjoy with friends. The contrast between his faithful service and the father’s silence creates a profound sense of **injustice and betrayal of trust**, i.e., *wounded fairness*.  

Later the father replies, “Thou art ever with me, and all that I have is thine,” showing mercy and forgiveness—but the elder brother’s initial hurt stems from feeling that his fairness has been denied despite his diligent obedience. While anger, envy, or pride may flare, they are symptoms of this wounded sense of fairness.
