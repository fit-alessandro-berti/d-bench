All three, but in an integrated way.

In Matthew 12:34–35, Jesus treats the heart as a hidden **treasury** or **storehouse**. Speech is the overflow of what is stored there: “out of the abundance of the heart the mouth speaketh.” So the mouth is not the origin of the problem; it is the outlet.

### 1. Words are **symptoms**

Jesus says, “How can ye, being evil, speak good things?” That implies that bad speech is a symptom of a bad heart. Just as a diseased tree produces diseased fruit, an evil heart produces evil words. The Pharisees’ words against Jesus were not merely a slip of the tongue; they revealed a deeper spiritual sickness.

So yes, words are symptoms. They indicate what is already inside.

### 2. Words are **choices**

The passage also says a good man “bringeth forth” good things from his treasure, and an evil man “bringeth forth” evil things. That phrase implies active production, not just passive leakage. We choose what we say, but our choices are shaped by the moral treasury within us.

That is why Jesus immediately goes on to say that people will give account for every idle word (Matthew 12:36–37). If words were only involuntary symptoms, judgment would seem unfair. But because they are also acts of the will, they carry moral responsibility.

### 3. Words are **revelations**

The heart is hidden. Other people cannot see it directly. But speech makes it visible. The “hidden stockpiles” of the heart are revealed when the mouth speaks. Especially under pressure, in conflict, or in moments of unguarded speech, what is stored inside comes out.

Jesus is not saying that an evil person can never say something polite or true. He is speaking of the overall pattern and spiritual fruit of a person’s life. The real direction of the heart eventually reveals itself in words.

### Conclusion

Matthew 12:34–35 presents words as **symptoms, choices, and revelations all at once**. They reveal the hidden moral condition of the heart, they flow from the heart’s treasure, and they are still chosen acts for which we are accountable. Therefore, the cure for bad speech is not merely learning better words; it is having the heart itself changed.
