**Answer**

The Pharisee is condemned for **pride / self‑righteousness** – specifically, for **using virtue as a self‑display** (and for the related “false doctrine” of claiming to be righteous simply by denying the sins of others).  

> *“God, I thank thee, that I am not as other men are, extortioners, unjust, adulterers, or even as this publican.”*  
> *“I fast twice in the week, I give tithes of all that I possess.”*

Here’s what happens in the text:

| Element | What the Pharisee does | Why it’s wrong |
|---------|-----------------------|----------------|
| **Comparison** | He says he is **“not as other men are”** – a direct comparison that elevates himself above sinners. | This is a **comparative self‑aggrandizement**; he treats his own character as superior to everyone else’s. |
| **False doctrine** | He teaches that his righteousness comes from **refusing to acknowledge his own guilt** and from pointing out the vices of others. | He promotes a **false teaching**: that one can be righteous merely by contrasting oneself with the wicked, ignoring the need for genuine repentance and divine grace. |
| **Virtue as self‑display** | He lists his fasting and tithes as proof of his holiness, but he does so **for show** rather than out of heartfelt devotion. | He turns his good works into a **public performance** to impress others, not to transform his soul. |

In short, the Pharisee’s pride (or arrogance) leads him to **misapply virtue**—he uses his piety as a trophy to claim superiority, while ignoring the reality that he is a sinner who needs forgiveness.

---

### Why the Tax Collector’s Posture Is Treated as Spiritually Truer

The publican (the tax collector) stands **far away**, does **not lift his eyes to heaven**, and instead **kneels down to his house**, saying:

> “God be merciful to me a sinner.”

His posture reveals three spiritual qualities that the Pharisee lacks:

1. **Humility** – He does not boast about his own righteousness; he is physically low and inward‑looking.
2. **Acknowledgment of sin** – He openly calls himself a “sinner,” recognizing his role as a tax collector and his need for forgiveness.
3. **Dependence on God’s mercy** – Rather than relying on his own good deeds or clever comparisons, he **asks God for mercy**.

Because he embodies **genuine repentance, humility, and trust in divine grace**, his attitude is described as “justified” and therefore **spiritually truer** than the Pharisee’s self‑righteous, comparative, and self‑disparaging stance.

---

#### Bottom line
- **Pharisee:** Condemned for **pride/self‑righteousness**—specifically, for **using virtue as self‑display** and for propagating a **false doctrine** that equates one’s own purity with mere contrast to others’ sins.  
- **Tax collector:** Treated as spiritually upright because he **shows humility, admits his sinfulness, and seeks God’s mercy**—a posture that reflects true repentance and reliance on divine grace.
