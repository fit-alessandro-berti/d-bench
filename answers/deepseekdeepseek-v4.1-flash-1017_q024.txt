In Ezekiel 36:26–27, God is not promising to make people merely behave better outwardly. He is promising to deal with the source of obedience—the heart.

**External compliance** is when a person’s outward actions conform to a rule while the inner self remains resistant, divided, fearful, self-serving, or unchanged. It can be motivated by fear of punishment, desire for reward, social pressure, reputation, habit, or self-interest. It may look like obedience, but the heart is still far from God (cf. Isa. 29:13; Matt. 15:8). External compliance can be real as far as it goes, but it does not reconcile or renew the person.

**A “new heart”** is God’s own transforming work in the center of the person—biblical “heart” means the core of will, thought, affection, conscience, and intention, not merely emotions. God removes the unresponsive “stone” and gives a living, tender, responsive “heart of flesh.” He also puts His Spirit within His people and “causes” them to walk in His statutes. In other words, obedience is no longer only commanded from outside; it is enabled and made fruitful from within by the Spirit. This does not erase human responsibility—the same verse says “you shall keep my judgments and do them”—but it restores the person’s agency and desire so that obedience flows from a renewed heart.

**Why is hardness described almost like a material condition?** Because Scripture treats sin not merely as isolated bad acts but as a condition affecting the whole person. “Stone” communicates hardness, deadness, rigidity, insensitivity, and unresponsiveness toward God. A stone heart is not simply a heart that has made a few wrong choices; it is a heart fixed in resistance. That is why a mere “softening” or external polish is not enough. The problem goes to the root, and the root must be replaced. You can polish stone, but you cannot make it alive. The metaphor is not saying the heart is literally made of rock; it is using material imagery to convey a real spiritual condition: the old heart is unresponsive to God and cannot renew itself.

There may also be a deliberate contrast: the law was given on stone tablets, but under the new covenant God writes His law on the heart (Jer. 31:33; 2 Cor. 3:3). External compliance leaves the law outside; the new heart receives it inwardly.

So the passage distinguishes between **behavior controlled from the outside** and **obedience produced by a renewed heart and the indwelling Spirit**. The new heart does not lead to lawlessness—it leads to actually keeping God’s judgments—but it does so as the fruit of divine grace, not as a veneer over an unchanged heart.
