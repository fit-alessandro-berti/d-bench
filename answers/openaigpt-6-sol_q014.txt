**Both—but the emphasis is on what treasure does to the heart.** Jesus commands his hearers to *lay up* treasure in heaven, then gives the reason: “where your treasure is, there will your heart be also.” What we choose to value and invest in helps determine where our heart settles. At the same time, what we already treat as treasure reveals what we love.

Possession, attention, and love can reinforce one another. We attend to what we count as valuable; we worry about losing it, plan around it, and give it our resources. That repeated attention can deepen our attachment. Conversely, choosing to use possessions for God’s purposes can redirect our attention and affection toward him.

So the passage is not simply a test—“look at your possessions to discover your heart”—nor a mechanical promise that giving something away instantly changes your feelings. It is a call to choose a lasting treasure, because **the heart follows what a person treasures**.
