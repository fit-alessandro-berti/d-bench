In this passage Jesus uses two complementary metaphors—**overflow** and **treasury**—to make speech the visible evidence of an invisible moral economy.

**The connection: heart as hidden stockpile**
- *“Out of the abundance of the heart the mouth speaks”* (v. 34): The heart is a reservoir; once it is filled with a particular moral quality, that quality *spills over*. You cannot long contain what is abundant inside.
- *“Good treasure … evil treasure”* (v. 35): The heart is also a *thesauros*, a storehouse or vault where moral “wealth” is accumulated over time. Speech is not random; it is the deliberate drawing forth (*propherei*, “brings forth”) from that hidden inventory.

So words are not isolated behaviors. They are the **production line** of an internal morality that has already been stockpiled.

**Symptoms, revelations, or choices? All three—integrated, not mutually exclusive.**

1. **Revelation / Disclosure**  
   The primary force of the metaphor is *disclosure*. The mouth makes public what the treasury keeps hidden. Jesus immediately applies this to the Pharisees: they call him Satan-inspired, and by that speech they *reveal* that their hearts are stocked with evil, not righteousness.

2. **Symptom / Diagnosis**  
   Speech is diagnostic. The rhetorical question—“How can ye, being evil, speak good things?”—assumes the word is a reliable indicator of the underlying disease. You do not find good fruit on a corrupt tree; you do not find holy words in a heart full of “vipers’” venom.

3. **Choice / Accountability**  
   The text is not pure determinism. The verb “bringeth forth” is active: the person *draws* from the treasure. And Jesus’ wider context (v. 36–37) says people will be *judged* by their words. That means speech is a responsible moral act. The “treasure” is built by repeated choices (what you listen to, cherish, and practice), and what you speak is both an outflow *and* a further ingestion—each word reinforcing the stockpile.

**How they work together**
- **Hidden stockpile** = what the heart treasures (values, habits, allegiances).
- **Speech** = the symptom that reveals it, the revelation that exposes it, and the choice that both draws from it and adds to it.

In short, Matthew 12:34–35 teaches that **words are the audible symptoms of an inner moral inventory**, but because that inventory is built by choices and spoken by responsible agents, they are also **revelations for judgment**. The “generation of vipers” cannot simply “choose” to sound good for a moment, because their treasury is corrupt; yet they remain accountable precisely because they are actively drawing evil from it.
