James exposes a contradiction between **honoring God and treating a person made in God’s image as an object of contempt**. The person we curse bears God’s likeness even when we have good reason to object to what they have done. Cursing them does not merely spoil a moment of worship; it calls into question what we mean when we say we honor God.

Speech reveals that disorder quickly because a word often escapes before we have examined the impulse behind it. In worship, praise may come readily; in an argument, a cutting remark may come just as readily. James’s point is not necessarily that the praise was insincere. It is that our reverence for God has not yet shaped how we speak to someone who angers us.
