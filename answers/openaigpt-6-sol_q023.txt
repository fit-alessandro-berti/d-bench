“Way” and “thoughts” name two inseparable sides of a person’s life. A *way* is the course one takes—what one does. *Thoughts* are the plans, judgments, and desires that help direct that course. The parallel lines are not describing two kinds of sinners; they call the same person to turn both outwardly and inwardly.

So repentance is more than changing visible behavior, but it is not merely feeling differently either. It means leaving a harmful path and relinquishing the thinking that sustains it. The invitation is hopeful rather than a demand to make oneself flawless first: the wicked person is told to **return to the Lord**, who “will have mercy” and “will abundantly pardon.” Mercy is possible even for someone whose conduct *and* intentions need to change.
