This passage uses a **source-and-outflow** metaphor: the heart is a hidden treasury, and speech is what is drawn out and made audible.

- **Heart** in biblical usage is the inner center of thought, desire, will, intention, and moral character—not merely emotions.
- **“Abundance”** suggests overflow: what fills the heart eventually comes out.
- **“Treasure”** suggests a stored stockpile: habits, loves, thoughts, and dispositions that have been accumulated.
- **The mouth** is the outlet. Speech is connected to the heart as fruit is connected to a tree (cf. Matt. 7:16–20; Luke 6:43–45).

So does this make words **symptoms, choices, revelations, or all three**? The best answer is: **all three, with distinctions.**

1. **Words are revelatory signs / “symptoms”**  
   In a diagnostic sense, speech reveals the hidden condition of the heart. Jesus says the mouth speaks “out of the abundance of the heart.” Words make visible—or audible—what was otherwise hidden. But “symptom” must not be taken in a merely medical, deterministic sense. Speech is not an involuntary reflex like a fever; it is the person expressing what is stored within.

2. **Words are morally responsible choices**  
   The immediate context makes this clear: “Every idle word that men shall speak, they shall give account thereof in the day of judgment” and “by thy words thou shalt be justified, and by thy words thou shalt be condemned” (Matt. 12:36–37). Jesus treats speech as something people are accountable for. Words are not morally neutral noise.

3. **Words are revelations of the heart**  
   Speech discloses the treasure from which it comes. It externalizes the inner life. It does not reveal every detail exhaustively, but habitual speech reveals the dominant orientation of the heart. An evil heart produces evil speech; a good heart produces good speech.

So the passage suggests that **words are morally accountable choices that reveal and express the heart’s stored character**. They are “symptoms” only in the sense of evidence or fruit—not in the sense of excusing the speaker. The point is not “my words are just symptoms, so I am not responsible,” but rather: **your words matter because they flow from and disclose what you treasure.**

One important nuance: this is a wisdom-like principle about the **habitual direction** of a person’s life, not a simplistic one-to-one rule for every isolated word. A good person can speak carelessly, and an evil person can say something true or kind. But the sustained pattern of speech exposes the treasury of the heart. That is why Jesus ties the passage so closely to judgment: words are both **revelation** and **responsible action**.
