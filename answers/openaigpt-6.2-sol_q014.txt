It says both, but the emphasis falls on **treasure training the heart**. What you treasure already reveals what you value. Yet Jesus doesn’t say, “Where your heart is, there your treasure will be.” He says, “Where your treasure is, there will your heart be also.” And he says it to explain a command: *lay up* treasure in heaven. Where you put your treasure matters because your heart follows it.

Possession, attention, and love are tied together by what you have a stake in. If you store up wealth on earth, you have reason to watch over it, worry about losing it, and plan around keeping it. Moth, rust, and thieves make that vulnerability concrete. The point isn’t that owning something automatically means you love it. It’s that what you count on and invest in has a claim on your attention, and what repeatedly claims your attention can come to command your love.

That’s why Jesus gives his hearers something to do, not just something to feel. In the same chapter, giving to the needy without seeking applause is a concrete example: you part with something valuable in trust that the Father sees what others don’t. Such a gift can express a love already present, but it can also deepen that love. Treasure is a sign of where the heart is—and a way of directing where it goes.
