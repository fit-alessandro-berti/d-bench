The verse treats love of the visible neighbor as a test of love of the invisible God because love for God is not merely an inward, private feeling or a religious idea. In 1 John, God is invisible: “No one has seen God at any time” (1 John 4:12). But God has commanded that love for him be expressed concretely in love for others, especially the brother or sister in the faith. The visible neighbor is the tangible object in front of us; if real love does not show itself there, where it has a concrete object, it cannot be genuine toward the unseen God.

The self-deception John exposes is **compartmentalized spirituality**—the idea that a person can love God while hating or disregarding others. Someone may claim to love God in worship, emotion, doctrine, or private devotion, yet harbor contempt, resentment, or indifference toward a visible brother. John says such a person is a “liar” because the claim contradicts the life. The unseen God can be “loved” in an abstract, self-controlled way; the seen neighbor makes real demands and reveals whether love is actually present.

So the visible neighbor is the test because love for the invisible God must become embodied. If it fails in the visible, tangible relationship, it is not truly there at all. As 1 John 4:21 says: “Whoever loves God must also love his brother.”
