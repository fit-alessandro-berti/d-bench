## Reveals or trains?

The grammar leans toward **training**, though the two readings aren't opposed.

**1. The verse gives a reason, not a diagnosis.** Verse 21 begins with "For," so it grounds the command in vv. 19-20. If treasure only revealed the heart, the logic would be "look at your treasure and see what you love." But the sentence is offered as the *motive* for choosing your treasure carefully: since the heart will end up where the treasure is, place the treasure well. That makes treasure the steering mechanism and the heart the follower.

**2. The direction of movement.** The treasure is the fixed point ("where your treasure is"), and the heart is located relative to it ("there will your heart be also"). The future tense, *will be*, suggests the heart is drawn or relocated, not just exposed. The word "also" suggests the heart is a second thing that follows the first.

**3. The revelatory reading isn't excluded.** If your heart is already with your treasure, you can read your treasure as evidence. Jesus' point is that this is a loop. What you invest in draws the heart, and where the heart goes, you invest more.

## How possession, attention, and love get tied together

The passage doesn't state the mechanism, but it implies one.

- **Possession creates a stake.** To "lay up" (*thēsaurizō*) is to deposit something into a storehouse. Once you have put something in, you are exposed to its fate.

- **A stake creates attention.** The threats named are moth, rust, and thieves: decay and theft. Anything stored on earth needs monitoring, and the list works as a portrait of anxiety. Earthly treasure must be guarded, so the mind keeps returning to it. Attention is being *conscripted*.

- **Sustained attention becomes attachment.** In biblical usage the "heart" (*kardia*) is not just emotion but the center of will, thought, and desire. When attention keeps returning to something, loyalty forms around it. What you watch over, you come to value, and what you value, you love.

- **Heavenly treasure reverses the pattern.** It is secure, so it doesn't demand defensive vigilance. The heart is freed from guarding and can be directed toward something worth loving, which fits the following verses about the single eye (6:22-23), the two masters (6:24), and anxiety (6:25-34).

## Context: how treasure in heaven is stored

The preceding verses (6:1-18) concern almsgiving, prayer, and fasting. Treasure in heaven is therefore built through practices, especially generosity. Giving is a way of deliberately relocating your stake, and Jesus implies that the heart will follow the hand.

## Summary

The text says the heart is *not autonomous*. It doesn't float free and then choose an object; it gets shaped by where you invest. Love is treated less as a spontaneous feeling than as something that grows along the lines of what you hold, tend, and guard. So the verse works as both mirror and lever, but its emphasis is on the lever.
