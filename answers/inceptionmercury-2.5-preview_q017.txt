

In this passage, "light" serves as a metaphor for moral clarity, divine presence, and unvarnished truth. The hatred of light arises not from the illumination itself, but from what it reveals. When reality is brought into focus, actions that rely on obscurity become visible.

The core tension lies between **truth** and **exposure**. For those whose actions contradict ethical standards, exposure acts as a threat. The desire not to be seen clearly is rooted in the fear of accountability. If deeds are hidden, they remain unchallenged; once exposed, they invite reproach or consequence. This dynamic creates a psychological defense mechanism where individuals prefer the safety of darkness—the state of ignorance or concealment—over the vulnerability of transparency.

**Shame** functions as the emotional catalyst in this relationship. It is the painful recognition that one’s actions do not align with a higher standard. When light forces a confrontation with reality, the gap between self-perception and actual behavior widens, generating shame. To avoid this discomfort, the individual rejects the source of illumination.

Conversely, those who align with truth welcome exposure. For them, light does not threaten but validates. Coming to the light allows their actions to be recognized as sincere and grounded in integrity. Here, visibility is not a weapon of judgment but a confirmation of authenticity. Thus, the preference for light or darkness ultimately reflects a choice between facing reality with integrity or seeking comfort in concealment.
