

This passage frames speech as the direct output of internal moral reserves. It likens the heart to a vault containing either good or evil "treasure," suggesting that language cannot exceed the quality of its source.

In terms of how words function:

*   **Symptoms:** They indicate the health of the inner life. Corrupt output points to a corrupt source.
*   **Revelations:** They uncover what is concealed. The mouth discloses the true nature of the stored abundance.
*   **Choices:** While utterances may feel automatic, the "treasure" represents accumulated habits and decisions. The character behind the words is formed through prior agency.

Thus, the passage implies words are all three: diagnostic signs, disclosures of hidden reality, and reflections of long-term moral formation.
