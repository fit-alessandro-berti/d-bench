The problem is not visibility. It's the craving to be seen.

The Greek makes that clear. Jesus doesn't say, "don't do your righteousness *in front of* people." He says don't do it *pros to theathenai autois* - "with a view to being beheld by them."

*Theaomai*, to be gazed at, is theater-language. To make people your audience.

That can't mean all goodness must be hidden, because just a chapter earlier Jesus said the opposite:

> "Let your light so shine before men, that they may see your good works, and glorify your Father which is in heaven." Matt. 5:16

Matthew 6:1 and Matthew 5:16 are not contradictory. They have different *telos*, different ends:

* 5:16: be seen so that **God** is glorified
* 6:1: be seen so that **you** are glorified

That shift of audience is what makes public goodness morally dangerous.

1. **It changes who you're serving.** Charity, prayer, fasting - the three examples that follow in Matthew 6 - are meant to be love toward neighbor and worship toward God. When done "to be seen," your neighbor becomes a prop and God becomes irrelevant. The real transaction is between you and the crowd.

2. **It changes your reward.** "Otherwise ye have no reward of your Father." Jesus repeats this in verses 2, 5, and 16: "They have their reward" - *apechousin ton misthon*, they have been paid in full. Human applause *is* payment, and it cancels out heavenly payment. You get what you aimed at, nothing more.

3. **It creates a hypocrite.** Jesus' word in this passage is *hypokrites* - literally an actor. Public goodness tempts you to split in two: the doer and the image-manager. You start curating righteousness instead of practicing it.

That's why Jesus says "Take heed" - *prosechete*, guard, watch. Public praise is powerfully addictive. You may start giving for love and find, almost without noticing, that you need the photo, the thanks, the reputation to keep giving.

Visibility itself is unavoidable - alms have to be received by someone to help them. The danger is when to be seen stops being the *circumstance* of goodness and becomes the *motive* for it.
