James is exposing a kind of moral schizophrenia: **a mouth that is reverent toward God and contemptuous toward people made in God's image.**

> "Therewith bless we God, even the Father; and therewith curse we men, which are made after the similitude of God."

The contradiction is theological before it is merely behavioral.

If human beings bear God's likeness (Genesis 1:26-27), then a person is not separate from the honor due to God. To praise the Creator while demeaning His image is like praising an artist while defacing his portrait. The speech is logically at war with itself.

James is not thinking only of profanity. "Cursing" here is speech that diminishes, condemns, slanders, mocks, or treats a person as worthless. Blessing God in worship on one occasion, then using words to tear down His image-bearers in ordinary life, reveals a divided heart: devotion upward, but no love outward.

That is why he adds, almost as a statement about the order of creation: *"these things ought not so to be."*

In the verses that follow he makes the point from nature: a spring does not pour out fresh water and salt water; a fig tree does not bear olives. Nature acts according to its kind. The inconsistent human mouth shows something unnatural — a heart not yet made whole.

### Why speech reveals this so quickly

James says earlier in the chapter that if a person could perfectly control his speech, he could control his whole body (James 3:2). The tongue is the hardest part to bridle because it is the closest to the heart.

Jesus said the same thing: *"Out of the abundance of the heart the mouth speaketh"* (Matthew 12:34).

Speech reveals moral disorder quickly because:

**1. It is spontaneous.**  
We can often manage our outward behavior for reputation's sake, but words slip out in irritation, humor, gossip, sarcasm, and haste — before our self-image has time to edit them.

**2. It is constant.**  
Most moral tests are occasional. Speech is continuous. Every conversation becomes a small revelation of what we love, fear, envy, admire, or despise.

**3. It exposes our real view of people.**  
Contempt usually speaks before it acts. A person may never physically harm another, yet reveal by habitual mockery, slander, or dismissal that he does not truly regard them as bearers of God's image.

**4. It is both thought and deed at once.**  
A word is the heart made audible, but it is also an action with real effects. It can bless, heal, corrupt, or destroy. So the tongue is not merely a symptom of inner disorder; it spreads that disorder into the world (James 3:6).

James's point is not simply, "watch your language." It is more searching: the tongue is a diagnostic instrument. Blessing and cursing from the same mouth show that worship has been separated from love, and religion from character.
