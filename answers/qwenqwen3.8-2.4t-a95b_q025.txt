

The danger in Matthew 6:1 is not simply that a good deed is visible, but that the doer seeks visibility as the reward.

The verse itself draws the line precisely:

> “Take heed that ye do not your alms before men, **to be seen of them**.”

The phrase “to be seen of them” reveals the problem: the purpose of the act. Public goodness becomes morally dangerous when it turns into performance, when the deed is done primarily to win human admiration rather than to honor God or serve others.

Visibility itself is not the root evil. Elsewhere Jesus says, “Let your light so shine before men, that they may see your good works, and glorify your Father which is in heaven” (Matthew 5:16). There, good works are seen — but the aim is not self-glory; the aim is that God be glorified.

So the issue is not:

- being seen,  
- doing good publicly,  
- or even receiving recognition in itself.

The issue is the craving to be seen, the inward desire for human praise as the ultimate reward. Jesus warns that if human applause is what one seeks, that applause becomes one’s reward — and there is no reward from the Father.

Public goodness is morally dangerous because it tempts the heart to substitute God’s approval with human approval. The deed may remain outwardly good, but the motive becomes corrupted. The person may appear generous, prayerful, or righteous while secretly making virtue into a means of self-display.

In short: **visibility is the occasion of the danger; the craving to be seen is the sin.** The verse calls for inward watchfulness, because the public nature of goodness can easily turn the heart away from God and toward the praise of people.
