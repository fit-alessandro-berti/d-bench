Paul defines victory as **refusing to let evil reproduce itself in you, while actively answering evil with good**. The aim is victory over evil, not merely victory over an enemy.

The context is crucial. Immediately beforehand, Paul forbids retaliation: “Recompense to no man evil for evil” and “Avenge not yourselves,” leaving final judgment to God (Romans 12:17–19). Feeding a hungry enemy and giving drink to a thirsty one therefore go beyond simply restraining revenge. They require concrete, potentially costly action for the enemy’s welfare.

Verse 21 describes two possible defeats:

- **“Be not overcome of evil”**: evil wins when another person’s wrongdoing determines your character and draws you into hatred, cruelty, or revenge.
- **“Overcome evil with good”**: good wins when you remain morally free, interrupt the cycle of retaliation, and do what is right regardless of the enemy’s conduct.

The difficult phrase is “heap coals of fire on his head.” Paul is quoting Proverbs 25:21–22, and the precise image is debated. Many understand it as the burning shame or remorse that undeserved kindness can awaken, possibly opening the way to repentance. Others connect it with the enemy’s accountability or judgment before God. Either way, it should not become an excuse for concealed revenge: feeding someone in order to enjoy his humiliation would merely make kindness another weapon. Verse 21 identifies **good**, not a desire to make the enemy suffer, as the overcoming power.

Nor does Paul guarantee that kindness will change the enemy. The enemy may remain hostile. Even then, evil has been denied one victory: it has not succeeded in making you act according to its own logic.

The command is therefore **not weak**, because it demands more than passivity or avoidance—it requires self-mastery, courage, and active beneficence. It is **not manipulative**, unless distorted into a technique for shaming or controlling someone. And it is **genuinely strong**, though its strength is moral and spiritual rather than the power to dominate another person.

This does not require assisting someone’s wrongdoing, remaining needlessly exposed to abuse, or abandoning justice and the protection of others. Its direct target is personal vengeance.

In short: **do not let evil choose your methods. Meet real need with real good, leave ultimate judgment to God, and refuse to let evil have the last word in your own actions.**
