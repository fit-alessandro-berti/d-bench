## The Contradiction

The passage exposes a split between how we treat God and how we treat those who bear His image, all through one mouth.

**1. The same instrument does opposite things.**
"Therewith" is repeated: *with it* we bless, *with it* we curse. James isn't describing two kinds of people, the pious and the profane. He describes one person, and he says "we," including himself. The worshiper who sings on Sunday is the one who slanders on Monday.

**2. The contradiction is theological, not just behavioral.**
James says we curse men "which are made after the similitude of God," echoing Genesis 1:26 and 9:6. To curse the image is to insult the Original. You cannot honor a king while spitting on his portrait. So the cursing doesn't merely sit awkwardly beside the blessing; it undermines it. It suggests the blessing was never quite what it claimed to be. John makes the same point: "he that loveth not his brother whom he hath seen, how can he love God whom he hath not seen?" (1 John 4:20).

**3. It is unnatural.**
In the verses that follow (3:11–12), James appeals to nature: a spring doesn't send out sweet and bitter water, and a fig tree doesn't bear olives. Creation is consistent with its source. The divided tongue is worse than inconsistent. It is a kind of moral monstrosity, something that "ought not so to be." This ties to James's larger concern with the "double-minded man, unstable in all his ways" (1:8; 4:8). The divided speech is a symptom of a divided heart.

## Why Speech Reveals Disorder So Quickly

**Speech is the overflow of the heart.**
Jesus: "Out of the abundance of the heart the mouth speaketh" (Matt. 12:34; Luke 6:45). Words aren't a separate compartment of life. They are what spills out when the vessel is jostled. James's point about the spring works the same way: what comes out tells you what's underneath.

**Speech is fast and cheap.**
Deeds usually take planning, effort, and opportunity. Words take a breath. There is almost no gap between impulse and expression, so speech bypasses the careful self-presentation we manage elsewhere. A curse under provocation reveals more than a prayer offered at a scheduled time.

**Guarded speech versus unguarded speech.**
Our "blessing" of God often happens in structured, public, rehearsed settings. Our cursing of people happens in traffic, in frustration, in private conversation, in comments about the absent. The gap between the two shows the distance between our performed self and our actual self.

**Speech reveals how we rank people.**
Cursing is a verbal verdict that a person is worthless or contemptible. Jesus treats contemptuous words ("Raca," "thou fool") as belonging to the same family as murder (Matt. 5:21–22). Our words show whether we actually believe others bear God's image or only affirm it in theory.

**The tongue is an index of the whole person.**
James has just said that whoever doesn't offend in word "is a perfect man, able also to bridle the whole body" (3:2), and that "no man can tame" the tongue (3:8). The tongue is small but diagnostic, the way a thermometer is small but tells you about the whole body. Earlier he warned that anyone who "seemeth to be religious, and bridleth not his tongue… this man's religion is vain" (1:26).

## The Point

James isn't only saying "watch your language." He is saying that your speech about people audits your worship of God. Honoring God and dishonoring His image cannot both be sincere, and the tongue, unfiltered and quick, is where the truth of the heart gets out first. The remedy he points to later is not better self-censorship alone but "the wisdom that is from above," which is "pure, then peaceable, gentle" (3:17): a heart made single, so that the spring gives one kind of water.
