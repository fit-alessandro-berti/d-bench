**The Pharisee’s condemnation is rooted in pride, which then expresses itself in several related sins.**  

| Element | How it appears in the text | Why it condemns him |
|---------|---------------------------|---------------------|
| **Pride (self‑righteousness)** | “They trusted in themselves that they were righteous” (v. 8); the Pharisee’s prayer begins with “God, I thank Thee that I am not as other men…” | Pride is the root of all the other failures. He thinks he is already justified by his own conduct, not by God’s grace. |
| **False doctrine** | He treats fasting and tithing as proof of his righteousness (“I fast twice in the week, I give tithes of all that I possess”). | He teaches (or lives out) the error that obedience to the law can earn salvation—a work‑based soteriology that contradicts the gospel. |
| **Comparison** | He contrasts himself with the publican, listing the vices he avoids (“not an extortioner, unjust, adulterer”). | By measuring himself against another, he inflates his own status and devalues the other, showing that his righteousness is measured by external standards, not by heart. |
| **Contempt** | “And despised others” (v. 7). | Contempt flows from pride; he looks down on the publican because he believes himself morally superior. |
| **Virtue as self‑display** | The fasting, tithing, and public prayer are performed publicly as evidence of his holiness. | He uses good works to prove his worthiness rather than to worship God, turning piety into a badge of self‑aggrandizement. |

In short, the Pharisee is condemned for **pride**, and that pride manifests as false doctrine, comparison, contempt, and the display of virtue as a self‑proof. All of these are symptoms of the same underlying error: trusting in one’s own righteousness rather than in God’s mercy.

---

### Why the tax collector’s posture is treated as spiritually truer

The publican’s behavior stands in stark contrast to the Pharisee’s and is highlighted for several reasons:

1. **Humility** – He does not lift his eyes to heaven or boast about his deeds. Instead, he acknowledges his sinfulness (“a sinner”).  
2. **Repentance** – The act of striking his breast is a visible sign of inner sorrow and contrition, a classic biblical image of a broken heart (cf. Psalm 51:3‑5).  
3. **Dependence on divine mercy** – He does not offer any merit of his own; he simply cries out to God for mercy, trusting that only God can forgive.  
4. **No self‑display** – He gives nothing to the temple; his prayer is a pure confession, not a performance for human approval.

Jesus tells the story to expose the gap between **human self‑justification** (the Pharisee’s “I am not like other men”) and **true righteousness** (the publican’s humble admission of need). The publican’s posture illustrates the kingdom principle that righteousness is received by those who recognize their need for grace, not by those who inflate themselves through works. Therefore, his prayer is “spiritually truer” because it embodies the humility, repentance, and reliance on God’s mercy that the Pharisee lacks.
