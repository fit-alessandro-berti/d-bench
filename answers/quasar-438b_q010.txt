This passage praises a paradoxical power: power that appears as its opposite.

It is not the power of obvious force, royal command, domination, or high reputation. It is the power of a supreme being who *chooses to humble itself*, taking the form of a servant, becoming like ordinary men, obeying even unto death. The praised power is mastery that can lower itself without being diminished: a self-overriding humility, a greatness that proves itself by accepting submission.

In plainer terms: Jesus/God-like power is praised as sovereign power voluntarily contracted into servant-form. It can be “equal with God” and yet make itself “of no reputation.” It rules not by 显示ing dominance but by being able to descend, obey, suffer, and die while still remaining God.

Why is humility joined to greatness more unsettling than open domination?

Because open domination is visible and therefore measurable. You can see the tyrant, blame the tyrant, resist the tyrant. But humility joined to greatness hides the power behind its opposite. It asks you to trust a power that looks low, humble, obedient, even sacrificial. The danger is that the greatness is not gone; it is merely disguised. A mind “which was also in Christ Jesus” may be the mind of a servant only because it has chosen servant-form. Its obedience may be voluntary, and voluntary obedience can be revoked; its death may be accepted, and accepted death can still be a mode of control.

So the unsettling quality comes from the contradiction:

- If power is openly dominant, you know where it stands.
- If power humbles itself, you no longer know whether its humble form is genuine submission or masked sovereignty.

The passage praises power as *inwardness* or *presence*: the power to be anything, to take any form, to rise to God or sink into servant, and to remain the same power throughout. That power is more unsettling because it is unlocatable. It may be everywhere and anywhere: in the obedient servant, in the mortal figure, in the mind that humbles itself to death. It is a power that cannot be opposed by resisting its outward form, because its outward form is humility itself.
