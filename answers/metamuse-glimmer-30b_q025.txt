In Matthew 6:1-4 the danger is not that men see you do good, but that you do good *in order that* men see you.

The Greek purpose clause is key: *pros to theasthai hyp’ autōn*, “to be seen of them.” The verb is intentional. Jesus is not prohibiting public almsgiving as such, he is prohibiting almsgiving done *for the audience*.

That intentionality corrupts the act in three ways:

1. **It changes the audience and the reward.** Alms, prayer and fasting are acts addressed to the Father. If they are performed *pros* men, the reward is already paid in the human applause they were staged for: “they have their reward.” A theocentric act is turned into an anthropocentric transaction.

2. **It makes virtue performative.** The word Jesus will use for this in v.2, *hypokrites*, is the stage-player, the actor. Public goodness becomes hypocrisy when the moral act is staged for effect rather than offered in secret to God. The danger is self-deception and vainglory: the good deed is kept as a means of reputation and self-justification.

3. **It severs love from humility.** Charity done in secret preserves the humility that makes it love. Charity done to be seen makes love instrumental: it seeks esteem, security, status. The motive becomes disordered love - loving the praise of men more than the approval of the Father.

Visibility is therefore not intrinsically evil. Matthew 5:16 can still say “let your light shine before men.” The problem is the *craving to be seen* that turns light into a spotlight on self. Incidental visibility is tolerable; intentional exhibitionism is what forfeits the heavenly reward because the act is no longer yours to God, it is already consumed by men.
