The difference is between **outward reformation** and **inward regeneration**.

### External compliance vs. a new heart

**External compliance** changes behavior, but not the person. It can be produced by fear, social pressure, self-discipline, or religious pride. A person may obey outwardly while remaining inwardly resistant, cold, or self-centered. That is why Jesus said of some religious people:

> “This people honors me with their lips, but their heart is far from me.” (Matthew 15:8)

External compliance may look good, but it cannot produce the love, delight, and true obedience God desires.

**Receiving a new heart** means God changes the inner source of a person’s life. In the new covenant, God does not just demand obedience; He gives a new nature that *wants* to obey. He takes away the stony heart—the hard, resistant, unresponsive center—and gives a living, responsive heart. Then He puts His Spirit within so that obedience flows from the inside out.

So external compliance says, “I will change what I do.”  
A new heart says, “God has changed who I am, and now I obey because I love Him.”

### Why is hardness described like a material condition?

Because in Scripture, sin is not only a matter of wrong actions; it is a **condition of the heart**. The “heart” in Hebrew thought is the center of the will, mind, and affections.

A stone is hard, dead, unfeeling, and unable to respond. A “stony heart” is a picture of humanity’s spiritual condition: resistant to God, unresponsive to His voice, unable to produce true life or love. A stone cannot gradually soften itself into living flesh. It must be **removed and replaced**.

That is why the promise is so radical:

> “I will take away the stony heart… and give you a heart of flesh.”

The “heart of flesh” does not mean a weak or sinful heart. It means a living, soft, teachable, responsive heart—one that can receive the Spirit and walk in God’s ways.

So the material image of stone versus flesh teaches that salvation is not mere improvement. It is a new creation. God does not polish the stone; He performs a heart transplant. That is why He says, “I will put my Spirit within you, and cause you to walk in my statutes.” True obedience is the fruit of a new heart, not the cause of it.
