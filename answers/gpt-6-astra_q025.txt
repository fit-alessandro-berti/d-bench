The moral danger is **not visibility itself, but the desire to make goodness a spectacle of oneself**. The key phrase is “**to be seen of them**”: it identifies the purpose of the act, not merely its setting.

Giving publicly can genuinely help someone. But when recognition becomes the aim, the giver risks turning the person in need into a prop and generosity into a way of securing admiration. The outward deed remains useful, while its inward purpose shifts from love of neighbor toward self-display.

That is why Jesus speaks of “reward.” In the verses that follow, those who give to win praise “have their reward”: the approval they sought. They should not confuse that approval with the Father’s approval.

This also explains why Matthew 6:1 need not contradict Matthew 5:16, where Jesus says to let others see your good works and glorify your Father. **The distinction is where the attention is meant to lead—to God, or to the giver.**

A searching test is: *Would I still do this good if nobody knew I had done it?* Secrecy is not the definition of goodness, but it can discipline the craving for applause.
