Paul puts bitterness and forgiveness directly against each other because they are rival roots of the same heart. Bitterness is unforgiveness that has soured and settled in; forgiveness is the grace that uproots it.

In Ephesians 4:31, bitterness (*pikria*) is not just a passing feeling. It is a deep, resentful disposition — the kind that has been nursed until it has become a way of seeing people. The words around it show what it does: it boils over into “wrath” and “anger,” and then leaks out through “clamour” and “evil speaking.” That is why it is contagious. Bitterness never stays private. It speaks, accuses, slanders, and poisons other people. Hebrews 12:15 calls it a “root of bitterness” that defiles many.

So is it a feeling, a habit, a chosen posture, or something contagious? In this text, it is all of those at once:

- It is a **feeling** — the sour, aching resentment inside.
- It becomes a **habit** — the repeated rehearsal of the offense.
- It is a **chosen posture** — Paul commands that it be “put away from you,” because we are not passive victims of our hearts.
- It is **contagious** — it springs up, spreads through words, and corrupts community life.

But then verse 32 gives the exact opposite: “Be ye kind one to another, tenderhearted, forgiving one another, even as God for Christ’s sake hath forgiven you.”

The word translated “forgiving” is *charizomenoi*, from *charis* — **grace**. To forgive is not just to stop feeling angry; it is to actively give grace to someone who doesn’t deserve it. And the standard is enormous: God, for Christ’s sake, has forgiven *you*. That is the pattern and the power. You don’t forgive by trying to manufacture a feeling; you forgive by letting the reality of God’s forgiveness in Christ loosen your grip on the wrong done to you.

That is why bitterness and forgiveness are placed so directly against each other. They cannot coexist. Bitterness is what grows where forgiveness is refused. Forgiveness is what kills the root of bitterness and replaces it with kindness, tenderheartedness, and grace.
