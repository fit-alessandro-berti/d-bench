The danger is **the craving to be seen**, not visibility itself. The verse does not prohibit public goodness; it prohibits goodness performed *“to be seen of them”* (Greek: *tou theathēnai autois*—literally, “for the purpose of being beheld by them”).

Here is why the motive, not the audience, is the moral hazard:

**1. The grammar specifies intent**
The prohibition is not simply “do not give before men,” but “do not give before men **to be seen**.” The purpose clause (*tou* + infinitive) limits the warning to those who make human approval the *telos*—the end goal—of their charity. If you give publicly but do not care whether anyone notices, you are not the target of this verse.

**2. Jesus Himself commands public goodness**
In the immediately preceding chapter, Jesus says: *“Let your light so shine before men, that they may see your good works, and glorify your Father which is in heaven”* (Matthew 5:16). If visibility were inherently corrupting, this command would be impossible. The difference is that in 5:16, the spectators’ gaze is directed past the good work to God; in 6:1, the gaze is arrested on the giver.

**3. The economics of reward**
The verse warns that those who perform for human eyes *“have no reward of your Father.”* The danger is a transactional corruption: you exchange the eternal, secret approval of God for the fleeting, public praise of people. The hypocrites “have their reward” (Matthew 6:2, 5, 16)—they get paid in full here and now, and the heavenly account remains empty. The moral danger is therefore **idolatry of the spectator**: making the creature’s applause the altar at which you sacrifice the purity of the act.

**4. The danger is hypocrisy (*hypokrisis*)**
The Greek word for “hypocrites” in the surrounding verses (6:2, 5, 16) means actors—those who play a role for an audience. When you crave to be seen, charity becomes theater. The alms are no longer an act of love toward the neighbor or obedience to God, but a performance of virtue. The moral danger is that the act ceases to be *yours*; it belongs to the crowd.

**Practical test:** The verse exposes the heart by asking what happens when the audience disappears. If you give anonymously, pray in secret, and fast without display, and those acts remain just as fervent—then you love the Father. If your goodness wilts when no one is watching, then you were not doing good; you were *being seen* doing good.
