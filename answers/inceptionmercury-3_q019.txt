The passage exposes a deep inner contradiction: **the same tongue that praises God is used to curse human beings who bear God’s very likeness.**

James points to the irony: in one breath, the person “blesses” God—the Father—and in another breath “curses” people made “after the similitude of God” (Genesis 1:26–27). This is self-contradictory because the glory of God is reflected in humanity. To despise and curse someone made in God’s image is, in effect, to insult God Himself. Blessing God while cursing His image-bearer is incoherent worship.

Why does speech reveal moral disorder so quickly? Because **speech is the overflow of the heart**. Jesus said, “Out of the abundance of the heart the mouth speaks” (Matthew 12:34). Words are not neutral; they reveal what is truly inside. A person can control outward behavior for a time, but the tongue often slips because it is the most immediate, frequent, and difficult member to control. James emphasizes this: “If anyone thinks he is religious and does not bridle his tongue, his religion is worthless” (James 1:26).

The tongue’s inconsistency is a quick moral diagnostic. It exposes double-mindedness: a divided heart that praises God on Sunday but curses His image-bearers on Monday. That is why James says plainly, “These things ought not to be so.”
