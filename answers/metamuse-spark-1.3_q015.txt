The sharpness is in the woodshop absurdity.

In Greek, it's not mote vs. a slightly bigger mote. It's *karphos* vs. *dokos*:

* *karphos* — a speck, a chip, a fleck of straw or sawdust floating in the eye.
* *dokos* — a load-bearing beam, a roof joist, the log that holds up a house.

Jesus, the carpenter's son, is picturing a man with a rafter jutting out of his face offering to do delicate eye surgery on someone else: "Let me pull out the mote out of thine eye."

You can't miss three things that make it sting:

**1. Reversed proportions.** We are hyper-observant about others and half-blind about ourselves. Verse 3 even uses two different verbs: you *beholdest* (*blepeis*, you stare at) your brother's speck, but you do not *consider* (*katanoeis*, you don't notice, ponder, take to heart) your own log. It's scrutiny for him, amnesia for you.

**2. Physical impossibility.** An eye with a log in it can't see. That is the point. The fault is not just that you are unkind — you are *incompetent* to help. A blind guide trying to lead someone with impaired vision will only injure him. How will you say, "Let me help," when, behold, you can't even see to help?

**3. Play-acting.** That's why verse 5 calls him straight out: *Thou hypocrite* — *hypokrita*, an actor in a mask. He is playing the doctor, the righteous elder brother, the clear-eyed saint, while hiding a glaring, unconfessed fault.

Everyone instantly recognizes himself in it. That's why it still convicts 2,000 years later. It's funny until it's about you.

### Why this isn't "never correct anyone"

Jesus does not say the brother has *no* problem. There really is a mote in his eye. It really needs to come out. And Jesus does not forbid discernment elsewhere — in the same sermon he tells us to discern false prophets by their fruits (Matt. 7:15-20), and elsewhere to go and tell a brother his fault (Matt. 18:15), to "judge righteous judgment" (John 7:24).

What he forbids is censorious, superior judgment — condemning from above while refusing examination from within. Notice the order:

> Thou hypocrite, **first** cast out the beam out of thine own eye; **and then** shalt thou see clearly to cast out the mote out of thy brother's eye.

Correction isn't canceled. It's sequenced and transformed:

**First:** self-judgment. Acknowledge the log — especially the log of pride, hard-heartedness, and judgmentalism itself that blinds you. Repent of it.

**Then:** you will *see clearly* — *diablepseis*, you'll see through, focus. Humility gives you clarity, gentleness, and credibility. You go from being a judge with a gavel to a brother helping a brother, who knows how much it hurts to get something out of your eye and how much you need mercy too.

Paul says the same thing later: "restore such an one in the spirit of meekness; considering thyself, lest thou also be tempted" (Gal. 6:1).

In short: Don't ignore the speck. Don't exaggerate the speck and ignore the log. Remove the log so you can truly, humbly help with the speck.
