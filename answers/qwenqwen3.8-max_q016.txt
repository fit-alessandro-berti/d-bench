The elder brother’s deepest problem is **discomfort with mercy itself**—but that discomfort reveals an even deeper root: **he has a servant’s heart in his father’s house**.

All the things you mention are present, but they function at different levels:

- **Anger** is the outward symptom.
- **Envy** is the craving for the celebration he thinks he deserves.
- **Wounded fairness** is the argument he uses to justify his resentment.
- **Pride in obedience** is the ground on which he stands.
- **Discomfort with mercy** is the deepest exposed problem, because it shows that he cannot share the father’s heart.

He says, “Lo, these many years do I serve thee.” That is the language of a slave, not a son. He has lived with the father as if the father were a master, and obedience were a wage. Because of that, when mercy is shown to the undeserving brother, it feels to him like injustice. Grace threatens his whole way of understanding the household.

His complaint is not merely, “My brother was forgiven.” His complaint is, “My brother was celebrated.” He cannot bear that the lost are found, that the dead are alive again, and that the father’s joy is real. He calls the prodigal “this thy son,” but the father corrects him: “this thy brother.” The elder brother refuses kinship; the father insists on it.

The father’s answer is decisive:

> “Son, thou art ever with me, and all that I have is thine.”

The elder brother already possessed everything. But he did not live in the joy of it. He lived as though the father were withholding. He wanted a kid to make merry with his friends, when in fact all the father’s wealth was already his. His problem was not that the father was unfair; his problem was that he did not know the father’s heart.

So if I had to choose among the options, I would say:

**His deepest named problem is discomfort with mercy itself.**  
But more fundamentally, that discomfort comes from **a self-righteous, mercenary heart that does not truly know the father**. He is lost, not in the far country, but inside the father’s house.
