**Both, but Jesus puts the emphasis on how treasure trains the heart.** If the question were only how to discover what you already love, the instruction might be “Look at your treasure.” Instead, Jesus says, “Lay up for yourselves treasures in heaven,” and gives a reason: “For where your treasure is, there **will** your heart be also.” What you store up reveals what you value now; the act of storing it up also helps shape what you will value.

Possession, attention, and love meet in the ordinary work of caring for what you count as yours. The images of moth, rust, and thieves make that concrete: once something is your treasure, you have reason to watch over it, fear its loss, and plan around keeping it. Love directs attention, but attention can also deepen love. The “heart” here is more than a feeling: it is the center from which you desire and choose.

The nearby teaching on giving in secret offers a positive example. Giving to someone in need can express compassion you already feel, but it can also teach you to notice a need you once passed by. And giving without an audience helps loosen the hold of another earthly treasure: other people’s praise. Jesus’ counsel is therefore more hopeful than a test of your motives. You need not wait for your loves to be perfectly ordered before you act; what you do with what you have can help order what you love.
