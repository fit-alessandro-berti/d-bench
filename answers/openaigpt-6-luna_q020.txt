Bitterness is called a **root** because it is more than a single angry act or sharp remark. It is an inward resentment that can take hold and keep feeding later words and actions. Like a root, it may be hidden before its effects are visible.

The image suggests three things:

- **Hidden growth:** Resentment can be nursed privately—through repeated grievance, suspicion, or refusal to forgive—even while a person appears outwardly calm.
- **Delayed damage:** A root can grow for a time before it produces bitter fruit. Likewise, resentment may later emerge in hostility, distrust, harsh speech, or broken relationships.
- **Spread to others:** The verse warns that “many” may be defiled. Resentment rarely stays entirely private: it can shape how someone treats others, invite allies into a grievance, or poison the atmosphere of a family or community.

The warning is not that every experience of hurt or anger is itself bitterness. It is about allowing resentment to become a settled, destructive force. “Looking diligently” calls the community to notice and address such growth early, pursuing peace and holiness rather than letting it take root.
