James is exposing a **split at the root of the religious life**: the same tongue that is used to bless God is being used to curse the very people who bear His likeness.

## 1. The inner contradiction

The contradiction is not merely “saying one thing and doing another.” It is deeper: it is a **vertical/horizontal split in the heart**.

- **Vertically**, the mouth is used to “bless God, even the Father.” To bless God is to acknowledge His goodness, His worth, His Fatherhood, and His claim on our praise.
- **Horizontally**, the same mouth is used to “curse men.” To curse people is to insult, slander, revile, or wish harm on them.

James says this ought not to be, because those “men” are **“made after the similitude of God.”** Human beings are not neutral objects; they are image-bearers of the Creator (Genesis 1:26–27; 9:6). Therefore, to curse them is implicitly to dishonor the God whose likeness they carry. You cannot consistently bless the Father while treating His children—or His image in them—with contempt.

So the contradiction is essentially this:

> A person or community claims to honor God with the lips, while using those same lips to tear down what God has made and loves.

James has already prepared for this. In James 1:26 he says that if someone “seem[s] to be religious” and does not bridle the tongue, that person “deceiveth his own heart” and his religion is worthless. Later, in James 4, he will call such a person “double-minded.” The problem is a divided heart: trying to hold God in one hand and hatred, envy, or contempt for neighbor in the other.

In short, James exposes the contradiction of **worship without love**, or **praise of God joined to malice toward God’s image-bearers**.

## 2. Why speech reveals moral disorder so quickly

Speech is one of the fastest and most reliable diagnostics of the heart for several reasons.

### a. Speech is the overflow of the heart

Jesus said, “Out of the abundance of the heart the mouth speaketh” (Matthew 12:34; Luke 6:45). Words are not manufactured in the mouth; they come from what fills the inner person. Religious actions can be rehearsed and staged. But ordinary speech—especially unguarded speech—leaks what is really inside.

That is why James says, “If any man offend not in word, the same is a perfect man” (James 3:2). The tongue is the final frontier of self-control. If the tongue is corrupt, the whole person is disordered.

### b. The tongue is small but decisive

James compares the tongue to a **bit**, a **rudder**, and a **fire** (James 3:3–6). Those images show that the tongue is small in size but enormous in influence.

- A bit turns a whole horse.
- A rudder steers a whole ship.
- A tiny spark sets a forest ablaze.

Because speech has such leverage, a few words instantly reveal the direction of a life. If the tongue is blessing God but cursing people, it shows that something is badly wrong at the source.

### c. A corrupted source cannot hide

James then asks:

> “Doth a fountain send forth at the same place sweet water and bitter? Can the fig tree bear olive berries?” (James 3:11–12)

The argument is from creation order. A spring should give one kind of water. A tree should bear its proper fruit. In the same way, a renewed heart should produce consistent speech. If blessing and cursing come from the same mouth, it proves that the “fountain” is still polluted.

This is why speech reveals disorder so quickly: **fruit shows the root almost immediately.** You may not need to watch a person for years to know whether envy, bitterness, pride, or selfish ambition rules the heart. The tongue will tell you.

### d. Speech is where “wisdom” becomes visible

James goes on to contrast two kinds of wisdom (James 3:13–18). The “wisdom from above” is “pure, peaceable, gentle, full of mercy.” But earthly wisdom produces “bitter envying and strife,” and “where envying and strife is, there is confusion and every evil work.”

Speech is the first place wisdom—or the lack of it—appears. A mouth that curses image-bearers shows that the person is being driven not by God’s wisdom, but by jealousy, selfish ambition, and disorder.

### e. The tongue is untamable by sheer willpower

James says, “The tongue can no man tame; it is an unruly evil, full of deadly poison” (James 3:8). Human effort alone cannot fix it. That is precisely why it exposes moral disorder so quickly: we can manage outward behavior for a while, but the tongue bypasses our self-presentation and reveals our need for inward transformation.

## The point

James is not merely giving etiquette advice. He is saying that **the tongue is a theological barometer**. A mouth that blesses God while cursing people made in His image proves that the heart is still double-minded and the religion is still superficial.

The cure is not simply “try harder to speak nicely.” The cure is a changed heart—what James elsewhere calls receiving “the engrafted word” (James 1:21) and living by “the wisdom from above” (James 3:17). When the heart is made single and clean, the same mouth will consistently bless God by blessing those who bear His likeness.
