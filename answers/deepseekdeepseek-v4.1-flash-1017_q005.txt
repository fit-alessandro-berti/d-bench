Enemy-love is hard to take seriously because it cuts against both instinct and ordinary justice. We naturally love those who love us, protect ourselves from threats, and want evildoers to be stopped or punished. Jesus does not deny that enemies exist; he names them—and then commands concrete action: bless, do good, pray. That is much harder than a warm feeling.

It is **not mainly sentimental**. Sentiment says, “Have nice feelings toward everyone.” Jesus commands specific practices: speak blessing rather than curses, act for another’s good, pray for persecutors. This kind of love can coexist with grief, anger, truth-telling, boundaries, and a demand for justice. It is not the same as trust, approval, reconciliation without repentance, or remaining in harm. Sentimentalized enemy-love is rightly dismissed because it is shallow and often irresponsible.

It is **not primarily strategic**, though it can have strategic fruit. Kindness can shame hostility, break cycles of revenge, and free a person from bitterness. But if enemy-love is practiced only because it “works,” it is not really love; it is clever reciprocity. It collapses when the enemy remains hostile or when kindness costs too much.

At its core, Jesus presents enemy-love as a **deeper form of moral strength**. It is the strength to refuse dehumanization, to renounce revenge, to will another’s true good, and to resemble the Father, who “maketh his sun to rise on the evil and on the good.” It is not passivity; it is self-mastery and grace-enabled courage. It says, “Evil is real, and you have harmed me—but I will not let your evil become my pattern.”

So enemy-love should be taken seriously precisely because it is not a greeting-card sentiment or a pragmatic technique. It is a demanding, cruciform strength: truthful about evil, protective of the vulnerable, committed to justice without hatred, and free enough to pray for the one who persecutes. In that sense, it is not weakness; it is the hardest kind of moral strength—love that resembles God’s impartial mercy.
