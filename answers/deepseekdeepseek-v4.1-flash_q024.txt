External compliance is conformity of behavior to a standard; receiving a “new heart” is the transformation of the person who behaves. In Ezekiel 36:26–27, God does not merely command Israel to try harder. He promises to remove the stony heart, give a heart of flesh, and put His Spirit within them so that they walk in His statutes. The difference is therefore root versus fruit: external compliance can be produced by fear, social pressure, habit, self-interest, or pride, while a new heart is God’s inward renewal of the mind, will, affections, and conscience.

The heart in Scripture is not merely the emotions; it is the controlling center of the inner life. A person can outwardly obey while inwardly resisting, loving something else, or merely avoiding punishment. That is external compliance. It may look like righteousness, but it does not change the source of action. By contrast, the new heart is given by God so that obedience flows from a renewed nature. The Spirit is placed within, and God “causes” His people to walk in His statutes. Obedience becomes fruit, not a mask.

Hardness is described almost like a material condition because sin is not just a series of bad choices; it is a settled condition of the inner self. Stone imagery communicates deadness, rigidity, coldness, and unresponsiveness. A stone cannot receive impressions or respond from within; it can only be shaped by external force. A stony heart may externally comply under enough pressure, but it cannot love, trust, repent, or delight in God. “Flesh,” by contrast, means living, tender, responsive humanity—not the sinful “flesh” Paul sometimes speaks of, but a heart that is alive to God.

That is why the change must be replacement, not mere improvement. You cannot polish a stone into flesh. The problem is not simply a lack of information or external rules; the problem is a dead, resistant core. So the verbs in Ezekiel are divine: “I will give,” “I will put,” “I will take away,” “I will give.” It is a heart transplant, a new creation. This does not make obedience optional—the promise explicitly says they will keep His judgments and do them—but it locates the power for obedience in God’s Spirit and a renewed heart rather than in external pressure alone.

So external compliance is obedience from the outside in; the new heart is obedience from the inside out. External compliance can coexist with hardness, but the new heart is the cure for hardness. Ezekiel’s promise points forward to the New Covenant in Christ and the gift of the Holy Spirit, who writes God’s law on the heart and enables His people to walk in it.
