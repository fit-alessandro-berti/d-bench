**How the passage connects speech to the heart's "hidden moral stockpile"**

Matthew 12:34–35 presents a cause‑effect metaphor rooted in ancient Near Eastern wisdom theology (cf. Prov 15:28; 22:11). The key links are:

| Verse | Image | Meaning |
|------|-------|---------|
| 34 | `"out of the abundance of the heart the mouth speaketh"` | The heart is a storage vessel (`thesaurus`—treasure). What fills it inevitably overflows in speech. |
| 34–35 | `generation of vipers` / `good treasure` / `evil treasure` | Moral condition is not abstract but generative. A `"good"` heart (`agathos`) produces `"good"` words (`kalos`); an `"evil"` heart (`saphos`) produces `"evil"` words (`poneros`). |
| 35 | `A good man... bringeth forth... an evil man... bringeth forth` | Speech is the inevitable outworking of one's inner spiritual inventory. There is no neutral ground: the source determines the stream. |

In short, the passage treats the heart as a **moral treasury** and speech as its **disclosed overflow**. Words are not random or purely environmental; they are the audible signature of what the heart has chosen to treasure.

---

**Are words symptoms, choices, revelations, or all three?**

The text encompasses all three, but with a clear priority:

1. **Revelations** – Speech *discloses* what is hidden. `"Out of the abundance of the heart the mouth speaketh"` frames words as a disclosure mechanism. They peer‑into the heart's true condition (cf. Luke 6:45: "out of the overflow of the heart the mouth speaks").

2. **Symptoms** – Speech *betrays* the heart's state. Like fruit on a tree (v. 33), words are the diagnostic evidence of inner health or corruption. They are not merely accompaniments but reliable indicators.

3. **Choices** – The passage holds listeners accountable (`"how can ye, being evil, speak good things?"`), implying that speech is an act of volition. However, the text locates the *origin* and *direction* of that choice in the heart's treasure. The "choice" is real but *derivative*—it is the heart's treasure expressing itself through speech, not a free‑floating moral calculus disconnected from inner reality.

**Bottom line:** The passage presents words primarily as **revelations that function as symptoms** of the heart's moral inventory, enacted through **choices** that are themselves rooted in what the heart treasures. In canonical order: **revelation → symptom → choice** (with choice being the human axis through which the heart's treasure is expressed, not the source of the speech's moral quality).
