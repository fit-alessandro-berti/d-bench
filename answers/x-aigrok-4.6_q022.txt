**They are inviting scrutiny of the inner life rather than requesting external protection or consolation, because they want alignment with God more than they want to feel safe or soothed.**

Psalm 139 as a whole already affirms God’s complete knowledge of the speaker (vv. 1–18). The closing petition therefore does not seek information God lacks; it asks God to *apply* that knowledge so that any “wicked way” (or “hurtful/offensive way”) can be exposed and the speaker led onto the “way everlasting.” This is a request for purification and course-correction, not merely for advocacy or comfort. The psalmist treats hidden motives and self-deception as more dangerous than external enemies, and treats God’s verdict as more trustworthy than self-justification.

The honesty modeled is therefore **radical, non-defensive self-exposure**. It assumes:
- The heart is not fully transparent even to itself.
- There may be real moral or spiritual distortion that the speaker cannot (or will not) see unaided.
- Truth about the self is preferable to a flattering or merely protective relationship with God.
- Correction is a form of leading, not an attack.

This stance rejects both self-righteousness (“I have nothing that needs searching”) and purely therapeutic religion (“just defend and comfort me”). It is the opposite of asking God to ratify one’s current self-image. Instead it treats God’s searching gaze as the condition for genuine guidance.

In short, the prayer values being *known and remade* over being *shielded or reassured*. That is a distinctive kind of spiritual honesty: willing to have one’s own interior life judged so that one can actually walk the path that lasts.
