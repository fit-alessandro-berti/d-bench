This passage is exposing a **divided heart using the same instrument for opposite purposes.**

> We use the tongue to worship God upward — and to tear down people outward — who are *made after the similitude of God.*

James' logic is this:

**1. You cannot separate God from His image-bearers.**
To "bless" is *eulogeo* — to speak well of, praise, worship. That's what we do on Sunday: "Bless we God, even the Father."

To "curse" is *kataraomai* — not just profanity, but to speak ill of, to belittle, to wish harm upon, to put down in anger, contempt, gossip, slander.

James says: If a person is made *kath' homoiōsin theou* — according to the likeness of God, Genesis 1:26-27 — then to curse the person is indirectly to insult the God whose likeness they carry.

It's the same contradiction John names later: "If a man say, I love God, and hateth his brother, he is a liar" 1 John 4:20.

James calls this double-mindedness, a major theme for him. James 1:8, 4:8. Profess piety with the mouth, practice contempt with the same mouth. Faith that doesn't change how we treat people isn't true faith. As James says earlier: "If any man... bridleth not his tongue... this man's religion is vain" James 1:26.

That's why he adds: "My brethren, these things ought not so to be." And then he gives nature pictures in verses 11-12: A spring cannot gush both fresh water and bitter salt water. A fig tree cannot bear olives. A single source must produce a single kind of outflow.

### Why does speech reveal moral disorder so quickly?

James has just argued this in 3:2-8:

**1. The tongue is closest to the heart.**
Jesus said it too: "out of the abundance of the heart the mouth speaketh" Matthew 12:34. Other sins need opportunity, planning, tools. Speech needs nothing. The thought becomes a word almost instantly, with far less filter. Anger, pride, envy, bitterness, self-righteousness leak out before we can stop them.

**2. The tongue is the hardest member to tame.**
James says if you can control the tongue, you can control the whole body (3:2). But "no man can tame the tongue; it is an unruly evil" (3:8). It's small like a bit, a rudder, a spark — but it steers and sets on fire the whole course of life. Because it's untamed, it is a perfect early-warning system for what's really ruling inside.

**3. The tongue is always with us.**
Hands can be tied, feet can stay home. The mouth goes with us into worship and into the argument in the parking lot afterward. That's why James picks worship language specifically: "therewith bless we God... and therewith curse we men." It's not hypothetical. It was likely literally happening — blessing God in the assembly, then cursing fellow church members, the poor, opponents.

In short: blessing God while cursing people made like God shows that the worship hasn't reached the heart. Speech exposes it first because speech *is* the heart overflowing.
