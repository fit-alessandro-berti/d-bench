Primarily, Matthew 6:19–21 says that **treasure trains—or locates—the heart**, though it also reveals the heart as a byproduct.

The key line is:

> “For where your treasure is, there will your heart be also.”

Notice the order and the future tense: **treasure is present; the heart will be there**. Jesus does not say, “Where your heart is, there your treasure will be,” though that is often true experientially. He says the heart follows the treasure. That makes the saying more than a diagnostic mirror; it is a warning and a discipleship strategy. The imperative “Lay up for yourselves treasures in heaven” assumes that what you invest in will shape what you love.

So the text says: **treasure reveals where the heart already is, but more fundamentally, treasure trains where the heart will go.** If you want your heart in heaven, put your treasure there.

### How possession, attention, and love tie together

1. **Possession** — “Treasure” is what you store, bank on, guard, and treat as yours. It is not merely an object; it is a center of security, identity, and value. Earthly treasure is vulnerable to moth, rust, and thieves, so it produces anxiety, hoarding, and defensive attention. Heavenly treasure is secure, so it produces trust and freedom.

2. **Attention** — The heart follows treasure because attention follows value. You think about, protect, worry over, and spend yourself on what you treasure. Attention is the daily currency of the heart. Where your money, time, imagination, and worries repeatedly go, there your heart is being pulled.

3. **Love** — In biblical language, the “heart” is the center of affection, will, loyalty, and thought—not just emotion. To love is to be attached to something as a perceived good. What you treasure becomes what you love and serve. That is why Jesus immediately goes on to say, “Ye cannot serve God and mammon” (Matt 6:24). Treasure and worship are linked.

So there is a feedback loop: love chooses a treasure; the treasure commands attention; attention deepens love; love expresses itself in possession, use, and service. Jesus interrupts that loop by commanding us to choose heavenly treasure. If you want to love God more, invest in what God values—mercy, justice, prayer, generosity, the kingdom. Your heart will follow your treasure. But also examine your life honestly: your bank statement, calendar, worries, and browser history often reveal where your treasure already is.

In short: **the text says treasure trains the heart, and in doing so it also reveals it.** Possession captures attention; attention cultivates love; love governs what we treasure. Jesus calls us to relocate our treasure to heaven so that our hearts may be relocated with it.
