The inner contradiction James exposes is **the inconsistency of using the same tongue to worship God while cursing people who bear God’s likeness**.

James says it plainly: with the same mouth we bless “the Lord and Father,” and with that same mouth we curse “men made in the likeness of God.” The contradiction is not merely that cursing is rude or socially unacceptable. It is **theologically absurd**:

- God made human beings in His own image (Genesis 1:27).
- To bless God is to honor the Creator.
- To curse a human being is to despise the image of the Creator.
- Therefore, one who blesses God and curses His image-bearer is acting out a self-defeating contradiction: praising the original while spitting on the portrait.

James intensifies this by saying “these things ought not so to be.” He then uses nature as evidence of the disorder:  
> “Does a spring pour forth from the same hole both fresh and salt water? Can a fig tree, my brothers, bear olives, or a grapevine produce figs?” (James 3:11-12)

Water from one spring is either fresh or bitter; a tree does not produce two kinds of fruit. The mouth should likewise not produce both blessing and cursing. When it does, something is deeply broken.

**Why does speech reveal moral disorder so quickly?**

Because speech is the **overflow of the heart**. Jesus said:

> “For out of the abundance of the heart the mouth speaks.” (Matthew 12:34)

Speech is not a surface-level problem. It is a symptom. When cursing comes out, it reveals that worship and love have not actually transformed the inner person. A person can say “I love God” while destroying another person with words, and that exposes a split between claimed faith and actual character.

James also points to the tongue as both small and powerful:

> “So also the tongue is a small member, yet it boasts of great things... It is set on fire by hell.” (James 3:5-6)

A person may control a horse with a bit and steer a ship with a rudder, but the tongue is uniquely hard to tame. Why? Because it is tied directly to the heart, and the corrupt heart from the fall will always leak out through speech.

So the inner contradiction is this: **we claim a Father in heaven, but we mistreat His children on earth.** Speech reveals moral disorder so quickly because words are the most immediate expression of what is truly inside us. The moment we curse someone made in God’s image, we betray that our blessing of God is hollow, and the fault is not in the tongue—it is in the heart.
