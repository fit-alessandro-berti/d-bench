The key difference is between **behavioral conformity** and **transformed character**.

### 1. External compliance is doing the right things without being changed inside.

External compliance means obeying God’s commands because you are supposed to—by duty, fear, habit, or social pressure. It can look right on the outside, but the inner life remains hostile, cold, or indifferent toward God.

The Israelites had the law, but their hearts were not truly God’s. They could perform rituals and keep rules, yet their inner selves were stubborn, rebellious, and spiritually dead. That is exactly the problem Ezekiel describes:

> “But the house of Israel rebelled against me in the wilderness. They did not follow my decrees… and my Sabbaths they utterly profaned” (Ezekiel 20:13).

Their failure was not merely a lack of effort; it was a fundamental heart problem.

### 2. A “new heart” means the inner person is remade.

In Hebrew, “heart” (*lev*) means far more than emotions. It refers to the **center of the person**—the will, mind, conscience, and deepest desires. To receive a new heart means God changes who you are at the core, so that you now *want* what He wants.

A new heart does not just obey; it loves. It does not just follow rules; it delights in them. The result is not forced compliance but genuine, spontaneous obedience:

> “I will put my Spirit within you, and cause you to walk in my statutes, and you shall keep my judgments and do them” (Ezekiel 36:27).

Notice: God says *I will* cause you to walk. That means the obedience comes from God’s work within, not merely human willpower.

### 3. Why is hardness described almost like a material condition?

Ezekiel describes a **stony heart** versus a **heart of flesh**. This is deliberate, visceral imagery.

- A **stony heart** is hard, dead, unresponsive, and incapable of being changed by external pressure. You cannot soften a stone by painting it or rubbing it. It must be removed.
- A **heart of flesh** is soft, alive, sensitive, and responsive to God. It feels, receives, and can be shaped.

By using this language, God reveals that sin is not just a set of bad choices—it is a **condition of the nature**. It is like a petrified heart that needs to be replaced, not improved.

This is why the text says:

> “I will take away the stony heart out of your flesh, and I will give you a heart of flesh” (Ezekiel 36:26).

The problem is too deep for moral renovation. It requires **divine surgery**—a new creation, not just a fix-up.

### Summary

| External Compliance | New Heart |
|---|---|
| Behavior performed from duty or fear | Obedience flowing from love and desire |
| Human effort | Divine transformation |
| Can appear righteous outwardly | Changes the inner person |
| Heart remains hard | Heart is made soft and alive |
| Law written on stone | Spirit written on the heart |

Ezekiel is describing the gospel promise: God does not merely demand better behavior; He gives a new nature. He replaces the stone with a heart of flesh, puts His Spirit within, and enables His people to walk in His ways. The hardness is a material condition because sin has become part of our very nature—so the cure must be a radical, supernatural rebirth.
