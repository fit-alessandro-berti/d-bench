The passage praises **power that does not need to grasp or display its status**. Christ’s greatness is shown not by making others serve him, but by freely taking the place of a servant and remaining obedient even at great cost. It is power expressed as self-giving love, not control.

Humility joined to greatness can feel more unsettling than open domination because domination is easy to recognize and resist: it says, “I am above you.” This kind of humility overturns the scale altogether. It suggests that having power does not entitle someone to use others—and that greatness may carry an obligation to serve them. It can expose our own wish for rank, even while offering a way beyond it.

The passage praises **chosen humility by one who has power**, not the forced humiliation of people who lack it.
