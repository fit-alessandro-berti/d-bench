The moral posture Paul requires is the posture of a **spiritually mature, self-distrusting restorer**: someone strong enough to confront sin, but gentle enough to heal; someone who speaks truth without standing over the other person as morally superior.

The three ideas are kept together because they correct each other:

- **“Restore”** sets the goal.  
  The person is “overtaken” — caught or entangled in a fault — not simply to be condemned, exposed, or defeated. Restoration means “mend,” like setting a broken bone or repairing a torn net. The aim is to bring the person back to spiritual health, usefulness, and fellowship.

- **“Spirit of meekness”** sets the manner.  
  Meekness is not weakness; it is strength under God’s control. It rules out harshness, sarcasm, public shaming, vengeance, or the pleasure of catching someone. The one who corrects must speak truth in a spirit that reflects the gentleness of Christ.

- **“Considering thyself, lest thou also be tempted”** sets the self-understanding.  
  The corrector must remember that they are not beyond the same danger. This protects against pride, self-righteousness, and hypocrisy. The person correcting should act as a fellow sinner, dependent on grace, who understands how easily one can fall.

So Paul does not encourage moral cowardice. He commands correction — but correction that is neither punitive nor self-inflating. Restoration without meekness becomes harshness. Meekness without restoration becomes mere tolerance of sin. Both without self-suspicion become pride. The “spiritual” person corrects from a heart made humble by awareness of their own need for grace.
