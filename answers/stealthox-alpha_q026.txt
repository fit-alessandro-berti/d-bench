In Matthew 12:34–35, Jesus presents the **heart as a storehouse** and **speech as its overflow**. The “treasure” of the heart is not merely emotion; in biblical terms, the heart includes a person’s desires, loyalties, beliefs, intentions, and will. What a person says, especially over time and under pressure, flows from that hidden inner supply.

So the connection is:

- **Words are symptoms**: they reveal the spiritual condition beneath them, like fruit reveals the nature of a tree.
- **Words are revelations**: they make visible what is otherwise concealed. The Pharisees’ slander against Jesus exposes not just bad speech but a corrupt inward orientation.
- **Words are choices**: Jesus does not treat speech as morally neutral or merely automatic. “A good man… bringeth forth good things” implies active moral production. People are responsible for what they say because speech expresses the will.

The best answer is therefore: **all three**. Words are symptoms of the heart’s condition, revelations of its hidden treasure, and moral choices that both express and reinforce that treasure.

Jesus’ point is not that every sentence gives a perfect picture of a person’s soul, since people can deceive or occasionally say true things from corrupt motives. Rather, the ongoing pattern of speech—what someone habitually blesses, curses, excuses, mocks, defends, or celebrates—reveals the “abundance” within. Good words must come from a heart stocked with goodness; evil words flow from a heart stocked with evil.
