**1.  What the verse actually says**

> “For where your treasure is, there will your heart be also.”  

The clause *“where your treasure is”* points to a **location‑statement**: the heart settles where the greatest value of a person’s life is placed.  In the language of the New Testament, the *kardia* is the centre of will, desire, and affection.  Thus the saying is saying that the heart’s true allegiance is revealed by the kind of treasure we keep “in store.”  

At the same time, the command that follows – *“lay up for yourselves treasures in heaven”* – is an **instruction to act**.  By deliberately storing up a different kind of treasure, we are exercising a discipline that **shapes** the inner life.  In other words, the text works both as a diagnostic (the treasure shows what the heart already loves) and as a formative command (the treasure we choose trains the heart).

So the passage does not force us to pick one of the two readings; it presents a **reciprocal dynamic**:

| Aspect | How it appears in the text |
|--------|----------------------------|
| **Revelation** | The heart’s present orientation is disclosed by where it rests.  If the treasure is earthly, the heart is corrupted by moth, rust, and thieves; if the treasure is heavenly, the heart is protected. |
| **Training** | The act of “laying up” treasure is a purposeful act of possession that molds the heart toward the values of the kingdom (faith, hope, love). |

**2.  Possession ↔ Attention ↔ Love – the three‑way tie**

1. **Possession** – “Lay not up … treasures on earth … but lay up … in heaven.”  
   *To possess* means to claim something as one’s own, to invest identity and security in it.  Whether the possession is money, status, or the “treasure” of Scripture, the act declares what we deem most valuable.

2. **Attention** – What we possess automatically **captures the mind and will**.  The biblical pattern is that “the heart follows the eye” (cf. Matt 6:21 echoing the ancient principle that the eyes guard the gates of the soul).  When we store up earthly riches, our thoughts, prayers, and desires orbit around those riches; when we store up God’s word or the promise of eternal life, our attention is fixed on the kingdom.

3. **Love** – The ultimate object of love is **God** (cf. 1 John 4:8, “God is love”).  The treasure that determines the heart’s location therefore decides **where our love rests**.  If the treasure is material, love becomes possessive, anxious, and transient; if the treasure is heavenly, love becomes generous, patient, and eternal.

**How the three are bound together**

- **Possession → Attention:** By laying up a particular treasure, we repeatedly **look at, think about, and meditate on** it.  The “store‑up” practice is a form of spiritual discipline that trains the mind (cf. 2 Cor 9:13, “the storing of the heart”).
- **Attention → Love:** What we attend to shapes what we love.  When the heart is fixed on the impermanent “moth and rust,” love is drawn toward security and self‑preservation.  When the heart is fixed on the incorruptible “treasure in heaven,” love is redirected toward God’s character and the fellowship of the body of Christ.
- **Love → Possession:** The love that is truly placed in God **re‑orients our possessions**.  The Great Commandment (Matt 22:37‑40) forces us to store up the only true treasure—God and His kingdom—so that all other “possessions” become secondary.

In short, **the treasure we lay up is the engine of the heart**.  It both **reveals** the heart’s hidden priorities and **trains** the heart to align with the kingdom.  The passage calls us to a conscious choice: abandon the corrupting “earthly” storehouses (money, fame, comfort) and invest in the incorruptible “heavenly” storehouse (the truth of God’s word, the presence of Christ, the hope of eternity).  When we do so, our possession becomes a lens that focuses our attention on God, and that focused attention produces a love that mirrors the very treasure we have laid up.  

**Practical takeaway:**  
Examine what you are “laying up” today.  If it is a pile of wealth, it is already training your heart to value security over sacrifice.  If you begin to “lay up” the treasure of Scripture, prayer, and community, you are simultaneously **possessing** something that protects your heart from rust, **directing your attention** away from fleeting anxieties, and **cultivating a love** that is rooted in the eternal rather than the temporal.  The verse thus teaches that the way we handle what we possess determines where our heart will dwell—and that dwelling place is the arena in which love lives.
