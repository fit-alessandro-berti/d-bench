“Cease to do evil” is the necessary first step, but Isaiah does not treat the absence of wrongdoing as enough. “Learn to do well” implies that goodness must be cultivated: people must practice judging fairly, defending the fatherless, and pleading for the widow.

The movement is from **stopping harm to actively seeking justice**. In this passage, goodness is not mere restraint or outward cleanliness; it is love made visible in how people treat those most easily neglected.
