### On Luke 6:37 and the Misread "No Judgments" Mantra

**"Judge not, and ye shall not be judged: condemn not, and ye shall not be condemned: forgive, and ye shall be forgiven."**  
This verse is one of the most frequently quoted–and most frequently misread–lines in Christian teaching. A common modern reduction turns it into: *"Don't ever say anything is right or wrong, or you'll be accused of judging."* That is not what the text says, nor is it the logic Jesus is advancing.

### How It Differs From "Moral Judgments Never Matter"

| **"Judge not" → "Moral judgments don't matter"** | **Luke 6:37 → Righteous discernment + merciless condemnation rejected** |
|---|---|
| **Presupposes moral relativism:** If no judgment matters, then no action is truly right or wrong, and accountability vanishes. It evacuates ethics of binding force. | **Presupposes moral reality:** Jesus assumes people *can* and *should* distinguish truth from error, light from darkness. Elsewhere he commands *"Judge with righteous judgment"* (John 7:24) and warns against false teachers (Matt 7:15–20). |
| **Goal: Comfort without cost.** It wants the appearance of tolerance without the work of truth, repentance, or justice. | **Goal: Heart posture.** It warns against the *manner* of judgment—especially hypocritical, self-righteous, or censorious condemnation—while affirming that right judgment, when done in humility and truth, is expected and good. |
| **Result: Moral anarchy or shallow unity.** Anything goes; no one can be held to a standard; sin is redefined as "preference." | **Result: Restored relationship.** The verse is followed in the same passage by illustrations of blind guides, trees and their fruit, and why people react the way they do. It’s a call to *integrity* in how we evaluate others, not a canceling of evaluation itself. |

In short: The biblical text does not say "don't judge." It says *"don't judge in the way that earns you judgment."* It prohibits a specific *attitude* of condemnation, not the *faculty* of discernment.

### Discernment vs. The Appetite to Condemn

This is the heart of the distinction the verse invites us to explore.

| **Discernment** | **Appetite to Condemn** |
|---|---|
| **Root motive:** Wisdom, truth, and love. It asks *"What is true? What is good? How can this be restored?"* | **Root motive:** Pride, self-righteousness, anger, or the desire to control. It asks *"How can I be proved right and you proved wrong?"* |
| **Orientation:** Forward-looking. Discernment seeks to distinguish error from truth so that people can turn, grow, or be corrected with grace. | **Orientation:** Retroactive/defensive. It seeks to assign fault, fix shame, or elevate oneself by lowering another. |
| **Self-awareness:** Always begins with the self. *"Why do you look at the speck in your brother's eye, and not notice the log in your own?"* (Matt 7:3–5). It holds its own heart under the light first. | **Self-blindness:** Often blind to its own far greater faults. The condemner is quick to see the splinter in others but oblivious to the beam in their own soul. |
| **Fruit:** Leads to restoration, careful guidance, or loving confrontation. *"Brothers, if anyone is caught in any transgression, you who are spiritual should restore him in a spirit of gentleness."* (Gal 6:1) | **Fruit:** Leads to division, shame, spiritual pride, or "spiritual violence." It often enjoys the other's failure (*schadenfreude*) or uses Scripture as a club. |
| **Standard:** Rooted in Scripture, the Spirit, and the character of Christ. It asks *"What does God say? How does love act?"* | **Standard:** Rooted in personal preference, cultural comfort, or the desire to win an argument. It asks *"What makes me look good? What side am I on?"* |
| **Outcome:** Greater clarity, repentance where needed, and safer community. | **Outcome:** Hardened hearts, broken relationships, and a false sense of moral superiority. |

### The Practical Tension

The verse isn't asking us to become moral idiots. It's asking us to **judge ourselves first**, and to **judge others as God judges**: with the intent of redemption, not retaliation.

- If you're pointing out sin to help someone walk in freedom → **discernment**.
- If you're pointing out sin to make yourself feel bigger, safer, or holier → **appetite to condemn**.

Luke 6:37 promises that the spirit you use toward others will ultimately return to you. The "judgment" Jesus worries about isn't the act of recognizing right from wrong; it's the *heart posture* that assumes it has the right to write someone else's eternal verdict—while ignoring its own need for forgiveness.

The verse doesn't mute the moral voice; it redirects it. It says: *Judge with the mercy you'd want to receive, or don't judge at all—because the standard you use will be the standard used on you.*
