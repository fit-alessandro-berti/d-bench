John treats love of the visible neighbor as a test because **love for God is meant to take visible form in how we treat people**. The brother or sister before us has needs we can see and faults we have to reckon with. That is where love becomes more than a feeling or a profession. The next verse makes the connection explicit: whoever loves God must also love his brother or sister.

The self-deception is mistaking a sincere feeling of devotion for a life shaped by love. A person may genuinely feel reverent toward God and yet excuse contempt or indifference toward another person. John calls that person a liar not necessarily because the feeling is feigned, but because the claim “I love God” is contradicted by the hatred they are willing to harbor.

The test is not whether we always feel affection or never get angry. It is whether we imagine that loving God allows us to withhold love from the person in front of us.
