

The text calls for holistic reliance on divine wisdom rather than human self-sufficiency. It requires surrendering the assumption that personal insight alone suffices for navigating life.

Humble self-distrust acknowledges the limits of finite perspective without dismissing reason itself. It admits that human judgment is fallible and incomplete. Conversely, anti-intellectualism rejects the value of inquiry or study altogether. Such rejection would conflict with the instruction to “acknowledge him in all ways,” which implies deliberate thought and engagement.

Thus, the faith described is not a refusal to think, but a choice to prioritize a transcendent viewpoint over purely autonomous reasoning. It merges active participation with surrender, ensuring decisions align with a broader, higher purpose.
