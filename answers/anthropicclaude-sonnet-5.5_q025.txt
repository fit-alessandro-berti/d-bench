## The short answer

The problem is the craving to be seen, but visibility is what makes that craving dangerous.

## What the text itself signals

**The key phrase is purpose, not location.** Jesus says "to be seen of them" (Greek *pros to theathēnai autois*, roughly "in order to be displayed to them"). The word is related to "theater." The warning is against giving as performance. Someone who gives where others happen to see is not the target. Someone who gives so that others will see is.

**The same sermon affirms visible goodness.** Just a chapter earlier: "Let your light so shine before men, that they may see your good works, and glorify your Father which is in heaven" (5:16). Both verses assume good deeds may be seen. The difference is in where the glory is meant to go: in 5:16 it goes to the Father, and in 6:1 it comes back to the giver. So visibility cannot be the core issue, or Jesus would be contradicting himself within a few verses.

## Why visibility is still a real hazard

If the problem is motive, why does Jesus steer people toward secrecy (6:3-4)? Because an audience changes the act:

1. **It changes who the act is for.** Giving is meant to be directed at the needy person and at God. An audience turns it toward the giver's reputation, and the recipient becomes a prop.
2. **It lets you be paid in the wrong currency.** "They have their reward" implies a completed transaction. The giver wanted admiration and got it. Nothing further is owed, because nothing further was sought.
3. **It makes motives harder to see.** Mixed motives are nearly universal, and public settings feed the vain ones. Secrecy works as a kind of experiment: would you still do this if no one knew?
4. **It is "hypocrisy" in the literal sense.** *Hypokritēs* means an actor. The act may be good, but the self presented through it is staged.

## A complication

Secrecy doesn't cure the craving. A person can give anonymously and then enjoy their own hidden virtue, or quietly cultivate a reputation for being someone who gives quietly. "Let not thy left hand know what thy right hand doeth" seems aimed even at self-congratulation, not only at other people's eyes. So the target is an inner orientation, and secrecy is a discipline that helps expose and starve it.

## A practical test

- Would I still do this if no one would ever know?
- Would I be disappointed if someone else got the credit?
- If the visibility is unavoidable or useful (leading a charity, setting an example), is my attention on the need and on God, or on how I'm coming across?

## Summary

Visibility is like a fire near dry grass: not sinful in itself, but it makes the real problem, the hunger for human approval, much more likely to take over. Jesus is less concerned with whether anyone sees than with who the act is ultimately for.
