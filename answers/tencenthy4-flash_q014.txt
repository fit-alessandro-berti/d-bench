Both — but the sentence’s main movement is from **treasure to heart**, not merely from heart to treasure.

Jesus says, “**Where your treasure is, there your heart will be also**.” That means treasure is not a neutral possession. What a person stores up, labors for, protects, and sacrifices for becomes the place where the heart is anchored.

So the text is:

- **Diagnostic**: your treasure reveals what you already value. What you save, spend, give, and pursue discloses your loves.
- **Formative**: your treasure trains the heart. Once your treasure is fixed somewhere, your attention, fears, hopes, and affections are drawn there.

The command itself — “Do not lay up… but lay up…” — assumes formation. If treasure merely revealed the heart, the command would be mostly observational. But Jesus commands a change of treasure because he knows that changing what we treasure changes what we become.

“Heart” here means more than emotion. In biblical language it is the center of the person: mind, will, desire, loyalty, imagination, and affection. So “where your heart is” means: where your whole life is oriented.

That ties possession, attention, and love together like this:

1. **Possession becomes treasure**  
   To “treasure” something is not just to own it; it is to treat it as precious, secure, and worth organizing life around. Earthly treasure is vulnerable — “moth,” “rust,” and “thieves” expose its instability. Heavenly treasure is kept by God and therefore can bear ultimate trust.

2. **Treasure commands attention**  
   Where the treasure is, the eye and mind go. This is why the following verses speak about the “eye,” serving God versus mammon, and anxiety over food and clothing. A person with earthly treasure will think about loss, security, comparison, and accumulation. A person with heavenly treasure seeks first the kingdom.

3. **Attention becomes love**  
   What the heart continually attends to, it learns to love; and what it loves, it seeks to possess and protect. If the treasure is money, status, or earthly security, love is trained into grasping and fear. If the treasure is God and his kingdom, love is trained into trust, generosity, and freedom.

So the relationship is reciprocal:

> The heart chooses treasure; the treasure then trains the heart.  
> What I love determines what I treasure; what I treasure determines what I attend to; what I attend to reshapes what I love.

That is why Jesus can say both, in effect: show me your treasure, and I will see your heart; but also, change your treasure, and your heart will be trained and relocated.
