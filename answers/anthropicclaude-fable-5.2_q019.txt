## The contradiction

James is exposing more than inconsistent behavior. The two acts cancel each other out. To bless God while cursing someone made in his likeness is to honor the original while despising the image. If people bear God's likeness, contempt for them reaches back to the one they resemble. So the cursing calls the blessing into question.

This is the "double-mindedness" James warns about throughout the letter (1:8, 4:8): a divided heart that wants devotion to God without that devotion governing how we treat people. The same point appears in 1 John 4:20, where no one can love the God he has not seen while hating the brother he has seen.

James follows with images from nature (3:11–12). A spring does not pour out both fresh and bitter water, and a fig tree does not bear olives. Nature is consistent with its source. If the mouth produces both blessing and cursing, the real problem is upstream, in the heart.

## Why speech reveals it so quickly

- **Speech is the shortest path from heart to world.** Deeds take planning and effort, which leaves time to manage appearances. Words come out before we can filter them. As Jesus said, "Out of the abundance of the heart the mouth speaketh" (Matt. 12:34).

- **Speech is constant.** We speak all day, in every mood, so the tongue eventually reports what is actually inside us. This is why James says that whoever does not stumble in word is "a perfect man" (3:2).

- **The tongue serves both great loves.** We use the same instrument to worship God and to address our neighbor. If love of God and love of neighbor have come apart inside us, the split shows there first.

- **Words show how we actually value people.** A curse is a verdict on someone's worth. It reveals whether we see others as image-bearers or as obstacles.

## The tone of the rebuke

James says "we," so he includes himself. And he says "these things ought not so to be," which treats the split as unnatural, not inevitable. The remedy he implies is a heart made whole, since a guarded tongue alone would leave the source unchanged. A changed source then shows in changed speech.
