Matthew 12:34-35 is built on a domestic economic metaphor. The heart is not here a seat of feeling but the *kardia*, the inner center of a person, the will-intellect-conscience. It is a *thesauros*, a storehouse or treasury where moral capital is laid up over time. The mouth is the outlet of that storehouse.

*Out of the abundance of the heart the mouth speaketh.* Greek *ek tēs perisseias tēs kardias*. *Perisseia* is overflow, surplus, that which is more than can be contained. Speech is not primarily a calculated performance but a hydraulic overflow. What is full inside must come out. That is why Jesus can ask rhetorically, "how can ye, being evil, speak good things?" An evil stockpile cannot produce good speech any more than a bad tree can bear good fruit, Matt 7:18.

A good man *out of the good treasure of the heart bringeth forth good things: and an evil man out of the evil treasure bringeth forth evil things.* The treasure determines the produce. The connection is therefore:

**Speech as symptom.** The word *phthanei* is used for bringing forth, as of fruit. The speech is the natural, almost involuntary fruit of the inner condition. It is the diagnostic sign of what is already stored. That is why Proverbs, which Jesus echoes, says "the mouth of the righteous is a fountain of life" Prov 10:11, and "A man's belly shall be satisfied with the fruit of his mouth" Prov 18:20. The symptom is reliable because it is not put on.

**Speech as revelation.** The overflow reveals to others what is hidden. The heart is by definition interior and hidden; the mouth makes it public. Jesus is using that revelatory logic against the Pharisees: your words of accusation betray the evil in the storehouse, just as in Matt 15:18-19, "out of the heart come forth evil thoughts... which defile the man". Speech is a self-disclosure.

**Speech as the consequence of choice.** The metaphor of a treasure implies accumulation. A treasury is not given once and for all; it is filled by repeated deposit. To have a good treasure is to have chosen, over time, what to store there. That is the moral responsibility behind the symptom/revelation. The passage does not make speech merely mechanical, it makes the heart the locus of moral formation. Guard the heart because "out of it are the issues of life" Prov 4:23; Matt 15:18.

So the passage presents words as first and foremost revelatory symptoms of a settled character. Because that character is itself the product of prior moral choices about what treasure to lay up, speech is ultimately an expression of choice, even if in the moment it feels spontaneous. You cannot speak good from an evil stockpile, and you cannot hide an evil stockpile from speech for long.
