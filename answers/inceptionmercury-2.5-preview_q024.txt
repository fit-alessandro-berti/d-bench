

The passage distinguishes between outward behavior and inward transformation. External compliance involves following statutes out of obligation or habit, without a change in motivation. Receiving a "new heart" signifies a fundamental shift in nature and desire. According to the text, true obedience results from this internal renewal, where God’s spirit enables the individual to willingly follow divine laws.

Hardness is described as a material condition—a "stony heart"—to illustrate rigidity and insensitivity. Unlike stone, which cannot feel or respond, flesh is living, pliable, and receptive. Depicting the old nature as something to be physically removed emphasizes that the previous state was incompatible with divine will. This metaphor suggests that spiritual renewal requires more than minor adjustments; it demands a complete replacement of the core being to allow for genuine responsiveness.
