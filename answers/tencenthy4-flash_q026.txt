Matthew 12:34–35 presents the heart as a kind of hidden storehouse and the mouth as its outlet:

- **“Out of the abundance of the heart the mouth speaketh”** — speech is an overflow. What is stored up inwardly eventually spills outwardly.
- **“The good treasure of the heart” / “the evil treasure”** — the heart holds accumulated moral and spiritual “stock”: loves, desires, loyalties, resentments, beliefs, virtues, vices.
- **“Bringeth forth”** — what is hidden is produced into visible form through words.

In context, Jesus is responding to the Pharisees’ accusation that he casts out demons by Beelzebul. Their slander is not treated as an isolated verbal mistake; it is “fruit” revealing the condition of the heart.

So the best answer is: **all three, but in different respects.**

1. **Words are symptoms** — They indicate the condition of the inner life. Speech is not random; it flows from what is abundant within.

2. **Words are revelations** — They disclose what is hidden. Just as fruit reveals the tree, speech reveals the moral character of the heart.

3. **Words are choices** — Jesus still treats speaking as something humans do and are accountable for. The passage uses active language: people “speak” and “bring forth.” Speech is not mechanical; it is a moral action.

The passage does not mean that an evil person can never say anything true, or that a good person never speaks wrongly. Rather, it teaches that **character shapes characteristic speech**. The heart is the deep source; words are the visible overflow; and because words are chosen, they both reveal and reinforce what is being stored in the heart.
