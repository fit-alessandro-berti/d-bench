The required posture is **humble, compassionate, and genuinely intent on repair—not on proving oneself superior**. Paul addresses those who are “spiritual,” meaning people led by the Spirit, whose fruit includes gentleness (Gal. 5:22–23). Their task is to help a person caught in wrongdoing return to a sound path, not to shame or discard them.

The three ideas belong together:

- **Restoration** names the goal: the person’s recovery, not merely the exposure or punishment of the fault.
- **Meekness** shapes the manner: correction should be gentle and patient, not self-righteous or harsh.
- **Self-suspicion** checks the corrector: remember that you, too, can be tempted—by the same wrongdoing, or by pride, contempt, and overconfidence in the act of correcting.

This self-awareness does not mean pretending the fault is harmless or refusing to intervene. It keeps intervention honest and merciful. As the next verse says, believers are to “bear one another’s burdens” (Gal. 6:2): correction is an act of shared responsibility, not superiority.
