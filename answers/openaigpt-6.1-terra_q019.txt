The contradiction is **claiming to honor God while dishonoring people made in God’s likeness**. James treats blessing God and cursing others not as unrelated acts, but as evidence of a divided moral life: our reverence for the Creator is inconsistent if it coexists with contempt for his creatures.

Speech reveals that disorder quickly because words often give immediate expression to what we harbor—anger, pride, resentment, or a desire to diminish someone. The same mouth can move from prayer to contempt in moments, exposing the gap between professed devotion and practiced love.

James is not saying that every criticism of another person is wrong. His concern is speech that curses or degrades people whose dignity rests in their being made in God’s image.
