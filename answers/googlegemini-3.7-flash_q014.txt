In Matthew 6:19–21, Jesus presents a profound psychology of human desire and spiritual economics. To answer your questions:

---

### 1. Does the text say treasure *reveals* the heart, or that it *trains* the heart?

**Grammatically and functionally, the text emphasizes that treasure *trains (and directs)* the heart, though in practice it does both.**

* **The Direction of the Text:** Notice the syntax of verse 21: *“Where your treasure is, there will your heart be also.”* 
  * Modern intuition often reverses this to say: *"Where your heart is, there you put your treasure"* (Heart $\rightarrow$ Treasure = Revelation). 
  * Jesus states the opposite trajectory: **Treasure $\rightarrow$ Heart**. The placement of the treasure acts as a gravitational pull or an anchor; the heart (*will, affection, allegiance*) inevitably migrates to wherever the investment has been made.
* **An Actionable Command:** Because the passage begins with an imperative (*“Lay up for yourselves…”*), Jesus treats treasure as an active instrument of spiritual direction. You cannot simply command your heart to love God by sheer willpower; however, you *can* choose where to direct your resources, time, and physical wealth. By intentionally sending your treasure ahead into the heavenly realm, you pull your affections toward God.

**Conclusion on this point:** While where you spend your resources certainly *reveals* what you have cared about in the past, Jesus’s primary point here is *formative*. **Treasure is a rudder that steers the heart.**

---

### 2. How Possession, Attention, and Love Become Tied Together

In biblical theology, the "heart" (*kardia*) is not merely the seat of raw emotion, but the central organizing hub of the human person—governing will, intellect, desires, and ultimate loyalty. This passage weaves possession, attention, and love together through a dynamic cycle:

#### A. Possession (What You Value and Guard)
* **Earthly treasure** is inherently fragile—subject to decay from within (*“moth and rust”*) and danger from without (*“thieves”*). 
* When you possess something corruptible, your security becomes tied to its preservation. Your possessions demand management, defense, and anxiety.

#### B. Attention (Where Your Mind and Vigilance Are Held)
* Because earthly possessions are vulnerable, they command constant **attention**. 
* If your treasure is in a bank, a reputation, or physical property, your mental bandwidth is consumed with market fluctuations, security systems, and preventative maintenance. 
* Conversely, heavenly treasure—acts of mercy, devotion to God, eternal investments—requires no defensive anxiety because it cannot be stolen or corroded. Your attention is liberated to rest in God’s sovereignty.

#### C. Love / Allegiance (What Possesses You)
* Prolonged attention naturally matures into **love and allegiance**. 
* Whatever holds your investments and captures your daily vigilance ultimately secures your deepest loyalty. This is why just a few verses later (v. 24), Jesus explicitly states: *“No man can serve two masters… Ye cannot serve God and mammon.”* 

---

### Summary Diagram of the Dynamic:

$$\text{Possession (Investment)} \longrightarrow \text{Attention (Vigilance/Focus)} \longrightarrow \text{Heart (Love/Loyalty)}$$

By commanding his followers to change the *location* of their treasure, Jesus is offering both a **diagnostic warning** (your investments are pulling you toward either decay or eternity) and a **transformative discipline** (if you want to love the Kingdom of God, invest heavily in it, and your heart will naturally follow).
