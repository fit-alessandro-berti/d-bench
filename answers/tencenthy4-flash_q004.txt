The trust asked for in Proverbs 3:5–6 is **wholehearted reliance on God’s wisdom, goodness, and overruling care**, joined with **humble submission of one’s own limited judgment**. It is not “turn off your brain and follow a vague feeling.” It is: **use your mind, but do not enthrone your mind.**

“Trust in the Lord with all thine heart” means more than intellectual agreement that God exists. In biblical language, “heart” includes the whole inner person—affections, will, desires, thoughts, loyalties. So the verse calls for personal reliance: “Lord, I belong to You; I rest in Your wisdom; I want my choices to be under Your rule.”

“Lean not unto thine own understanding” does not mean that understanding is worthless. Proverbs itself repeatedly praises wisdom, knowledge, instruction, and discernment. The Bible encourages learning, careful thought, seeking counsel, and testing what is true. The problem is not thinking; the problem is **leaning**—treating one’s own reasoning as final, sufficient, and authoritative.

Humble self-distrust says:

> “My reasoning is a gift from God, but it is finite, fallen, biased, and incomplete. I will use it, but I will not worship it.”

That is very different from anti-intellectualism.

Anti-intellectualism says:

> “Don’t study. Don’t think. Don’t examine evidence. Just trust your instinct.”

That is not what Proverbs teaches. In fact, anti-intellectualism can become another form of self-trust: trusting one’s gut, mood, suspicion, or “feeling” rather than submitting all of life to God. Biblical faith is not irrational; it is **God-centered rationality**. It believes that God is the source of truth and that wisdom is to be pursued humbly.

“In all thy ways acknowledge him” means that God is to be recognized in every area of life—not merely in religious moments. That includes decisions about work, money, relationships, health, speech, ambition, and suffering. Practically, this means praying, seeking God’s word, asking for wisdom, examining motives, listening to wise counsel, and being willing to be corrected.

So the passage is not saying, “Ignore facts.” Nor is it saying, “Never plan.” Rather, it says:

> “Gather facts. Think carefully. Make plans. Seek counsel. But do all of it under God, holding your conclusions loosely and trusting Him to direct you.”

“And he shall direct thy paths” does not mean God will always give an easy path or immediately explain everything. It means that the person who walks in humble submission will be guided by God according to His wisdom—not merely left to the shifting judgment of self.

In short:

- **Anti-intellectualism** rejects thought and learning.
- **Humble self-distrust** rejects self-rule.
- **Biblical trust** uses every God-given faculty while bowing every faculty to God.

The kind of trust being asked for is not blind ignorance but **submissive confidence**: confidence in God’s character and wisdom, and submission of one’s own understanding to Him.
