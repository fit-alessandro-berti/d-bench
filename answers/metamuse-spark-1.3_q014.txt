It says the second — and that's what makes it so striking.

We tend to read it backwards, as if Jesus said: *"Where your heart is, there your treasure will follow."* That is true: you spend on what you love.

But Jesus says it the other way around:

> *For where your treasure is, there will your heart be also.*

Your heart follows your treasure. Treasure doesn't just **reveal** the heart, it **trains** the heart.

That's why verse 21 is given as the reason — "for" — for the two commands before it: don't stockpile on earth, do stockpile in heaven. Choose your storehouse wisely *because* your inner life will relocate to wherever you put your valuables.

### How possession, attention, and love get tied

1. **Possession demands attention.** In Jesus' world "treasure on earth" was vividly insecure: fine garments that *moth* eats, grain and metals that *brosis* — eating, rust, decay — consumes, houses of mud-brick that *thieves dig through* and steal. To own such things is to worry about them: to insure, guard, check, maintain, fret over the market or the lock on the door.

Matthew makes that link explicit in the next verses: "The eye is the lamp of the body" [6:22-23]. What you treasure captures your gaze. You keep looking at it.

2. **Sustained attention becomes love.** In Scripture *kardia*, heart, is not just emotion. It's the center of will, thought, allegiance, worship — the steering wheel of the person.

What you constantly think about, check on, protect, and plan around, you come to love and serve. Psychologically and spiritually, investment creates attachment. A stock, a house, a closet, a bank account starts as a tool and ends as a master. That's why this ends in 6:24: "You cannot serve God and mammon."

3. **Love then justifies further possession.** It becomes a loop. That is exactly why earthly treasure is spiritually dangerous — not just because moths and thieves can take it, but because anxiety and devotion get tethered to something that will rot.

Heavenly treasure works by the same mechanism, in reverse. Jesus has just been talking about secret almsgiving, prayer, fasting [6:1-18]. To give, forgive, obey, bless when no one repays — that is "laying up treasure in heaven." It's secure, incorruptible, so it doesn't breed anxiety. And because you have placed something precious there, your attention and affection begin to *migrate* there. Colossians says it the same way: "Set your minds on things above" [Col 3:2].

In short: Don't wait to feel your heart move toward God so that you will then invest. Invest in the kingdom, and your heart will follow the investment.

That's why this text is both diagnostic *and* formative. Look at your bank statement, calendar, and worry-list to see where your heart already is. Then move your treasure on purpose to retrain where your heart will be.
