James is exposing a divided, double-minded heart masquerading as devotion.

The tongue is made for one use in worship: *to bless God, even the Father.* Blessing God is to invoke His name, to acknowledge His holiness, goodness and lordship, to praise the Creator. That is the liturgical, reverent use of the mouth.

And the same tongue, in the same day, with the same breath, is used to *curse men which are made after the similitude of God.* To curse is not merely to swear, it is to invoke evil, judgment and contempt on another. And the other is not an abstract creature but an image-bearer. To curse a man made in the image of God is functionally to curse what is made after God’s likeness.

So the contradiction is theological and moral at once: you cannot reverently praise the Father while contemptuously demeaning His image. You are blessing the source and cursing the reflection. To honour God you must honour what bears His image, for in the OT the one who curses God’s people curses God. James’ point is that the worship is hollow. It is a compartmentalised piety: sacred speech in church, profane speech in the marketplace. The mouth is saying “Father” while the heart is saying “I am above this person.” That is double-mindedness, James 1:8, trying to serve God and the spirit of the world.

Speech reveals moral disorder quickly because speech is the unguarded overflow of the heart.

* Speech is constant and involuntary. We act once; we speak hundreds of times a day. The tongue has no filter that we can sustain.
* Speech is the outward index of the inner disposition. Matt 12:34, “out of the abundance of the heart the mouth speaketh.” You do not have to be caught in a large sin to be exposed; the first angry word, the first slander, the first prayer and the first curse coming from the same mouth betrays a fractured character.
* The tongue is small but it sets the whole course of life. James 3:5-6: it is a little member that can set a great forest ablaze. It cannot be tamed by willpower alone; it shows where the real lordship lies. Fresh water and bitter water cannot come from the same spring, v.11-12.

In short, you cannot bless the Father and curse His image with integrity. The same mouth doing both proves the heart has not been made whole by the wisdom from above. That is why James says *these things ought not so to be.*
