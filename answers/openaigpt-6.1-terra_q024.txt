**External compliance** is doing what is required because of pressure, habit, fear of consequences, or a desire to appear righteous. The action may be right, but the person’s desires and loyalties may remain unchanged.

Receiving a **“new heart”** means that God changes the inner person—the will, desires, and capacity to respond to him. In the passage, God also promises to put his Spirit within his people, so that obedience grows from an inwardly renewed relationship rather than merely from an external rule. The contrast is not between behavior and feeling: the promised renewal leads to actual obedience.

The image of **stone and flesh** makes the problem vivid. Stone is hard and unresponsive; living flesh is sensitive and responsive. The metaphor suggests that Israel’s hardness is not merely a shortage of instruction, as though another command alone would fix it. Something deeply entrenched must be transformed. God promises not simply to tell his people what to do, but to give them a heart that can respond.

In Ezekiel’s context, this promise is addressed to Israel in exile: God will restore his people and renew them so they can live faithfully in covenant with him.
