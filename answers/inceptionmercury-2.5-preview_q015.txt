Contrasting a tiny speck with a massive log creates an absurd visual that highlights the irrationality of judging others while ignoring one’s own glaring flaws. The physical impossibility of performing delicate surgery with a plank obstructing one’s vision underscores the futility of moral criticism without self-awareness.

Regarding hypocrisy, the text condemns the pretense of righteousness when one remains flawed. However, it does not ban correction outright. Instead, it sets a prerequisite: self-examination must precede intervention. Once the self-imposed blindness is removed, the observer gains the clarity needed to help others responsibly. Thus, the passage prioritizes humility over prohibition, allowing for righteous aid after personal rectification.
