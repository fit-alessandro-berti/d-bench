In Galatians 6:1, Paul is not describing *if* correction should happen, but *how*. The three elements you name are inseparable because each one guards the others from distortion.

> "if a man be overtaken in a fault [paraptoma - a slip, a misstep, not a calculated rebellion], ye which are spiritual, restore such an one in the spirit of meekness; considering thyself, lest thou also be tempted."

### 1. The posture required

**a) "Ye which are spiritual" - Qualified, not entitled.**

Paul does not mean an elite class of super-Christians. In Galatians 5, "spiritual" means led by the Spirit and bearing the *fruit* of the Spirit - love, joy, peace, longsuffering, gentleness, goodness, faith, *meekness* (5:22-23).

The qualification to correct is not moral perfection, but proven meekness and dependence on the Spirit. If you are not walking in that, you are disqualified from the work.

**b) Restoration as the telos - *katartizo***

The verb translated "restore" is *katartizein*. It is used for:

1. mending fishing nets (Matt. 4:21)
2. setting a dislocated bone or joint in medicine

It does not mean to punish, to expose, to make an example of, or to win an argument. It means to bring back to wholeness and usefulness within the body.

That sets the moral posture: the person is not a problem to be removed but a *member* to be repaired. Correction is successful only if the brother is made whole again. Anything that wounds further has failed, even if it is theologically correct.

**c) Meekness as the manner - *en pneumati prautetos***

Meekness (*prautes*) in the New Testament is not timidity, niceness, or softness. It is power under control - strength that refuses to crush.

It is required here for two reasons:

1. The person has been "overtaken." He is caught by the sin more than he chose it. Harshness will break, not mend.
2. Without meekness, correction becomes domination. The gentle manner proves the motive is love, not superiority.

**d) Self-suspicion as the safeguard - *skopon seauton***

"Considering thyself, watch yourself attentively."

Paul adds immediate self-surveillance. Why? "Lest thou also be tempted" can mean two temptations:

1. Tempted to the *same* fault - No one is immune. 1 Cor. 10:12, "let him that thinketh he standeth take heed lest he fall."
2. Tempted to pride, harshness, and a censorious spirit in the act of correcting itself - which is often the greater danger.

Self-suspicion keeps the corrector from a position of judge over the fallen, to a position of fellow-sinner beside him: "There, but for grace, go I."

### 2. Why they must be kept together

Paul binds them together because separated, each becomes a vice:

**1. Restoration without meekness becomes surgery with a hammer.**
Truth without gentleness may be accurate, but it re-wounds. It uses restoration as a pretext for scolding. Paul says the *spirit* in which you restore determines whether you actually restore.

**2. Meekness without restoration becomes permissiveness.**
A refusal to confront in the name of "being nice" leaves the dislocated joint out of socket. That is not kindness. True meekness still does the painful work of setting the bone, but gently. See the next verse: "Bear ye one another's burdens" (6:2) - restoration is burden-bearing, not burden-ignoring.

**3. Both without self-suspicion become self-righteousness.**
Without considering yourself, you forget your own vulnerability. Correction then breeds pride in the corrector, creating two problems instead of healing one. The Pharisee thanks God he is not like other men; the spiritual man fears he could be.

In short:

* **Restoration** gives correction its *aim* - wholeness, not victory.
* **Meekness** gives correction its *tone* - gentleness, not severity.
* **Self-suspicion** gives correction its *humility* - fellowship, not superiority.

Take any one away and the act of correction ceases to be Christian. It becomes either cruel, or cowardly, or proud.

That is why Paul can call this whole action the fulfillment of "the law of Christ" (6:2) - which is love that stoops to bear what it did not cause.
