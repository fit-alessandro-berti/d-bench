**The problem is not being seen; it is wanting to be seen as the purpose of the act.** Visibility is the occasion for the danger, but the disordered desire for recognition is what makes the goodness morally dangerous.

Jesus assumes that almsgiving is good and that the Father rewards goodness. His concern is what the giver is *actually doing*. A person can perform the same act—giving money to someone in need—with very different moral orientations:

- **“I give because God has given to me, and I love him and his people.”**
- **“I give so that everyone will admire my generosity and think well of me.”**

The second act can still benefit the recipient, but it becomes a performance of goodness as well as a good deed. The giver’s ultimate concern has shifted from God and the person in need to their own reputation.

The phrase **“to be seen of them”** identifies that wrong intention. The problem is not simply knowing that others are watching, or even experiencing some pleasure when they approve. It is making human approval the act’s goal—using charity to establish moral status, secure praise, or reinforce a self-image of being righteous.

This also explains the warning about reward. Jesus is not saying that public charity is inherently worthless. Rather, he is exposing a fundamental inconsistency: a person cannot treat human applause as their reward while also expecting God to reward them as an act of love and obedience to him. The giver may receive the praise they sought, but they have not oriented the act toward the Father “which is in heaven.”

**Practical implication:** Jesus encourages hidden goodness when it is possible, not because visibility is sinful, but because privacy can expose and correct a disordered motive. Yet public generosity is sometimes necessary—for example, when charitable action itself bears witness, expresses communal responsibility, or confronts injustice.

The clearest contrast is:

> **Public goodness is safe when, even if no one were watching, love of God and neighbour would remain its reason. It is dangerous when being watched has become the reason.**
