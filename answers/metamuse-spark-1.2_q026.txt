In Matthew 12:34-35, Jesus makes speech the *overflow and display case* for what is stored invisibly inside a person. The two metaphors he uses answer your question directly.

> "for **out of the abundance** *[ek tou perisseumatos]* **of the heart the mouth speaketh.**
> A good man **out of the good treasure** *[ek tou agathou thesaurou]* **of the heart bringeth forth** *[ekballei]* **good things: and an evil man out of the evil treasure bringeth forth evil things."**

### 1. The Connection: Heart as Storehouse

In biblical anthropology, the *heart* - *kardia* - is not just emotion, but the inner center of the person: mind, will, affections, and moral character.

Jesus uses two images to connect that hidden center to speech:

**a) Abundance / Overflow - *perisseuma*:** What fills the heart to overflowing inevitably spills out the mouth. Speech is like water rising over the rim of a vessel. It suggests pressure and plenitude - you cannot contain indefinitely what you are full of.

**b) Treasure / Storehouse - *thesauros*:** In the ancient world, a *thesauros* was a chest, vault, or storeroom where valuables were accumulated over a long time, coin by coin. Jesus is saying the heart is a vault. Every thought entertained, desire cultivated, truth or lie accepted, resentment nursed, act of mercy or cruelty - is a deposit. Over time those deposits become a stockpile, a character.

Speech, then, is not a random, isolated act. It is a withdrawal from that accumulated stockpile.

This is why Jesus asks the Pharisees in verse 34:

> "O generation of vipers, how can ye, *being evil*, speak good things?"

He is not just insulting them. He is making a logical point about moral causality. The Pharisees had just attributed his exorcisms to Beelzebub, 12:24. Their slander was not a slip of the tongue. A consistently evil heart cannot produce consistently good speech, any more than a poisoned spring can produce clean water. To speak truly good things requires a transformed source.

Luke's parallel makes it explicit: *"For every tree is known by his own fruit."* Luke 6:44-45

### 2. Are Words Symptoms, Choices, or Revelations? All Three.

The passage holds all three together without contradiction:

#### 1. Words as SYMPTOMS - Involuntary Diagnosis
Speech is *symptomatic* in a medical sense. A doctor does not cause the disease by observing the fever; the fever reveals the disease.

Jesus presents speech as an almost involuntary symptom. You do not have to instruct the mouth to speak what is in the abundance of the heart - it just *will*. Habitual speech patterns - cynicism, boasting, flattery, complaining, encouragement, truth-telling, slander, grace - are diagnostic. They tell what condition the heart has been in long before the words were spoken.

Proverbs says the same: *"Keep thy heart with all diligence; for out of it are the issues of life."* Proverbs 4:23

#### 2. Words as REVELATIONS - The Treasure Put on Display
A symptom is private, but a revelation is public. The second metaphor, *thesauros*, moves beyond symptom to *display*.

A treasure is hidden until the owner opens the chest and *bringeth forth* - *ekballei* - its contents. The verb is active and deliberate: to cast out, to exhibit, to put on the table.

Words make the invisible visible. What was hidden in the dark storeroom of the heart is brought into the light where others can see and evaluate it. This is why hypocrisy is ultimately impossible to sustain. You can perform goodness for a time, but the vault will eventually be opened by your own mouth.

Speech does not *create* the treasure; it *reveals* it.

#### 3. Words as CHOICES - Moral Acts for Which We Are Accountable
If words were *only* symptoms and revelations, we would not be morally responsible for them - they would be mere reflexes. Jesus closes the loophole in the very next verses:

> "But I say unto you, That every idle word that men shall speak, they shall give account thereof in the day of judgment. For by thy words thou shalt be justified, and by thy words thou shalt be condemned." Matthew 12:36-37

To *bring forth* is a choice. The deposits in the treasure were themselves accumulated by a long series of choices - what we have dwelt on, permitted, justified, and rehearsed. And the withdrawal - the speaking - is itself a new moral choice.

We are not judged for having a fever, but we are judged for what we *do* with the contents of our heart when we speak. Words are acts of the will. They wound or heal, they bear false witness or tell truth, they bless or curse. James 3 calls the tongue a small fire that can set a whole forest ablaze for this reason.

### Synthesis: A Cycle

The three work as a cycle:

**Choices -> Become Stockpile -> Becomes Character -> Overflows as Symptoms -> Reveals the Stockpile -> Is Judged as a New Choice**

1.  We **choose** what we store - bitterness or forgiveness, envy or gratitude, lust or purity, truth or deceit.
2.  That storage **accumulates** into a treasure, a settled character - *being evil* or *being good*.
3.  That character inevitably **symptomatically overflows** in speech, often when our guard is down - in anger, stress, or casual talk.
4.  That overflow **reveals** to ourselves and others what was hidden.
5.  And that revelation is itself a **chosen act** for which God holds us accountable, because speech does real moral work in the world.

In short: Jesus does not say "Watch your words and your heart will become good." He reverses it: "Make the tree good, and its fruit will be good." Matthew 12:33. Lasting change in speech requires a transformation of the treasure itself - which, in Matthew's theology, is the work of repentance and new birth, not mere behavior modification.
