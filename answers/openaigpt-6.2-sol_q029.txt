Paul calls for **correction without moral superiority**. The “spiritual” person is not someone who stands above temptation, but someone whose way of correcting another shows the Spirit’s fruit—especially gentleness (Galatians 5:22–23).

- **Restoration names the goal.** The point is not to shame the person or prove who is right, but to help them turn from the wrong and return to faithful life in the community.
- **Meekness names the manner.** Gentleness does not mean pretending the fault is harmless. It means telling the truth without humiliating the person.
- **Self-watchfulness names the safeguard.** “Considering thyself” means remembering that the other person’s vulnerability is also yours. You may be susceptible to the same fault, or to pride and harshness in the very act of correcting it.

The three belong together because the way you correct someone can either serve or defeat the purpose of correction. Paul’s posture is neither “It doesn’t matter what you did” nor “I would never do what you did,” but “This matters, and I want to help you put it right, knowing that I too can stumble.”
