In Matthew 6:1, the moral danger of public goodness is not **visibility itself**, but the **craving to be seen** (often called "righteousness for show"). The verse exposes a heart-level motive: doing good primarily to be observed and praised by others, rather than to honor God or genuinely serve people.

Here’s why visibility alone isn't the core problem:

- **Goodness can be genuinely good even when public.** Many forms of virtue (charity, justice, hospitality, teaching, service) are meant to be seen and acknowledged. The early church was told to let their light shine before others (Matthew 5:16), and the New Testament itself commends public acts of love and discipleship that others can witness.
- **The danger lies in the * motive*.** The Greek phrase behind "to be seen of them" points to a self-referential desire: the person is acting to gain human approval, to feed vanity, or to appear virtuous. In that case, the good deed becomes a tool for self-glory rather than an expression of love for God and neighbor. That's why the verse warns that such a person "has no reward from your Father in heaven"—the reward belongs to God, not to the crowd, and the crowd's applause temporarily fills a spiritual void but cannot satisfy the heart's true purpose.
- **Visibility is neutral; the craving is not.** A humility that refuses to do good in public, or a public act done only for secrecy, can also mask fear, pride, or a desire to be "seen" as secretly good. The biblical issue is always the heart's orientation: *Is this done for God's glory and neighbor's good, or for my own recognition?* When the craving to be seen dominates, even noble actions become morally distorted because they are no longer offered in trust to God.

In short: **the problem is not that the good is seen; it's that the person *craves* the seeing.** Public goodness becomes spiritually dangerous when it shifts its audience from God to human spectators, turning charity into prestige and faith into performance. The remedy isn't necessarily to hide goodness, but to anchor it in a heart that serves God first and trusts Him to reward the hidden intentions that do not seek the spotlight.
