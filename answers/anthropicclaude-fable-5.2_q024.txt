**External compliance versus a new heart**

External compliance is behavior shaped by pressure from outside, such as fear of consequences, social expectation, or habit. The command stays foreign to the person, something pressing against the will rather than flowing from it. It lasts only as long as the pressure does, and it can coexist with resentment or indifference.

In Hebrew thought, the heart (*lev*) is not mainly the seat of emotion. It is the center of will, thought, and desire, the place where a person decides. So a new heart means a change in the wanting itself, not just better behavior.

The goal in verse 27 is still obedience: "ye shall keep my judgments, and do them." The statutes are not discarded. What changes is the source of obedience. The law moves from outside the person to inside, an idea paralleled in Jeremiah 31:33, where the law is written on the heart.

This answers Israel's history as Ezekiel saw it. The people had the law, the temple, the prophets, and the covenant, and still ended in exile. Ezekiel's diagnosis is that more instruction and stronger incentives would not help, because the faculty that responds to God was itself the problem.

**Why hardness is described as a material condition**

- **Stone is unresponsive, not merely disobedient.** You cannot persuade a stone. The image suggests incapacity: a will so settled in resistance that it can no longer feel or respond. Flesh, by contrast, is living and can be moved.

- **The remedy matches the diagnosis.** If the problem were ignorance, teaching would suffice. If it were weakness, encouragement would. A stone heart cannot be reformed, only replaced, so the language is surgical rather than educational.

- **God is the subject of every verb.** "I will give... I will put... I will take away... I will cause." A stone heart cannot soften itself. Earlier, Ezekiel commands the people to "make you a new heart" (18:31). Here, what was commanded is given.

- **Stone has echoes elsewhere.** The law was written on tablets of stone, and Israel's idols were made of stone. The Psalms say that those who worship idols become like them (Psalm 115:8). Paul later draws the contrast directly: "not in tables of stone, but in fleshy tables of the heart" (2 Corinthians 3:3).

**Agency restored**

The passage ends with human action: "*ye* shall keep my judgments." The divine work does not replace human agency but restores it. A heart of flesh is not a programmed heart. It is one finally capable of responding.
