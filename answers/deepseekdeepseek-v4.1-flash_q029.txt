Galatians 6:1 calls for a **gospel-shaped, cruciform posture** in correction: humble, gentle, restorative, and self-aware. It is not the posture of a judge, prosecutor, or superior, but of a physician and fellow patient.

The key elements:

- **“Ye which are spiritual”** — correction is to be done by those led by the Spirit, not merely by the self-confident or morally elite. In Galatians, the Spirit’s fruit includes love, gentleness, and self-control (Gal. 5:22–23), and the flesh produces rivalry, anger, and conceit (5:19–21, 26).
- **“Restore such an one”** — the Greek word *katartizete* means to mend, set a broken bone, repair nets, or equip. The goal is not punishment but healing, reconciliation, and reintegration into the community.
- **“In the spirit of meekness”** — *praotēs* is not weakness; it is strength under control, gentleness, humility, and patient non-combative love. It is the meekness of Christ (Matt. 11:29).
- **“Considering thyself, lest thou also be tempted”** — this is holy self-suspicion. It means, “I am not above this sin. I too am vulnerable. I correct only by grace.” It is not moral paralysis or endless guilt; it is sober vigilance.

Why are restoration, meekness, and self-suspicion kept together?

Because they guard correction from becoming deformed:

- **Restoration without meekness** becomes harsh, punitive, and destructive. It breaks the bruised reed instead of healing it.
- **Meekness without self-suspicion** becomes condescending or fake. It can mask superiority: “I’m gently helping you poor sinners.”
- **Self-suspicion without restoration** becomes passive tolerance or endless introspection. It never actually addresses sin.
- **Restoration without self-suspicion** becomes self-righteous surgery: removing the speck while ignoring the beam.

Together they create a **safe, redemptive, Christlike correction**: truth without cruelty, holiness without pride, accountability without condemnation. The corrector must remember that they too could fall, that the goal is the brother’s restoration, and that only the Spirit’s gentleness can heal. This is the law of Christ: bearing one another’s burdens in love (Gal. 6:2).

So the required moral posture is not “I am better than you,” but **“There but for the grace of God go I; let me help you back to wholeness with the same gentleness I myself need.”**
