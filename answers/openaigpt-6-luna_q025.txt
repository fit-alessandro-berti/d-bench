The danger is not simply that people might see a good deed. It is that being seen becomes the reason for doing it: the deed is turned into a performance, and human praise becomes its reward.

That is why the verse says “to be seen of them.” Visibility can tempt or expose a self-seeking motive, but it does not automatically make goodness wrong. Matthew 5:16, for example, speaks of people seeing good works and glorifying the Father. The contrast is between goodness that points beyond the doer and goodness staged to win admiration for the doer.

So the question is not only *Who can see this?* but *Whose approval am I seeking?*
