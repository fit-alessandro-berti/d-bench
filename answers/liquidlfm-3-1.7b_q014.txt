**Short answer:**  
The passage points to **training / aligning** the heart with the treasure we place in heaven—not merely “revealing” it. When we commit our possessions (both material and spiritual) to the heavenly realm, we *shape* our hearts so that they also dwell there. In other words, the choice of treasure **teaches** and **guides** the heart’s disposition.

---

## 1. What the text actually says  

> **Matt. 6:19‑21 (NIV)**  
> “Lay not up for yourselves treasures upon earth, where moth and rust doth corrupt, and where thieves break through and steal:  
> But lay up for yourselves treasures in heaven, where neither moth nor rust doth corrupt, and where thieves do not break through nor steal:  
> **For where your treasure is, there will your heart be also.**”

### Key phrases
| Phrase | Meaning |
|--------|---------|
| **“Lay not up … upon earth”** | Avoid storing wealth or good works on the ground – the earth is full of decay and theft. |
| **“…but lay up … in heaven”** | Choose a higher, purer place. Heaven is described as a realm where nothing corrupts or robs. |
| **“For where your treasure is, there will your heart be also.”** | The location of one’s treasure mirrors the location of one’s heart. |

---

## 2. Reveal vs. Train – Which sense fits?

### A. “Reveals” (a passive mirror)
If the text only said “your heart will be in the same place as your treasure,” it would sound like a simple identification: *you see* the heart matching the treasure. But the surrounding language stresses **avoidance of corruption**, **rejection of theft**, and **seeking purity**. Those are actions that *change* the heart, not just show it.

### B. “Trains” (an active formation)
The imperative “lay up” (to set apart, to devote) and the warning against earthly temptations imply **action**: we must *choose* to direct our lives upward. The promise “there will your heart be also” is a **future outcome** of that deliberate choice. Hence the emphasis is on **how** we handle our treasure—by training the heart to follow it.

Most scholars therefore read this as **formation/training**: the heart learns to be holy when we place it in God’s kingdom.

---

## 3. How **possession**, **attention**, and **love** become linked

| Element | Textual cue | Connection to the other two |
|---------|-------------|-----------------------------|
| **Possession (treasure)** | “Lay up for yourselves treasures in heaven” – we must *own* something valuable beyond our own hands. | The very act of *holding* or *setting aside* treasure signals ownership. |
| **Attention** | “Lay up” = *set aside*, *dedicate*; the whole passage turns away from “upon earth” (our daily worries) toward “in heaven” (God’s realm). | Our focus (attention) shifts from worldly concerns to heavenly priorities. |
| **Love** | Implicit in the command to love God and neighbor (cf. John 13:34; Luke 6:20); also expressed by “treasures for yourselves” – God wants us to store our good works for Him. | Love is the motive that draws us to heaven and the reason we choose heavenly treasures. |

### The chain of action
1. **Turn attention** away from earthly, corrupt stores and toward the heavenly realm.  
2. **Place possession** (spiritual and/or material) there—this is the “treasure” we are asked to keep.  
3. **Train the heart** to match that treasure; the heart will “be also” in heaven.  

Thus, **possession → attention → love** form a triad that together shape the heart.

---

## 4. Practical take‑aways

- **Avoid “moth and rust” and “thief”** – don’t accumulate worldly riches or prideful status symbols.  
- **Seek “heavenly treasures”** – faith, righteousness, the Kingdom of God, and the presence of Christ. These are not gold bars but spiritual assets that produce lasting value.  
- **Direct your gaze** (attention) upward: pray, worship, serve, and live righteously.  
- **Cultivate love** for God and for others; love is the fruit of a heart that has been trained in heaven.

When these three elements work together, the heart no longer rebels against the chosen treasure; it *resides* alongside it, experiencing peace, joy, and eternal life.

---

### Bottom line
The passage **does not simply declare** that a heart “shows” its treasure; it **teaches** us to **place** our treasures in heaven and **forms** our hearts accordingly. In doing so, it binds **possession** (what we hold dear), **attention** (where we look), and **love** (why we choose to do so) into a single, transformative plan for living out our faith.
