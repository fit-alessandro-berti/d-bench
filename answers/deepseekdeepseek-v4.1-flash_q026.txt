Matthew 12:34–35 ties speech to the heart by using two metaphors: **overflow** and **treasure**.

- “Out of the abundance of the heart the mouth speaketh” — the heart is pictured as a reservoir that, when full, spills out through words.
- “Good treasure” / “evil treasure” — the heart is also a storehouse of accumulated moral content: desires, habits, loves, intentions, beliefs, and dispositions.
- A “good man” or “evil man” brings forth speech from that stored treasury.

So the passage connects speech with hidden moral stockpiles by saying that **words are not morally neutral or self-generated**. They come from what is already stored in the heart.

### Are words symptoms, choices, revelations, or all three?

The best answer is: **all three, though not in the same way.**

1. **Words are symptoms.**  
   They are diagnostic evidence of the heart’s condition. Like fruit from a tree (Matt 12:33; Luke 6:43–45), speech shows what kind of moral life is producing it. Jesus uses this to expose the Pharisees: their blasphemous words reveal a poisonous inner condition.

2. **Words are revelations.**  
   They disclose what is hidden. The heart is invisible to others, but speech makes it audible. Not every word reveals the whole heart exhaustively, but characteristic speech reveals the dominant treasure. What fills the heart eventually comes out of the mouth.

3. **Words are choices.**  
   Speaking is a human act, and Scripture holds people accountable for words (Matt 12:36–37). Jesus asks, “How can ye, being evil, speak good things?”—He treats their speech as morally responsible. Yet the choice is not free-floating; it is shaped by the heart’s treasury. We choose words, but those choices flow from what we have stored up.

### So the passage’s logic is something like:

**Heart → abundance → mouth → words.**

Words are therefore **chosen expressions that symptomatically reveal the heart’s moral stockpile**. They are not *only* symptoms, as if a person had no responsibility. They are not *only* choices, as if words could be detached from character. They are revelations of the inner person, and they are morally accountable actions.

One qualification: this is a wisdom principle about characteristic speech, not a mathematical guarantee about every single syllable. A good person can still stumble in speech (James 3:2), and an evil person can sometimes say something true. But the prevailing direction of a person’s speech reveals the prevailing treasure of the heart.

In short: **words are symptoms and revelations of the heart, and they are also choices—but choices sourced in the hidden moral treasury of the heart.**
