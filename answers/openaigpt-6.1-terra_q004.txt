The passage asks for **wholehearted trust in God**: a willingness to rely on God’s wisdom and character rather than treating one’s own judgment as the ultimate authority. “In all thy ways acknowledge him” suggests bringing every part of life under that trust, not merely turning to God when one’s plans fail.

**Humble self-distrust** means recognizing that our understanding is limited and can be distorted by pride, fear, or desire. It encourages reflection, prayer, correction, and wise counsel.

That is different from **anti-intellectualism**, which dismisses reason, evidence, or learning as unimportant. Proverbs itself values wisdom, understanding, and sound advice. The contrast is not *thinking versus trusting*, but using one’s understanding humbly versus leaning on it as self-sufficient.

The promise that God will “direct thy paths” expresses confidence in divine guidance, though it need not mean that every decision will be obvious or every path easy.
