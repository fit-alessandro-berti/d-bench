Jesus’ saying does both, but **the text explicitly says treasure *reveals* the heart; the larger command implies it also *trains* the heart.**

### 1. Revelation in v. 21
The conclusion is indicative, not just imperative: *“Where your treasure is, there your heart will be also”* (ὅπου ἔστιν ὁ θησαυρός σου, ἐκεῖ ἔσται καὶ ἡ καρδία σου). The future “will be” is predictive/diagnostic. It functions like a spiritual X‑ray: **your accumulated possessions disclose where your real affections lie.** You do not need to guess whether you love comfort, status, or security; look at what you are storing, guarding, and anxious about. The heart (ἡ καρδία)—the seat of will, desire, and loyalty—follows the treasure. So, first and foremost, **treasure reveals the heart.**

### 2. Formation in vv. 19–20
But Jesus does not leave it at observation. He *commands* a change: “**Do not** lay up… **but lay up** treasure in heaven.” The imperatives treat treasure as **formative**. The logic runs like this: because your heart will go where your treasure is, **choose where to put your treasure** in order to direct your heart away from corruptible goods and toward God’s kingdom. Thus:
- **Revelation:** “What you own shows what you love.”
- **Training:** “What you choose to store shapes what you will love.”

The two are inseparable. You cannot say, “I love God,” while hoarding what moths destroy; and if you begin storing treasure in heaven, you begin pulling your heart away from earth’s anxiety.

### How possession, attention, and love are tied together

| Element | What it is in this text | How it connects |
|---|---|---|
| **Possession (treasure)** | Concrete, accumulated value—money, goods, security. It is *where* you have invested “store” (θησαυρός). | Treasure is not mere property; it is **objectified love**. You guard what you value. |
| **Attention** | The vigilance required to protect against “moth and rust” and thieves. Earthly treasure demands **anxious care** (cf. Matt 6:25–34). You must watch it, maintain it, fear its loss. | Where your treasure is, your **mind and worry** go. Possession captures cognitive and emotional energy; it “corrupts” because it binds you to fragility. |
| **Love (heart)** | The center of affection and commitment (kardia). | The heart follows the treasure. If your possessions are on earth, your heart is pinned to what is temporary; if they are in heaven, your heart is anchored to God and his kingdom. |

**The causal loop Jesus exposes:**
1. **Possession directs attention:** You must pay attention to what you own, especially when it is vulnerable.
2. **Attention deepens attachment:** The more you guard and calculate around earthly goods, the more you are emotionally invested in them.
3. **Attachment is love:** Your “heart” is now where your treasure is—your desires, hopes, and anxieties orbit that object.

But the command reverses the loop deliberately:
- **Direct possession to heaven** (give, serve, seek the kingdom).
- **This redirects attention:** You stop anxious guarding of perishable things; you begin seeking God’s reign (6:33).
- **The heart follows:** Your love is retrained—shaped by security in God rather than by fragility.

### A brief synthesis
The text is not merely saying, “Show me your bank statement and I will know your heart.” It is saying, **“Your bank statement reveals your heart, so change your banking practices—because your heart will go wherever you put your treasure.”** Possession is the vehicle; attention is the mechanism; love is the destination. Earthly treasure corrupts because it binds all three to decay; heavenly treasure frees them because it binds them to God.
