## Short answer

The verse teaches **both**, but in a specific order: **treasure trains (or carries) the heart, and precisely because it trains the heart, it also reveals the heart**.

The arrow in the saying runs from **treasure → heart**, not the other way around. Jesus does not say, “Where your heart is, there your treasure will be.” He says, “Where your treasure is, there your heart will be also.” Your stored-up treasure is the weight that pulls your heart after it.

So the primary emphasis is **formation**: what you store up *disciples* your loves. The “revealing” use—“show me your bank account and I’ll show you your heart”—is a true application, but it is secondary. It works only because treasure and heart are bound together.

---

## 1. The logic of Matthew 6:19–21

Jesus gives two imperatives:

1. **Do not** lay up treasures on earth.
2. **Do** lay up treasures in heaven.

Then He gives the reason: **“For where your treasure is, there will your heart be also.”**

The word “for” matters. Jesus is not mainly saying, “Your heart is already in heaven, so your treasure will follow.” He is saying: **“Put your treasure in a different place, because your heart goes wherever your treasure is.”** The future tense—*“will be”*—is a promise and a warning:

- If you bury your life in perishable things, your heart will be dragged down to earth, anxiety, and loss.
- If you invest in God’s kingdom, your heart will be lifted toward God.

The two kinds of treasure are contrasted by their **security**:

- Earthly treasure: moth, rust, corruption, thieves.
- Heavenly treasure: no corruption, no theft.

Earthly treasures cannot bear the weight of ultimate love. They decay and can be taken. Heavenly treasure is kept by God and cannot fail. Therefore heavenly treasure is the only treasure worthy of your heart’s full trust and love.

---

## 2. Reveals or trains? Actually: trains, and therefore also reveals

### It trains the heart

This is the main point. Jesus assumes that the heart is not changed by sheer willpower. The heart follows the treasure. So if you want your heart set on God, you must **re-locate your treasure**.

Luke’s parallel makes this even clearer:

> “Sell your possessions and give alms; make for yourselves purses that do not wear out, an unfailing treasure in heaven… For where your treasure is, there your heart will be also.”  
> — Luke 12:33–34

Selling and giving are not mere outward acts; they are the way the treasure is transferred to heaven. And the heart follows.

So Jesus is giving a strategy for transformation: **change the practice, and by God’s grace the affection will follow.** This is not mechanical—Matthew 6:1–18 warns that hypocritical religious acts gain only human praise. But it *is* the normal path of discipleship: embodied, practical treasure-laying before the Father becomes the means by which love is re-trained.

### It also reveals the heart

Because the heart always goes with the treasure, your actual treasures become a mirror.

If you want to know where your heart is being anchored, look at:

- what you spend money on
- what you sacrifice for
- what you worry about losing
- what you give away
- what you daydream about
- what you trust for security

Your treasure discloses your heart—not perfectly, because only God sees the heart fully, but truly enough for self-examination. Jesus’ point is not merely “Your stuff shows what you love,” but the stronger truth: **“Your stuff is taking your heart somewhere.”**

So:

- **Treasure trains the heart** → because the heart follows treasure.
- **Treasure reveals the heart** → because the heart has already followed your treasure.

---

## 3. How possession, attention, and love are tied together

In this passage, “heart” is not just emotion. In Scripture, the heart is the control center of the whole person: **will, love, trust, intention, and attention**. So when Jesus says the heart follows the treasure, He is tying together three things:

### Possession: your treasure

“Treasure” means stored-up security, value, and hope. It is what you possess and rely on.

- Earthly treasure = money, property, status, anything treated as ultimate security.
- Heavenly treasure = God himself, his kingdom, his approval, and the reward he gives.

Possessions are not neutral here. What you store becomes your functional trust. Jesus personifies the issue in the next verse: you cannot serve **God and mammon** (Matthew 6:24). Your treasure is really your master.

### Attention: where the heart looks and rests

The “heart” includes attention. Where your heart is, there your thoughts, worries, plans, and gaze go.

That is why the very next verses speak about the **eye**:

> “The light of the body is the eye: if therefore thine eye be single, thy whole body shall be full of light.”  
> — Matthew 6:22

A “single” or healthy eye is focused, generous, undistracted devotion to God. An “evil” eye is covetous, anxious, grudging attention turned toward earthly security. Then Jesus speaks of **serving** a master:

> “No man can serve two masters… Ye cannot serve God and mammon.”  
> — Matthew 6:24

So attention is tied to allegiance. What you attend to reveals what you serve. And earthly treasure especially captures attention, because it is insecure: it must be watched, protected, and fretted over (see Matthew 6:25–34 on anxiety).

### Love: the heart’s ultimate loyalty

Love is the deepest movement of the heart. To treasure something is to love it, trust it, and devote yourself to it.

- To treasure God is to love him with all the heart (Matthew 22:37).
- To treasure the world is to love the world (1 John 2:15).
- To serve mammon is to love money and hate or despise God (Matthew 6:24).

So love is not separate from treasure. **Your treasure is your love made concrete.**

---

## 4. The循环: how the three form one another

You can think of it as a loop:

1. **Love** directs **attention**: you focus on what you love.
2. **Attention** gathers and guards **possession/treasure**: you spend time, energy, and resources on what you attend to.
3. **Treasure** then carries the **heart** back: what you store becomes the gravitational center that pulls your love and attention after it.
4. That reinforced love again directs attention, and the cycle continues.

This is why sin is so powerful: earthly treasure captures attention, attention stores more treasure, and the heart is dragged further into love of the world. It becomes an idolatrous loop.

Jesus breaks the loop at the point of **treasure**:

> “Lay up for yourselves treasures in heaven.”

He does not say, “Try harder to feel love for God.” He says, **relocate your treasure**—through generosity, kingdom-seeking, secret obedience to the Father—and your heart will follow.

---

## Bottom line

Matthew 6:19–21 teaches that **treasure forms the heart**.

- Your **possessions** are not merely symptoms of your spiritual state; they are formative. They either anchor your heart to what perishes or lift it to what lasts.
- Your **attention** follows your treasure, because the heart goes where its treasure is.
- Your **love** is bound up with your treasure, because what you treasure is what you ultimately trust and serve.

So the verse is both a **warning** and a **promise**:

- **Warning:** If your treasure is on earth, your heart is being trained for anxiety, loss, and idolatry.
- **Promise:** If by grace you store up treasure in heaven—through faith, generosity, and seeking first God’s kingdom—your heart will be drawn there as well.

The way to change the heart is not merely to grit your teeth and try to love God more. It is to **move the treasure**, and to trust that the heart will follow where the treasure has gone.
