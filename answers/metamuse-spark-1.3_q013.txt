This is not God asking Israel to add a little ethics to their worship. It is God filing for divorce from their worship. The language is deliberate and total.

> *I hate, I despise... I will not smell... I will not accept... neither will I regard... I will not hear...*

Smell, sight, hearing — every sense by which God was said to “enjoy” a sacrifice in Leviticus is shut down. Burnt offerings, grain offerings, peace offerings of fat beasts, feast days, solemn assemblies, songs, harps — the whole liturgical checklist is named just to be rejected.

Why so sharp? Because what Amos is exposing is not imbalance. It’s fraud.

### 1. The context: piety covering predation

Amos is preaching to prosperous Northern Israel under Jeroboam II. Religion is booming at Bethel and Gilgal. At the same time, as Amos 2:6-8; 5:10-12; 8:4-6 describes:

they sell the righteous for silver and the needy for a pair of shoes, they trample the poor and exact a tax of wheat from him, they take bribes and push aside the needy in the gate, they hate the one who reproves in court.

They kept the court corrupt Monday to Saturday and then went to church on Sunday to sing loudly. That is why God says “*your* feast days,” “*your* offerings,” “*thy* songs.” He disowns them. They no longer belong to the covenant; they belong to the performers.

God is not anti-liturgy — He commanded those feasts and offerings. He is anti-liturgy-turned-lie, as Isaiah 1:11-17, Hosea 6:6, and Micah 6:6-8 say in the same tradition.

### 2. Why not “better balance”?

Balance implies you have two good things in the wrong proportions: a little too much singing, a little too little justice. Let’s fix the ratio.

Amos says justice is not the garnish to worship. It is the substance worship is supposed to express.

You can’t “balance” worshiping God with crushing people God loves, any more than you can balance faithfulness to your spouse with occasional adultery. A husband who beats his wife and then brings her flowers doesn’t need balance — he needs to stop, and the flowers without repentance are an insult.

That’s why it becomes “noise” to God. The Hebrew is strong: not melody but *hamon*, din, clamor. While the choir sings inside, the cry of the robbed poor outside is louder in God’s ears. The worship doesn’t neutralize the injustice; the injustice poisons the worship.

Three frauds are happening at once:

**a) Cover-up:** Ritual as camouflage. If we look religious enough, no one — including God, we hope — will notice the business practices.

**b) Bribery:** Sacrifice as payoff. The fat beasts were the most expensive offering. The logic is pagan: give God His cut and He’ll look the other way. God says, “I will not even look.”

**c) Compartmentalization:** A sanctuary-God separate from a marketplace-God. They thought holiness stopped at the temple door. Amos says the God of Bethel *is* the God of the city gate where justice is done — or perverted.

This is spiritual self-deception at its worst: confusing performance with relationship, aesthetics with obedience, God’s patience with God’s approval.

### 3. What He wants instead

> *But let judgment run down as waters, and righteousness as a mighty stream.*

*Mishpat* — judgment, public justice, what happens in the courts and markets — and *tsedaqah* — righteousness, right relationships, covenant loyalty in daily life.

The image is of a wadi in flood in winter, and even better, a *nahal etan*, a perennial, never-drying stream. Not occasional justice for festivals. Not seasonal generosity. A constant, unstoppable, cleansing flow that washes everything.

Martin Luther King Jr. famously quoted this because he heard it rightly: God does not want beautiful worship services *about* justice. He wants justice itself to become the nonstop worship.
