The posture is **humble, gentle, and restorative**: the goal of correction is to help a person turn from wrongdoing and be restored, not to shame or triumph over them.

Paul keeps the three together because they guard the purpose of correction:

- **Restoration** directs correction toward the person’s good and their place in the community.
- **Meekness** shapes how it is done: with patience and care rather than harshness or superiority.
- **Self-suspicion** reminds the corrector that they too are vulnerable to temptation. “Considering thyself” makes correction an act of solidarity, not a claim to moral invulnerability.

Paul’s “spiritual” people are therefore not a moral elite looking down on others; they are to act in a way consistent with the Spirit’s gentleness. This does not mean excusing wrongdoing or avoiding accountability. It means addressing it truthfully while remembering that the corrector also needs grace.
