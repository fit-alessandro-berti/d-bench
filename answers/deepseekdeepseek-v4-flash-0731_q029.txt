In Galatians 6:1, Paul is describing a specific moral posture for those who seek to correct a fellow believer who has fallen into sin. The required posture is **humble, gentle, and self-aware**, not superior or self-righteous.

The verse says: *“Brethren, if a man be overtaken in a fault, ye which are spiritual, restore such an one in the spirit of meekness; considering thyself, lest thou also be tempted.”*

### What kind of moral posture is required?

1. **Meekness** — The correction is not harsh, proud, or domineering. It is offered with gentleness and humility, recognizing that the goal is healing, not humiliation.
2. **Self-suspicion** — Paul tells the one who corrects to “consider thyself.” This means the corrector must remain aware of his or her own weakness and capacity for failure. There is no place for moral superiority.
3. **Restoration** — The purpose of correction is to restore, not to punish or discard. The Greek word used here, *katartizete*, also means to mend, repair, or set a broken bone. So the correction is like surgery: gentle, careful, and aimed at healing.

### Why are restoration, meekness, and self-suspicion kept together?

They belong together because **correcting someone without these qualities becomes an act of pride, not love**.

- If you try to restore someone **without meekness**, your correction becomes harsh and self-righteous.
- If you try to restore someone **without self-suspicion**, you forget that you too are fallible and in need of grace.
- If you have meekness and self-suspicion but **without restoration**, you become passive or indifferent, letting the person remain broken when love should move you to help.

Paul ties them together because the only safe posture for any spiritual person who confronts another’s sin is **an attitude that views both people as standing before God’s grace** — the one being corrected and the one doing the correcting. The “spiritual” person is not one who has never fallen, but one who knows they are also capable of falling, and therefore treats the broken brother or sister with the same mercy they themselves need.
