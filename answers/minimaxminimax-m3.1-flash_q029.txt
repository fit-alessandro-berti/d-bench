Paul requires a posture of **humble, restorative accountability**—neither passive tolerance nor self-righteous punishment.

- **Restoration** establishes the goal. The person overtaken in fault is to be helped back into fellowship and maturity, not simply exposed, defeated, or excluded. Discipline is meant to heal and form, not to humiliate.
- **Meekness** shapes the manner of correction. Paul’s “meekness” is not weakness or reluctance to confront sin; it is strength governed by humility, patience, and respect for the other person. Those who are “spiritual” are therefore not a superior class who can look down on sinners.
- **Self-suspicion** keeps correction from becoming a performance of moral superiority. “Considering thyself, lest thou also be tempted” means that the corrector must recognize their own vulnerability before God. It does not necessarily imply that the corrector will commit the same sin, but it refuses the illusion of moral immunity.

These qualifications belong together because each guards against a distortion of the others:

1. **Meekness without restoration** becomes mere leniency that leaves the person and community unchanged.
2. **Restoration without meekness** can become domination or public shame.
3. **Self-suspicion without restoration** can become self-condemnation, suspicion, or paralysis.
4. **Restoration and self-suspicion together** make correction reciprocal rather than hierarchical: the one who corrects is also accountable and in need of correction.

The immediate context reinforces this. Paul has just called believers to share one another’s burdens (Galatians 6:2) and then warns against the pride that makes someone think themselves superior while regarding others as nothing (6:3). Thus “you who are spiritual” does not authorize a ruler-and-subject approach to wrongdoing. Rather, those who are spiritually mature should discern, correct, and restore in a spirit that is themselves willing to be examined and restored.

In short: **restoration is the end, meekness is the manner, and self-suspicion is the necessary brake on pride.** Correction here is an act of loving solidarity, requiring both courage to address sin and humility about one’s own.
