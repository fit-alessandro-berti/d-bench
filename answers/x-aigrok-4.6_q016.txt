The elder brother’s surface reactions (anger at the feast, envy of the calf, a wounded sense of fairness) all flow from a single root. He has reduced sonship to wage-labor: “these many years do I serve thee, neither transgressed I at any time thy commandment.” Because he believes he has earned the father’s favor, he cannot rejoice when the same favor is given freely to the undeserving. The father’s mercy therefore appears to him not as good news but as a threat to the moral order he has constructed. That is why he will not go in, why he will not even call the prodigal “my brother,” and why the father’s final entreaty is required. The deepest problem is discomfort with mercy itself.

Final answer: discomfort with mercy itself
