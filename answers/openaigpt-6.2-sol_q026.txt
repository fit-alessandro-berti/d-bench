All three—but the passage puts the emphasis on **speech as revelation**. “Out of the abundance of the heart” pictures words as an overflow: what has accumulated within eventually spills into what we say. The image of “treasure” adds the idea of a storehouse. The heart, in this biblical sense, is not just the seat of feeling but the center of desire, judgment, and will. What we habitually cherish or harbor supplies what we “bring forth.”

- **Symptoms:** Words are effects of an underlying condition. Contemptuous speech, for example, may be the outward symptom of resentment long harbored.
- **Choices:** The speaker still “bringeth forth” the words. The influence of the heart does not excuse the act of speaking; the next verses hold people accountable for their words.
- **Revelations:** Because words come from that inner store, they can make visible what otherwise remains hidden—even when the speaker did not intend to disclose it.

In context, Jesus is responding to Pharisees who attribute his healing work to demonic power. He treats their accusation not merely as a verbal mistake but as evidence of how they judge what is good. The point is not that one stray sentence gives us a complete map of someone’s heart. It is that speech is morally revealing precisely because it is both something we do and something that comes from what we have stored within.
