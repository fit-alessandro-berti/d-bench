Short answer: **it says both, but the main force of the text is that treasure *forms* or *trains* the heart.**  

Jesus says:

> “Lay up for yourselves treasures in heaven… For where your treasure is, there will your heart be also.”

The command is **“lay up”**—an imperative. If the statement only *revealed* the heart, it would be a diagnosis: “Look at what you treasure and you’ll know what your heart is.” But Jesus does not stop there. He commands us to put treasure in a different place, and then says the heart **will follow**. So treasure is not only a mirror of the heart; it is also a kind of **magnet** for the heart.

In the Bible, “heart” means more than feelings. It means the inner self: your mind, will, affections, and loyalties. So “where your treasure is, there your heart will be” means: your attention, desire, and love will be pulled toward whatever you most value and store up.

Here’s how possession, attention, and love become tied together:

- **Possession** = “treasure.” It is what you consider valuable, what you invest in, what you keep.
- **Attention** = “where.” Treasure is always located *somewhere*. You can store it on earth, or you can store it in heaven.
- **Love** = “heart.” The heart is the center of your love and loyalty.

The text says these are not separate. What you own/treasure draws your attention; what you attend to becomes where your love goes. If your treasure is on earth, your heart will be tied to earth—anxious, corruptible, security-driven. If your treasure is in heaven, your heart will be set on God, His kingdom, and what lasts.

So if we must choose: **the text does not simply say “your treasure reveals your heart.” It says your heart will follow your treasure.** That is why Jesus can command us to lay up treasure in heaven. He is not telling us to fake it; He is telling us that by actually investing in what God values, our hearts are reoriented. Possession, attention, and love are not three separate things—they are one movement.
