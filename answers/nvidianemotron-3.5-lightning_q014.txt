The verse functions **both ways, but grammatically and covenant-wise it leans most directly toward: *treasure reveals the heart's true orientation*, while simultaneously *training the heart* toward whatever it owns.*

Here’s how the three dynamics coil together:

### 1. “Where your treasure is, there your heart will be also”
The Greek construction (*hoti hopōthen estin ho thēsauros sou, estai kai hē kardia sou*) sets up a fixed point of alignment. It doesn’t say “your heart *causes* your treasure” or “your treasure *causes* your heart.” It says the two **co-locate**: what you treasure *is* where your heart ends up. 

- **Revelatory:** If you pour your money, time, and energy into retirement funds, status, or material comfort, the text says your heart is already aligned with those things. Your treasure *shows* what you’re living for.
- **Formative:** What you repeatedly invest in reshapes your affections. Earthly treasures have a “corruptive” pull (moth, rust, thieves) that gradually trains the heart to distrust God and love things that fade. Heavenly treasures train the heart toward eternity, generosity, and trust in God’s provision.

### 2. How possession, attention, and love tie together
| Element | How it functions in the verse |
|---------|------------------------------|
| **Possession** | “Lay up treasures” implies ownership and stewardship. You can’t treasure what you don’t possess or regularly handle. Possession is the tangible expression of what you’ve chosen to hold. |
| **Attention** | Jesus says the heart *follows* the treasure. Your attention is the pipeline: where you look, what you monitor, what you plan for—this is where your treasure draws your focus. The eye is the lamp of the body (Matt 6:22); the treasure is the object that fills it. |
| **Love** | The heart’s “being there” is a love-language. In biblical usage, “heart” = the center of desire, devotion, and allegiance. You love what you treasure; you treasure what you love. The verse effectively rewires the direction: normally we think “I love → I possess,” but Jesus says “I possess → I love.” |

### 3. The practical movement
- **Earthly cycle:** Possession → Attention → Love → Heart reoriented toward the temporary → Anxiety, idolatry, fragility.
- **Heavenly cycle:** Possession → Attention → Love → Heart reoriented toward the eternal → Freedom, generosity, trust.

The text isn’t saying “have things and your heart will change” (prosperity gospel) nor “your heart is fixed and treasure doesn’t matter” (dualism). It’s saying **your treasure is the leverage point**: it both *diagnoses* where your heart currently lives, and *directs* where it will grow.

**Bottom line:** Matthew 6:19–21 says treasure *reveals* the heart’s true master, and *trains* the heart to serve that master. The only way to change the heart’s allegiance is to change the treasure you’re laying up.
