Amos is not saying, “Do a little more justice and keep the rest of your worship as it is.” He is exposing a更深入 problem: their worship has become a religious cover for covenant betrayal.

In the immediate context, Israel is not lacking in ritual. They have feast days, solemn assemblies, burnt offerings, grain offerings, peace offerings, songs, and instruments. The issue is the life behind those forms. Just before this passage Amos condemns them for hating the reprover, trampling the poor, taking exactions of grain, bribing judges, and perverting justice in the gate. So God refuses their worship not because worship itself is bad, but because their worship is being used to disguise disobedience.

That is why the rejection is so sharp: God will not “smell,” “regard,” or “hear” their offerings and music. The language is total: he refuses the whole sensory package of worship. The sweet aroma, the costly sacrifice, the beautiful melody—all of it has become offensive because it presents fellowship with God while the worshipers are practicing what God hates.

The fraud is religious formalism joined to moral corruption: using outward devotion as a substitute for repentance, using sacrifice as a way to buy God off, using religious emotion and ceremony to create the appearance of covenant faithfulness while ignoring justice. It is not merely “imbalance”; it is counterfeit worship. They want the comfort, identity, and security of religion without surrendering the sins that religion condemns.

This is why the prophets often speak this way: God does not accept worship from unrepentant hands. Sacrifice is not a neutral good work that can offset oppression. If justice is absent, the offering itself becomes part of the hypocrisy.

Verse 24 then gives the positive demand:

> “But let judgment run down as waters, and righteousness as a mighty stream.”

“Judgment” and “righteousness” mean justice, covenant faithfulness, and right dealing—especially toward the poor and vulnerable. The image of a flowing stream suggests something abundant, continuous, and public. Justice is not to be an occasional religious gesture; it is to shape the whole life of the community.

So the passage exposes the fraud of worship without obedience: liturgy without justice, music without mercy, sacrifice without repentance, and religious privilege without moral faithfulness.
