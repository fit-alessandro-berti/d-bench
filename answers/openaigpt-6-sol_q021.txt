Pride is linked to blindness because it makes a person a poor judge of their own limits. The proud mistake success for invulnerability, dismiss warnings, and treat dependence on others—or on God—as weakness. The “fall” is therefore not only a punishment for guilt; it can grow out of arrogance itself.

The second verse makes the choice sharper. To “divide the spoil with the proud” is to share in an apparent victory, perhaps even to profit from their contempt for others. Being “of an humble spirit with the lowly” means giving up that status and gain in favor of people one might otherwise overlook. The proverb does not say that hardship automatically makes someone virtuous. It suggests that humility sees reality more clearly, while arrogance can make even a winner unable to see how precarious their position is.
