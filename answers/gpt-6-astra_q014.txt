**Both—but the passage’s movement gives special weight to treasure directing the heart, not merely revealing it.** Jesus does not simply say, “Look at your treasure to discover what you love.” He commands, “Lay up for yourselves treasures in heaven,” and then explains why: “For where your treasure is, there will your heart be also.”

### Treasure reveals the heart

What we treat as treasure discloses what we regard as valuable, secure, and worth pursuing. Money is central to the surrounding passage, which culminates in the warning that we cannot serve both God and mammon (6:24). But “treasure” is not simply everything we happen to own. It is what we store up **as our wealth and security**.

In biblical usage, the “heart” is more than emotion: it is the center of thought, desire, intention, and allegiance. Where we locate our treasure therefore reveals something about the orientation of our whole life.

That is not a license to read someone’s heart straight off their bank balance. Possessing something and making it one’s treasure are related, but not identical.

### Treasure also trains the heart

The command assumes that what we do with our resources matters for what we become. Jesus does not tell us to wait until our hearts are perfectly ordered before changing our practices. He tells us to place our treasure differently.

The wording—“there **will** your heart be also”—fits this forward-looking force, though the future tense alone does not establish a psychological theory. The larger point is practical: **our investments do not merely express our commitments; they help deepen them.**

What we accumulate, protect, and depend upon repeatedly claims our concern. Conversely, practices of generosity and faithfulness can cultivate attachment to God’s purposes. In the immediate chapter, giving to the needy is one concrete example of life oriented toward the Father rather than human recognition.

### Possession, attention, and love

The connection can be described this way:

- **Treasure gives us a stake:** “Something important to me is here.”
- **That stake draws attention:** we watch it, protect it, anticipate its future, and fear its loss.
- **Repeated attention can deepen attachment:** our hopes and choices increasingly organize themselves around it.

“Attention” is not an explicit term in these verses, but it helps explain how treasure and heart become bound together. The relationship also runs the other way: we invest in what we already love. It is a reinforcing movement, not a one-way mechanism.

The contrast between earthly and heavenly treasure makes the stakes clear. Earthly treasure is vulnerable to decay and theft; life organized around it rests on what cannot finally be secured. Heavenly treasure locates one’s lasting good with God.

**So the saying is both diagnostic and formative: your treasure shows where your heart is, and placing your treasure helps determine where your heart will go.** Jesus is not merely asking, “What do you love?” He is also asking, “What are your ways of storing, spending, and giving teaching you to love?”
