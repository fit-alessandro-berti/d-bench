James 3:9–10 exposes a deep inner contradiction: the same tongue blesses God and curses people who are made in God’s likeness. That is not merely bad manners; it is a theological contradiction. To bless God as Father while cursing those who bear His image is to honor the Creator with one breath and dishonor His image with the next.

The reason this matters is that human beings are “made after the similitude of God” (Genesis 1:26–27). Cursing a person is therefore not neutral; it is an implicit attack on the dignity of the One whose image they bear. In that sense, blessing God and cursing His image-bearers cannot consistently come from the same heart. James says, “My brethren, these things ought not so to be,” because it reveals a divided mind and a disordered love.

Speech reveals this moral disorder quickly because the tongue is the most direct overflow of the heart. Jesus taught that “out of the abundance of the heart the mouth speaketh” (Matthew 12:34). Words are harder to control than outward actions, and they expose our inner loves, resentments, and loyalties before we can censor them. James has already said the tongue is small but powerful, hard to tame, and capable of great damage (James 3:2, 8). So when praise and cursing come from the same mouth, it reveals that the heart itself is double-minded.

The contradiction is like a spring producing both fresh and bitter water, or a fig tree bearing olives—impossible without something being wrong at the source. Thus, the real problem is not just the tongue; it is the heart that has not yet been fully brought under the love and rule of God.
