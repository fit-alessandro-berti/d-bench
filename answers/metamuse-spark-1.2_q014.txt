In this text, Jesus says your treasure **trains** your heart - not just reveals it. Most people misquote it backwards.

We tend to read it as:

> "Where your heart is, there your treasure will be also."

That would mean treasure *reveals* the heart. Look at your bank statement to see what you love.

But Jesus says the opposite:

> *"For where your treasure is, there will your heart be also."* - Matt. 6:21

The grammar is deliberate. The treasure leads. The heart follows.

### 1. Reveals or Trains?

**Reveals** would be diagnostic: Your heart already exists in a certain state, and your spending shows it.

**Trains** is formative: Your heart will go where you send your treasure. Your heart *becomes* shaped by what you do with what you value.

The Greek makes this clear:

> *hopou gar estin ho thesauros sou, ekei estai kai he kardia sou.*

> "For where is your treasure, there will be also your heart."

*estai* - **will be** - is future tense. Place your treasure now, your heart will follow there later. It's a law of formation.

That doesn't mean it *doesn't* reveal - secondarily it does. If the heart always follows treasure, then looking at where your treasure has gone is a reliable way to find where your heart has gone. But the *mechanism* Jesus describes is training, not just revealing. He is not giving you a thermometer; He is giving you a rudder.

This is why the command in verse 19-20 comes first:

> *Me thesaurizete hymin thesaurous* - "Do not treasure up treasures for yourselves"

It's a cognate verb - literally "do not treasure treasures." Jesus treats treasuring as an *activity* you do, not just a feeling you have. And that activity moves your heart.

As Augustine put it: "Love follows what you cling to."

### 2. How Possession, Attention, and Love Become Tied Together

In the Sermon on the Mount, Jesus ties three things that modern people try to keep separate:

**1. Possession [Thesauros] = What you claim as yours**
*Thesauros* is not a momentary purchase, it's a storehouse, a hoard, what you have stockpiled and secured. In the ancient world, treasure was cloth vulnerable to moths, metal vulnerable to rust *brosis* - eating/corrosion, and anything vulnerable to thieves who would literally "dig through" *dioryssousin* mud-brick walls.

To possess is to invest. When you lay up something, you declare: "This is my security, my future, my worth."

**2. Attention [Heart as Watchman] = What you guard and gaze at**
What you possess automatically captures your attention, because you have to protect it. You worry about moth, rust, and thieves. You check on it. You think about it.

This is made explicit in the very next verse:

> "The eye is the lamp of the body... where your eye looks, your attention goes." 6:22-23

And *kardia* in Hebrew thought does not mean just emotion. The heart is the center of attention, will, intellect and devotion - the control center of the person. Where your attention goes, you go.

You cannot store your treasure in one place and keep your heart in another. Your mind will orbit what you have decided is valuable, because you have to watch over it.

**3. Love [Heart] = What you worship and become**
Because your attention is now fixed there, your affection follows. Psychologically and spiritually, we love what we attend to. What we repeatedly look at, think about, and protect is what we come to love.

This is why the passage ends with:

> "No one can serve two masters... You cannot serve God and Mammon." 6:24

Mammon is not just money. It is treasure personified as a master that demands your attention and love. Possession becomes devotion.

So the chain Jesus establishes is:

> **Possession -> Attention -> Affection**

You put your treasure somewhere -> you keep your attention there to guard it -> your heart takes root there and learns to love it.

An earthly storehouse trains your heart to be anxious, fragile, and earthbound, because moths, rust and thieves can take it. A heavenly storehouse - what Jesus elsewhere describes as generosity, mercy, almsgiving, doing the Father's will, 6:1-4 - trains your heart to be secure, expansive, and God-ward, because nothing can take it.

In one sentence: **You do not love and then place your treasure; you place your treasure and then you learn to love.**

That is why the command is so practical. If you want to know how to change your heart, don't start by trying to manufacture a feeling. Move your treasure. Give, invest, store your time, money, and best energy in the Kingdom, and your heart will inevitably follow it there.
