The core sin is **self-righteous pride**. That pride expresses itself through **comparison, contempt, and the use of virtue as self-justifying display**. An implicit false doctrine is also present: that one can stand before God on the basis of moral or religious superiority rather than on the basis of mercy.

The Pharisee’s statements may have been factually true. He may not have been an extortioner or adulterer, and he may really have fasted and tithed. Jesus is not condemning fasting, tithing, or moral discipline as such. He is condemning what those things have become in the Pharisee’s prayer: **credentials by which he reassures himself and looks down on others.**

So the layers are:

- **Root:** pride / trust in himself  
- **Mechanism:** virtue used as self-display and spiritual credit  
- **Expression:** comparison — “not as other men … even as this publican”  
- **Attitude:** contempt toward the publican  
- **Implicit theology:** I am accepted by God because I am better than others and am scrupulously religious  

Notice that the Pharisee’s prayer contains no confession, no request, and no plea for mercy. He does not really come to God empty; he comes to God already satisfied with himself. As the text says, he prayed “with himself,” not only to God but about himself.

The tax collector is spiritually truer not because bowing, standing far off, or beating the breast are magically virtuous acts. Those are outward signs of an inward reality. He knows the truth about himself before God: **he is a sinner who cannot save himself.** He does not compare himself with worse people. He turns only to God and asks for the one thing he cannot earn:  
> “God be merciful to me a sinner.”

That is why he goes home justified. His humility is not the basis of his justification; it is the posture of someone ready to receive justification as mercy. The Pharisee tried to establish his own righteousness; the tax collector asked God for righteousness.

The parable’s principle is simple: **the one who exalts himself will be humbled; the one who humbles himself will be exalted.** True spiritual health is not “I am better than others,” but “God have mercy on me.”
