Matthew 12:34-35 does not treat words as neutral sounds. It treats them as **spending from a hidden account**.

Jesus says this not as a proverb in the air, but as an indictment. The Pharisees have just seen a demon-possessed man healed and explained it away: "He casts out demons by Beelzebub" (12:24). That slander is what Jesus is diagnosing. Right before, in v.33: "make the tree good, and his fruit good; or else make the tree corrupt, and his fruit corrupt."

Then He gives two pictures of the heart-mouth connection:

**1. Overflow: "out of the abundance [perisseumatos] of the heart the mouth speaketh."**
The picture is hydrological. The heart is a reservoir, the mouth is the spillway. What fills to excess *must* spill over. Poison in, poison out.

That's why He starts: "O generation of vipers, how can ye, being evil, speak good things?"

A viper stores venom in hidden glands. You don't see it until it bites. In the same way, He calls them a *brood* of vipers — their blasphemy against the Spirit wasn't a slip of the tongue, it was a bite revealing a venom-gland inside. An evil *being* cannot produce sustained good *speaking*.

**2. Treasury: "a good man out of the good treasure [thesauros]... bringeth forth... an evil man out of the evil treasure..."**
Luke 6:45, the parallel, makes it even clearer.

*Thesauros* is not a passing feeling. It's a storehouse, a treasure-chest, a vault where you deliberately accumulate and guard what you value. Over time, by what you cherish, rehearse, resent, desire, justify — you stockpile a moral capital. The mouth then *spends* it.

The verb is important: *ekballei* — he "casts, throws, brings forth." It's active. The same verb used for casting out demons. Words are an expulsion of what's inside.

So are words symptoms, choices, or revelations? Jesus holds all three together:

**1. Words as symptoms.**
They are diagnostic, almost involuntary. You can perform goodness for a while, but under pressure, in opposition, in unguarded moments, the abundance leaks. The Pharisees couldn't fake "good things" because the reservoir wasn't good. As Proverbs says, guard the heart "for out of it are the issues of life" (Prov. 4:23), and James 3 will later develop: the tongue is hard to tame because the problem is deeper than the tongue.

**2. Words as choices.**
A symptom is not an excuse. The treasure metaphor makes us curators, not just carriers. A treasure is collected piece by piece. Every entertained lust, nurtured grudge, loved truth, practiced mercy is a deposit. And *bringing forth* is an act — you reach in and choose to spend. That's why Jesus goes on in the very next verses:

> "every idle word that men shall speak, they shall give account thereof in the day of judgment. For by thy words thou shalt be justified, and by thy words thou shalt be condemned." vv. 36-37

If words were *only* symptoms, judgment would be unfair. They are also deeds for which we are responsible.

**3. Words as revelations.**
Words unveil what is hidden — to others now, and to God finally. The evil treasure was invisible until the Pharisees opened their mouths about Jesus. In that moment, their theology, their envy, their allegiance became public. Words pull the inside to the outside.

In short: **the heart stocks, the mouth spends, and the spending shows the stock.**

That's both sobering and hopeful. Sobering, because mere speech-management — learning to "speak good things" while "being evil" — is impossible in Jesus' logic. The tree itself must be made good. Hopeful, because a stockpile can be changed. The Bible's answer is not better acting, but a new heart — Ezekiel's heart of flesh for a heart of stone — which then has a new treasure to spend.
