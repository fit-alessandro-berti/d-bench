The posture is **gentle accountability**: take the fault seriously without treating it as proof of your superiority. “Restore” names the aim. The person has stumbled, and the point of speaking is to help them regain their footing, not to make them feel small.

Meekness is the manner that fits that aim. It does not mean calling the fault harmless or avoiding a hard conversation. It means being willing to speak plainly without contempt. That is why Paul adds “considering thyself.” He does not ask you to doubt that a wrong has been done; he asks you to doubt your own immunity to temptation.

The three belong together: restoration tells you **what you seek**, meekness **how to seek it**, and self-examination **why you have no grounds to look down on the person you correct**. You approach them as someone who may one day need the very help you are offering.
