The passage rejects worship so sharply because the problem is not **too much worship and too little justice**. It is worship being used to sustain a relationship with God that the worshipers’ treatment of other people contradicts.

Earlier in Amos 5, the charges are concrete: they trample the poor, take bribes, and deny the needy justice at the city gate—the place where disputes were judged. Yet the festivals, offerings, and music continue. Religious life looks vigorous while public life is corrupt.

The fraud is therefore **the claim that honoring God ceremonially can coexist with systematically dishonoring those whose cause God defends**.

### Why “better balance” would miss the point

Asking for balance could imply that worship and justice are separate contributions to a spiritual account: enough sacrifice might compensate for exploitation, or a little charity might make the exploitation tolerable. Amos rejects that accounting. God cannot be bribed with offerings, and injustice cannot be consecrated by a beautiful service.

Notice the repeated refusals: “I will not smell,” “I will not accept,” “I will not hear.” Every sign by which worshipers might assume their worship reaches God is shut down. What they hear as sacred music, God calls “noise.”

### What makes this spiritual fraud?

It is not simply that imperfect people are worshiping; that would exclude everyone. It is that religious observance is functioning as a substitute for repentance—as reassurance that all is well without relinquishing practices that harm others. The worshipers need not be consciously pretending. They may sincerely mistake religious intensity for faithfulness.

“But let judgment run down as waters” supplies the alternative. Here “judgment” means justice, not merely punishment. The image is of justice and righteousness flowing abundantly and persistently through communal life—not appearing as occasional charitable gestures.

Amos is not asking for a slightly more socially conscious liturgy. He is exposing **worship that claims communion with God while refusing God’s demands**. The sharp rejection breaks that false assurance: worship cannot make an unjust life right while leaving its injustice intact.
