This text argues primarily that treasure **reveals** the heart, though it carries an implicit exhortation that choosing treasure will ultimately **train** or direct the heart. Here is how possession, attention, and love become tied together in the passage.

### 1. Revelation vs. Training
**The explicit claim is revelation.**
Verse 21 states, "For where your treasure is, there will your heart be also." This functions as a diagnostic principle: your investments (where you store value) expose your true affections. Jesus is telling the listener that external choices are evidence of internal reality. You cannot claim to love God while hoarding wealth on earth; your "treasure" gives away the location of your "heart."

**The implicit claim is training (orientation).**
While the verse reads as a statement of fact, the context is a command (imperative): "Lay not up... But lay up..." By commanding the listener to store treasure in heaven, Jesus implies that the choice of treasure *shapes* the heart. If the heart follows the treasure, then choosing heavenly treasure draws the heart toward heaven, and choosing earthly treasure anchors the heart to the earth. Thus, the act of selecting treasure is an act of training the affections.

### 2. The Connection of Possession, Attention, and Love
The passage ties these three concepts into a single relational dynamic:

*   **Possession (The Object):**
    This is the "treasure" itself. The text contrasts two types of possession: perishable material wealth (subject to "moth and rust" and "thieves") and imperishable spiritual capital (secure in heaven). Possession here is not just ownership, but *storage*—where you accumulate what you value.

*   **Attention (The Alignment):**
    "Where your treasure is" implies focus and priority. You pay attention to what you protect and accumulate. Earthly treasure demands anxious attention (guarding it from decay and theft); heavenly treasure requires attention to eternal values. The geography of your treasure determines the geography of your focus.

*   **Love (The Heart):**
    In biblical anthropology, the "heart" is not just the seat of emotion, but the center of will, desire, and love. The text posits that love is not free-floating; it is tethered to what you value most. You love what you treasure.

### Summary of the Tie
The logic of the passage creates a feedback loop between these three elements:

1.  **Possession dictates Attention:** What you store up is what you worry about and focus on.
2.  **Attention confirms Love:** Your focus reveals what you truly love (your heart).
3.  **Love justifies Possession:** You continue to possess/store what you love.

Therefore, the text suggests that you cannot separate what you **have** (possession) from where your mind rests (attention) and what you cherish (love). To change what you love, you must change what you treasure; to change what you treasure, you must change where you invest your possessions.
