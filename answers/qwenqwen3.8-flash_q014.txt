Yes—but the emphasis in the Greek syntax is more on **treasure forming the heart** than merely exposing it.

## Short answer

Matthew 6:21 says:

> “For where your treasure is, there will your heart be also.”

That word “there will” is important. The verse is not only saying, *“Your heart is revealed by your treasure.”* It is also saying, *“Your heart will go where your treasure goes.”* In other words, what you treasure trains, binds, and locates the heart.

At the same time, there is a revealing aspect: you can often discover where your heart truly is by looking at where you invest your treasure. But the verse’s main logic is formative: **treasure pulls the heart after it.**

---

## 1. Treasure reveals the heart — but primarily it forms the heart

The Greek is roughly:

> ὅπου γάρ ἐστιν ὁ θησαυρὸς ὑμῶν, ἐκεῖ καὶ ἡ καρδία ὑμῶν ἔσται  
> “For where your treasure is, there also your heart will be.”

Notice two things:

1. **“Where your treasure is”** — present tense, current reality.  
   This does have a diagnostic function. If you want to know where your heart is, look at what you are treasure-ing.

2. **“There your heart will be”** — future tense, consequence.  
   This is where the verse becomes deeper: it is not merely descriptive; it is causal. What you treasure over time determines the settled orientation of your heart.

So Jesus is not saying only:

> “Your heart betrays your priorities.”

He is saying more forcefully:

> “Your treasures will eventually take your heart with them.”

That is why it is a warning.

If your treasure is earthly security, approval, pleasure, status, money, comfort, or reputation, your heart will be tethered there — because those things demand attention, anxiety, and devotion. If your treasure is the kingdom of God, righteousness, mercy, faithfulness, generosity, worship, and the things of eternity, your heart will be drawn there — even if imperfectly at first, it will be trained toward God.

---

## 2. Possession: what you treasure becomes what rules you

In biblical language, “treasure” is not simply what one owns. It is what one counts as valuable enough to store, defend, and rely upon.

So “possessing treasure” is not neutral. The question is:

> Do I possess the treasure, or has the treasure begun to possess me?

Earthly treasures are said to be vulnerable:

- moth corrupts,
- rust destroys,
- thieves break through and steal.

That vulnerability is not only economic; it is spiritual. If you make your security depend on something perishable, your heart becomes anxious, fragile, and enslaved to what it fears losing.

In Scripture, the “heart” is not merely the emotional organ. It is the center of the person: intellect, desire, will, imagination, hope, fear, and affection. So when Jesus says, “where your treasure is, there will your heart be also,” He is saying:

> Your deepest self will end up living near whatever you have chosen as your security.

If your treasure is money, your heart will live in its neighborhood — worrying about gains and losses, craving more, fearing insecurity, calculating risk, comparing yourself with others.

If your treasure is God’s kingdom, your heart will live in that neighborhood — thinking about obedience, worship, mission, generosity, holiness, justice, eternity.

The heart follows the treasure.

---

## 3. Attention: where the treasure is, the heart is also “located”

Jesus uses spatial language: **“where… there.”**

This is profound. He is saying that the heart has a “place.” Wherever you place ultimate value, your attention settles there.

You attend to what you treasure.

That may seem obvious, but Jesus makes it a spiritual principle:

- what you treasure captures your attention;
- your attention shapes your affections;
- your affections shape your identity.

This connects directly with the next verses in Matthew 6:

> “The eye is the lamp of the body…” (Matthew 6:22)

The eye is about attention. Where are you looking? What are you fixing on? What do you watch? What do you pursue? What do you keep returning to mentally and practically?

The human heart is not free-floating. It attaches itself to what it considers most valuable.

So the order Jesus gives is:

> Treasure → Heart → Life.

Not merely:

> “You must feel differently about money.”

Rather:

> “Choose what you will truly treasure. Your heart will follow.”

That is why discipleship is not just about correcting feelings. It is about reordering desires through practices: how we earn, spend, give, store, worry, celebrate, and sacrifice.

---

## 4. Love: the heart follows what it values

This is the deepest link.

Possession is external. Attention is internalized. Love is the heart’s settled attachment.

You do not usually love something simply because you once chose it. You love it more and more as you treasure it. The heart is trained by repeated acts of valuation.

That is why Jesus says in verse 21:

> “For where your treasure is, there will your heart be also.”

He is describing a law of the soul:

> We become attached to what we continually treat as valuable.

If you treasure approval, your heart will be enslaved to approval.

If you treasure comfort, your heart will be ruled by comfort.

If you treasure security, your heart will be dominated by fear of losing it.

If you treasure God, your heart will be drawn toward Him — not automatically, not effortlessly, but by the gravitational pull of what you value.

The opposite order is also true in a fallen world: because the heart is already disordered, it chooses disordered treasures. That is the doctrine of sin in a compact form. The heart chooses idols because it loves them. But once those idols are chosen as treasure, they further train and bind the heart.

So possession, attention, and love are tied together in a cycle:

1. **You value something.**  
   You decide it is worth storing.

2. **You give it attention.**  
   You think about it, manage it, protect it, expand it.

3. **You love it.**  
   It becomes your center of security, delight, or identity.

4. **It possesses your heart.**  
   You are no longer merely using it; it is using you.

This is exactly the logic behind the following verses:

> “No one can serve two masters… You cannot serve God and mammon.”  
> (Matthew 6:24)

Mammon is not merely money. It is wealth as rival god — something that commands trust, loyalty, attention, and devotion.

You cannot serve two ultimate masters because your heart cannot be finally located in two treasure-places at once.

---

## 5. Earthly treasure vs. heavenly treasure

The contrast is not merely between “money” and “charity.” It is between two kinds of value systems.

### Earthly treasures

These are things that:

- look valuable now,
- promise security,
- attract attention,
- corrupt or vanish,
- eventually leave the heart exposed to loss.

They are not necessarily evil in themselves. Food, clothing, money, homes, relationships, achievements — these are created goods. But they become dangerous when made into treasures, when they become the place where the heart ultimately rests.

### Heavenly treasures

These are things stored under God’s keeping:

- acts of obedience done for God rather than applause,
- mercy to the poor,
- justice practiced faithfully,
- prayer,
- generosity,
- fidelity in hidden places,
- love that costs,
- hope anchored in God’s kingdom.

These are “heavenly” because they belong to the life of God and are not destroyed by moth, rust, or thieves. Their value is not merely future reward, though Jesus speaks of reward; they reorient the heart toward what truly lasts.

---

## 6. So does the verse say treasure reveals or trains the heart?

The best answer is:

> It says treasure reveals the heart, because the heart always follows the treasure; but it says even more that treasure trains the heart, because “where your treasure is, there your heart will be.”

More precisely:

- **Revealing sense:** “If I want to know your heart, tell me what you treasure.”
- **Forming sense:** “What you treasure will gradually make your heart.”

The grammar supports the second as the main force: **“there your heart will be.”** It is not a neutral observation. It is a warning: treasure is a magnet. If you place your treasure where it can corrupt, your heart will be corrupted too. If you place your treasure where God keeps it, your heart will be drawn toward heaven.

---

## Final synthesis

In Matthew 6:19–21, possession, attention, and love become tied together like this:

**Possession** begins when you choose what you will store and rely on.  
**Attention** grows as what you store becomes what you think about, worry about, and return to.  
**Love** follows as your heart becomes settled in the place of your treasure.

Jesus’ warning is not simply, “Care about the right things.” It is deeper:

> “Be careful what you treasure, because what you treasure will eventually become the place of your heart.”

Or in one sentence:

> You show your heart by your treasure, but you shape your heart by what you treasure.
