The difference is between **doing what is commanded from the outside** and **being changed so that obedience grows from within**.

### External compliance versus a “new heart”

External compliance can regulate behavior without changing its source. A person may obey because of pressure, fear of consequences, habit, or concern for reputation, while remaining inwardly resistant. The conduct changes; the underlying disposition may not.

In biblical language, the **heart** is more than the seat of emotions. It is the center of thought, desire, judgment, and will. Receiving a “new heart,” then, means a transformation of the person’s orientation toward God—not merely stronger feelings or better self-discipline.

The outward act might look the same in both cases. The difference is that, with a new heart, obedience expresses a developing inward responsiveness rather than merely submission to an external demand. This does not imply instant moral perfection.

### Why stone must be replaced with flesh

The material imagery makes the depth of the problem vivid:

- **Stone** is rigid and unresponsive. It does not receive an impression or respond to a living relationship.
- **Flesh** is living, sensitive, and responsive. Here, “flesh” is positive: it means a living heart, not sinful desire.

The problem is therefore not presented as a few bad habits attached to an otherwise healthy center. The center itself has become resistant. God does not promise simply to polish the stone or write more instructions on it; he promises to **remove and replace it**. The metaphor portrays renewal as something deeper than moral refurbishment.

Verse 27 explains what makes that renewal possible: **“I will put my spirit within you.”** The repeated “I will” emphasizes God’s initiative. Yet the result is still human action: “ye shall keep my judgments, and do them.” Divine renewal enables lived obedience rather than making obedience irrelevant.

In the passage’s larger setting—God’s promised restoration of Israel after exile—this is also a communal, covenantal promise, not only an account of individual inward experience.

In short: **external compliance changes what a person does; the new heart changes the inward source from which the person acts.** Stone becoming flesh depicts the movement from resistance to living responsiveness.
