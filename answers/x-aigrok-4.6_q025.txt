**The craving to be seen (the motive), not visibility itself.**

The verse does not condemn public almsgiving or good works as such. It condemns doing them *in order to be seen* (“to be seen of them”). The purpose clause is decisive: the moral danger is the desire for human applause, which converts the act into a transaction for earthly honor and thereby forfeits the Father’s reward. Visibility is incidental; the heart’s orientation is not.

This is consistent with the surrounding teaching in Matthew 6 (alms, prayer, fasting): Jesus repeatedly contrasts “before men, to be seen” with “in secret,” and He Himself performed public acts of mercy. The problem is hypocrisy and vainglory—using righteousness as a performance—rather than the mere fact that others notice. When the craving for recognition is absent, public goodness can remain oriented toward God.
