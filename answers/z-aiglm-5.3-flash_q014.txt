## Both are true — but the grammar tips the scale

The verse says the heart *follows* the treasure: "where your treasure **is** … there your heart **will be**." Note the tenses in the Greek: the treasure ἐστιν (present — it already lies where you put it), while the heart ἔσται (future — it is on its way). The heart lags behind the ledger and travels toward it. That single future verb is why the passage is an engine more than a mirror: Jesus is not primarily telling you to read your bank statement to discover your heart; he is telling you that you can *move* your heart by moving your deposits.

And the logic of the passage demands this. Verses 21 begins with γάρ ("for") and grounds the two commands of vv. 19–20. If treasure only *revealed* the heart, the commands would be pointless — you'd change nothing, you'd merely learn something. But if treasure *leads* the heart, then commanding a change of vault is precisely how you command a change of affection. The imperative frame makes formation the point. (Revelation is still real — James 5:2–3 says corroded riches become "evidence" against you — but that is a byproduct of a principle whose engine is directional.)

## How the three become tied together

**Possession: treasure as stored life.** The command is a cognate play — θησαυρίζετε θησαυρούς, "treasure up treasures" — and it is not forbidden as such, only located: "for yourselves"… in *which* vault? To lay up treasure is to project your labor, your security, your future self into an object. The object now carries a claim on you, and its enemies become yours: moth (decay from within), corrosion (βρῶσις, "eating"), thieves *digging through* the mud-brick wall (the sudden threat from without). Possession is therefore vulnerability, and vulnerability mandates vigilance. This is why the passage flows straight into anxiety (6:25–34): earthly treasure generates perpetual watching.

**Attention: the eye between the vault and the heart.** Verses 22–23 sit between treasure and masters for a reason. The "single/healthy eye" (ἁπλοῦς — which also connotes generosity) versus the "evil eye" (the standard Jewish idiom for stinginess and covetousness; cf. Matt 20:15) shows that *how you behold wealth* is itself moral, and it lamps or darkens the whole inner life. Attention is the funnel: what you have banked, you must watch; what you watch, you begin to want.

**Love: attention consolidated into loyalty.** Verse 24 completes the chain — "you cannot serve God and mammon." In biblical idiom, love is not first a feeling but a *service*: sustained attention ripening into trust, desire, and allegiance (Deut 6:5). So the loop closes: possession → attention → love → more possession. Treasure is love made material; love is treasure converted back into devotion.

## Why this matters for reading Jesus' strategy

The surrounding chapter makes the mechanism explicit. In 6:1–18, almsgiving, prayer, and fasting are all to be done ἐν τῷ κρυπτῷ, "in secret," where "your Father who sees" is the auditor. Strikingly, in 6:6 Jesus says to enter your **ταμιεῖον** — your storeroom, the very chamber where treasure was kept. Two vaults, then: the earthly storeroom exposed to moth and burglars, and the secret room with the Father, which no thief can tunnel into because its location is the Father's own gaze. Luke makes the deposit procedure even plainer: "Sell, give alms… provide a treasure in the heavens that does not fail" (Luke 12:33–34).

So the command is not that God needs your deposits. It is that *you* need the relocation of your heart, and only deposits will carry it there. The inversion is beautiful: you keep treasure in heaven (6:20), and heaven keeps an inheritance for you (1 Pet 1:4) — your heart ends up where your wealth is kept by God.

## Synthesis

Possession, attention, and love are one gradient. What you possess demands your vigilance; vigilance ripens into desire; desire matures into the service Scripture calls love. Treasure therefore both *reveals* the heart (as testimony) and *trains* it (as discipline) — two edges of one principle. As Augustine put it, love is the weight of the soul; it falls toward what it banks. Jesus' counsel is accordingly not "check your feelings" but "change your address": move the vault, and the heart will make the journey after it.
