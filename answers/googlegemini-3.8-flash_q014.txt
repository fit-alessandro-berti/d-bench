In Matthew 6:19–21, Jesus presents a psychological and spiritual principle that is both diagnostic and formative, though the grammar and context indicate that **treasure primarily *trains* (or directs) the heart, even as it inevitably *reveals* it.**

Here is an analysis of how this dynamic works and how possession, attention, and love are bound together.

---

### 1. Reveals vs. Trains: The Direction of the Gravity

While modern readers often read verse 21 as a passive diagnostic—*“Look at your bank statement to see what you love”* (revelation)—the syntax and the context of the Sermon on the Mount make it **active and pedagogical (training).**

* **The grammatical sequence:** Jesus does not say, *“Where your heart is, there you will put your treasure.”* That would mean the heart leads and the treasure merely reflects it. Instead, he reverses the direction: **“Where your treasure is, there will your heart be also.”** Treasure is the anchor; the heart is the boat dragged along in its wake.
* **The imperative context:** Verses 19 and 20 are active commands: *“Do not store up...”* and *“Store up...”* Jesus is commanding a deliberate physical action (how one allocates material resources) precisely to guide an internal affection. 

Therefore, **treasure trains the heart.** If you intentionally place your resources, time, and security into heaven (e.g., radical generosity, care for the poor, kingdom work), your affections and desires will eventually follow. However, because this principle is an unbreakable law of human nature, it inevitably **reveals** the heart as well: wherever a person has committed their resources, their deepest loyalties are laid bare.

---

### 2. How Possession, Attention, and Love Become Tied Together

Jesus illustrates an inescapable psychological loop connecting what we own, what we think about, and what we love.

#### A. Possession Creates Vulnerability
Jesus characterizes earthly treasure by its fragility: *moth, rust, and thieves.* 
To possess earthly treasure is to invite vulnerability. The moment you accumulate physical wealth or status, you enter into a state of defense. You must preserve it from decay (natural loss) and theft (hostile agents). 

#### B. Vulnerability Commands Attention
Because earthly possessions are perishable and insecure, they demand **vigilance**. 
* Where you store your treasure is where your **attention** will inevitably drift. 
* If your treasure is in the stock market, your attention will be tied to financial news. 
* If your treasure is your reputation, your attention will obsess over what people say about you. 
The threat of "moth and rust" forces a person into constant, anxious maintenance. Attention is not neutral; it is consumed by whatever carries the greatest perceived risk to our well-being.

#### C. Attention Cultivates Love (The Heart)
In the biblical worldview, the "heart" (*kardia*) is not merely the seat of fleeting emotion, but the central engine of the human being—the source of will, desire, allegiance, and ultimate love. 

**Love follows attention, and attention follows investment.** You cannot maintain an ongoing, vigilant interest in the preservation of an object without coming to love it, depend upon it, and serve it. By securing your life with earthly possessions, your heart becomes bound to the earth; by casting your lot with the imperishable things of God, your heart is freed from anxiety and anchored in eternity.

---

### Summary

The text ties these three together in a chain of cause and effect:
1. **Possession:** What you choose to store up and value.
2. **Attention:** The vigilance and mental energy required to sustain and protect that investment.
3. **Love:** The ultimate orientation of the heart, which inevitably worships whatever it relies upon for security and life.

Jesus's warning is that you cannot possess earthly treasure without that treasure possessing your attention, and eventually claiming your love. To cure an earthbound heart, one must begin by reallocating the treasure.
