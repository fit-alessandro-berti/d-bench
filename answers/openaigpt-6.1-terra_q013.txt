Amos rejects the worship so sharply because the issue is not that Israel has *too much worship* and too little justice, as though the two could be balanced against each other. The worshippers are treating religious devotion as compatible with a society that oppresses the poor, takes bribes, and denies justice in the courts (Amos 5:11–12).

The fraud is the claim to honor God while disregarding what God demands of them. Their feasts, sacrifices, and music present an image of faithfulness; meanwhile, their conduct toward vulnerable people contradicts it. The ceremonies may even reassure them that God approves of a social order that he condemns.

That is why God calls their songs “noise” and refuses their offerings. The problem is not music, festivals, or sacrifice in themselves. It is **worship being used as a substitute for righteousness—and as cover for injustice**.

“Let judgment run down as waters, and righteousness as a mighty stream” calls for justice to flow continuously through communal life, not appear only occasionally alongside religious observance. Worship and justice are not rival duties to be kept in balance; the prophet insists that genuine devotion to God must be reflected in how people treat one another.
