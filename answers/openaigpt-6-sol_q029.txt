Correction here calls for **humble, gentle responsibility**, not moral superiority. The aim is to *restore* the person—to help them return to faithful living—rather than to shame or defeat them.

Paul keeps restoration, meekness, and self-suspicion together because each guards the others. A desire to restore prevents correction from becoming mere condemnation. Meekness shapes how that help is given. And “considering thyself” reminds the corrector that they, too, are vulnerable to temptation—including the temptation to pride while correcting someone else. The fault should be taken seriously, but so should the person, and so should one’s own frailty.
