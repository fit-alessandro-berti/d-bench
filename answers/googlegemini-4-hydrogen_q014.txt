### 1. Does treasure *reveal* the heart, or *train* the heart?

Looking closely at the logic and phrasing of the passage, Jesus’s primary point is that **treasure trains (or directs) the heart**, though it secondarily ends up **revealing** it as well.

* **The primary emphasis: Treasure trains and steers the heart.**
  Notice the order of the argument. Jesus does not begin by saying, *"Change your heart, and then your spending will change."* Instead, he gives a command about concrete action—*"Lay not up for yourselves treasures upon earth... But lay up for yourselves treasures in heaven"* (vv. 19–20)—and then grounds that command with the word *"For"*: *"For where your treasure is, there **will** your heart be also"* (v. 21).
  The future tense (*"will be"*) is crucial. It describes a directional pull: **your heart follows your investment.** If you deliberately relocate what you value and store up toward heaven, your inner life and affections are trained to follow it there. Conversely, if you invest heavily in fragile earthly goods, your heart is tethered to the earth.

* **The secondary implication: Treasure reveals the heart.**
  While v. 21 states a forward-looking principle (*where you put it, your heart will go*), it also functions diagnostically in the present. Because the heart reliably trails after one's treasure, looking at where a person has already laid up their resources inevitably **reveals** where their heart currently lives. Still, in the flow of Jesus's teaching, the diagnostic insight serves the practical command: you can reshape your heart by changing where you place your treasure.

### 2. How possession, attention, and love tie together

In these three verses, **possession**, **attention**, and **love** form a single chain: what you store up captures your vigilance, and what captures your vigilance captures your devotion.

1. **Possession (*"Lay up for yourselves treasures"*):**
   "Laying up" (*thesauroús*—storing or hoarding) is an act of staking your security and surplus in a particular realm. Possession here is not passive; it is an intentional choice about where to build a reserve.

2. **Attention (shaped by value and vulnerability):**
   Jesus highlights the mechanics of earthly possession by naming its threats: *moth, rust,* and *thieves* (v. 19). Because earthly treasures are perishable and stealable, owning and storing them demands constant attention—maintenance, guarding, worry, and monitoring. Even when treasure is stored in heaven (v. 20), placing your investment there shifts your attention toward what endures rather than what decays. Whatever you try to secure requires your mental energy and focus.

3. **Love (*"there will your heart be also"*):**
   In biblical usage, the **heart** is not merely emotion; it is the center of desire, loyalty, will, and love—the seat of what a person truly lives for. By saying the heart follows the treasure, Jesus shows that love is not purely spontaneous or detached from daily choices. 

Put together, the passage maps out a cycle of attachment:
* **Possession** decides *what you invest in and try to secure*.
* **Attention** locks onto *protecting and caring for that investment* (especially when it is threatened by decay or theft).
* **Love** (*the heart*) takes root *where your hope, security, and attention are parked*.

You cannot keep your treasure on earth and expect your love to stay in heaven—because whatever you commit yourself to storing and guarding will eventually own your heart.
