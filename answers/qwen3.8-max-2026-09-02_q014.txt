The text says, most strictly, that **the heart follows treasure**. That single idea can be read both ways:

- **Treasure reveals the heart**, because what you store up exposes what you truly value.
- **Treasure trains the heart**, because where you deliberately store value shapes where your desires, hopes, and loyalties will go.

Jesus does not say, “Where your heart is, there your treasure will be.” He reverses it: **“Where your treasure is, there your heart will be also.”** The heart is portrayed less as an independent origin of desire than as something that is drawn, tethered, and formed by what we treat as valuable.

So the passage is both diagnostic and formative. It diagnoses: *look at your treasure and you will see your heart.* It also instructs: *put your treasure in heaven, and your heart will learn to live there.*

---

## 1. Treasure reveals the heart

In biblical language, the “heart” is not merely emotion. It is the inner center of a person: thought, will, desire, loyalty, imagination, and moral direction. To say that the heart is where your treasure is means that your deepest orientation is disclosed by what you are storing up.

Earthly treasure reveals an earthbound heart: a heart seeking security, status, control, or permanence in things that moth and rust corrupt and thieves can steal. Heavenly treasure reveals a heart oriented toward God and his kingdom.

So if someone asks, “What do I really love?” Jesus gives a searching test: look at what you are accumulating, protecting, and relying upon. Treasure is a mirror of the heart.

---

## 2. Treasure trains the heart

But the passage is not merely a passive observation. Jesus gives commands:

> “Lay not up for yourselves treasures upon earth…”  
> “Lay up for yourselves treasures in heaven…”

This implies agency. We are to choose where we store our treasure. And that choice is not neutral; it forms us.

If you continually invest in what is perishable, your heart becomes anxious, grasping, and temporary. If you invest in what is eternal — generosity, righteousness, mercy, faithfulness, prayer, seeking God’s kingdom — your heart is drawn toward God. Treasure becomes a kind of school for the heart.

In that sense, Jesus is not only telling us to discover what we love. He is telling us to place our treasure where our love ought to be, because the heart will follow.

---

## 3. Possession, attention, and love become tied together

The passage binds three things tightly:

### Possession

“Treasure” is what we store up as our security and good. But Jesus subtly shows that what we possess begins to possess us. Earthly wealth claims our care, our fear, our planning, and our hope. It promises permanence, but moth, rust, and thieves expose its fragility.

Possession here is not simply ownership. It is trust. It is where we place our future.

### Attention

Where treasure is stored, attention gathers. The heart is drawn to what is valuable. Later in the same chapter Jesus speaks of the eye:

> “If your eye is healthy, your whole body will be full of light…”  
> — Matthew 6:22

That reinforces the point: treasure fixes the gaze. What we store up becomes what we look at, think about, worry over, and return to. Attention is the bridge between treasure and the heart.

### Love

Love, in this context, is not mainly sentimental feeling. It is ultimate loyalty and delight. Whatever consistently receives our attention and is treated as our treasure becomes what we love. If our treasure is earthly, our love is bound to the perishable. If our treasure is heavenly, our love is fixed on God and what belongs to his kingdom.

So the movement is something like this:

> What we possess as treasure captures our attention;  
> what captures our attention shapes our desire;  
> what shapes our desire becomes what we love;  
> and what we love becomes the home of the heart.

---

## In short

Jesus is saying that the heart is not detached from our treasure. Treasure is both a **sign** of the heart and a **school** for the heart. What we store up shows what we love, and what we choose to store up trains us to love it more.

Therefore the command to “lay up treasures in heaven” is not merely about future reward. It is about present formation. Jesus calls us to place our treasure where it cannot decay, so that our attention, affection, and loyalty may be anchored in God rather than in what perishes.
