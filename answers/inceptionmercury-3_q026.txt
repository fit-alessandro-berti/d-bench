Jesus’s image is both stark and vivid: the heart is a hidden storeroom, and the mouth is its outlet. In Matthew 12:34–35, He is answering the Pharisees, who have just accused Him of casting out demons by the power of Satan. He calls them a “brood of vipers” and then explains why their words give them away.

So, are words symptoms, choices, revelations, or all three? The passage suggests **all three—with the emphasis on revelation and symptom**.

## 1. Words are symptoms

> “For out of the abundance of the heart the mouth speaks.” (Matthew 12:34)

Jesus compares speech to an overflow. The heart is like a container filled to the brim, and the mouth is the place where the excess spills out. If a person’s heart is filled with bitterness, anger, lust, pride, or unbelief, those things will eventually leak into their words. Conversely, if the heart is full of faith, love, gratitude, and holiness, those qualities will show up in speech.

That is why Jesus asks, “How can you, being evil, speak good things?” The answer is that they cannot, any more than a poisoned spring can produce clean water. Words are **symptoms** of an inward condition—they are the observable signs of a hidden moral state.

## 2. Words are revelations

The “abundance” of the heart is not just any inner feeling; it is the deeply hidden moral stockpile of a person’s life. Jesus calls this stockpile a **treasure**:

> “A good man out of the good treasure of the heart brings forth good things: and an evil man out of the evil treasure brings forth evil things.” (Matthew 12:35)

The Greek word for treasure is *thēsauros*, which literally means a storehouse or treasury. The heart, in biblical thought, is the center of the whole inner person—thoughts, desires, intentions, memory, and will. What is stored there may be invisible to others, but speech reveals it. Jesus sees the Pharisees’ blasphemous accusation as a revelation of the evil treasure within them. Words make the hidden self public. They are not merely sounds; they are disclosures.

## 3. Words are choices

But the passage does not reduce speech to a mechanical symptom. Jesus speaks to the Pharisees as responsible moral agents. He says, “How can *you*, being evil, speak good things?”—implying that their evil speech is both an expression of their character and an act for which they are guilty.

A few verses later, Jesus makes this even more explicit:

> “But I say to you that for every idle word men may speak, they will give account of it in the day of judgment. For by your words you will be justified, and by your words you will be condemned.” (Matthew 12:36–37)

Words are not just random overflow; they are **chosen actions**. We choose what to say, and those choices matter. At the same time, our choices are shaped by what we have stored in our hearts. Over time, we either deposit good treasure—by filling our minds with Scripture, truth, prayer, and love—or we deposit evil treasure—by entertaining sin, resentment, pride, and lies. Then, when a moment of pressure comes, we speak out of that accumulated store.

## All three together

So the answer is **all three**:

- **Symptoms**, because speech is the visible sign of an invisible heart condition.
- **Revelations**, because speech exposes the hidden moral treasure of a person’s life.
- **Choices**, because we are accountable for what we say and for what we have allowed to fill our hearts.

The deepest point of the passage is that speech is not a neutral instrument. It is the overflow of what a person truly is. That is why Jesus uses it to expose the hypocrisy of the Pharisees: their words did not merely insult Him; they revealed that their hearts were stocked with evil. And by implication, the remedy is not merely to control one’s tongue, but to have the heart changed—so that the treasure inside becomes good, and the words that flow out of it become good too.
