Psalm 139 ends with a surprising request: after celebrating that God knows him completely and after expressing zeal for God’s justice, the psalmist does not say, “Defend me and comfort me.” He says, “Search me.” That is significant.

He asks to be searched because he knows that **comfort and defense can leave the self unexamined**. Comfort may soothe pain, and defense may protect a reputation, but neither necessarily exposes hidden motives, self-deception, or a “wicked way.” The psalmist wants more than relief; he wants truth. He trusts that God’s scrutiny is not rejection but loving refinement. God already knows him, but by asking to be searched, he places himself willingly under God’s truth: “I may not see myself rightly; show me what is really there.”

This models a particular kind of honesty:

- **Non-defensive honesty** — I do not need to protect my image before God.
- **Humble honesty** — I may be wrong about myself; my motives may be mixed.
- **Repentant honesty** — I am willing to name what is sinful or destructive, not rationalize it.
- **Hopeful honesty** — exposure is not the end; it leads to “lead me in the way everlasting.”

Notice that the prayer does not stop with “see if there be any wicked way.” It moves to “lead me.” So this is not morbid self-hatred or endless introspection. It is the honesty of someone who wants to be made whole, not merely made comfortable. It also guards against self-righteousness: before asking God to judge the wicked, the psalmist invites God to judge him.

So the honesty modeled here is: **“Know me truly, show me honestly, and lead me graciously.”** It is the posture of a person who trusts God’s love enough to be fully known and corrected.
