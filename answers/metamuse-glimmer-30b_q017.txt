Light is hated in John not because it burns, but because it reveals. In John's Gospel light is not a metaphor for pleasantness, it is a personal reality: "The Light has come into the world" is Christ, the one who makes what is real visible.

> "men loved darkness rather than light, because their deeds were evil"

The condemnation, the krisis, is not a sentence imposed from outside. It is self-chosen. Light exposes the gap between how we present ourselves and how we actually live. To love darkness is to love a place where that gap can be kept hidden.

**Truth is exposure.** To "do truth" in John 3:21 is not to recite correct propositions. It is to live in such a way that your deeds can stand in the light. Truth is correspondence with reality. The deeds of someone doing evil are not correspondences, they are contradictions. They require a cover.

**Exposure produces shame.** Shame is not just embarrassment at being caught. It is the pain of being seen clearly when you have been living under a false self-image. Evil deeds hate the light because the light *reproves* them - elencho in verse 20, to expose, to test, to bring to judgment. To be reproved is to have the pretense stripped off.

That is why the one who does evil does not come to the light, *lest his deeds should be reproved.* The fear is not primarily punishment, it is being known. To be known is to lose control of the story you tell about yourself. Darkness offers anonymity, the ability to act without an audience, to remain the author of your own identity. Light forces an audience: God, and ultimately yourself.

This is the primal human temptation visible already in Genesis 3. After the fall Adam and Eve hide. Hiding is not a solution to guilt, it is a symptom of it. The desire not to be seen clearly is the desire to stay in self-deception. As long as you remain in darkness you can believe your own excuses.

That is why the dynamic is inverted for the one who does truth. He comes to the light *that his deeds may be made manifest, that they are wrought in God.* He wants to be seen because being seen confirms that his life is real and not a performance. For him exposure is not shameful, it is vindicating. The light does not create goodness; it reveals whether it is there.

So light is hated precisely because it loves us enough to see us. Darkness lets us keep our deeds to ourselves. Light makes them manifest, and manifestation is the beginning of either shame that drives us further into hiding, or the relief of being finally seen and being able to live in God.
