Amos is not saying that Israel worships too much and practices justice too little. He is saying that **injustice has made a lie of its worship**. Earlier in the chapter, Amos accuses the people of trampling the poor, taking bribes, and denying the needy justice at the city gate, where disputes were heard. The same community that asks God to receive its offerings refuses a fair hearing to those who come before its judges.

The fraud is to treat participation in worship as evidence of God’s favor while ignoring what God requires. It need not be a conscious deception: people may sing with genuine feeling and still mistake that feeling for faithfulness. The sharpness of “I will not hear” breaks that illusion. What the worshippers hear as melody, God calls noise.

A call for better balance would leave room for the idea that a generous offering could offset a corrupt verdict. Amos denies that the two can be traded against each other. “Let judgment run down as waters” calls not for an occasional good deed alongside the feasts, but for justice to flow steadily through public life. Justice is not a rival to worship; it is what makes the community’s profession of loyalty to God credible.
