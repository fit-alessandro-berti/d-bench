The image is sharp because of its **absurd contrast**: a person with a huge beam lodged in his own eye is fussing over a tiny speck in someone else’s. The eye is especially apt: the very organ needed to see another’s fault is obstructed by the critic’s own. The picture is both comic and uncomfortable.

The passage condemns the **hypocrisy of noticing others’ faults while ignoring one’s own**—not the act of correction itself. Its key words are “first” and “then”: first remove the beam from your own eye; then you can see clearly enough to help your brother. Moral correction remains possible, but it should follow honest self-examination and be offered with humility rather than self-righteousness.
