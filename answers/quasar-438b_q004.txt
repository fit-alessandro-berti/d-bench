It is asking for **wholehearted, humbling self-distrust**: trust God not as a dogma against intellect, but as a discipline of not over-relying on your own judgment--not claiming that you can, by yourself, know or manage everything.

- **Humble self-distrust:**  
  You are finite, situated, fallible. You reason well much of the time, but your own understanding is limited, partial, sometimes proud. So you should withhold final authority from yourself: do not "lean" solely on your own understanding. Trust the Lord as a higher-than-human guide, and let your paths be directed by him.

- **Mere anti-intellectualism:**  
  This would be a blanket rejection of reason: "Don't think; just obey God." This Psalm line need not mean that. It is concerned with intellectual humility, not intellectual impotence.

So the trust asked for is faith in God as **guiding authority**, coupled with **deference to him in your ways**. This is trust because it involves relying on God for direction. It is humble because it asks you not to overestimate your own understanding.

It is not "worship God blindly," since the Psalm immediately qualifies the stance: God directs thy paths. That implies guidance over abdication. The Psalmist takes God as a superior court, not as a club with which to beat the intellect.
