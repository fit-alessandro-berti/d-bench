In Matthew 12:34–35, Jesus responds to the Pharisees after they accuse him of casting out demons by the power of Satan. Rather than treating their accusation as a mere tactical insult or a momentary lapse in judgment, Jesus traces their words back to their interior character. 

In doing so, he connects speech to hidden moral stockpiles through two vivid metaphors—**overflow** and **storage**—and frames words as **all three**: symptoms, revelations, and choices.

---

### 1. How Speech Connects to Hidden Moral Stockpiles

In biblical thought, the "heart" (*kardia*) is not merely the seat of emotion; it is the hidden command center of a person’s intellect, desires, loyalties, and moral will. Jesus uses two complementary images to show how this inner life governs the tongue:

* **The Metaphor of Overflow ("abundance of the heart"):** The Greek word translated as *abundance* (*perisseuma*) literally means "surplus" or "overflow." The image is of a vessel filled to the brim; whatever fills the interior inevitably spills out when jostled. Speech is not manufactured on the spot in the mouth—it is the spillover of what already saturates the inner self.
* **The Metaphor of the Storehouse ("treasure of the heart"):** The word *treasure* (*thesauros*) refers to a treasury, vault, or storehouse—a hidden place where things are accumulated and kept over time. Every person builds up an invisible stockpile of thoughts, grudges, affections, and values. When someone speaks, they "bring forth" (*ekballei*, literally "draw out" or "cast out") from that pre-existing inventory. 

Combined with the preceding verse about a tree and its fruit (v. 33), the passage argues for **moral continuity**: you cannot consistently draw out of your mouth what you have not deposited in your heart.

---

### 2. Symptoms, Revelations, or Choices?

Rather than choosing one of these categories, the passage weaves all three together into a single moral psychology:

#### Words as Symptoms
Jesus asks rhetorically, *"How can ye, being evil, speak good things?"* Just as a diseased tree naturally yields bad fruit (v. 33), a corrupted heart organically produces corrupted speech. In this sense, words are **symptoms** of an underlying condition. While a person might briefly fake polite or pious language, under pressure—or in unguarded moments—the true condition of the heart surfaces. Trying to fix evil speech without changing the heart is like taping healthy fruit onto a rotting tree.

#### Words as Revelations
Because a storehouse is hidden from view, no one can directly inspect another person’s heart. Speech therefore functions as a **revelation**—the mechanism that makes the invisible interior public. Just as a tree is *known* by its fruit, a person's hidden loyalties are exposed by their words. In context, the Pharisees appeared outwardly righteous, yet their slander against Jesus unmasked what they had actually been stockpiling within.

#### Words as Choices
If words were *only* automatic symptoms, moral accountability would break down. Yet Jesus immediately follows these verses by declaring that people will give an account on the day of judgment for every careless word, and by their words they will be justified or condemned (vv. 36–37). This underscores that words are also **choices**, operating on two levels:
1. **Active agency in speaking:** The verb "bringeth forth" implies deliberate action—like a steward reaching into a storeroom and pulling something out.
2. **Responsibility for the stockpile:** The prior command in verse 33 (*"make the tree good"*) implies that people are responsible for cultivating their inner character. Even when an "idle" or impulsive word slips out in the heat of the moment, it reflects countless earlier choices about what thoughts, resentments, or virtues a person allowed to accumulate in their heart.

---

### Summary

Matthew 12:34–35 rejects the common excuse, *"I didn't mean it; it just slipped out."* Instead, it presents speech as the point where hidden character becomes visible action. Words are **symptoms** because they flow organically from who we are; they are **revelations** because they expose what we hide inside; and they are **choices** because we are morally accountable both for what we say and for the inner storehouse we cultivate over time.
