In Ezekiel 36:26–27, the difference is between **conforming one’s behavior to a command** and **being inwardly renewed so that obedience grows from a changed disposition**.

### External compliance versus a “new heart”

External compliance can mean doing what is required because of pressure, fear of consequences, habit, or the desire to appear righteous. The action may be right while the person remains inwardly resistant.

A “new heart” reaches deeper. In biblical language, the **heart** is not merely the seat of feelings; it is the center of thought, desire, judgment, and will. God is promising to renew the people at the level from which their choices arise—not simply to impose better conduct.

The passage therefore does not oppose inward renewal to outward obedience. It joins them: “I will put my spirit within you, and cause you to walk in my statutes.” **Obedience is the intended expression of renewal, not a substitute for it.**

### Why describe hardness as a material condition?

The contrast between **stone and flesh** makes spiritual resistance almost tangible:

- **Stone** is unresponsive. It cannot feel, receive correction, or respond with love.
- **Flesh**, here, means living tissue: sensitive, responsive, capable of being moved. It does not carry the negative sense of “sinful nature” that “flesh” has in some other biblical contexts.

Calling the heart “stony” suggests that the problem is more than a lack of information or an occasional bad decision. The people’s resistance has become entrenched. More instructions alone will not make stone alive.

That is why the image is replacement rather than surface repair. God does not merely polish the stone into a more respectable shape; he gives a living heart in its place. The repeated **“I will”** emphasizes that this renewal is God’s gift and initiative.

In context, this is a promise of Israel’s restoration after defilement and exile, not simply advice about individual self-improvement. God promises to restore a people whose relationship with him has broken down, making renewed covenant faithfulness possible through his Spirit.

In short: **external compliance changes what a person does; a new heart changes the inward source from which the doing comes.** The material metaphor expresses how radical that change must be—from resistant and unresponsive to alive and receptive.
