Paul places bitterness and forgiveness side by side because they are opposing ways of carrying an injury. Bitterness holds onto a wrong in a way that can harden into anger and spill into “clamour” and “evil speaking.” Forgiveness answers the wrong with the grace one has received from God in Christ.

So “bitterness” here is **more than a passing feeling**. Feeling hurt or angry is not itself the same as being bitter. In this list, bitterness looks like a settled, nursed resentment—an inward posture that can become a habit and affect how people speak to and treat one another. In that sense, it can spread through a community.

Paul’s answer is not “pretend you weren’t hurt.” It is to put away what resentment is becoming and to practice kindness, tenderheartedness, and forgiveness. Forgiveness need not mean immediate trust or the removal of wise boundaries; it means refusing to make the wrong the basis for ongoing malice.
