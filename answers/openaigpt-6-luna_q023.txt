“Way” points to the person’s lived direction—the habits and actions others can see. “Thoughts” points to the intentions, plans, and inner outlook that shape those actions. Repentance therefore reaches the whole person: it is not merely stopping a behavior while inwardly holding on to the purposes behind it, nor feeling regret without changing direction.

“Thoughts” need not mean every passing or unwanted thought. Here it refers especially to the settled counsel and orientation of the unrighteous person. The call is to turn from both the path and the inner allegiance that sustains it.

The promise is that God will have mercy on the one who returns—not because repentance earns pardon, but because God is “abundant” in forgiving. In the surrounding verses, God’s thoughts and ways are higher than ours; that helps underscore how far divine mercy exceeds what people may expect. No part of the person’s former life is beyond the reach of that mercy.
