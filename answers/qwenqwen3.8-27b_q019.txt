

James 3:9–10 is exposing a **divided heart that tries to worship God while despising people**.

The contradiction is this:

> “With the same mouth we bless God, the Father, and curse human beings who are made in the likeness/image of God.”

The passage is not merely saying that people are inconsistent. It is showing a deeper spiritual disorder: **a person can honor God in the act of worship and dishonor God’s image in the act of contempt, all from the same mouth.**

The “inner contradiction” is a failure of love. The heart is giving God religious words on one side, while giving His image-bearers bitter words on the other. To bless God as “Father” while cursing people made after His similitude is to treat God and His family in a way that cancels out. It is like honoring a king while spitting on his son. It reveals a heart that has not truly brought its vertical devotion and horizontal relationships into alignment.

James says, “Out of the same mouth proceedeth blessing and cursing,” not only to note a practical inconsistency, but to expose a **moral and spiritual disorder**. The tongue becomes the place where the inner life leaks out. A person may say “God bless you,” “the Lord help you,” “we pray for you,” and then, when frustrated, angry, or contemptuous, call the same person names. The two streams come from the same source: the heart.

Speech reveals moral disorder so quickly because **words are one of the fastest expressions of the inner person**. Unlike some hidden motives, speech is immediate, public, relational, and hard to fully take back. It does not require much preparation or great effort. A person may be careful with actions, but the tongue moves quickly. It is the most visible sign of what is happening inside.

In a deeper sense, speech reveals disorder because it exposes **where a person truly honors God**. If we truly believe that people are made in the image of God, then how we speak to them cannot be detached from how we speak about God. When we speak harshly to people made in God’s likeness, we are not only sinning against them; we are also, in James’s logic, dishonoring the image of the One we claim to bless.

So the passage exposes this:

- **Worship and contempt from the same mouth.**
- **Love for God in words but contempt for God’s image-bearers in relationships.**
- **A divided allegiance: God is honored in the mouth while people are defamed in the same mouth.**
- **A heart that has not yet brought its piety and its treatment of others into harmony.**

James’s conclusion, “My brethren, these things ought not so to be,” is not just a mild observation. It is a warning that the tongue is one of the clearest mirrors of moral disorder. It reveals quickly what the heart has been nurturing: either love that flows toward God and neighbor alike, or a divided affections that blesses God with the lips and wounds people with the words.
