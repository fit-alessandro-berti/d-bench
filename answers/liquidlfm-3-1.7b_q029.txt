**The moral posture called for**

Paul’s instruction in Galatians 6:1 sets a posture of **humility, self‑examination and meekness**.  
He tells the spiritual brothers to:

1. **“Restore such an one in the spirit of meekness”** – i.e., to bring the sinner back to right standing **gently, without pride or domination**.  
2. **“Considering thyself, lest thou also be tempted”** – to **look at your own condition** and be alert to the ways you might be swayed by worldly desires.

In plain terms, the required attitude is:

- **Humility** – not boasting about your ability to fix others; you acknowledge your own weakness.  
- **Self‑scrutiny** – constant, honest examination of your motives and state.  
- **Meekness** – acting with patience, gentleness and a willingness to yield to God’s will, rather than imposing your own will forcefully.

---

### Why are *restoration*, *meekness* and *self‑suspicion* kept together?

| Element | What it does | How it fits with the others |
|---------|--------------|-----------------------------|
| **Self‑suspicion** (considering thyself) | Makes you aware of your own faults and the temptations that might pull you away from God. | Provides the **pre‑condition** for any correction. Only after you know where you stand can you safely offer help. |
| **Meekness** (spirit of meekness) | Guides the **manner** of correction—gentle, loving, non‑retaliatory. | Ensures that when you restore someone, you do so without causing shame or pride, preserving the other’s dignity. |
| **Restoration** (bringing the sinner back) | The actual act of healing and returning the person to right standing. | Is the **result** of the preceding steps: you first see your own failure (self‑suspicion), then you act with meekness, and finally you perform the restoration. |

Together they form a **coherent ethical loop**:

1. **Examine yourself** → you realize you’ve slipped into a fault.  
2. **Recognize the danger of temptation** → you stay alert to worldly influences.  
3. **Respond with meekness** → you offer correction in a humble, patient way.  
4. **Effectively restore** → the sinner is brought back to the path of righteousness.

If any link breaks down—say, you’re too proud to admit fault, or you’re too hard‑hearted to give a gentle hand—the risk of either repeating the same mistake or alienating the sinner rises sharply. That’s why the three ideas are presented side by side: they reinforce one another and keep the whole process spiritually sound.

---

### Bottom line

- **Required moral posture:** humility, honest self‑reflection, and meek service.  
- **Why they’re united:** Self‑suspicion gives you the insight; meekness determines how you deliver that insight; restoration is the tangible outcome. All three work together to prevent future temptation and to truly heal the broken relationship between the believer and God.
