The text suggests that goodness is a practiced form of love and justice, with restraint as only its threshold.

**Ceasing evil leaves a space that must be filled.**
If the passage ended at "cease to do evil," the ideal person would be merely harmless. But a person can wrong no one and still be useless to everyone. Isaiah treats innocence as the clearing of ground, not the building.

**The context shows that the evil was partly neglect.**
A few verses later, the indictment reads: "they judge not the fatherless, neither doth the cause of the widow come unto them" (1:23). The people's sin lay in what they failed to do as well as what they did. So "cease to do evil" and "learn to do well" are not fully separable: one cannot stop the evil of indifference except by starting to care.

**"Learn" implies that goodness is a skill.**
One doesn't learn to refrain; one simply stops. But doing good must be learned, like a craft, through attention, practice, and correction. It also takes discernment: "seek judgment" suggests that justice isn't always obvious and has to be searched out.

**The good is immediately made concrete.**
Isaiah doesn't leave "do well" abstract. It means seeking justice, relieving the oppressed, defending the fatherless, and pleading for the widow. These verbs are active and directed at people who have no advocate and cannot repay. Goodness here is not an inner state of purity but taking up someone else's cause.

**This is the washing.**
Just before this, God rejects the people's sacrifices and prayers because "your hands are full of blood" (1:15). The command to "wash you" is answered not with more ritual but with justice. Hands are made clean by being put to work for the vulnerable.

**The order still matters.**
Restraint comes first for a reason: one cannot plead for the widow while defrauding her. But Scripture consistently refuses to stop there. "Depart from evil, and do good" (Ps. 34:14) has the same two-step shape, as does Ephesians 4:28, where the thief is told not only to stop stealing but to work so that he has something to give.
