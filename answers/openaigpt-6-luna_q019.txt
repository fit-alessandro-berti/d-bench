James is exposing a split between **the God we claim to honor and the people we are willing to dishonor**. We bless God, yet curse people who bear God’s likeness. The contradiction is not merely that our words are inconsistent; it is that our worship has not shaped how we regard God’s creatures.

Speech reveals this disorder quickly because it gives outward form to our inner posture. Words can spill out before we have carefully managed our image: contempt, anger, or cruelty may show what reverence for God has failed to reach. In James’s picture, the same mouth cannot coherently praise the Creator while treating his image-bearers with contempt.

That does not mean every careless word defines a person. James’s point is that a pattern of blessing and cursing calls for self-examination—and change. “These things ought not so to be.”
