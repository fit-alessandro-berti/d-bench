

The passage rejects worship sharply because the rituals are not merely imbalanced; they are offensive in their context. The people maintain elaborate ceremonies while actively perpetuating social injustice. Suggesting "better balance" would imply the worship itself is valid, but God declares it detestable due to the moral dissonance between their liturgy and their lives.

The fraud exposed is hypocrisy: using religious performance to mask ethical failure. It assumes God can be appeased by sacrifices and songs while ignoring the poor and the covenantal call for righteousness. The text demands that ethical integrity precede or replace empty ceremony, insisting that true devotion is measured by justice, not just ritual.
