

Amos 5:21–24 does **not** reject worship because worship is inherently bad. It rejects this particular worship because it has become a **counterfeit** — a religious performance that claims God’s favor while the people’s lives are filled with injustice.

The sharpness of the rejection is the point.

## 1. This is not “imbalanced worship”; it is counterfeit worship

If God had merely said, “Your worship is good, but you need to be more just in daily life,” that would imply that their rituals still have independent value and can be combined with social sin. But Amos is saying something far more severe:

> Their worship has become a cover for unrighteousness.

They were observing feasts, sacrifices, songs, and solemn assemblies — but at the same time the community was marked by oppression, corruption, and injustice. In that context, their worship was not “lacking a little justice.” It was **using religion to avoid repentance**.

That is why God says, in effect:

- I will not “smell” your sacrifices.
- I will not regard your offerings.
- Take away your music from me.
- I will not hear it.

The language is deliberately harsh because the worship has become an offense. It is not merely incomplete; it is **deceptive**.

## 2. The fraud exposed: religion used as a substitute for obedience

The moral and spiritual fraud here is that worship had become a substitute for true covenant faithfulness.

Israel could come to the temple, bring offerings, sing songs, and celebrate festivals, and then return to lives marked by:

- oppression of the poor,
- dishonest business practices,
- corrupt leadership,
- perversion of justice,
- religious complacency,
- a false sense of security.

The deeper problem was not that they worshipped “too much” and acted rightly “too little.” The problem was that worship had become a way of saying:

> “We are God’s people. We keep the rites. Therefore we are acceptable.”

That is the spiritual fraud.

They were treating worship as though it could stand alone — as though sacred music, festivals, and sacrifices could secure God’s approval even while their hands were stained with injustice.

## 3. The “your” language is important

Notice the repeated possessive:

- **your** feast days
- **your** solemn assemblies
- **your** burnt offerings
- **your** meat offerings
- **your** peace offerings
- **your** songs
- **your** viols

This is not neutral. Amos is showing that the worship has become **their own** — a human performance, not God’s acceptable worship.

It has become a religious identity system rather than a response to God. They are not worshipping God in a way that flows from obedience; they are using worship to maintain their self-image as “the people of God.”

That is why God says, in effect: “This is not my worship. This is your performance.”

## 4. Why the rejection is so total

God rejects the whole package — sacrifices, feasts, music, solemn assemblies — because the problem is not one part of their religion. The problem is the whole religious system as it has become.

A few reasons:

### A. The worship was a claim on God’s favor

Their offerings and feasts implied fellowship with God. But a people who oppress the vulnerable cannot claim the blessing and protection of a God who commands justice.

So God refuses the claimed fellowship:

> I will not regard your peace offerings.

A peace offering was a communal meal associated with communion and favor. To refuse it is to say: “There is no communion between us under these conditions.”

### B. The worship was masking their sin

If God merely rebuked the injustice, they could respond, “But we keep the feasts. We bring the sacrifices.” The rituals could become their defense.

So Amos removes the cover.

The prophet is saying: your worship is not a solution. It is part of the problem. It is the mask behind which the real sin is hidden.

### C. The worship had become self-justifying

This is the deepest spiritual danger.

When people use external piety to prove their own righteousness, worship becomes a form of self-worship. The festival becomes a mirror in which they admire themselves rather than a place where they confront their sin before God.

The songs and music are not rejected because God dislikes music. They are rejected because the music has become the sound of a people who think they are holy.

So God says:

> Take away from me the noise of your songs.

Not because the melody is bad, but because it belongs to a religion that has no justice.

### D. The worship was disconnected from covenant life

Israel’s worship was not meant to be separate from covenant obedience. The same God who commanded burnt offerings also commanded:

- justice,
- mercy,
- protection of the poor,
- faithfulness in marriage,
- integrity in business,
- humility before God,
- love of neighbor.

When worship is separated from those obligations, it becomes empty.

That is why Amos says:

> But let judgment run down as waters,  
> and righteousness as a mighty stream.

This is the only acceptable “worship” in this context: a life flowing with justice and righteousness.

## 5. The moral/spiritual fraud in one sentence

The passage exposes the fraud of **public piety used to hide private and social corruption**.

More specifically, it exposes:

1. **Transactional religion** — the belief that rituals can buy divine approval.  
2. **Compartmentalized faith** — being “holy” on festival days and unjust in daily life.  
3. **Self-justification** — using worship as proof that one is already in right standing with God.  
4. **False security** — assuming that religious identity guarantees God’s blessing even amid sin.  
5. **Institutional hypocrisy** — maintaining the forms of religion while the community itself is unjust.

## 6. The contrast: worship versus justice

The final verse is the corrective:

> But let judgment run down as waters,  
> and righteousness as a mighty stream.

This image is powerful.

“Judgment” here means right ruling, justice, the establishment of what is right. “Righteousness” means covenant faithfulness, right relationship, moral and social integrity.

They are not to be occasional duties. They are to flow like water — continuously, abundantly, inevitably, and with force.

In other words, true religion is not a festival interrupted by life. True religion is a stream of justice shaping the whole community.

## 7. The bottom line

The passage rejects worship so sharply because the worship has become a **lie**.

It is not that God wants “balance” between ritual and ethics. It is that their worship has become a counterfeit of religion — an outward performance that claims to honor God while violating the heart of God’s covenant.

The fraud is this:

> They are offering God the sound of religion while refusing to live out the reality of God’s righteousness.

So God refuses the offerings, the feasts, and the music. Not because He hates worship, but because He hates worship that is disconnected from justice, mercy, and repentance.
