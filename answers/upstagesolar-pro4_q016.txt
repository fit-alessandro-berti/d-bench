Based on the text of Luke 15:28–32 and the theological context of the Parable of the Prodigal Son, the elder brother's deepest problem is best understood as a combination of **spiritual pride in his obedience** which fundamentally manifests as a **discomfort with mercy itself**.

Here is the breakdown of why this is the core issue:

1.  **Pride in Obedience (The Root):** The elder brother defines his relationship with his father through transactional faithfulness ("I serve thee, neither transgressed I..."). He believes his obedience earns him status and rewards. He sees himself not as a son loved by his father, but as a **servant** who has fulfilled a contract ("Lo, these many years do I serve thee"). This indicates a heart motivated by duty and merit rather than love and relationship.
2.  **Wounded Fairness (The Symptom):** His complaint ("thou never gavest me a kid") stems from a mindset of meritocracy. He believes goodness should be rewarded systematically. When grace is given to the wasteful brother, it violates his sense of justice because he does not understand that the father's love is not a wage to be earned but a gift to be shared.
3.  **Discomfort with Mercy (The Heart Issue):** Ultimately, his refusal to enter the feast ("would not go in") shows that his transactional mindset cannot accommodate unmerited grace. He cannot rejoice in the father's mercy because he relies on his own righteousness. His anger toward the brother is secondary to his lack of compassion for the brother and his misunderstanding of the father's heart.

**Conclusion:**
While anger and wounded fairness are the visible symptoms, they are fueled by **pride in obedience**. He believes he is "better" than the younger brother, and therefore deserves more. This pride creates a barrier that makes **mercy itself** impossible for him to accept or celebrate. He loves the reward of his service more than he loves his father's heart for the lost.
