In context, Jesus is answering the Pharisees’ claim that his work was empowered by evil (Matthew 12:24–32). He has just compared a tree with its fruit: what comes out of a person, like fruit from a tree, discloses the source it comes from.

The “treasure” is the heart’s accumulated moral and spiritual orientation—what a person loves, believes, desires, and nurses. “Out of the abundance” means that speech is not detached from that inner life: when the heart is full of malice or contempt, those things tend to spill into words; when it is oriented toward what is good, good words can flow from it.

So the passage presents speech as **all three**:

- **A symptom:** words are outward signs of an inward condition.
- **A revelation:** they make the heart’s hidden commitments visible, as the Pharisees’ accusation revealed their hostility.
- **A choice:** the words are still morally accountable actions, not merely involuntary evidence. Jesus goes on to say people will answer for their words (12:36–37).

The image does not mean every careless phrase perfectly diagnoses someone’s whole character, or that a person is trapped forever by their present “stockpile.” Its force is that speech has a source: persistent patterns of words expose what the heart is storing and cherishing, and speakers are responsible for what they bring forth.
