# Speech as Overflow from the Heart's Treasury

This passage is a remarkable piece of moral psychology compressed into two verses, and the answer to your question — **all three** — emerges when you examine the precise metaphors Jesus selects and the causal logic he builds.

---

## The Two Metaphors That Govern Everything

### 1. *Abundance* (περισσεύματος — perisseumatos)
> "Out of the **abundance** of the heart the mouth speaketh."

This word means *surplus, overflow, excess*. It carries hydraulic imagery: a reservoir that is full will spill over its rim. The implication is **symptomatic** — speech is not manufactured on demand but *discharges* from whatever the heart holds in excess. One does not choose to overflow; overflow happens whenever content exceeds containment.

### 2. *Treasure* (θησαυροῦ — thesaurou)
> "Out of the good **treasure** of the heart bringeth forth good things..."

A *thesaurus* is a **storehouse** — accumulated, inventoried wealth. This metaphor introduces something abundance alone does not: **the store was built**. Treasure is *amassed over time* through prior choices, exposures, loyalties, and dispositions. And the verb **ἐκβάλλει** (*ekballei* — "bringeth forth," literally "throw out, produce") introduces **agency** into the extraction. One draws out of the storehouse what one decides to display.

---

## Three Layers Held in Tension

### **Words as Revelations**
The heart's contents are *hidden*. No one can see the moral stockpile directly. Speech **unmasks** it. This is precisely what happens in context: the Pharisees declare that Jesus casts out demons by Beelzebub (Matt 12:24), and Jesus reads that declaration as a **diagnostic window** into their hearts. Their words exposed what they thought they had concealed — a reservoir of evil. Jesus didn't need to speculate about their inner lives; their tongues **testified** against them.

> Words function as **evidence brought into the open** from a previously sealed archive.

### **Words as Symptoms**
The causal grammar is unmistakable: *ek* ("out of") + *perisseumatos* ("abundance") establishes a **causal chain** from heart → mouth. In this framing, words are **byproducts of an underlying condition**, like a fever revealing infection. The impossibility rhetoric confirms this: *"How can ye, being evil, speak good things?"* The answer the rhetorical question demands is: **they cannot**. Evil hearts *cannot* produce good speech any more than a thorn bush produces figs (the parallel in v. 33). The evil is not in the *act of speaking well or badly*; the evil is **prior and deeper**.

> Words function as **effects caused by a moral condition already in place**.

### **Words as Choices**
And yet — **ἐκβάλλει** (*ekballei*) is a **deliberative verb**. One *brings forth*, *produces*, *throws out*. This is not an involuntary spasm but an act of extraction from the storehouse. Moreover, the entire passage is structured as **accusation and judgment**: Jesus is *holding these people accountable* for what they have said. If words were merely symptoms — if they carried no moral weight of their own — the rhetoric of judgment would collapse. The fact that Jesus places such weight on the blasphemy of the Holy Spirit (v. 31–32) and warns that "every idle word" will be judged (v. 36) proves that words are **morally significant acts**, not merely passive indicators.

> Words function as **accountable acts performed by a moral agent**.

---

## The Synthesis: A Storehouse Built by Choices, Overflowing by Nature, Revealing by Spill

The treasure metaphor is the key that reconciles all three:

| Moment | Function | Mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| **Past** | The storehouse is *built* | Choices, loyalties, exposures sediment into character over time — this is **agency** |
| **Present (overflow)** | The storehouse *discharges* | What is held in abundance spills out naturally — this is **symptom** |
| **Present (extraction)** | The storehouse is *raided* | One *brings forth* what one decides to present — this is **choice** |
| **Effect** | The storehouse is *exposed* | What spills out reveals what was hidden — this is **revelation** |

So words are **all three simultaneously**:

- **Revelations** of what lies concealed in the heart
- **Symptoms** of a moral condition that precedes and causes them
- **Choices** — morally weighty acts for which the speaker bears responsibility

---

## Why This Triune Function Matters

The brilliance of the passage is that it refuses to let us resolve the tension in one direction:

- If words were **only** symptoms, we could excuse people for what they say ("I couldn't help it — my heart made me do it").
- If words were **only** choices disconnected from character, we could let someone speak well while living evilly, and Jesus explicitly denies this possibility ("how can ye, *being evil*, speak good things?").
- If words were **only** revelations, they would be neutral windows with no moral gravity of their own — yet Jesus warns of *judgment* for idle words.

The passage insists on a **layered causality**: character (formed by prior choices) constrains and produces speech (itself an accountable act) which reveals the hidden character. The tongue is both **window and weapon** — it exposes the self *and* it sins on its own account.

This is why Jesus says in v. 36 that *"every idle word that men shall speak, they shall give account thereof in the day of judgment."* Words are not merely indicators. They are **ledger entries** — drawn from a moral treasury that is being audited.
