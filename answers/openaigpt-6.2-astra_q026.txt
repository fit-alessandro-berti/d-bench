**All three—but revelation is the emphasis.** Jesus pictures the heart as a storehouse of moral dispositions. “Out of the abundance” suggests overflow: what fills the heart tends to find its way into speech. In that sense, words are **symptoms** of an inward condition. But “bringeth forth” also describes something a person *does*: speaking is an act for which the speaker is responsible, not merely an involuntary symptom.

The context shows how the three fit together. The Pharisees have attributed Jesus’ healing work to demonic power. Their accusation is a **choice** about how to describe what they have seen, and that choice **reveals** their hostility. The warning is not that one stray remark discloses everything about a person. It is that words matter both for what we do by saying them and for what they disclose about the heart from which they come.
