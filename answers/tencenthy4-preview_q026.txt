In this passage Jesus is not giving a mere communication tip. He is diagnosing the Pharisees. They have just accused him of casting out demons by Satan (Matt 12:22–32), and Jesus treats their words as the audible evidence of an invisible condition.

The short answer is: **all three**. Words are at once **symptoms/fruit**, **revelations**, and **responsible choices**—three ways of describing the same act.

## 1. The heart as a hidden treasury

Jesus uses two overlapping images: **tree/fruit** (v. 33) and **treasure/overflow** (vv. 34–35).

In biblical language, the “heart” (*kardia*) is the hidden control center of the person—mind, will, affections, desires, and moral direction. Jesus pictures it as a **treasure-house** (*thesauros*): a storehouse or vault where a person’s true riches are kept (cf. Matt 6:19–21; 13:52).

That “treasure” is moral. There is a **good treasure** and an **evil treasure**. It consists of what a person has stored up over time: loves, thoughts, beliefs, memories, intentions, grudges, fears, and ultimate allegiances. The “abundance” (*perisseuma*) is the fullness or overflow of that store. The mouth does not invent something new; it **dispenses what is already in the vault**.

This is also why Jesus calls them a **“generation of vipers.”** A viper’s bite releases venom that was already stored in the creature. Their blasphemous accusation against the Holy Spirit’s work is poison from a poisoned heart.

## 2. Words as symptoms / fruit

If by “symptom” you mean “outward manifestation of an inward condition,” then yes—Scripture presents speech that way here.

> “Out of the abundance of the heart the mouth speaketh.”  
> “A good man out of the good treasure… bringeth forth good things: and an evil man out of the evil treasure… bringeth forth evil things.”

The movement is **heart → mouth**. Speech is the overflow, the fruit, the withdrawal from the heart’s account. Earlier in the chapter Jesus says, “the tree is known by his fruit” (12:33). A good tree cannot ultimately produce bad fruit, and a corrupt tree cannot ultimately produce good fruit.

So words are not cosmetic. They are **organically connected** to the heart. An evil heart will, by its own inner logic, bring forth evil speech. In that sense, speech is a kind of spiritual symptom: it tells you what is really inside.

But it is important to say: this is not a passive symptom like a cough or a fever. It is the **fruit** the whole person bears.

## 3. Words as revelations

Because the heart is hidden, speech is how the heart becomes **known**.

Jesus says the tree is “known by his fruit.” The Pharisees claim to be evaluating Jesus, but their own words reveal *them*. By calling the work of the Spirit “Beelzebul,” they disclose what is in their treasury: unbelief, envy, hardness, and opposition to God.

This revelatory role becomes explicit in the next verses:

> “Every idle word that men shall speak, they shall give account thereof in the day of judgment. For by thy words thou shalt be justified, and by thy words thou shalt be condemned” (Matt 12:36–37).

Words are not trivial. On the day of judgment they will function as **public evidence** of the heart’s hidden contents. “By your words you will be justified or condemned” does not mean verbosity saves or that a careless slip automatically damns apart from grace; it means words are the audible form of faith or unbelief, and therefore the proper evidence of the heart’s direction.

Even “careless” or “idle” words matter because even they are withdrawals from the heart’s store.

## 4. Words as choices / responsible acts

This is where we must be careful. If words were only symptoms, the Pharisees could say, “My heart made me do it.” But Jesus holds them **fully accountable**.

He addresses them as moral agents:

> “O generation of vipers, how can ye, being evil, speak good things?”

That is not an excuse; it is an indictment. And he immediately adds that people will “give account” for their words.

The metaphor of treasure actually implies **accumulation by choice**. In Matthew 6, Jesus tells people to “lay up for yourselves treasures in heaven,” and says, “where your treasure is, there will your heart be also” (6:20–21). The heart follows what we treasure. Here, the heart’s treasure produces speech. The treasury is not an impersonal fate imposed on a person; it is the settled result of what that person has loved, practiced, and stored up (cf. Prov 4:23).

Also, Jesus can say in the same context, “Make the tree good” (12:33). That would be meaningless if speech were mere mechanical determinism. The organic connection between heart and speech does not remove responsibility; it establishes it. We are responsible for the kind of tree we are and for the treasure we are storing.

## 5. All three, together

So the relationships look like this:

- **Heart → word:** Speech flows from the heart’s abundance.  
  Therefore words are **fruit/symptoms** of the heart’s condition.

- **Word → heart:** Because the heart is hidden, speech makes it visible.  
  Therefore words are **revelations/signs** of the heart.

- **Person → word:** The speaker is not a passive patient but a moral agent.  
  Therefore words are **choices/acts** for which we are accountable.

None of these cancels the others. The fact that words flow “necessarily” from the heart does not make them involuntary reflexes. The heart *is* the person—his or her loves, beliefs, and will. What flows from the heart is therefore the person’s own act.

## The point

Jesus is saying, in effect:

> “The mouth spends the currency of the heart. What comes out of you is not accidental. It is your treasure becoming audible.”

For the Pharisees, their blasphemous speech revealed a venomous treasury. For anyone who would follow Jesus, the implication is that real change of speech requires real change of heart. You cannot simply edit your vocabulary; you must have the treasure changed—by repentance, by the indwelling word of Christ, and by the Spirit’s renewal. Conversely, because words are accountable acts, how we speak is one of the ways we either store up good treasure or confirm the evil treasure within.

So: **symptoms, choices, and revelations—all three.**
