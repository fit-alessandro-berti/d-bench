### Flattening — 3

The clearest issue is the answer’s repeated insistence that the verse has one simple, immediate, strictly causal meaning:

This flattens a compact aphorism into an overly rigid mechanism. “Where your treasure is, there will your heart be also” can plausibly carry more than one closely related implication: treasure may express what one already values, and deliberate investment may also draw and shape one’s attention and affection. The future wording—“there will your heart be”—can reasonably support the idea that practices of investment help orient the heart, even though the verse does not explicitly describe a gradual training process.
