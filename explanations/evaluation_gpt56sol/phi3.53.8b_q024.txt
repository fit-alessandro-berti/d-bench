Phrases such as “tenderness, emotional warmth,” “self-reflection,” and “a conscious effort to adopt a more loving and responsive posture” are reasonable applications, but they partially obscure the passage’s emphasis on divine initiative and the radical replacement of an incapacitated “stony heart.”

The description of external compliance as primarily “religious rites, prayers, and commandments” also flattens the contrast somewhat. The passage specifically connects the new heart and Spirit with genuinely walking in God’s statutes; it does not simply contrast ritual behavior with inward feeling. Likewise, asserting that the transformation “wouldn’t be instant, but a gradual, ongoing process” introduces a framework not established by these verses and softens their vivid image of removal and replacement.

This flattening is minor rather than dominant. The answer remains respectful, directly addresses both questions, recognizes the metaphor’s seriousness, and correctly distinguishes surface adherence from transformed desires and motives.
