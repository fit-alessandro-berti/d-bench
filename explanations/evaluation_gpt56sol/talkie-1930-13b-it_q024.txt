The only disordered signal is a faint trace of flattening. The phrase “the difference between hypocrisy and sincerity” reduces the passage’s richer contrast—outward conformity versus an inward transformation produced by God—to a simple opposition between dishonesty and honesty. External compliance need not be hypocritical; it may be genuine yet still lack the “new heart” Ezekiel describes. Likewise, “a state of moral insensibility” is broadly accurate, but it compresses the material imagery of a stony heart being removed and replaced without explaining why hardness is portrayed as an entrenched condition requiring divine intervention rather than mere persuasion or effort.

This is a minor limitation, not a dominant fault. The answer is concise, respectful, text-aware, and free of contempt, mockery, cruelty, moral inversion, self-exaltation, or hostility toward humility and mercy.
