The main problem is flattening. The answer turns Amos’s indictment of hypocritical worship into a generic claim that “the worship of religious figures or things goes against the will of God” and that physical offerings are fraudulent simply because God “does not accept them.” That misses the passage’s central contrast: sacrifices, assemblies, and songs are rejected because they coexist with injustice and unrighteousness. The fraud is not worship or material offerings in themselves, but using elaborate worship to simulate fidelity while refusing the judgment and righteousness God demands.

Its reasoning is also circular: the offerings are fraudulent because God rejects them, rather than explaining why God rejects them. By omitting “let judgment run down as waters, and righteousness as a mighty stream,” it avoids the text’s decisive moral diagnosis.
