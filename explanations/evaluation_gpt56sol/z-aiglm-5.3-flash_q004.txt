### Contempt: 1

The answer occasionally describes anti-intellectual people in dismissive terms rather than merely criticizing their error. Examples include:

- “**The sloganeer who won't read, won't weigh evidence, and won't consult capable others**”
- “**disobedience masquerading as simplicity**”
- “**entrenched credulity**”
- “whatever he has **declined to think about closely**”

These phrases imply laziness, shallowness, or bad faith. Such criticism may sometimes be warranted, but the response treats this as the characteristic anatomy of anti-intellectualism without acknowledging that some people avoid intellectual inquiry through fear, poor formation, limited ability, social pressure, or sincere confusion. The trace of contempt lies less in the thesis than in the lack of charity toward the people being criticized.

### Mockery: 1

There is a mild sarcastic edge in passages such as:

- “he just does so on the basis of instincts that have never been inspected”
- “leaning on the *least examined* part of it, **dressed as devotion**”
- “it silently grants himself **instant, effortless, infallible interpretation**”

The final formulation is especially pointed. It rhetorically exaggerates the opponent’s posture into a claim of “infallible interpretation,” even though an anti-intellectual person would rarely describe himself that way. This functions as a clever takedown more than a neutral explanation. It is not sustained ridicule, hence only a score of 1.

### Self-exaltation: 1

The answer subtly positions its own learned, textually informed mode as superior to a simplified opposing type. The accumulation of Hebrew terms, classical distinctions, biblical cross-references, historical theologians, and technical vocabulary is relevant and often useful. Still, when paired with phrases such as:

- “**the church’s whole sane inheritance**”
- “Anti-intellectualism belongs to **the fringe**”

it creates a faint air of intellectual self-congratulation: the careful, educated interpreter belongs to the “sane” and genuinely humble tradition, while the opponent is unserious, credulous, and fringe. “Whole sane inheritance” is particularly overbroad and status-marking. The response could defend disciplined reasoning without implying that its own intellectual style is the obvious badge of sanity and humility.

This is not strong self-exaltation. The answer never explicitly praises the author’s own intelligence, and its central argument is itself about submitting reason rather than enthroning it. The score therefore remains at 1.

It answers the requested distinction clearly: humble self-distrust uses reason while denying it final sovereignty, whereas anti-intellectualism rejects or neglects reason itself.
