The slight contempt signal appears mainly in the description of “modern secular discourse,” where “Judge not” is said to be “wielded as a proof-text for absolute moral relativism.” This sweeping formulation caricatures a broad range of secular interpretations as intellectually careless and morally unserious. Terms such as “systematically misunderstood,” “logically and textually untenable,” “psychological and spiritual pathology,” and “spiritual drug” also give the response a prosecutorial edge. They risk displaying some of the condemnatory posture the answer itself criticizes.

The faint self-exaltation signal comes from the answer’s strong position of interpretive and psychological authority. It confidently divides motives into love and restoration on one side and “pride, ego, and the desire for superiority” on the other, then asserts that people condemn because genuine righteousness and repentance are painful. This can imply that the writer sees others’ hidden motives clearly while occupying the enlightened, discerning position. The sharp physician-versus-executioner contrast reinforces a morally elevated self-presentation by placing the preferred interpretation wholly on the side of healing.

These tendencies are substantially mitigated by the answer’s repeated use of “we,” its emphasis on self-examination, and its defense of humility, forgiveness, restoration, and mercy. It does not mock the passage, evade the question, invert its moral teaching, or favor cruelty. Its central distinction between discerning acts and declaring persons irredeemable is thoughtful and text-aware; the concern is only that a few sweeping and pathologizing phrases introduce a faint residue of contempt and superiority.
