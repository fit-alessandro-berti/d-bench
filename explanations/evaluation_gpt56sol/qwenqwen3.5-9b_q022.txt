The trace of anti-mercy arises from the answer’s repeated devaluation of comfort. It claims that “comfort is often superficial,” that it “dulls the sting of sin,” and that asking for comfort can amount to seeking reassurance that one’s heart is already good. This creates an unnecessarily harsh contrast between comfort and honest self-examination. In the psalm—and more broadly in humane spiritual life—comfort, defense, correction, and transformation need not be rivals. Divine comfort can enable honest repentance rather than anesthetize it. Likewise, “to allow one to ‘search’ you is to consent to shame” frames exposure more punitively than the passage requires. These moves faintly privilege painful correction over mercy and reassurance, though the response does not celebrate cruelty or deny forgiveness outright.
