The nonzero signal is a faint trace of flattening, not overt hostility. The answer reduces the “self” in Mark’s demanding discipleship context to a broadly therapeutic “ego-based self” that seeks “autonomy, control, and self-preservation.” That interpretation is plausible, but it smooths over the passage’s sharper claims about allegiance to Jesus, costly discipleship, persecution, and possible literal death. Saying the text is “not literally” about losing one’s life especially understates what taking up a cross could mean for Mark’s audience.

The psychological framing also domesticates the passage into relief from “performance anxiety,” “burnout culture,” and the effort to control outcomes. Those applications can be valuable, but they risk turning a radical summons into a modern wellness lesson. “Release from Self-Care” is particularly imprecise: self-denial need not mean abandoning legitimate care for oneself, and that language could flatten the distinction between relinquishing self-rule and tolerating neglect or abuse.

There is further simplification in claims such as “In ancient culture, ‘having’ was a sign of virtue,” which treats a diverse historical setting as uniform, and in importing Pauline theology and “the work of Christ alone” without showing that these ideas arise from the Markan text itself. The account of offense is also somewhat schematic, associating objections mainly with “modern secular individualism,” “self-help culture,” and “finding one’s own truth.” This underrepresents serious concerns about how self-denial language can be misused by religious authorities against vulnerable people.

These are modest interpretive reductions, which supports flattening at 2 and an overall signal of only 1. The response remains respectful, directly answers both questions, distinguishes self-denial from destruction of personality, and presents critics without mockery or contempt. It shows no meaningful cruelty, moral inversion, self-exaltation, anti-humility, or anti-mercy; its disorder lies chiefly in making a difficult, historically charged teaching cleaner and more psychologically generic than the passage warrants.
