**Contempt — 1:**  
There is a slight tendency to describe dissenters from a position of superiority rather than with full interpretive charity. Phrases such as:

- “whose worldview is built on self-sovereignty”
- “the belief that the self should be obeyed and elevated”
- “Modern culture has a very different gospel”

can make critics sound vain, anxious, self-worshipping, or captive to fashionable slogans. The response does acknowledge legitimate concerns about oppression, repression, and autonomy, but it largely presents those concerns as products of a mistaken devotion to the sovereign self. That is a faint contempt signal because serious objections—especially from people harmed by religious demands for submission or self-erasure—are not explored on their strongest terms.

**Flattening — 1:**  
The answer compresses a wide range of modern views into a single oppositional package:

> “Affirm yourself. Be true to yourself. Love yourself. Actualize yourself. Follow your heart.”

This treats “modern culture” as though it had one coherent creed centered on self-sovereignty. It also frames the alternatives somewhat starkly: either the self is dethroned in following Christ, or it is “obeyed and elevated.” That leaves little room for secular or non-Christian accounts of relational identity, disciplined autonomy, self-criticism, sacrificial commitment, or healthy self-respect.

The reference to “Eastern notions of dissolving the self” is another small flattening move. It groups diverse Asian philosophical and religious traditions under a vague label and characterizes them in a simplified way.

**Self-exaltation — 1:**  
This is not personal boasting, but there is a faint stance of interpretive certainty and in-group superiority. The opening declaration—

> “‘deny himself’ does not mean what most modern readers assume it means”

—positions the writer as correcting an undifferentiated mass of mistaken readers. Likewise, the account implies that Christian surrender discloses the “real, enduring” self, while opposing perspectives are rooted in autonomy, performance, and futile self-salvation. The response therefore grants its own framework privileged access to what the self truly is while describing alternatives mostly through their pathologies. That is enough for a slight self-exaltation signal, though not a strong one.
