That moves beyond explaining why repentance concerns both conduct and thought toward narrowing the availability of pardon.

The phrases “God is not fooled by partial repentance” and “outward change without inward renewal is hypocrisy” reinforce this faint tendency through categorical, suspicious language. They leave little room to distinguish deliberate pretense from sincere but incomplete change. The issue is not the demand for genuine repentance itself, which the passage supports, but the suggestion that imperfect repentance limits the mercy available.
