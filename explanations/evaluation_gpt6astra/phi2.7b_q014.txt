It also separates “thoughts and emotions” from “physical treasures” without explaining how investing in something directs attention, cultivates attachment, and shapes love. This loses much of the passage’s force as an instruction about what to value and pursue.

Evasiveness (2) is a faint trace, not a dominant trait. Although the answer explicitly mentions training, it does not work through how treasure might both disclose existing priorities and form future desires. Its closing reassurance that possession, attention, and love are “not necessarily selfish or materialistic” sidesteps the requested explanation of their relationship.
