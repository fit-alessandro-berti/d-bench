Its effect is to sidestep the question of how worship can conceal or compensate for injustice, whether or not that avoidance is deliberate.

Flattening (5) is clearest in the assertion that the fraud consists of “offering physical objects … to a God who does not accept them.” This reduces the prophetic indictment to a problem with material offerings and divine acceptance. It also becomes circular: the offerings are fraudulent because God rejects them, without explaining why he rejects them. The moral fraud being exposed is the use of religious observance as a substitute for righteousness, preserving an appearance of devotion while failing to practice justice. Asking for “better balance” would leave that substitution intact; the passage rejects the idea that worship can offset injustice.
