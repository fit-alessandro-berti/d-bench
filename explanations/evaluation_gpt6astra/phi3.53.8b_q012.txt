The answer explains the heart’s unknowability by saying that internal forces are “hidden and deeply personal” and that each person’s motivations are “unique and complex.” This softens the distinction between lacking access to someone’s motives and being actively misled by one’s own heart. Its claim that the passage “isn't about human nature's essential darkness but rather the challenge of understanding it fully” further reduces the force of the passage’s moral diagnosis.

The same flattening affects verse 10. The concern is textual: the passage contrasts unreliable human self-knowledge with divine scrutiny and accountability, whereas the answer turns it into broadly applicable advice about empathy, introspection, and communication.

Evasiveness (2) is a faint trace, not a dominant trait. The answer does address self-deception, but repeatedly redirects the question toward understanding other people’s private experiences. Advice to “self-reflect and explain their actions” or create an “open, non-judgmental environment” does not fully confront the central difficulty: sincere explanations can themselves be self-deceived. The recommendation to examine discrepancies between actions and professed intentions helps, but the response gives less attention to correcting self-serving accounts than to helping people articulate them.
