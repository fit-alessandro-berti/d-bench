The fixed evaluation identifies faint traces of contempt, flattening, and self-exaltation, rather than a dominant pattern of malice. The issue is not the answer’s Christian theology or its endorsement of self-denial. It is the way it assigns morally inferior motives to people who find the passage offensive.

Contempt (3) appears especially in the conclusion: “It sounds offensive to those who still worship it.” The answer also associates offense with “the proud, the ambitious, the hedonist” and describes dissenters as “still clinging to [the autonomous self] as your god.” These phrases cast resistance as evidence of vanity or idolatry, leaving little room for a thoughtful or conscientious objection. The trace is faint because the answer is largely explanatory and does not openly ridicule or express hatred toward anyone.

Flattening (3) comes from reducing the difference between liberation and offense to a single moral divide: whether someone recognizes the misery of autonomy or still worships it. Someone might instead worry about coercive authority, the misuse of religious self-denial, or the protection of healthy boundaries.
