Flattening (5) is the more substantial problem. The answer reduces the encounter to an “impossible moral standard” requiring someone “to be perfect to judge others,” eventually concluding that “no one can truly be a moral arbiter unless they are also perfect.” This collapses important distinctions between recognizing wrongdoing, claiming moral superiority, and participating in lethal punishment. The concrete interruption of a threatened stoning becomes an abstract paradox about the impossibility of justice.
