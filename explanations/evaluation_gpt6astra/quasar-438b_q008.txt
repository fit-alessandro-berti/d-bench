The evasiveness score reflects how the response obstructs its own answer with enormous repetitions of “Jesus” and “apost,” a regeneration instruction, and repeated drafts. This captures the visible-versus-invisible contrast but risks reducing a relationship of love to a verification procedure. Statements such as “God is distant, silent, and infinitely interpretable” also turn invisibility into a sweeping claim about divine absence or indeterminacy. Calling God an “abstraction” can fit the hypocrite’s self-deception, but the response sometimes blurs that perspective with its own account of God.

These traces are limited by substantial strengths. The nonzero scores therefore concern obstructive presentation and slight interpretive reduction, rather than the answer’s central moral conclusion.
