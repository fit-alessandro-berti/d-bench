Contempt: 1 — a faint trace of dismissive framing

The clearest example is the heading “It offends the Modern Cult of Self-Actualization.” Calling it a “cult” adds a disparaging charge beyond explaining the tension between discipleship and individual autonomy. The paragraph then groups “self-care, self-expression, self-sufficiency, and ‘being true to yourself’” together as modern ideals opposed to Jesus’s teaching.

The concern is not that the answer criticizes self-centeredness. It is that this grouping lightly belittles pursuits that can also involve healthy boundaries, integrity, creativity, or recovery from abuse. It risks making people who resist self-denial sound merely attached to their egos.

Likewise, “the part of us that demands to be the god of our own lives” can be a legitimate theological description of pride. In conjunction with the closing contrast, however, it risks casting concerns about autonomy as presumptuous self-enthronement. That is a faint contempt signal, not overt hostility or mockery.
